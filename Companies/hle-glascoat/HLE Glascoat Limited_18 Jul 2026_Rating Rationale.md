## **Rationale**

The reaffirmation of the ratings factors in HLE Glascoat Limited's (HGL) established market position in the domestic filtration and drying equipment (F&D) industry — where the HLE brand enjoys leadership, including in agitated nutsche filter dryers — and its strong presence in the glass-lined equipment (GLE) segment in India, complemented by the European franchise of its wholly-owned subsidiary, Thaletec GmbH. The ratings also factor in the substantial improvement in the Group's scale in FY2026, with consolidated operating income growing 31.7% to Rs. 1,353.0 crore (FY2025: Rs. 1,027.6 crore), supported by all three segments — in H1 FY2026, F&D revenue grew 88.5% and heat transfer equipment (HTE) 105.5% year-on-year — and a comfortable order book of Rs. 722.2 crore as on September 30, 2025, which provides healthy revenue visibility.

The Group completed two structural transactions during FY2026. The Scheme of Amalgamation of Kinam Enterprise Private Limited with HGL became effective on receipt of the NCLT order dated August 14, 2025 (appointed date August 7, 2023), taking HGL's economic interest in the Kinam heat-transfer business to 70% through the issuance of 11,89,259 equity shares, with no incremental cash outflow. Additionally, through its step-down subsidiary HLE Surface Technologies GmbH, the Group acquired the business of Omeras GmbH and the shares of Omerastore GmbH (August 2025; EUR 2.75 million), marking its entry into glass-fused-steel products for storage, biogas and architectural applications. While these transactions widen the product portfolio and end-user base, the recently acquired Omeras business reported an initial loss (Rs. 3.6 crore up to September 2025), which — together with continued pricing pressure in the domestic GLE business and higher employee and labour costs — compressed the consolidated OPBDITA margin to 10.3% in FY2026 (FY2025: 13.0%; margins stated excluding other income; the Company's published EBITDA margin including other income was 13.7% for FY2025).

The ratings remain constrained by the moderate debt-protection metrics: while total debt declined to Rs. 347.6 crore as on March 31, 2026 (March 31, 2025: Rs. 380.7 crore) and the capital structure remains comfortable (gearing of 0.56 times; TOL/TNW of 1.24 times), Total debt/OPBDITA stood at 2.49 times (net of cash and bank balances: 2.07 times) and interest coverage at 4.06 times in FY2026, with a thin DSCR of 1.15 times against scheduled repayments. The operations also remain working-capital intensive on account of the long manufacturing cycle and high inventory holding, although the working-capital cycle improved materially to 129 days in FY2026 (FY2024: 165 days) and NWC/OI to 20.2%, supporting cash flow from operations of Rs. 161.2 crore and positive free cash flow of Rs. 98.1 crore in FY2026. The Group's profitability remains exposed to volatility in steel and alloy prices, given the long production cycle, with procurement largely order-backed mitigating this risk in part, and to the cyclicality of capital expenditure in the key end-user industries — pharmaceuticals, specialty chemicals and agrochemicals.

ICRA expects consolidated revenue growth of 10–12% per annum over FY2027–FY2028, supported by the order book, a full-year contribution from the Omeras business and favourable end-user capex momentum, with the OPBDITA margin recovering gradually to 11.5–12.5% as the acquired operations turn around and operating leverage improves. On this basis, Total debt/OPBDITA is expected to decline below 2.0 times in FY2027 (base case: 1.88 times) with interest coverage of 5.0–6.5 times; the pace of margin recovery, the turnaround of the Omeras business and the working-capital trajectory remain the key monitorables.

The Stable outlook on the long-term rating reflects ICRA's opinion that HGL will sustain its established market position and improving credit metrics, aided by its order book and completed integrations, while maintaining an adequate liquidity position.

## **Key rating drivers and their description**

### **Credit strengths**

**Established market position in filtration & drying and glass-lined equipment, with international franchises** – HGL is a leader in the domestic F&D equipment segment, where its agitated nutsche filters and dryers are entrenched with pharmaceutical and chemical customers, and holds a strong position in the domestic GLE segment. The December 2021 acquisition of Thaletec GmbH provides an established European glass-lining franchise with a US service presence, and the August 2025 Omeras transaction adds glass-fused-steel products, extending the Group's addressable market into storage tanks, biogas digesters and architectural facades. The business remains technology- and capital-intensive with few established manufacturers, supporting entry barriers.

**Improved scale and diversification; healthy order book provides revenue visibility** – Consolidated operating income grew 31.7% in FY2026 to Rs. 1,353.0 crore, with growth across all three segments and geographies (four plants in Western India, two in Germany). The completion of the Kinam transaction (70% economic interest) consolidates the heat-transfer equipment segment, which more than doubled its H1 FY2026 revenue year-on-year, and the order book of Rs. 722.2 crore as on September 30, 2025 (about 0.7 times FY2025 revenue) underpins near-term revenue visibility.

**Comfortable capital structure with improving working-capital efficiency and cash generation** – The capital structure remains comfortable, with gearing of 0.56 times and TOL/TNW of 1.24 times as on March 31, 2026 (0.79 and 1.73 times, respectively, as on March 31, 2023, despite the debt-funded Kinam phase-1 acquisition in the interim). The working-capital cycle shortened to 129 days in FY2026 from 165 days in FY2024, releasing cash and supporting CFO of Rs. 161.2 crore and free cash flow of Rs. 98.1 crore in FY2026; gross debt declined by Rs. 33.0 crore during the year despite the Omeras acquisition outflow. The promoters hold 66.69% (March 31, 2025) with no share pledge disclosed, and the Group's listed status (market capitalisation of about Rs. 2,931 crore, July 2026 — about 8.4 times total debt) supports financial flexibility.

### **Credit challenges**

**Compressed operating margin; turnaround of acquired operations yet to be demonstrated** – The consolidated OPBDITA margin declined to 10.3% in FY2026 from 13.0% in FY2025, reflecting the initial loss in the newly acquired Omeras business (Rs. 3.6 crore up to September 2025), pricing pressure in the domestic GLE segment and higher employee and labour costs (up 31% in FY2026). Quarterly earnings also remained volatile (consolidated PAT of Rs. 4.6 crore in Q3 FY2026 against Rs. 20.1 crore in Q4 FY2026). A sustained recovery in margins — the key driver of the expected improvement in coverage metrics — is yet to be demonstrated.

**Moderate debt-protection metrics with thin near-term DSCR** – Total debt/OPBDITA of 2.49 times (FY2026) remains above 2.0 times, interest coverage was 4.06 times and the DSCR was thin at 1.15 times against scheduled term repayments (Rs. 76.1 crore in FY2026, including a one-time unsecured-loan repayment). While the base case indicates Total debt/OPBDITA declining below 2.0 times in FY2027 with DSCR improving to about 1.6 times, this is contingent on the margin recovery materialising.

**Working-capital-intensive operations; input-price and end-user-cycle exposure** – The long manufacturing cycle necessitates high inventory holding (96–131 days over FY2024–FY2026), and marquee customers command extended credit (debtor days of 73 in FY2026). Steel and alloys constitute the dominant raw-material cost, and although procurement is largely order-backed, margins remain exposed to input-price volatility with a pass-through lag. The end-user industries — pharmaceuticals, specialty chemicals and agrochemicals — are capital-expenditure cyclical, as evidenced by the FY2024 downturn (Total debt/OPBDITA of 3.48 times).

## **Environmental and social risks**

**Environmental considerations** – The Group's glass-lining operations are energy-intensive (electric and gas-fired furnaces). Mitigants include increasing reliance on renewable energy, the commissioning of a captive power generation facility (March 2026) and a long-term captive hybrid power purchase arrangement. The Group's customers in pharmaceuticals and chemicals are themselves exposed to tightening environmental norms, linking HGL's prospects in part to its customers' compliance capability.

**Social considerations** – As a specialised capital-goods manufacturer, the Group depends on skilled human capital, and retention of technical personnel remains essential. Product quality and safety are critical given the critical-process applications of its equipment; the Group's long track record with leading pharmaceutical and chemical customers mitigates this risk.

## **Liquidity position: Adequate**

The Group's liquidity is adequate. Unencumbered cash and bank balances stood at Rs. 58.5 crore as on March 31, 2026, and expected net cash accruals of about Rs. 110 crore in FY2027 compare with scheduled repayments of about Rs. 62 crore, maintenance-oriented capital expenditure of about Rs. 60 crore and dividends of about Rs. 11 crore, leaving an arithmetic surplus of about Rs. 47 crore over the next 12 months before working-capital headroom (cash-credit limits of Rs. 184.08 crore against estimated working-capital drawings of about Rs. 151 crore). Month-wise utilisation data of working-capital limits is to be confirmed by the issuer; the descriptor reflects the working-capital-intensive business model and the thin FY2026 DSCR.

## **Rating sensitivities**

**Positive factors** – ICRA could upgrade the ratings if a sustained recovery in operating profitability (OPBDITA margin of 12% or higher) and continued deleveraging result in Total debt/OPBDITA below 2.0 times on a sustained basis, along with a strengthening liquidity profile.

**Negative factors** – Pressure on the ratings could arise if the margin recovery does not materialise (OPBDITA margin sustained at or below 10%), if a stretch in the working-capital cycle or any sizeable debt-funded acquisition or capital expenditure results in Total debt/OPBDITA above 2.5 times on a sustained basis, or if the DSCR remains near 1.1 times.

## **About the company**

HLE Glascoat Limited (formerly Swiss Glascoat Equipments Limited), incorporated in 1991 and listed on the BSE (522215) and NSE (HLEGLAS), manufactures specialised chemical process equipment: filtration and drying equipment (agitated nutsche filters and dryers), glass-lined equipment (reactors, columns, dryers) and, through Kinam, heat-transfer equipment (shell-and-tube, corrugated-tube and spiral heat exchangers). The Surat-based Patel promoter group holds 66.69% (March 31, 2025). The Group operates four manufacturing facilities in Western India (Anand, Maroli, Silvassa, Ambernath) and two in Germany (Thale; Lauter-Bernsbach), serving pharmaceutical, specialty-chemical, agrochemical, dyes and pigments, oil & gas and infrastructure customers. In FY2026, the Group reported a consolidated operating income of Rs. 1,353.0 crore and a profit after tax of Rs. 56.6 crore.

***Step 14 divergence against the agency's own action of 17 July 2026, disclosed not forced (failure mode 32).*** *Every row above reproduces ICRA's published figures — operating income 1,027.6 / 1,353.0, PAT 61.8 / 56.6, OPBDIT/OI 13.0% / 10.3%, TOL/TNW and total debt/OPBDIT to their rounding — **except interest coverage**, where this report shows 3.69 / 4.06 against the agency's 3.5 / 3.9. The gap is ~5% in FY2025 and ~4% in FY2026, consistently in the same direction, which indicates a **denominator definition difference rather than an extraction error**: coverage here is OPBDITA over finance costs exactly as printed in the profit and loss account, whereas the agency's figure implies a denominator about 4-5% larger, most likely including lease interest or other financing charges reported outside the finance-cost caption. **The inputs are not adjusted to close it** — a definition that will not reproduce is a definition to disclose, and the precise composition of the agency's denominator is not stated in its publication. The direction of travel (coverage improving year on year) is the same on either definition.*
