## Rationale

The ratings reaffirmation, and the resolution of the Rating Watch with Positive implications (placed on December 12, 2025), factor in the successful completion of JSW Steel Limited's (JSL) strategic 50:50 joint venture with Japan's JFE Steel Corporation for the Bhushan Power and Steel Limited (BPSL) business, which has materially improved the company's consolidated leverage and liquidity profile. Following the transfer of the BPSL steel undertaking via slump sale into the JSW–JFE JV (JSW Kalinga Steel/JSW Sambalpur Steel) and its deconsolidation from end-FY2026, JSL's consolidated net debt reduced to about Rs. 53,900 crore as on March 31, 2026 (company-reported) from about Rs. 80,300 crore as on December 31, 2025, and consolidated **net debt/OPBDITA improved to about 1.9 times in FY2026 (company-reported 1.81 times) from about 3.5 times in FY2025** — better than the 2.1–2.2 times ICRA had earlier expected for FY2027–FY2028. The transaction brought in JFE equity (first tranche of Rs. 7,875 crore received in March 2026, the balance expected by the first half of FY2027) and, together with the deconsolidation of BPSL's ~Rs. 5,000 crore of net debt, delivered aggregate deleveraging of about Rs. 37,000 crore, while retaining JSL's 50% economic interest and adding JFE as a long-term technology partner.

The ratings continue to derive strong support from JSL's position as India's largest steel producer, with a large and rising scale of operations (consolidated operating income of Rs. 185,470 crore and crude steel production of about 30.1 million tonnes in FY2026), a diversified flat-and-long product portfolio, geographically spread manufacturing footprint, and a healthy domestic market position (about 90% of sales in the domestic market, with India capacity utilisation of about 96% in FY2026). Operating profitability recovered in FY2026, with the OPBDITA margin rising to about 16.1% (from a cyclical low of 13.6% in FY2025) and blended EBITDA per tonne for Indian operations improving through the year, aided by higher realisations supported by the safeguard duty (12%, in force from December 2025) and volume growth. ICRA also draws comfort from management's stated financial discipline: the Annual Report discloses self-imposed leverage caps of 3.75 times net debt/OPBDITA and 1.75 times net debt/equity (FY2026 actuals of 1.81 times and 0.51 times respectively, per the company, are well within these), with management guidance indicating an intent to tighten the net debt/OPBDITA ceiling further towards 3.0 times.

These strengths are tempered by the inherent cyclicality of the steel industry and JSL's exposure to volatile raw-material costs, particularly imported coking coal (captive coking coal remains limited, with a target of about 50% self-sufficiency only by FY2031), and to a lesser extent iron ore (about a third met captively currently). The most significant rating sensitivity is JSL's **large, largely debt-funded capital-expenditure programme**: the Board has approved total capex of about Rs. 1.26 lakh crore over FY2027–FY2031 to raise India steel capacity from about 31.9 mtpa to about 48.8 mtpa by FY2030 (and, with JV capacity, to a targeted ~78 mtpa by FY2032), spanning brownfield expansions at Vijayanagar and Dolvi, greenfield projects at Jagatsinghpur (Odisha) via JSW Utkal Steel, the BMM Ispat acquisition, and new JVs with JFE and POSCO. This programme, with FY2027 capex guided at about Rs. 22,000–24,000 crore, will absorb a large share of internal accruals and could pressure leverage — particularly if the steel cycle weakens — even as the recent monetisation has created headroom. Coverage and return metrics remain modest for the rating (interest coverage of about 3.3 times and RoCE of about 10.5% in FY2026), reflecting the capital intensity and current point in the cycle.

The Stable outlook reflects ICRA's opinion that JSL will sustain its market leadership and improved leverage profile through FY2027, with the deleveraging benefit of the JFE transaction and cyclical earnings recovery providing a cushion against the elevated capex commitments, and that management's leverage ceiling will anchor financial policy through the expansion phase.

**A note on FY2026 reported earnings:** JSL's FY2026 consolidated profit after tax (Rs. 25,508 crore; Rs. 22,316 crore attributable to owners) is inflated by a **non-cash exceptional gain of Rs. 18,051 crore** on the BPSL slump sale into the JV. On the company's own disclosure, **normalised net profit excluding exceptionals was Rs. 8,698 crore in FY2026** (Rs. 3,475 crore in Q4 FY2026); ICRA's assessment is based on operating performance and this normalised earnings level, not the exceptional-inflated headline.

## Key rating drivers and their description

### Credit strengths

**India's largest steel producer with a large scale, diversified product mix and strong market position** – JSL is the largest steel producer in India with consolidated operating income of Rs. 185,470 crore and crude steel production of about 30.1 million tonnes in FY2026, operating a diversified flat-and-long product portfolio (HR/CR coils, galvanised, electrical and value-added grades, plus long products) across a multi-location footprint (Vijayanagar, Dolvi, Salem and others), with overseas operations in the US and Italy. Scale, product breadth and a ~90% domestic sales mix provide resilience through the steel cycle, well in excess of the sector's "strongest" scale threshold.

**Materially improved leverage following the JFE joint venture; stated financial discipline** – The completed BPSL–JFE transaction deleveraged the consolidated balance sheet by about Rs. 37,000 crore, taking net debt/OPBDITA to about 1.9 times in FY2026 from about 3.5 times in FY2025, and TOL/TNW to about 1.56 times (from 1.95 times). Management maintains disclosed leverage caps of 3.75 times net debt/OPBDITA and 1.75 times net debt/equity (FY2026 actuals 1.81 times and 0.51 times per the company), with guidance to tighten towards 3.0 times — signalling balance-sheet discipline through the expansion phase; the JFE partnership also adds technological depth in value-added products.

**Partial raw-material integration and captive power, supporting cost competitiveness** – JSL benefits from partial backward integration (about a third of iron ore met captively, with 13 operational mines and a target of ~50% by FY2032; coking coal integration being built via the Mozambique MdR and Australian Illawarra assets), captive power, and a natural hedge on imported coal via import-parity-linked realisations.

### Credit challenges

**Large, largely debt-funded capex programme is the principal rating sensitivity** – The approved ~Rs. 1.26 lakh crore capex over FY2027–FY2031 (India capacity to ~48.8 mtpa by FY2030, targeted ~78 mtpa including JVs by FY2032) will consume a large share of internal accruals and expose the company to project-execution, funding and offtake risks. Should the steel cycle weaken during the build-out, consolidated leverage could rise again despite the current headroom. This maps directly to the negative rating sensitivity below.

**Inherent cyclicality and exposure to volatile input costs and imports** – Steel is highly cyclical; profitability swung from an OPBDITA margin of 16.1% (FY2024) to 13.6% (FY2025) and back to 16.1% (FY2026). JSL remains exposed to imported coking coal price and currency volatility (an unrealised forex loss of about Rs. 1,080 crore was booked in Q4 FY2026 on USD appreciation), and to low-priced imports that pressure domestic realisations — partly mitigated, for now, by the safeguard duty.

**Modest coverage and return metrics for the rating** – Interest coverage (about 3.3 times) and RoCE (about 10.5%) in FY2026 are modest, reflecting capital intensity and the current point in the cycle; sustained improvement depends on the earnings ramp from new capacity and a supportive pricing environment.

**Contingent liabilities, impairment and group-complexity monitorables** – The auditor's Key Audit Matters (FY2026 consolidated) flag: (a) disclosed contingent liabilities of about Rs. 11,148 crore (Rs. 5,008 crore of disputed tax/legal claims and Rs. 6,140 crore of forest-development-tax/fee claims); (b) recoverability of about Rs. 10,700 crore of goodwill/PP&E/CWIP/advances relating to certain loss-making businesses or projects on hold; and (c) significant related-party transactions within a complex group (46 subsidiaries, 19 joint ventures and 5 associates as on March 31, 2026), including corporate guarantees provided on behalf of overseas subsidiaries (e.g. JSW Steel Netherlands, Periama Holdings, Acero Junction) and loan-loss allowances against certain overseas exposures. These are manageable relative to JSL's scale and net worth but remain monitorables from an asset-quality and governance perspective.

## Environmental and social risks

**Environmental considerations** – Steelmaking is energy- and emissions-intensive; JSL, as a primary BF-BOF producer, has high carbon intensity and faces transition risk (including the EU CBAM, effective January 1, 2026, on exports) and rising decarbonisation capex needs. Mitigants include a growing renewable-power portfolio (1 GW commissioned, further 2.5 GW approved) and efficiency initiatives, but the transition entails significant long-term investment.

**Social considerations** – The sector carries health-and-safety, land-acquisition and rehabilitation/resettlement risks, especially for greenfield projects (e.g. Odisha). Community and labour issues can delay expansion timelines and raise costs; these are pertinent given the scale of the planned build-out.

## Liquidity position: Adequate

JSL's liquidity is Adequate. Against sizeable scheduled repayments and a heavy capex commitment (about Rs. 22,000–24,000 crore in FY2027), the company had consolidated cash and current liquid investments of about Rs. 41,500 crore as on March 31, 2026, supported by strong operating cash generation (consolidated OPBDITA of about Rs. 29,821 crore in FY2026), the incoming second JFE tranche (about Rs. 7,875 crore), and demonstrated financial flexibility as a large, listed issuer with strong banking access. The improved net-debt position and management's 3.0x leverage ceiling underpin the descriptor; however, the descriptor is Adequate rather than Strong given the magnitude of committed capex and the working-capital and refinancing needs typical of a large steel operation.

## Rating sensitivities

**Positive factors** – The rating could be upgraded if JSL sustains net debt/OPBDITA comfortably below ~2.0 times and improves interest coverage and RoCE on a sustained basis, while executing its expansion without a material deterioration in the financial risk profile.

**Negative factors** – Pressure could arise if the large, debt-funded capex programme (or a cyclical downturn in steel prices/spreads) drives consolidated net debt/OPBDITA above ~3.0 times or Total debt/OPBDITA above ~4.5 times on a sustained basis, or if interest coverage weakens materially; a large debt-funded acquisition or slippage in project execution/funding would also be negative.

## About the company

JSW Steel Limited (JSL), the flagship company of the JSW Group, is the largest steel producer in India, with installed capacity of about 31.9 mtpa in India (and additional overseas capacity in the US and Italy). It manufactures a diversified range of flat and long steel products across plants at Vijayanagar (Karnataka), Dolvi (Maharashtra), Salem (Tamil Nadu) and other locations. In FY2026, JSL entered a 50:50 JV with JFE Steel (Japan) for the BPSL business and announced a further 50:50 greenfield JV with POSCO (South Korea) in Odisha. The promoter (Sajjan Jindal group) held about 45.32% as on March 31, 2026 (rising towards ~46.7% following the Piombino Steel merger), with a portion of promoter holding pledged.
