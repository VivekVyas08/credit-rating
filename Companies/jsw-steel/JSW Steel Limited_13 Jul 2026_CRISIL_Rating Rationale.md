### <u>Basis of preparation — one paragraph, so every number below reconciles</u>

Two debt bases appear in this report and in the model workbook, always labelled: **(A) AR headline basis** — borrowings excluding leases (Rs 95,532 crore FY26) minus cash and cash equivalents (Rs 41,662 crore) = **net debt Rs 53,870 crore; ND/EBITDA 1.81x** — the figures the company itself publishes in its net-debt bridge; and **(B) Model basis** — total debt including lease liabilities (Rs 99,310 crore) minus cash and current liquid investments (Rs 41,507 crore) = **net debt Rs 57,803 crore; ND/EBITDA 1.94x** — the more conservative measure used in the scorecard and projections. Acceptances (Rs 19,817 crore) sit in trade payables under Ind AS and are outside both; adding them raises FY26 leverage by ~0.7x in a sensitivity. All three bases are computed side-by-side in the model's **Debt Breakup tab**, and the statutory totals (87,984 / 98,752 / 99,310 for FY24/25/26) tie exactly to the AR balance sheet and screener.in.

### <u>Detailed Rationale</u>

Crisil Ratings has assigned its 'Crisil AA/Stable/Crisil A1+' ratings to the bank facilities, non-convertible debentures and commercial paper programme of JSW Steel Limited (JSL).

**Operating performance recovered strongly in fiscal 2026.** Consolidated operating income grew 9.9% to Rs 185,470 crore (Rs 168,824 crore in fiscal 2025), on sales volumes of ~30.1 million tonnes (up ~10%), a blended realisation of ~Rs 61,600/tonne, support from the 12% safeguard duty on flat products, and moderating coking-coal costs. EBITDA rose 30% to Rs 29,821 crore, with the margin recovering to 16.1% from the fiscal 2025 trough of 13.6% — EBITDA/tonne of ~Rs 9,900 against ~Rs 8,600 in fiscal 2025. Normalised PAT was ~Rs 8,698 crore (reported PAT of Rs 22,316 crore includes the Rs 18,051 crore non-cash exceptional gain on the BPSL transaction and is not comparable).

**The defining credit event: BPSL moved into the 50:50 JSW-JFE joint venture (March 27, 2026), transforming leverage.** The two-phase transaction will deliver ~Rs 37,250 crore of total deleveraging per the AR. Phase one is in the fiscal 2026 numbers: on the AR's own net-debt bridge, consolidated net debt fell from Rs 76,563 crore to **Rs 53,870 crore**, taking **ND/EBITDA to 1.81x (AR basis) from 3.34x** — the best print since fiscal 2022 (the AR's six-year series: 2.56x/1.35x/3.13x/2.58x/3.34x/1.81x for FY21-26) — with net gearing of 0.51x against the company's stated caps of 3.75x ND/EBITDA and 1.75x ND/equity. The second tranche of **Rs 7,875 crore** of JFE equity is expected in June 2026. The weighted average interest rate at Indian operations fell 92 bps to 6.17% at March 2026.

**The forward question is capex discipline through the cycle — and the base case answers it comfortably.** JSL has guided fiscal 2027 capex at Rs 22,000-24,000 crore (vs Rs 15,595 crore spent in fiscal 2026) within a Rs 1,26,161 crore programme over 4-5 years, taking capacity from ~35.7 mtpa (30 mtpa India) towards ~50 mtpa by fiscal 2031. Crisil Ratings' 5-year base case (model workbook, this case): revenue Rs 178,051 crore in fiscal 2027 (dip on BPSL deconsolidation, then ~9% growth) rising to Rs 249,028 crore by fiscal 2031; EBITDA Rs 29,378 → 43,580 crore (margin 16.5% → 17.5% on integration gains); **net debt/EBITDA (model basis) holding at 1.92x → 1.59x through the programme** — well inside the management ceiling of 3.0x. Under stress (revenue growth −5pp and margin −250bps every year, the Iron & Steel methodology's named variables), fiscal 2028 interest coverage stays ~2.75x and net leverage ~2.5x — above management's comfort ceiling but inside the 3.75x covenant, with deferrable capex as the lever.

The ratings therefore reflect JSL's dominant business risk profile — India's largest steelmaker with rising value-added mix and deepening integration — and a structurally improved financial risk profile, partially offset by steel-cycle exposure, the scale of the capex programme, and moderate promoter pledge (~11.8%).

### <u>Key Rating Drivers - Strengths</u>

### Dominant market position in Indian steel — the sector benchmark on CRISIL's market-position factors

India's largest steelmaker: ~35.7 mtpa consolidated installed capacity, ~30.1 mt sold in fiscal 2026, pan-India manufacturing (Vijayanagar — the world's largest single-location steel plant — Dolvi, Salem, plus JVML), a rising value-added and special-products share (~60% of sales), export franchise across 100+ countries, and brand/distribution depth in retail (JSW Shoppe). On the factors CRISIL's criteria name for market position — scale, product mix, distribution, pricing access — JSL is the benchmark. Domestic steel demand growth of ~8-9% and the safeguard duty support realisations through the ramp to ~50 mtpa by fiscal 2031.

### Deepening backward integration structurally improving the cost curve

Captive iron ore meets ~50% of requirement (13 operating mines in Karnataka/Odisha); coking-coal integration is targeted at ~25% by fiscal 2031 via Mozambique, the announced Australian JV and domestic washeries; captive power (incl. renewables tie-ups) and Rs ~15,000 crore of cost-saving projects sit inside the capex programme. EBITDA/tonne recovered to ~Rs 9,900 in fiscal 2026, with broker consensus (case documents) building towards ~Rs 10,800 by fiscal 2028; each Rs 1,000/tonne is ~Rs 3,000 crore of EBITDA at current volumes — the integration programme is the margin-trajectory driver in the base case.

### Structurally improved financial risk profile with strong flexibility

Post-BPSL leverage is the strongest since fiscal 2022 on either basis (1.81x AR / 1.94x model); interest coverage 3.3x (2.7x fiscal 2025); TOL/TNW improved to 1.56x (1.95x); tangible net worth Rs 105,475 crore. Gross cash accruals of ~Rs 18,300 crore (normalised PAT + depreciation) against manageable maturities; financial flexibility from a ~Rs 3.17 lakh crore market capitalisation, demonstrated access to global capital (US$3.84 bn of international bonds since 2014; US$1.8 bn syndicated ECBs in the past 24 months; funding mix 64% FC/36% INR), the incoming Rs 7,875 crore JFE tranche, and a falling cost of debt (6.17%).

### <u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>

### Steel-cycle exposure with weak input-output price linkage

Realisations are import-parity-linked while coking coal is globally priced — the weak linkage CRISIL's criteria flag for volatile margins. Fiscal 2025 is the live example: margin 13.6% and ND/EBITDA 3.34x (AR basis) only twelve months before the current print. Safeguard-duty continuation is a policy variable, not a structural mitigant; China supply behaviour remains the key exogenous risk.

### Aggressive capex programme and moderate promoter pledge

The Rs 1,26,161 crore programme is large relative to existing operations — an explicit risk-appetite consideration under CRISIL's management-risk criteria — and fiscal 2027 spend nearly doubles fiscal 2026's. Promoter pledge (~11.8% of promoter holding), though reduced, is a monitorable. Mitigants: phased and deferrable capex (65% steelmaking growth, discrete modules), the 3.0x internal leverage ceiling with a demonstrated deleveraging track record, JFE equity participation, and entering the cycle at 1.8-1.9x leverage rather than the 3.3-3.5x of the prior capex phase.

### <u>Liquidity:</u> Strong

Sources over the next 12 months: cash and cash equivalents of Rs 41,662 crore (AR; Rs 41,507 crore counting only cash + current liquid investments), gross cash accruals of ~Rs 18,300-19,000 crore, the Rs 7,875 crore JFE tranche (June 2026), and meaningful undrawn fund-based limits. Claims: guided capex of Rs 22,000-24,000 crore, scheduled debt maturities (manageable; weighted maturity extended by recent refinancing), dividends (~Rs 1,700-1,800 crore at Rs 7.1/share), and working capital. Coverage of claims by sources is comfortable without assuming refinancing; the large committed capex is what keeps the descriptor at Strong rather than Superior.

### <u>ESG Profile</u>

Steel is hard-to-abate; JSL's stated trajectory — renewable share rising (RE tie-ups at Vijayanagar/Dolvi), ~38-42% specific-emission reduction pathway, slag valorisation — is among the more articulated in Indian steel, and the JFE partnership adds technology depth (electrical steel). Governance: professional board with established disclosures; promoter pledge and group-company transactions are the monitorables. ESG is credit-neutral at the current rating level; carbon-border mechanisms (EU CBAM from 2026) are a medium-term exports monitorable.

### <u>Outlook:</u> Stable

Crisil Ratings expects JSL to sustain net leverage well below its 3.0x ceiling through the capex cycle, supported by volume growth, integration-led margin gains and demonstrated financial discipline.

### <u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>

**Upward factors** — Net debt/EBITDA sustained below 1.5x through the capex programme with EBITDA/tonne above ~Rs 11,000; elimination of promoter pledge; completed coking-coal integration reducing margin volatility.

**Downward factors** — Net debt/EBITDA above 3.0x on a sustained basis (capex outspend into a downcycle); interest coverage below 2.5x; a large additional debt-funded acquisition; material increase in promoter pledge.

### <u>About the Company</u>

JSW Steel Limited, flagship of the JSW Group, is India's largest steel producer: ~35.7 mtpa consolidated installed capacity across Vijayanagar (Karnataka), Dolvi (Maharashtra), Salem (Tamil Nadu), JVML and overseas units (Ohio/Texas, USA; Piombino, Italy), with a roadmap to ~50 mtpa by fiscal 2031. Products span HRC, CRC, coated products, TMT, wire rods, alloy steel and electrical steel (JFE partnership). BPSL (4.5 mtpa) moved to a 50:50 JV with JFE Steel on March 27, 2026. Promoter holding ~44.9% (~11.8% of it pledged).
