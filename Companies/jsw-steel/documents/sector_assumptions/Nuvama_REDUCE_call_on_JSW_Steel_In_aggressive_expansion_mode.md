## **In aggressive expansion mode** 

JSW reported better-than-expected consolidated adjusted EBITDA of INR92.5bn (our estimate: INR84.3bn), up ~40% QoQ, driven by higher steel prices and volume growth, partly offset by higher RM cost. Indian operation’s adjusted EBITDA was INR91.1bn, up ~40% QoQ, while adjusted EBITDA/t increased to INR11,622 (up INR2,837 QoQ). 

We expect Q1FY27E EBITDA/t to rise by INR800–1,000 QoQ amid higher steel prices, offset by higher cost. JSTL will add ~17mtpa steel capacity by FY30 at an investment of ~INR1,262bn. We deconsolidate Bhushan Power. We increase FY27E/28E EPS by ~12%/18% factoring in higher margins. We have a TP of INR1,129 (earlier INR1,057) at 9.5x FY28E EV/EBITDA; ‘REDUCE’. Stock trades at 10.6x FY28E EV/EBITDA. 

#### **India: Higher prices and volume boost earnings** 

The company has recognised one-off grant income of INR4.99bn (INR4.62bn is for earlier duration) in other operating income. This is after Directorate of Industries approved to transfer incentives available to the Dolvi Coke Project Ltd. earlier (the company merged with JSW Steel in FY20). Indian operations recorded adjusted EBITDA (excluding unrealised forex loss and one-off grant income) of INR91.1bn, up 40% QoQ, and EBITDA/t of INR11,622, up INR2,837/t QoQ. This was due to higher steel prices (increase of ~INR3,800/t QoQ), higher volume (up 5.6% QoQ to 7.84mt) amid inventory liquidation of 0.7mt, partially offset by higher coking coal cost (up USD16/t QoQ). Management stated that iron ore cost remains almost flat QoQ. They have guided that higher steel prices will offset overall cost increase of ~INR3,000/t QoQ (higher RM cost + freight, fluxes and gas cost), thereby improving margins further. 

#### **In an aggressive expansion mode; Bhushan Power shifted to JV** 

JSTL is aggressively building steel plants. It announced a new capex of ~INR260bn to set up 5mtpa brownfield steel capacity in Karnataka (by FY30). Last quarter, it announced INR316bn to set up 5mtpa flat steel capacity at Odisha (by FY30). Besides, it is buying BMM Ispat (capacity of 0.9mtpa) from promoter’s group company, diluting equity by ~2%. Total approved capex is ~INR1,261.6bn to be incurred during FY27–31, increasing steel-making capacity in India by ~17mtpa to 53.3mt (including 4.5mtpa JV with JFE). It further has plans to increase India steel capacity to 78mtpa by FY32 (consisting of 62mtpa at JSW level and 16mtpa at the JV level). We believe that JSTL’s consolidated debt (excluding JV) is likely to be under control (net debt/EBITDA<2.0x) despite huge capex over the next five years.

# **Q4FY26 conference call takeaways** 

## **Macro - Positive** 

- India’s steel demand recovered in Q4FY26 amid seasonal pickup in construction activities, rising by 10.4% YoY to 44.61mt. Overall for FY26, demand increased by 7.9% YoY to 164.2mt. Management expects 7–9% YoY demand growth in FY27 too. 

- JSW increased domestic HRC price by INR2,000/t in April-26 and further increased by INR1,000/t in May-26. Management expects steel price to remain range-bound in near term. 

## **Quarterly performance** 

- JSW reported Indian operations’ Q4FY26 crude steel sales volume of 7.84mt, up 5.6% QoQ. Overall India capacity utilisation stood at 96% (excl. Vijayanagar BF-3; 87% including). It sold 7.06mt in domestic market (up 6.8% QoQ /up 5% YoY) and it constitutes 90% of volume (Q3: 91% of volume). Exports were 0.78mt (~10% of total volume) and was down 3.9% QoQ in Q4FY26. Adjusted Indian operation EBITDA stood at INR91.1bn, up 40% QoQ and EBITDA/t of INR11,622, up INR2,837/t QoQ, primarily driven by increase in blended realisation by INR3,722/t QoQ to INR62,210/t. 

- US operations’ reported EBITDA decreased sharply to USD0.08mn (Q3: 3.06mn) as plant ramp-up started in Jan-26 post shutdowns for caster upgrade and adverse weather. Management guided for higher volume and profitability in FY27. Italy operations’ EBITDA decreased to USD 4.22mn (down 20% QoQ) in Q4FY26 due to lower sales volume (down 6% QoQ to ~67kt). 

- The company has recognised one-off grant income of INR4.99bn (INR4.62bn is for earlier duration) in other operating income. This is after Directorate of Industries approves to transfer the incentives available to Dolvi Coke Project Ltd. earlier (the company merged with JSW Steel in FY20). 

- Blended steel realisation increased by ~INR3,800/t QoQ for domestic operations. 

- During Q4FY26, average coking coal cost inched up by ~USD16/t QoQ (in line with guidance) while iron ore cost was almost flat QoQ. 

- Due to high currency volatility (USD appreciating versus INR), it has incurred unrealised forex loss of INR10.8bn on a forex loan at consolidated level. Adjusted consolidated EBITDA was up ~40% QoQ at INR92.5bn (reported EBITDA: ~INR86.3bn). 

- The exceptional gain of INR178.9bn includes INR180.5bn gain on slump sale of BPSL steel to 50:50 JV with JFE Corporation and INR1.63bn expense arising from implementation of New Labour Laws.

# **Company Description** 

JSW Steel Limited (JSTL) is the largest steel producer in India with installed steel capacity of ~37.9mtpa, including its JV (4.5mtpa) and USA (1.5mtpa). It is an efficient convertor. Its product portfolio in flat and long steel products includes hot rolled (HR) coils, sheets and plates; cold rolled coils and sheets; galvanized products; galvalume products; non-grain oriented electrical steel (CRNGO); prepainted galvanised products (colour coated sheets/coils); pre-painted galvalume products; wire rods; special steel bars/wires; rounds and blooms, and angles. 

The company has plants in over six locations in India, including Vijayanagar in Karnataka, Salem in Tamil Nadu, and Tarapur, Vasind, Kalmeshwar and Dolvi in Maharashtra. 

# **Investment Theme** 

The safeguard duty (11.5% in FY27/11% in FY28) imposed by India until FY28 support steel prices from major downside risk. Further, steel demand is expected to rise by 7–9% in FY27. We expect EBITDA/t for JSTL to average INR10–11k/t in FY26–28E (FY14-22 average was INR9,830/t) which is primarily due to continuous volume growth along with improved product mix. 

# **Key Risks** 

- Global recession leading to lower demand in India, thereby impacting volume 

- Higher exports from China, pressuring global steel prices 

- Higher imports, affecting realisation and volume 

- Higher-than-expected fall in steel prices and continued higher coking coal prices
