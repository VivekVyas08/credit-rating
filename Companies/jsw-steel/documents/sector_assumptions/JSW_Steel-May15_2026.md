## **Quick Snapshot** 

- Consolidated revenue rose by 14.2% y-o-y and 11.3% q-o-q to Rs. 51180 crore, driven by record steel sales volumes of 7.97 MT (up 6% y-o-y). 

- Adjusted EBITDA rose 50% y-o-y to Rs. 9713 crore, rose ~50% y-o-y and 47% q-o-q. Adj. EBITDA margin rose 460 bps y-o-y, beating estimates of 19.1% driven by stronger realisations, partly offset by higher coking coal costs. 

- Consolidated PAT stood at Rs. 19243 crore, significantly boosted by an exceptional gain of Rs. 18,051 crore from the slump sale of BPSL’s steel business to JSW JFE Steel Ltd. 

- At CMP, JSW Steel trades at 9.1x/7.2x for FY27E/FY28E EV/EBITDA, we maintain our BUY rating with revised PT of Rs.1500. 

## **Result overview** 

- Strategic expansions:  The JV with Japan’s JFE Steel Corp for the Bhushan Power & Steel (BPSL) business for high-quality steel products, deleveraging of ~Rs. 37,000 crore. In April 2026, a new 50:50 joint venture was established with POSCO (South Korea) for a 6 MTPA greenfield integrated steel factory in Dhenkanal, Odisha, with the goal of commissioning by 2031 and producing high-grade flat steel, including automotive grades. 

- Additionally, the JSW Steel’s board approved a 5 MTPA brownfield expansion at JVMLVijayanagar with a capital expenditure of Rs. 26,000 crore. By FY30, Vijayanagar would be the largest single-location steel factory in the world at about 25 MTPA. Furthermore, at an enterprise value of ~Rs. 6,400 crore, the Board approved the acquisition/amalgamation of BMM Ispat with the potential to expand to 1.8 MTPA at little further costs. 

- Record quarterly sales of 7.97 MT, up 6% y-o-y and 4% q-o-q due to healthy domestic demand for steel products. India business’ capacity utilisation stood at 96% in Q4FY26. BF-3 expansion Is from 3 MTPA to 4.5 MTPA is currently under testing and commissioning. 

- The 12% safeguard duty on steel imports that came in December 2025 is going to play a key role in supporting domestic price recovery, as domestic steel prices rose by approximately Rs. 3,800 per tonne in Q4FY26. 

- Sales of value-added and special products hit a record 4.82 MT in Q4FY26, up 17% y-o-y, accounting for 62% of total Indian sales. 

- A final dividend of Rs. 7.1 per share was declared for FY26. 

## **Our Call** 

Overall steel demand is projected to grow 7-9% for FY27, led by infrastructure, construction, industrial, renewables, automotive, and consumer durables. Latest key strategic deals will ramp up production capacity, reduce net debt and enhance longterm centric growth prospects. Moreover, a strong recovery in domestic HRC prices in the last month suggests a sharp margin recovery in the near term. At CMP, JSW Steel trades at a current valuation of 9.1x/7.2x for FY27E/FY28E EV/EBITDA, we maintain our Buy rating with revised PT of Rs. 1500, backed by strategic expansion plans, improved operational performance, better realisations on higher steel prices and focusing on higher value-added products. 

## **Key Risks** 

Changes in government regulatory policies, quality control norms, and labour laws.

# **Q4FY26 conference call highlights:** 

- **Capex plans:** Management outlined that the company is expected to spend Rs. 22,000-24,000 crore in FY27. Also, the approved growth plan entails a total capex of Rs. 126,000 crore to be spent over the next four to five years. 

- Total Capex is eyed at 78.5 MTPA capacity (including JVs, mining, downstream) estimated at ~ Rs. 2,26,000 crore over 6-7 years, with annual run-rates potentially increasing to Rs. 30,000–35,000 crore. 

- **Vijayanagar:** BF-3 extension from 3 mt to 4.5 mt in testing; incremental volumes from Q2FY27. 

- Dolvi Phase III will expand from 10 mt to 15 mt, with a completion date of September 27. 

   - **Guidance:** Management highlighted the total capacity in India operations, along with joint ventures, is close to 80 million tonnes by FY32 (including Ohio capacity of 1.5 million). 

- JSW Steel revised its maximum leverage cap (net debt to EBITDA) to 3 from 3.75, reflecting a stronger balance sheet post- deleveraging. 

- India’s steel demand is expected to grow at a healthy rate of 7% to 9% in FY27. 

   - **FY27 production guidance:** 29.75 MT; sales guidance: 28.6 MT (includes BMM Ispat, excludes JFE JV volumes). 

- **Raw material security:** Company expects coking coal costs to be higher by $12-15 per tonne in Q1FY27, with overall costs expected to increase by approximately Rs. 3,000 per tonne, although margins are projected to remain positive considering this rise in costs. 

- Iron ore and coking coal together account for about 60% of the cost of goods sold, with other cost elements including exchange rates, sea freight, and fluxes, which are impacted by Middle East disruptions. 

- Company’s exposure to gas-linked production is very limited, at 5% to 6% of total production volume, minimising its impact on overall costs.
