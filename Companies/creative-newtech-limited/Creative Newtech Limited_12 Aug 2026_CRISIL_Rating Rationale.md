### **Detailed Rationale**

This independent assessment arrives at **'Crisil BBB+/Stable'** for the long-term bank facilities of Creative Newtech Limited (CNL), with a short-term rating of **'Crisil A2'**.

The rating reflects CNL's established position as a technology-products distributor with a two-decade operating record, genuine principal relationships (including national distribution for OM SYSTEM, the Olympus imaging carve-out, and licensed Honeywell-branded products) and a distribution footprint spanning South Asia, South-East Asia, the Middle East and Africa. Consolidated revenue grew **52.1% to Rs 2,700 crore** in fiscal 2026 from Rs 1,774 crore, with profit after tax up 32% to Rs 70 crore and return on capital employed of 12.6%. Growth has continued into fiscal 2027, with first-quarter revenue up 21.2% year-on-year to Rs 476 crore.

These strengths are materially offset by **a sharp, single-year deterioration in the financial risk profile** and by the structurally thin margins inherent to distribution. Total debt rose **4.5 times to Rs 324 crore** as on March 31, 2026 (from Rs 72 crore), taking gearing to **0.84 time** from 0.23 time and the total indebtedness ratio to **1.42 times** from 0.72 time. Interest coverage fell to **4.41 times** from 5.38 times, and finance costs doubled to Rs 21 crore. Total debt to operating profit rose to **3.56 times** from 1.31 times.

The expansion was funded almost entirely through short-term borrowing and supplier credit, and it has consumed the company's liquidity. **Cash fell to Rs 5.4 crore from Rs 43.9 crore**, taking the cash-to-indebtedness ratio — a measure CRISIL applies specifically to traders — to **0.040 time from 0.248 time**. Critically, **trade receivables grew 137.7% to Rs 565 crore against revenue growth of 52.1%** — nearly three times as fast as sales — extending receivable days to **76 from 49** (and from 31 two years earlier). Receivables now stand at **1.46 times networth**.

Operating margin, at **3.37%**, provides limited absorptive capacity: a 100-basis-point slip in gross margin on fiscal 2026 revenue equates to roughly Rs 27 crore, or about 30% of operating profit. Applying CRISIL's risk-coverage test for traders, a 20% loss across receivables and inventory would consume **38% of networth**, with cover of only **2.6 times** against 5.0 times two years earlier.

The rating nonetheless remains in the moderate-safety band rather than lower because the leverage build funded **real, profitable revenue growth rather than losses**; short-term debt of Rs 324 crore is backed by receivables and inventory of Rs 739 crore; gearing remains below 1 time; and interest coverage above 4 times leaves headroom against the current interest burden.

# **Analytical Approach**

**Methodology scope — corrected.** CNL is a **distributor**, not a manufacturer: purchases of stock-in-trade were 94.5% of fiscal 2026 revenue, all revenue is disclosed as sale of traded goods, and gross margin is 7.9%. Accordingly this assessment applies **Section II (trading companies)** of CRISIL's manufacturing, trading and corporate services criteria as the primary scope — governing both the business-risk factor set (inventory, receivables, supplier and forex risk) and the ratio emphasis (total indebtedness, cash-to-indebtedness and risk coverage) — with Section I as the base framework and Section IV for ratio definitions.

*Scope caveat, disclosed:* Section II notes limited applicability where an entity also has significant manufacturing operations. CNL has a brand-licensing and contract-manufacturing arm which **is not separately quantified in any document supplied**; on the 94.5%-purchases evidence it is not yet significant relative to distribution, but this could not be verified and is stated as a limitation.

Financials are assessed on a **consolidated** basis (operating subsidiaries with non-controlling interests of Rs 23.7 crore).

# **Key Rating Drivers — Strengths**

**Established distribution franchise with two decades of operating record and credible principals:** CNL has built a multi-brand technology-distribution platform with an active distributor network across South Asia, South-East Asia, the Middle East and Africa. It is the national distributor for OM SYSTEM in India — the brand carved out of Olympus imaging, whose management has publicly credited CNL's *"extensive distribution network, dedication to excellence and proactive assistance"* — and handles Honeywell-branded products under licence. Principal relationships of this kind are slow to build and are the core franchise asset in distribution, where the distributor's value to the brand owner is reach and reliability rather than product.

**Sustained profitable growth with an improving revenue trajectory:** revenue grew 52.1% to Rs 2,700 crore in fiscal 2026 after a flatter fiscal 2025 (Rs 1,774 crore against Rs 1,713 crore), with profit after tax rising 32% to Rs 70 crore and operating margin improving modestly to 3.37% from 3.08%. Momentum continued into the first quarter of fiscal 2027 with revenue up 21.2% year-on-year. Return on capital employed of 12.6%, while down from 14.0%, remains adequate for a distribution business.

**Strategic shift toward higher-margin brand ownership:** the company is deliberately migrating from pure volume distribution toward brand licensing and own-brand development — described in its annual report as moving *"from a volume-led distributor to a high-margin, brand-first enabler."* If executed, this addresses the structural constraint of the distribution model directly, and gross margin trends will be the test of whether it is working.

# **Key Rating Drivers — Weaknesses**

**Sharp single-year deterioration in leverage and coverage:** total debt rose 4.5 times to Rs 324 crore in fiscal 2026, lifting gearing to 0.84 time from 0.23 time and total indebtedness to 1.42 times from 0.72 time, while interest coverage fell to 4.41 times from 5.38 times and total debt to operating profit rose to 3.56 times from 1.31 times. In the first quarter of fiscal 2027 finance costs were up 153% year-on-year, annualising near Rs 30 crore against Rs 21 crore for the whole of fiscal 2026 — the interest burden is currently compounding faster than the revenue it funds.

**Receivables growing far faster than sales, with liquidity consumed:** trade receivables rose 137.7% to Rs 565 crore against 52.1% revenue growth, extending receivable days to 76 from 49 and reaching 1.46 times networth, while cash fell to Rs 5.4 crore from Rs 43.9 crore and cash-to-indebtedness collapsed to 0.040 time. **No receivables ageing, customer-concentration disclosure or management explanation of the extension is present in the supplied documents**, so it cannot be determined whether this reflects deliberately purchased growth through extended channel credit or a deterioration in collections — a distinction central to the credit.

**Structurally thin margins with high export exposure and no evidenced hedging:** at a 3.37% operating margin and 7.9% gross margin, the business has minimal capacity to absorb price, currency or credit shocks — a 100-basis-point gross-margin movement equates to about 30% of operating profit. Compounding this, the majority of revenue is earned outside India (61.5% in fiscal 2025, the latest disclosed split), and **no foreign-exchange hedging policy was found in any supplied document**. CRISIL's trading criteria treat a documented, consistently applied hedging policy as a positive; its absence here is unverified rather than confirmed negative, but it is an unquantified exposure on a thin-margin base.

# **Liquidity: Stretched to Adequate**

Cash and bank balances of Rs 22.2 crore as on March 31, 2026 cover under 7% of short-term borrowings of Rs 324.3 crore, and gross cash accrual of Rs 71.8 crore is 0.22 time total debt. The position depends on continued rollover of working-capital lines and on receivables converting to cash on schedule. Asset cover is present on paper — receivables and inventory of Rs 739 crore against Rs 324 crore of short-term debt, with a current ratio of 1.68 times — but **average bank-limit sanction and utilisation data, CRISIL's own named liquidity test, are absent from the supplied documents** and would be required to complete this assessment. The descriptor is deliberately not upgraded without them.

# **ESG Profile**

The ESG profile of CNL is assessed as **neutral to its credit risk profile**, on limited disclosure.

Technology-products **distribution has a low direct environmental footprint** — the business is warehousing, logistics and channel management rather than manufacturing, so Scope 1 emissions are limited — but it carries **indirect exposure through electronic-waste and packaging** across the products it moves, and a growing regulatory obligation under India's Extended Producer Responsibility framework as the company adds brand ownership and contract manufacturing. **Social impact is low-to-moderate**, reflecting a modest direct workforce.

**ESG highlights:**

- **Governance:** the company maintains a constituted **CSR Committee of four members** with a functioning meeting cadence, and files under the standard listed-company governance framework on BSE and NSE. A **CFO transition occurred during the review period** — Mr Abhijit Kanvinde resigned on April 14, 2026 and Mr Ajit Thakur was appointed on May 14, 2026 — which coincided with a year in which total debt rose 4.5 times; finance-function continuity through the current working-capital expansion is a governance monitorable rather than a concern on the evidence available.
- **Audit:** the fiscal 2026 audited results carry a **declaration of unmodified opinion**.

**Disclosed limitation — this section is materially constrained by the document set.** No fiscal 2025-26 annual report was supplied (financials came from the audited results filing), and consequently **no BRSR, no emissions or energy data, no diversity or safety metrics, and no board-composition detail were available for review**. Under the applicable format an ESG assessment is mandatory; where the underlying disclosure is absent, that absence is stated rather than the section being dropped or filled with generic assertion. **A complete ESG assessment requires the FY2025-26 annual report and BRSR.**

# **Outlook: Stable**

The leverage build funded profitable revenue growth rather than losses, the company remains solidly profitable, and short-term debt is backed by current assets at more than two times cover. CNL is expected to sustain double-digit revenue growth while gearing stabilises as the working-capital base normalises against the enlarged revenue run-rate.

**Upward factors**

- Interest coverage sustained above 5.5 times and total indebtedness below 1.0 time, with operating margin improving toward 4.5-5% as the brand-led strategy delivers

**Downward factors**
- Receivable days extending beyond about 90, or any material bad-debt provision or foreign-exchange loss
- Interest coverage falling below 3.5 times, or total indebtedness rising above 2.0 times
- Inability to roll over or expand working-capital lines in line with revenue growth
