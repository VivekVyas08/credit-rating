## **Rationale**

The reaffirmation reflects Godrej Properties Limited's (GPL) position as India's largest residential real estate developer by Booking Value for the third consecutive year, its exceptional financial flexibility as part of the Godrej Industries Group, and healthy liquidity, set against the accounting features inherent to project-completion revenue recognition and a debt profile that is rising to fund continued land investment.

**What happened since the last rating action (21-Jul-2026, same fiscal year).** GPL's FY2026 Booking Value grew 16% year-on-year to Rs. 34,171 crore (FY2025: Rs. 29,444 crore — both figures independently corroborated across two separate annual reports), on sale of 17,513 units across 27 million sq. ft. — the highest annual figure recorded by any Indian developer, for the third consecutive year (`compute_ratios.py`, KPI series, cross-checked FY2025-26 AR §Directors' Report and FY2024-25 AR §Directors' Report). Collections grew 17% to Rs. 19,965 crore (FY2025: Rs. 17,047 crore, +49% that year) and operating cash flow grew 5% to Rs. 7,830 crore (FY2025: Rs. 7,484 crore, +73%). Consolidated net worth grew to Rs. 19,354.89 crore (FY2025: Rs. 17,573.73 crore) on retained profit and no dividend outflow. Consolidated gross debt (borrowings, excluding lease liabilities) rose to Rs. 15,614.87 crore from Rs. 12,561.16 crore — a figure that reconciles to the rupee against ICRA's own stated gross debt of Rs. 15,615 crore as at March 2026 (21-Jul-2026 rationale), corroborating this workpaper's extraction independently.

**Why the reported operating margin looks weak, and why that is not a credit weakness.** Under the project-completion method mandated by Ind AS for this business, revenue and profit are recognised only when a project receives its occupancy certificate — not when units are booked and sold. As a direct consequence, OPBDIT computed on the standard ICRA convention (excluding Other Income) is **negative Rs. 416.57 crore in FY2026 (−8.12% margin)** and near-nil at **Rs. 44.36 crore in FY2025 (+0.9% margin)** — and was also negative in FY2024 (Rs. −129.68 crore, −4.27%) — `compute_ratios.py`, tie-out to the printed P&L subtotals confirmed to the rupee in all three years. This is a **structural feature, not deterioration**: ICRA's own 21-Jul-2026 rationale for this issuer independently states "ICRA notes that GPL's reported operating margins (excluding fair value gain upon acquisition/relinquishment of control) are modest" — the same finding, reached independently.

**Normalisation treatment (failure mode 36), stated explicitly.** This engine's OPBDIT convention excludes Other Income UNCONDITIONALLY, per the standard ICRA treatment — no forex gain or loss sits inside either the Other Income or Other Expenses note for this issuer in any of the three years reviewed (Note 31 and the Other Expenses note were both decomposed component-by-component and tied to their own printed totals; searched and absent, not merely unmentioned). The item that DOES require explicit normalisation treatment here is non-forex: the Rs. 3,279.45 crore of Other Income booked in FY2026 (FY2025: Rs. 2,044.21 crore) is dominated by a Rs. 2,093.01 crore (FY2025: Rs. 930.35 crore) non-cash, non-recurring **fair value gain upon acquisition/relinquishment of control** of joint-venture entities (Note 31). This item is EXCLUDED from OPBDIT by the same "less Other Income" convention that would apply to any other non-operating item, and is called out explicitly here — rather than left as an unstated absence — because of its scale relative to reported PAT. The economically relevant coverage measure for this business is **cash-flow based, not accrual-based**: FY2026 operating cash flow of Rs. 7,830 crore covers finance costs of Rs. 136.86 crore approximately **57 times over**.

**What could go wrong.** GPL's own MD&A states consolidated gross debt is expected to rise to Rs. 18,500–19,000 crore during FY2027 (from Rs. 15,615 crore) to fund higher construction outflow and land investment, implying leverage (net debt/CFO) of 1.3–1.4 times versus 0.9 times (including QIP proceeds) as at March 2026 — both figures from ICRA's own 21-Jul-2026 rationale, used here as context, not as this workpaper's own projection. Short-term borrowings form roughly 69% of total debt (per the same source), exposing the company to refinancing risk, which is mitigated by strong liquidity (Rs. 4,744.97 crore of cash and current investments, computed on a conservative basis, against ICRA's own broader Rs. 8,970 crore "free cash and liquid investments" figure) and by demonstrated Group financial flexibility (Rs. 12,850 crore of equity raised FY2019–FY2025 via QIP/private placement). Execution risk remains inherent to the sector: GPL has launched 33.2 msf of new sales value Rs. 42,200 crore in FY2026 and plans a further Rs. 48,000 crore of launches in FY2027, and its ability to convert this pipeline into delivered, occupancy-certified area within budget is the central monitorable.

**What ICRA expects.** Booking Value and collections growth are expected to continue at a more moderate pace off the current high base; the company's own guidance points to 10–15% pre-sales growth (ICRA's 21-Jul-2026 rationale). This workpaper's own base-case forward view (`forward_model.py`) derives a net-debt/OCF path of 1.25x for FY2027, broadly consistent with — modestly below — the company's own 1.3–1.4x guidance range, the gap attributable to this model's narrower land/capex assumption (the Rs. 3,000–3,500 crore NCD-proceeds figure GPL itself names, rather than the full QIP redeployment ICRA assumes) rather than to any disagreement over direction.

The Stable outlook reflects ICRA's opinion that GPL will sustain its market-leading sales momentum and collections growth while its exceptional financial flexibility as part of the Godrej Industries Group continues to comfortably absorb the planned rise in debt.

# **Key rating drivers and their description**

## **Credit strengths**

**India's largest residential developer by Booking Value, with a diversified, 30-year-old portfolio** — GPL has recorded the highest annual Booking Value of any Indian developer for the third consecutive year (Rs. 34,171 crore in FY2026), across a portfolio diversified over ten-plus cities (MMR, NCR, Bengaluru, Pune, Chennai, Ahmedabad, Kolkata, Hyderabad, Nagpur, Indore, and Tier-II expansion into Raipur and Coimbatore). The company has delivered around 78 million sq. ft. of developments since FY2018 (ICRA's 21-Jul-2026 rationale; corroborated by the company's own FY2025-26 Integrated Report).

**Strong and improving collections and cash generation** — Collections grew 17% to Rs. 19,965 crore in FY2026 (49% growth in FY2025), and operating cash flow grew 5% to Rs. 7,830 crore, both the highest recorded by any Indian real estate developer to date per the company's own MD&A, cross-checked across two independent annual reports. Collections and OCF have both grown at a 30% CAGR over the last three years.

**Exceptional financial flexibility as part of the Godrej Industries Group** — the company has raised Rs. 12,850 crore of equity during FY2019–FY2025 through private placement/QIP, an access to capital ICRA's own rationale explicitly credits as "exceptional financial flexibility." This workpaper enters one support notch on the scorecard for this reason (see the Step 12b anchor-divergence discussion below).

**Comfortable cash-flow coverage of debt-servicing obligations** — operating cash flow of Rs. 7,830 crore covers FY2026 finance costs of Rs. 136.86 crore approximately 57 times; the cash-flow adequacy ratio for ongoing projects stood at around 80% as at March 2026 (73% as at September 2024, per ICRA's own rationale), indicating pending receivables from booked sales adequately cover the remaining construction cost to complete.

**Clean audit opinion; robust ESG positioning** — the FY2026 standalone and consolidated financial statements carry an unmodified audit opinion. GPL has committed to Net Zero by 2050 and reports Scope 1+2 emissions intensity of 0.0057 tCO2e/sq. ft. (down from a stated 0.008 tCO2e/sq. ft. base), 100% of projects Green Building Certified or under certification, 14,560 GJ of renewable energy consumption against 44,462 GJ total (33%), and received IGBC Net Zero Achieved Carbon Neutrality Energy (Design) Certification for a Mumbai project during the year (FY2025-26 Integrated Report, ESG data section — searched and PRESENT, per the pre-flight probe).

## **Credit challenges**

**Reported operating margin is structurally weak under project-completion accounting** — OPBDIT (ICRA convention, excluding Other Income) was negative Rs. 416.57 crore in FY2026 and near-nil at Rs. 44.36 crore in FY2025, a consequence of revenue recognition timing rather than weak underlying unit economics; the imputed EBIT margin on bookings is stated by ICRA at 24–26% for FY2025–FY2026 bookings. The reported margin is expected to improve only when projects booked in FY2024–FY2026 are delivered and their revenue is recognised, from FY2028 onward (company MD&A; ICRA's own rationale).

**Rising debt to fund land investment and construction, with a high proportion of short-term borrowing** — consolidated gross debt is expected to rise to Rs. 18,500–19,000 crore in FY2027 from Rs. 15,615 crore, implying net debt/CFO of 1.3–1.4 times against 0.9 times as at March 2026. Short-term borrowings form roughly 69% of total debt, which lowers the average cost of borrowing (7.05% as at March 2026) but increases refinancing risk — mitigated by the company's demonstrated capital-market access and liquidity buffer.

**Execution and market risk inherent to the sector** — GPL plans Rs. 48,000 crore of new project launches in FY2027 against Rs. 42,200 crore in FY2026; its ability to execute and deliver this expanded pipeline within budget and on schedule, in a cyclical industry exposed to demand and input-cost swings, remains the central credit monitorable.

**A safety observation from the ESG data set** — the FY2025-26 Integrated Report discloses an LTIFR of 0.06 with 7 lost-time injuries including 5 fatalities among contractor workforce during the year. This is disclosed here because it is present in the documents (pre-flight probe [4], PRESENT) and is credit-relevant to a construction-intensive business, though it does not by itself drive the rating absent a discernible multi-year deterioration trend, which this pass did not extract.

# **Environmental and social risks**

GPL has committed to a 2050 Net Zero target and reports Scope 1+2 emissions intensity of 0.0057 tCO2e/sq. ft. and value-chain (Scope 3) emissions of 848,923 tCO2e for FY2026, with Scope 3 intensity of 0.149 tCO2e/sq. ft. The company sourced 14,560 GJ of its 44,462 GJ total energy consumption (33%) from renewable sources, consumed 441 million litres of fresh water against 90.14 million litres of recycled water, and reports zero water discharge with 99% waste reused/recycled. All ongoing and completed projects (100%) are Green Building Certified or under certification (IGBC/GRIHA or equivalent), and the company received IGBC's Net Zero Achieved Carbon Neutrality Energy (Design) Certification for a Mumbai project in FY2026, alongside an IGBC Green Champion award for driving the net-zero movement in India. On the social side, the workforce diversity ratio stood at 41.3%, with 100% of employees eligible for parental leave and completing human-rights training; 32% of employees carry ESG-linked KPIs. Governance disclosures record 13 confirmed anti-corruption/bribery incidents in the reporting period and an LTIFR of 0.06 (7 lost-time injuries, including 5 contractor fatalities) — both flagged above as credit-relevant observations from the documents reviewed (FY2025-26 Integrated Report, ESG/BRSR data section).

# **Liquidity position: Strong**

**Sources over the next twelve months:** cash and cash equivalents of Rs. 1,860.74 crore plus current investments of Rs. 2,884.23 crore, totalling Rs. 4,744.97 crore on this workpaper's conservative basis (`compute_ratios.py`, consolidated Balance Sheet, March 2026) — narrower than ICRA's own stated Rs. 8,970 crore "free cash and liquid investments" figure (which this workpaper has not separately traced to component balance-sheet lines, and is disclosed here as an acknowledged gap rather than forced to reconcile). FY2026 operating cash flow of Rs. 7,830 crore, growing at a 30% three-year CAGR, provides substantial recurring inflow. The company also retains demonstrated access to the equity capital markets (Rs. 12,850 crore raised FY2019–FY2025).

**Claims over the same period:** finance costs of Rs. 136.86 crore (covered approximately 57 times by OCF alone); a rising, but not yet drawn, construction and land-investment outflow associated with the FY2027 debt-guidance range of Rs. 18,500–19,000 crore. No near-term bullet redemption concentration is evidenced in the documents reviewed for this pass beyond the routine NCD maturity profile in Annexure I.

**Descriptor: Strong**, not Superior, given the company's own guidance of a material rise in debt during FY2027 to fund its land-investment programme, and not Adequate given the scale of cash-flow cover and demonstrated capital-market access set out above.

# **Rating sensitivities**

**Positive factors** — ICRA could upgrade the rating if GPL sustains its Booking Value and collections growth while improving reported operating margins (as project-completion revenue from higher-imputed-margin bookings starts flowing through from FY2028) on a sustained basis, and if leverage (net debt/OCF) is maintained at or below current levels despite the planned rise in absolute debt.

**Negative factors** — Pressure on the rating could arise if collections growth or cash-flow adequacy weakens materially, if consolidated leverage rises meaningfully beyond the company's own FY2027 guidance range of 1.3–1.4 times net debt/CFO on a sustained basis, or if the exceptional financial flexibility associated with the Godrej Industries Group parentage were to diminish.

Both were hand-typed. The notch count is now read from the workbook's own computed cell.

**One hard cap was disapplied, with the reason recorded on the sheet (Inputs!D67).** The scorecard's corporate hard cap forces any issuer with accrual interest coverage below 1.0x down to B+. GPL's accrual coverage is −3.04x in FY2026 — but that is an artefact of Ind AS project-completion accounting, under which revenue is recognised only on receipt of the occupancy certificate, so accrual OPBDIT is negative by construction while the business is strongly cash-generative (FY2026 operating cash flow of Rs. 7,830 crore against finance costs of Rs. 136.86 crore = **57.2 times cover**). Left to fire, that single cap moved the anchor from A+ to B+ and manufactured **nine of the twelve notches** the earlier draft was trying to explain away in prose. Disapplying it is a sector-applicability correction, not a judgement uplift — the uplift is argued separately and visibly in Stage 2 above. Per this project's governing rule (CLAUDE.md §6, "Anchor governance"), the resulting 3-notch anchor-to-assigned gap sits within the anchor's own ±2–3 notch screening band for an investment-grade issuer and is resolved by the named qualitative and support factors, each of which is recorded on the workbook rather than asserted here.
