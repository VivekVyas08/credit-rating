### **Entity note (read first)**

This case is filed as "Vedanta". **Vedanta Limited and Hindustan Zinc Limited (HZL) are separate listed legal entities** — Vedanta Ltd holds **60.71%** of HZL and the **Government of India holds 27.92%**. Every financial document supplied for this case (three Integrated Annual Reports and four quarterly filings) is **Hindustan Zinc Limited's**; the sole Vedanta-Limited document is a broker note, which is a context reference and not a data source. This rationale is therefore issued on Hindustan Zinc Limited, with the Vedanta relationship assessed as the parent-linkage factor — which is where it materially affects the credit.

### **Detailed Rationale**

This independent assessment arrives at **'Crisil AAA/Stable'** for the long-term bank facilities of Hindustan Zinc Limited, with a short-term rating of **'Crisil A1+'**.

The rating reflects HZL's position as **the world's largest integrated zinc producer** operating at first-quartile global cost, and a financial risk profile that is best-in-class by a wide margin. Revenue from operations grew 19.8% to **Rs 40,844 crore** in fiscal 2026, with operating profit rising to **Rs 22,064 crore** at a margin of **54.0%** — up from 47.2% two years earlier — and profit after tax of Rs 13,832 crore (33.9% of revenue). Cost of production fell 9% year-on-year to a **five-year low of US$959 per tonne**. Return on capital employed was **58.3%**, interest coverage **25.0 times**, gearing **0.39 time**, and the company closed the year in a **net cash position of Rs 4,945 crore** (liquid assets of Rs 13,673 crore against total debt including leases of Rs 8,728 crore).

For a mining credit the decisive question is whether profitability survives the commodity cycle, and HZL's cost position answers it: at first-quartile cost with meaningful silver by-product credits, the company remains cash-generative at zinc prices that would push higher-cost producers into losses.

**The principal constraint is not operating — it is the parent relationship.** In fiscal 2025 HZL paid dividends of **Rs 12,253 crore, equal to 118% of that year's profit**, of which Rs 7,439 crore accrued to Vedanta Limited and Rs 3,421 crore to the Government of India. Net worth fell that year from Rs 15,195 crore to Rs 13,326 crore. **The rating rests on what happened next:** even after upstreaming more than an entire year's earnings, HZL closed fiscal 2025 with interest coverage of 15.9 times and total debt to operating profit of 0.63 time; in fiscal 2026 the payout normalised to 31% of profit (Rs 4,225 crore), net worth rebuilt by Rs 9,303 crore to Rs 22,629 crore, and the company swung back to net cash. A balance sheet that absorbs a greater-than-100%-of-earnings extraction and remains comfortably investment grade is demonstrating, not asserting, its resilience.

Structural protections limit how far extraction can go: the **27.92% Government of India shareholding**, HZL's separate listing, SEBI's majority-of-minority approval requirement for material related-party transactions, and an **undertaking given to the National Company Law Tribunal to maintain a minimum net worth of Rs 5,000 crore** (against Rs 22,629 crore currently — 4.5 times headroom).

The rating also factors in a governance item requiring monitoring. The auditor's report on the fiscal 2026 consolidated financial statements carries an **Emphasis of Matter** drawing attention to *"allegations made during the year by a short seller, and ongoing matters for which information has been sought by regulators/authorities"* in relation to Vedanta Resources Limited and its subsidiaries (Note 43). **The audit opinion is not modified**, and management's assessment — supported by legal evaluation — is that the allegations are without merit and no adjustment to the financial statements is required. The item is nonetheless rating-relevant because open regulatory enquiries touching the controlling group bear on governance and, potentially, on group financial policy.

# **Analytical Approach**

Assessment is on a **consolidated** basis for Hindustan Zinc Limited.

**Methodology scope — corrected.** The case was created under the generic manufacturing master criteria. HZL is a **mining and non-ferrous metals** producer, so **Section V (materials sector)** of CRISIL's manufacturing, trading and corporate services criteria governs the sector factor set — pricing characteristics, cost position, ore reserves and reserve replacement, production diversification, regulatory and geographical concentration risk — with Section I as the base framework and Section IV for ratio definitions. Section V's scope expressly covers mining and non-ferrous metals.

**Parent/group linkage — no notching in either direction.** Under CRISIL's parent/group criteria a subsidiary whose standalone profile is stronger than its parent's may be capped by group considerations. Here the insulating factors are specific and enforceable — the Government of India's 27.92% stake, separate listing, SEBI's related-party framework and the NCLT minimum-net-worth undertaking — and HZL requires no parental support in any scenario evidenced by these documents. It is therefore rated on its standalone profile: no uplift, and no cap.

# **Key Rating Drivers — Strengths**

**World-leading market position at first-quartile cost, the decisive factor for a commodity producer:** HZL describes itself, on the evidence of its integrated mine-to-metal operations, as the world's largest integrated zinc producer. Cost of production fell 9% year-on-year to a five-year low of **US$959 per tonne** in fiscal 2026. Cost leadership is what determines survival through a commodity downcycle — CRISIL's own materials criteria state that *"a low-cost position is important in an industry where producers have limited pricing flexibility"* — and HZL's position means it remains strongly cash-generative at price levels that would render marginal producers loss-making. Silver produced as a by-product provides a further natural hedge against zinc-price weakness.

**Exceptional and improving profitability and returns:** operating margin expanded from 47.2% (fiscal 2024) to 51.0% (fiscal 2025) to **54.0% (fiscal 2026)**, while revenue compounded 18.8% annually across the same period to Rs 40,844 crore. Return on capital employed rose to **58.3%** from 42.6% two years earlier. Profit after tax grew 78% over two years to Rs 13,832 crore. These are not cyclical-peak artefacts alone — the margin expansion is substantially cost-driven, with the production-cost decline directly evidenced.

**Net cash balance sheet with very high debt protection:** liquid assets of Rs 13,673 crore (cash, bank balances and current investments) exceeded total debt including lease liabilities of Rs 8,728 crore at March 31, 2026, giving **net cash of Rs 4,945 crore**. Gearing improved to 0.39 time from 0.82 time, total outside liabilities to tangible net worth to 0.87 time from 1.59 time, total debt to operating profit to 0.40 time, and interest coverage to **25.0 times** from 15.9 times. Gross cash accrual of Rs 17,622 crore in fiscal 2026 was approximately twice total debt.

# **Key Rating Drivers — Weaknesses**

**Aggressive and parent-driven dividend policy:** HZL upstreamed Rs 12,253 crore in fiscal 2025 — **118% of that year's profit after tax** — reducing net worth by Rs 1,869 crore, before normalising to a 31% payout (Rs 4,225 crore) in fiscal 2026. Distribution policy is set in the interest of a 60.71% controlling shareholder whose wider group carries materially higher leverage than HZL itself, and the fiscal 2025 episode demonstrates capacity for extraction well beyond annual earnings. While the balance sheet absorbed it without stress, a structural escalation — sustained payouts above earnings, debt-funded special dividends, or intercompany exposure to the parent — would be a direct negative for the rating.

**Governance matter flagged by the statutory auditor:** the fiscal 2026 audit report carries an Emphasis of Matter on short-seller allegations concerning Vedanta Resources Limited and its subsidiaries, together with ongoing matters on which regulators and authorities have sought information. The opinion is unmodified and management, supported by legal evaluation, considers the allegations without merit with no financial-statement adjustment required. The outcome of the regulatory enquiries is nonetheless not determinable at this date and remains a monitorable.

**Inherent commodity-price and concentration exposure:** zinc, lead and silver realisations are LME-linked and HZL is a price-taker, so earnings are cyclical irrespective of cost position. Operations are geographically concentrated in Rajasthan, and mining royalty of Rs 4,948 crore in fiscal 2026 (12.1% of revenue) is a large regulated cost subject to policy change.

# **Liquidity: Superior**

Liquid assets of Rs 13,673 crore at March 31, 2026 exceeded total debt of Rs 8,728 crore, and gross cash accrual of Rs 17,622 crore was roughly twice total debt. Against current borrowings of Rs 3,638 crore and non-current borrowings of Rs 4,614 crore, internal resources alone are more than sufficient. The fiscal 2025 stress case is instructive: even with Rs 12,253 crore of dividend outflow in a single year, all obligations were met comfortably and coverage remained at 15.9 times. The descriptor follows from that arithmetic rather than from any assumption about undrawn bank lines, for which utilisation data was not available.

# **ESG Profile**

The ESG profile of HZL supports its strong credit risk profile.

The zinc mining and smelting sector has a **significant environmental impact**, being energy- and water-intensive and dependent on natural resources — zinc ore and coal — as key inputs, with material emissions and waste generation. The sector's **social impact is also significant**, given a large workforce across owned operations and value-chain partners, the effect of mining operations on host communities, and inherent occupational health and safety hazards. HZL is actively mitigating these risks, and the disclosures reviewed are of a standard consistent with an integrated-reporting framework.

**ESG highlights:**

- **Emissions and climate:** the company has a stated goal of achieving **Net Zero emissions by 2050 or sooner**, supported by an interim target of a **25% absolute reduction in Scope 3 GHG emissions by FY2030** against its base year. Renewable capacity is being scaled through a contracted **530 MW of round-the-clock renewable power**, delivered in tranches (200 MW and 250 MW contracted capacities disclosed), alongside a further 70 MW.
- **Low-carbon product transition:** HZL markets **EcoZen, described as Asia's first low-carbon zinc**, enabling downstream customers — principally galvanised-steel producers — to reduce their own Scope 3 emissions. This matters commercially as well as environmentally: it positions the company favourably as its largest end-market faces decarbonisation pressure.
- **Water and biodiversity:** the company reports being **3.32 times water positive**, with a stated target of a **50% reduction in freshwater consumption**. It operates a structured biodiversity programme including Biodiversity Action Plans and use of the Integrated Biodiversity Assessment Tool (IBAT) across mine sites.
- **Social:** **gender diversity in executive roles rose from 12% in FY2019 to 26%**, and the company tracks and discloses lost-time injury frequency rate (LTIFR) per million hours worked as a core safety metric.
- **Governance:** the company is separately listed with a **27.92% Government of India shareholding**, which — together with SEBI's majority-of-minority approval requirement for material related-party transactions — provides a structural governance check on the 60.71% controlling shareholder. Set against this, the fiscal 2026 auditor's report carries an **Emphasis of Matter** concerning short-seller allegations against the controlling group and ongoing regulatory information requests (discussed under Key Rating Drivers), which is the principal governance monitorable.

ESG considerations are of growing importance to investors and lenders. HZL's commitment to these principles supports stakeholder confidence and its access to domestic capital markets, which is relevant given the share of market borrowing in its debt mix.

*Source: Hindustan Zinc Limited Integrated Annual Report FY2025-26 (all quantitative ESG figures above are the company's own disclosures) and the fiscal 2026 auditor's report.*

# **Outlook: Stable**

HZL is expected to sustain its cost leadership and very strong debt-protection metrics, with the net cash position and 25 times interest coverage providing substantial absorptive capacity against commodity-price cyclicality and against continued dividend upstreaming at historical levels.

# **Rating sensitivity factors**

**Upward factors:** Not applicable — the rating is at the highest level on the scale.

**Downward factors**
- A structural escalation in cash extraction by the parent — sustained payouts materially above earnings, debt-funded special dividends, or intercompany loans/guarantees/exposure to Vedanta group entities
- An adverse outcome from the regulatory matters referred to in the auditor's Emphasis of Matter, or any development impairing HZL's governance independence from the group
- A sustained commodity downcycle or cost escalation taking operating margin below approximately 30% on a sustained basis, or net debt to operating profit above 1.5 times
