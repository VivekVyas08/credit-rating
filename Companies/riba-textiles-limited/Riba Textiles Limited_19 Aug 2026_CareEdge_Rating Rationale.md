## **Rationale and key rating drivers**

The ratings assigned to the bank facilities of Riba Textiles Limited (RTL) derive strength from the
company's established track record of over three decades in terry towel manufacturing, its
demonstrated deleveraging over FY2023–FY2025, and a marked improvement in the working capital
cycle. Overall gearing improved to 0.63x as on March 31, 2025 from 0.76x a year earlier, TOL/TNW to
0.85x from 1.00x, and tangible net worth rose to ₹97.90 crore from ₹89.40 crore entirely through
internal accrual.

These strengths are tempered by the company's modest scale, thin and deteriorating profitability,
and — most materially — a decline in debt coverage despite lower debt. PBILDT margin moderated to
7.52% in FY2025 from 7.99%, and interest coverage weakened to 3.78x from 4.24x even as total debt
fell to ₹61.39 crore from ₹67.68 crore. The cause is visible in the components: finance cost rose
26.7% year on year while PBILDT rose only 12.8%, against revenue growth of 19.8%. **Growth is being
delivered at the expense of margin**, and that is the central tension in the credit.

The ratings further factor in the susceptibility of margins to cotton and yarn price volatility, a
fragmented and competitive home-textile industry, and dependence on export demand.

## **Rating sensitivities: Factors likely to lead to rating actions**

### **Positive factors**

- Sustained improvement in PBILDT margin above 9.0% alongside revenue growth, with interest
  coverage restored above 5.0x on a sustained basis.
- Overall gearing sustained below 0.50x with total debt to PBILDT below 2.0x.

### **Negative factors**

- PBILDT margin declining below 6.5%, or interest coverage falling below 3.0x on a sustained basis.
- Overall gearing rising above 1.00x, or total debt to PBILDT exceeding 3.5x.
- Elongation of the working capital cycle reversing the FY2025 gains, or any deterioration in
  liquidity from the already-thin cash position.

## **Outlook:** Stable

The Stable outlook reflects CareEdge Ratings' expectation that RTL will sustain its improved
capital structure and working capital discipline over the medium term, and that revenue growth will
continue, while margins remain range-bound at current levels. The outlook does not assume a
recovery in profitability.

## **Detailed description of key rating drivers:**

### **Key strengths**

**Established track record of over three decades in terry towel manufacturing**

Incorporated in 1989 (CIN L18101DL1989PLC034528) and based at Panipat, Haryana, RTL manufactures
terry towels and home textiles. A track record of this length through multiple cotton cycles
provides operational stability and established customer and supplier relationships, and is
reflected in the company's ability to grow revenue 19.8% in FY2025 without a corresponding increase
in the working capital cycle.

**Demonstrated deleveraging with net worth built through internal accrual**

The capital structure has strengthened consistently. Overall gearing improved to 0.63x as on
March 31, 2025 (0.76x as on March 31, 2024; 0.75x as on March 31, 2023) and net overall gearing to
0.60x from 0.70x. TOL/TNW improved to 0.85x from 1.00x. Total debt reduced to ₹61.39 crore from
₹67.68 crore, driven by a reduction in current borrowings to ₹35.65 crore from ₹42.30 crore, while
long-term borrowings were broadly stable at ₹25.74 crore.

Critically, the improvement is accrual-driven rather than the result of an equity infusion — share
capital was unchanged at ₹9.65 crore across all three years, and the entire increase in net worth
came from reserves, which rose to ₹88.24 crore from ₹79.74 crore.

**Marked improvement in the working capital cycle**

Receivable days improved to 45 in FY2025 from 61 in FY2024, and inventory days to 31 from 38, with
the current ratio improving to 1.38x from 1.33x. Trade receivables reduced in absolute terms to
₹36.22 crore from ₹41.59 crore despite revenue growing 19.8% — indicating genuinely tighter
collection rather than a base effect. For a working-capital-intensive textile manufacturer this is
a meaningful operational improvement and the primary driver of the debt reduction.

### **Key weaknesses**

**Thin and deteriorating profitability**

PBILDT margin moderated to 7.52% in FY2025 from 7.99% in FY2024 and 7.70% in FY2023. The company's
own disclosed operating margin shows a sharper decline — to 12.56% from 16.63%, a fall of 24.45% as
reported in its key-ratio table. PAT margin similarly eased to 2.87% from 3.21%.

In absolute terms PBILDT rose to ₹22.25 crore from ₹19.73 crore, but the 12.8% increase materially
lagged revenue growth of 19.8%. Raw material consumed rose to ₹177.78 crore from ₹146.19 crore — a
21.6% increase, faster than revenue — indicating incomplete pass-through of cotton and yarn cost
inflation. Margin recovery is the single most important monitorable for the rating.

**Weakening debt coverage despite lower debt**

This is the defining feature of FY2025 and warrants emphasis. Interest coverage declined to 3.78x
from 4.24x, and the company's own disclosed debt service coverage ratio eased to 1.47x from 1.52x —
**even though total debt fell**. Finance cost rose 26.7% to ₹5.89 crore from ₹4.65 crore against a
12.8% rise in PBILDT.

A borrower reducing debt while its coverage deteriorates is being squeezed on price rather than
quantum, whether through higher interest rates on renewed facilities or a shift in the borrowing
mix. Total debt to PBILDT did improve, to 2.76x from 3.43x, so the leverage picture and the
coverage picture point in opposite directions. The ratings weight the coverage trend more heavily
because it measures the ability to service, not merely the size of, the obligation.

**Modest scale and susceptibility to raw material volatility**

Revenue of ₹295.86 crore in FY2025 places RTL among smaller participants in a highly fragmented
home-textile industry. Scale limits bargaining power with both cotton suppliers and institutional
buyers, and constrains the ability to absorb input-cost inflation — as the FY2025 margin
performance demonstrates. Cotton and yarn prices are the principal exposure, and pass-through is
typically lagged.

**Thin absolute liquidity**

Cash and bank balances stood at ₹2.54 crore as on March 31, 2025, down from ₹4.77 crore, against
current borrowings of ₹35.65 crore. While the current ratio of 1.38x is adequate and the working
capital cycle has tightened, the absolute cash cushion is limited and leaves little buffer against a
demand or collection shock.

## **Liquidity: Adequate**

Liquidity is adequate but not strong. The current ratio improved to 1.38x as on March 31, 2025 and
the working capital cycle tightened materially, supporting operating cash generation. Gross cash
accrual of approximately ₹13.97 crore (PAT ₹8.50 crore plus depreciation ₹5.47 crore) provides
reasonable cover against long-term borrowings of ₹25.74 crore. Against this, free cash and bank
balances of ₹2.54 crore are thin, and the company operates with substantial current borrowings of
₹35.65 crore, implying continued reliance on working capital limits.

_A dated bank-facility utilisation statement and a debt repayment schedule were not among the
documents reviewed; the liquidity assessment rests on the balance-sheet and accrual evidence cited.
This is recorded rather than presented as a complete liquidity review._

## **Environment, social, and governance (ESG) risks**

**Environmental:** Textile processing is water- and energy-intensive, with dyeing and finishing
generating effluent that is subject to pollution-control regulation. Cotton sourcing carries
indirect exposure to agricultural water use and to climate impacts on crop yield, which transmit to
the company as raw-material price volatility. The company discloses Corporate Social Responsibility
governance in its annual report; quantified emissions, energy and water intensity data were **not
located** in the documents reviewed — searched across all three annual reports for `Scope 1`,
`Scope 2`, `emission intensity` and `water consumption`, with no quantified disclosure returned.
As an unlisted-scale filer the company is not required to publish a BRSR with the metrics that
larger issuers disclose.

**Social:** The industry is labour-intensive, so workforce safety, contract-labour practices and
working conditions are the material social exposures. Export-oriented home textiles additionally
face buyer-mandated social-compliance audits, where a lapse can cost order flow rather than merely
reputation.

**Governance:** The company is listed (BSE 522215) and files under SEBI LODR. The Board includes a
Chairperson and Whole-time Director from the promoter family, with a Chief Financial Officer and
Company Secretary in place. The statutory auditor, Ashwani K Sindwani & Co., issued an **unmodified
opinion** on the FY2025 financial statements, and the audit report records that "nothing has come
to our attention, which causes us to believe that any material uncertainty exists" regarding going
concern. No qualification, adverse opinion or Emphasis of Matter was identified in the documents
reviewed. Promoter-family concentration in executive roles is a governance consideration typical of
companies of this scale.

## **About the company and industry**

Riba Textiles Limited, incorporated in 1989 and listed on the BSE (scrip 522215), manufactures terry
towels and home textiles from its facility at Panipat, Haryana. The company is promoter-managed,
with Asha Garg as Chairperson and Nitin Garg as Whole-time Director. It has no subsidiaries, joint
ventures or associates.

The Indian home-textile industry is fragmented and export-oriented, with Panipat a long-established
cluster for terry towels and made-ups. Demand is driven by domestic consumption growth and by
export orders from North America and Europe. Margins across the industry are structurally thin and
highly sensitive to cotton and yarn price movements, with pass-through to buyers typically lagged
by one to two quarters.

## **Correction note — 19 August 2026**

After this assessment was drafted from the FY2022-23 to FY2024-25 annual reports, CareEdge
Ratings' published press release of **01-Jul-2026** was supplied. It postdates every document in
the case file and materially extends the picture. The rating conclusion is unchanged —
**CARE BBB; Stable / CARE A3+** — but four things in the analysis above are superseded or were
unavailable, and are corrected here rather than silently edited.

**1. The rated amounts are now known, not estimated.** ₹29.00 crore fund-based long-term term loan
maturing March 2029, and ₹65.00 crore fund-based long-term/short-term working capital limits —
₹94.00 crore in aggregate, corroborating the annual report's figure. The Rating Action table and
Annexure I above have been updated.

**2. The company was on Credit Watch with Negative Implications, and this was unknowable from the
documents supplied.** The United States imposed a 50% tariff on imported textiles and apparel;
CareEdge placed RTL on watch. The action of 01-Jul-2026 **removed the watch and assigned a Stable
outlook** following the tariff's reduction to 10%, together with the company's diversification
toward Latin America. None of this appears in annual reports ending March 2025.

**3. FY2026 reverses the trends this assessment identified.** The deleveraging described above did
not continue:

| | FY2025 | FY2026 | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total operating income | 295.86 | **255.38** | −13.7% |
| PBILDT | 22.68 | 22.71 | flat |
| PBILDT margin (%) | 7.67 | **8.89** | improved |
| PAT | 8.50 | 8.13 | −4.4% |
| Tangible net worth | 97.90 | **106.02** | +8.3% |
| Total debt | 61.39 | **75.88** | +23.6% |
| Overall gearing (x) | 0.63 | **0.72** | worsened |
| Interest coverage (x) | 3.85 | **3.50** | worsened |

Revenue fell on weaker demand from key export markets, and gearing rose on higher working-capital
borrowing. The Outlook section above anticipates that "revenue growth will continue" — that
expectation is not borne out by FY2026 and should be read as superseded. Encouragingly, margin
*improved* to 8.89% on a favourable raw-material environment, which is the opposite of the
margin-compression concern raised in Key weaknesses.

**4. Geographic concentration was the live risk, and is easing.** Exports were about 89% of gross
sales in FY2026, with the United States falling to roughly 20% of sales from about 29% in FY2025 as
the company expanded into Chile, Saudi Arabia, Brazil, the UAE and Peru. Top-10 customers were
about 34% of operating income. Installed capacity is 8,400 metric tonnes per annum.
