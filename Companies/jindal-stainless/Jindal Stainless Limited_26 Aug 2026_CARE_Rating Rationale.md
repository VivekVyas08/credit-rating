## **Rationale and key rating drivers**

The reaffirmation reflects Jindal Stainless Limited's (JSL) position as India's largest stainless steel producer and, more specifically, the fact that its scale is now translating into unit economics that have improved rather than merely held. Finished-goods sales volume rose 8.1% to approximately 2.57 million tonnes (MT) in FY2026, and PBILDT per tonne rose to approximately Rs. 21,640 from Rs. 19,630 in FY2025 — so the earnings improvement is not simply a volume effect. Consolidated PBILDT of Rs. 5,560.43 crore in FY2026 (FY2025: Rs. 4,666.63 crore) was earned on a PBILDT margin of 12.94%, against 11.87% in FY2025 and 12.20% in FY2024, on total operating income of Rs. 42,954.66 crore.

The financial risk profile is the second anchor and is comfortable for the rating category on every measure CareEdge weighs. Overall gearing was 0.42 times at March 31, 2026 on a tangible net worth of Rs. 17,900.83 crore; total debt to PBILDT was 1.34 times and net debt to PBILDT 0.81 times; interest coverage was 9.79 times. Return on capital employed was 19.25% on average capital employed. None of these has deteriorated materially through a period in which the company spent approximately Rs. 4,225 crore of capital expenditure in FY2026 alone — gearing has moved only between 0.36 and 0.46 times across FY2023–FY2026 while the net fixed-asset block nearly doubled, from Rs. 9,776.21 crore to Rs. 18,041.62 crore.

Capacity is being added on a scale that will define the next three years. The 1.2 MTPA melt shop in Indonesia (PT Glory Metal Indonesia) was commissioned during FY2026 ahead of schedule, taking group melting capacity to 4.2 MTPA against roughly 3 MTPA in India, with the Indonesian output intended for further processing in India. Goodwill on consolidation rose to Rs. 1,247.38 crore from Rs. 899.24 crore over the year, reflecting acquisition activity alongside organic expansion. Management's stated aspiration is finished-goods sales of 3.5 MTPA by FY2029.

The ratings remain constrained by the characteristics of the industry rather than by anything specific to the issuer's balance sheet. Stainless steel realisation is driven by nickel and chrome, neither of which JSL controls, and the company's own disclosures identify import competition and trade-policy shifts as live exposures — the annual report notes global stainless melt output growing only 0.3% in CY2026 and 2.2% in CY2027, a demand backdrop that leaves limited room for the industry to absorb new capacity without price pressure. Working-capital intensity has improved substantially (operating net working capital fell from 11.3% of revenue in FY2023 to 7.0% in FY2026) and CareEdge does not assume that improvement continues. Execution of the remaining capital expenditure to schedule and budget, and commissioning of the added capacity into utilisation rather than into inventory, remain the principal monitorables.

## **Rating sensitivities: Factors likely to lead to rating actions**

### **Positive factors**

- Sales volume sustained above 3.2 MT with PBILDT per tonne sustained above Rs. 22,000 on a consolidated basis, evidencing that the added melt and downstream capacity is being absorbed at unchanged unit economics.
- Overall gearing sustained below 0.30 times and total debt to PBILDT sustained below 1.00 time, with the current capital-expenditure programme substantially complete.

### **Negative factors**

- PBILDT per tonne sustained below Rs. 16,000, or PBILDT margin sustained below 10.5%, on account of realisation pressure or unrecovered input-cost inflation.
- Overall gearing rising above 0.75 times, or total debt to PBILDT above 2.50 times, on a sustained basis — whether from a debt-funded acquisition, a cost overrun, or a working-capital build that reverses the recent improvement in intensity.
- Material delay or cost overrun in commissioning the added capacity, deferring the earnings contribution on which the projected debt reduction depends.

## **Analytical approach:** Consolidated

JSL is analysed on a consolidated basis. Its subsidiaries and step-down entities in India, Indonesia and Spain are operationally inseparable from the parent — the Indonesian melt capacity exists to feed Indian downstream processing — and financial linkages run throughout the group. A standalone view would exclude both the newly commissioned melt platform and a material part of the group's processing chain. The entities consolidated are listed in Annexure-2.

## **Outlook:** Stable

The Stable outlook reflects CareEdge's expectation that JSL's leading domestic position and improving unit economics will sustain PBILDT broadly in the Rs. 6,000–6,700 crore range over FY2027–FY2028, and that leverage will peak in FY2027 as the current capital-expenditure programme completes and decline thereafter. It further reflects the assessment that the balance sheet carries sufficient headroom to absorb a cyclical downturn in stainless realisation without migrating from the assigned rating.

## **Key strengths**

**Largest domestic stainless steel producer, with scale now visible in unit economics rather than volume alone** – Finished-goods sales volume of approximately 2.57 MT in FY2026 (FY2025: 2.377 MT) was accompanied by PBILDT per tonne of approximately Rs. 21,640 against Rs. 19,630 the previous year — a 10.2% improvement in the per-tonne measure on top of 8.1% volume growth. Net sales realisation was approximately Rs. 1.67 lakh per tonne. For a producer of a substantially commoditised product, sustaining a rising per-tonne spread through a period of capacity addition is the relevant evidence of market position, and it is what separates JSL from a marginal domestic producer.

**Integrated and increasingly international manufacturing platform** – Group melting capacity reached 4.2 MTPA during FY2026 with the commissioning of the 1.2 MTPA PT Glory Metal Indonesia melt shop ahead of schedule, against approximately 3 MTPA in India, with the Indonesian output routed to Indian downstream processing including hot rolling, annealing and pickling. The company reports a single business segment under Ind AS 108, and the group operates manufacturing across multiple Indian locations alongside the new Indonesian platform. This structure gives JSL both a raw-material and a capacity hedge that a single-location domestic producer does not have.

**Financial risk profile that has absorbed a near-doubling of the asset base without leverage drifting** – The net fixed-asset block rose from Rs. 9,776.21 crore (FY2023) to Rs. 18,041.62 crore (FY2026), and capital expenditure was approximately Rs. 4,225 crore in FY2026 alone. Across the same four years overall gearing moved only within a 0.36–0.46 times band, total debt to PBILDT within 1.10–1.37 times, and interest coverage stayed between 7.63 and 11.05 times. Tangible net worth grew from Rs. 11,009.70 crore to Rs. 17,900.83 crore, entirely through retained earnings. That combination — heavy investment funded largely from accrual, with leverage flat — is the core of the rating.

**Working-capital discipline that has released, not consumed, cash during expansion** – Operating net working capital fell from 11.3% of revenue in FY2023 to 9.9%, then 8.3%, then 7.0% in FY2026. In absolute terms it declined from Rs. 4,050.81 crore to Rs. 3,008.34 crore even as revenue grew 20.3% over the same period. Expansion phases at steel producers typically consume working capital; here it has funded part of the capital expenditure.

## **Key weaknesses**

**Earnings exposed to nickel and chrome, and to import competition the company does not control** – Stainless realisation is set by input metal prices and by the landed price of imports, neither of which JSL sets. The company's own disclosures identify import pressure and evolving trade policy as live exposures, and the annual report's own industry data — global stainless melt output growing 0.3% in CY2026 and 2.2% in CY2027 — describes a market with limited capacity to absorb new supply without price effect. A stress case flat on volume and 6% lower on realisation, at a 10.5% margin, produces revenue of about Rs. 40,377 crore and PBILDT of about Rs. 4,240 crore, taking total debt to PBILDT to approximately 1.76 times and interest coverage to approximately 7.3 times (FY2026 closing debt held flat at Rs. 7,460.33 crore, priced at 7.8%). Both remain inside the rating category, which is why this is a weakness rather than a constraint.

**Foreign-currency exposure is a persistent and material earnings contributor, and it is shrinking** – JSL has reported a NET FOREIGN-CURRENCY GAIN in each of the last four years, recorded inside Other Expenses: Rs. 68.48 crore (FY2023), Rs. 190.04 crore (FY2024), Rs. 154.37 crore (FY2025) and Rs. 78.41 crore (FY2026). Two things follow. First, the contribution is material — FY2024's gain was 4.0% of that year's PBILDT and FY2025's 3.3% — so a reversal would be felt in the margin, and the FY2026 gain is already less than half FY2024's. Second, the exposure is structural rather than incidental: it arises against a foreign-currency loan base of only about Rs. 350.05 crore, which is far too small to produce gains of this size through translation on borrowings, so it is predominantly trade and working-capital driven and will grow with the export book and the Indonesian operation. Because CareEdge treats non-debt foreign exchange as operating, these gains sit inside PBILDT and are not stripped out; the margin series above therefore includes them, and a reader should read the margin trend with that in mind.

**A large capital-expenditure programme still in flight, with the earnings contribution ahead of the spend** – Approximately Rs. 4,225 crore was spent in FY2026 and the base case assumes a further Rs. 4,000 crore in FY2027 tapering thereafter. The Indonesian melt shop is commissioned but the downstream capacity intended to process its output, and the volume ramp toward management's 3.5 MTPA aspiration, are still to be delivered. Debt is projected to peak in FY2027 before declining; a delay in that ramp defers the deleveraging rather than preventing it, but it would extend the period at peak leverage.

**Related-party density within the wider Jindal group** – Transactions with group entities including Jindal United Steel Limited and Jindal Coke Limited are disclosed and appear to be conducted at arm's length, and the FY2025 divestment of the residual Jindal Coke holding reduced one such linkage. The volume of intra-group dealing nonetheless remains a governance factor that warrants continued monitoring, and it is the reason the management and governance assessment is not scored at the strongest level.

## **Liquidity: Strong**

Unencumbered liquid assets stood at **Rs. 2,931.60 crore** at March 31, 2026 — cash and cash equivalents of Rs. 415.12 crore, other bank balances of Rs. 2,518.91 crore and current investments of Rs. 1.12 crore, less Rs. 3.55 crore disclosed as under lien with banks. The encumbrance was checked against the annual report's own security and lien disclosures rather than assumed away, and at 0.1% of the total it is immaterial; the balance is genuinely available.

Against that, PBILDT of Rs. 5,560.43 crore covered finance costs of Rs. 567.93 crore 9.79 times in FY2026, and internal accrual (PAT plus depreciation) was Rs. 4,244.87 crore. The current ratio was 1.25 times. The funding-gap analysis underlying the base case shows total debt peaking at approximately Rs. 7,815 crore in FY2027 and declining in every year thereafter, with the company reaching a net cash position by FY2031 — that path is an output of the capital-expenditure and accrual arithmetic, not an assumption.

The descriptor is **Strong** rather than Superior because the liquid balance of Rs. 2,931.60 crore, while ample against near-term obligations, does not by itself cover the remaining committed capital-expenditure programme; the company relies on continuing operating cash generation to do that. That generation is strong and has been consistent, but it is a flow rather than a stock, and Superior is reserved for issuers whose stock of liquidity covers their commitments outright.

## **Assumptions/Covenants**

Base case: finished-goods sales volume of 2.78 MT in FY2027 rising to 3.60 MT by FY2031 — a deliberate discount to management's 3.5 MTPA-by-FY2029 aspiration, which the base case reaches only in FY2031; net sales realisation growing 1.0–1.5% per annum; PBILDT margin of 12.8–13.2%; capital expenditure of Rs. 4,000 crore in FY2027 tapering to Rs. 2,400 crore by FY2031; interest priced at 7.8% on opening debt; effective tax rate of 25%; dividend payout of 12%; operating net working capital held at 7.2% of revenue rather than assuming the recent improvement continues. No covenant breach was identified in the documents reviewed.

## **Environment, social, and governance (ESG) risks**

**Environmental** – Stainless steel production is energy- and emissions-intensive and JSL's environmental exposure is material rather than nominal. The company reports low-carbon operations and circular resource use as strategic priorities, directed 61.13% of research and development investment and 10.69% of capital expenditure toward environmental and social improvement during FY2026, and reports 100% sustainable sourcing of inputs. It has entered a 15-year partnership with Harsco Environmental to double annual slag-processing capacity to 0.72 million tonnes at its Jajpur unit, a circular-production measure with a direct waste-diversion effect. The company identifies deforestation-linked sourcing requirements and potential carbon-pricing mechanisms as forward regulatory exposures in its own risk disclosure. Environmental risk is assessed as **moderate to high** in absolute terms — inherent to the sector — but is not a rating constraint at the assigned level.

**Social** – The principal social exposures are workforce safety in heavy manufacturing and community relations around the Jajpur and Hisar operations. The company reports a total workforce of 5,303, tracks women in STEM roles as a disclosed metric, and reports community programmes including the Stainless Swachhata Abhiyaan waste-management initiative and skilling partnerships. The materiality sweep across the document set identified no labour dispute, major safety controversy or community objection. Social risk is assessed as **low to moderate** and does not constrain the rating.

**Governance** – The audit opinion is unmodified in every year reviewed, issued by joint statutory auditors (Walker Chandiok & Co LLP and Lodha & Co LLP), with no qualification, Emphasis of Matter or going-concern flag identified. The board separates the Managing Director and the Chief Executive Officer / Chief Financial Officer roles. The offsetting factor is the density of related-party transactions within the wider Jindal group, discussed under Key weaknesses. Governance risk is assessed as **moderate**.

## **About the company and industry**

Jindal Stainless Limited, incorporated in 1970 and part of the Jindal group, is India's largest stainless steel producer. It manufactures across multiple Indian locations, principally Jajpur (Odisha) and Hisar (Haryana), and reports a single reportable business segment under Ind AS 108. Group melting capacity reached 4.2 MTPA during FY2026, comprising approximately 3 MTPA in India and the 1.2 MTPA PT Glory Metal Indonesia melt shop commissioned during the year, with Indonesian output routed to Indian downstream processing. The company sells across the 200, 300 and 400 series and has been increasing the share of value-added and cold-rolled products. Finished-goods sales volume was approximately 2.57 MT in FY2026. Management has articulated an aspiration of 3.5 MTPA of finished-goods sales by FY2029. The company operates research and development capability internally and has expanded its export presence, naming Japan, South Korea, Taiwan and Germany among markets strengthened during FY2026.

The Indian stainless steel industry is driven by construction and infrastructure, automotive and railways, process industry and consumer durables demand. It is exposed to nickel and chrome price volatility, since both are priced internationally, and to import competition, particularly from Chinese-origin material routed through ASEAN free-trade agreements. The annual report's own industry data places global stainless melt shop production at 64.16 MT in CY2025, rising to an expected 64.35 MT in CY2026 and 65.76 MT in CY2027 — growth of 0.3% and 2.2% respectively, indicating a broadly stable but not expanding global demand backdrop.

**Computation basis, stated rather than implied.** PBILDT is struck as profit before exceptional items, share of equity-method investments and tax, plus finance costs and depreciation, less other income. That base is not an analyst construct — the annual report publishes its own EBITDA on exactly this definition and this engine reproduces it to the paisa in both years it is disclosed (FY2025 Rs. 4,666.63 crore; FY2026 Rs. 5,560.43 crore). Two adjustments follow from CareEdge's own criteria and are applied symmetrically by item:

1. **Non-debt foreign-exchange result stays inside PBILDT, and on this issuer needs no adjustment at all.** The Other Expenses note (FY2025-26 note 34; FY2023-24 note 33) reports a NET GAIN on foreign currency transactions and translation in every year — Rs. 68.48 crore, Rs. 190.04 crore, Rs. 154.37 crore and Rs. 78.41 crore across FY2023 to FY2026 — as a credit inside Other Expenses. It is therefore already captured in the base, and nothing is restored. An earlier version of this analysis added back Rs. 15.57 crore (FY2023) and Rs. 70.04 crore (FY2025) on the assumption that the gain sat in Other Income and had been stripped by the "less other income" term. It never sat there, and those two figures were not the profit-and-loss line in any case: they were the "net unrealised foreign exchange (gain)/loss" rows from the CASH-FLOW RECONCILIATION, which capture only the unrealised portion. The correction removes Rs. 70.04 crore from FY2025 PBILDT and Rs. 15.57 crore from FY2023. The exposure is classified non-debt because the foreign-currency loan base of approximately Rs. 350.05 crore is far too small to generate results of this size through translation on borrowings; that remains a stated judgement.
2. **Profit or loss on sale of assets and investments is non-operating and is removed** — profit on sale of current investments (FY2026 Rs. 3.75 crore, FY2025 Rs. 4.85 crore) sits in Other Income and the base has already excluded it. Loss on disposal of property, plant and equipment is NOT isolated as a line in the Other Expenses note; the cash-flow reconciliation implies about Rs. 11.61 crore in FY2026, or 0.2% of PBILDT. It is left UNADJUSTED and disclosed here rather than adjusted off the cash-flow reconciliation, which had already been misread once in this case for exactly this kind of item.

**Exceptional items are excluded by construction**, since the base begins below them. The FY2025 loss of Rs. 7.06 crore on the Jindal Coke stake and the FY2026 gain of Rs. 17.40 crore under Ind AS 103 both sit inside exceptional items per notes 39 and 40, and are therefore **not** added back a second time.

**Tangible net worth** is total equity including non-controlling interest, less goodwill and intangibles. Deferred tax assets are **not** deducted — that deduction belongs to CareEdge's financial-sector definition and does not apply to a manufacturer. JSL's intangibles comprise mine development expenses, software, customer relationships, trademarks and customer/vendor contracts, none of which fall within the four critical-to-operations exceptions CareEdge admits.

**Total debt** is borrowings plus lease liabilities. This is the **unadjusted** measure: CareEdge's adjusted leverage also incorporates acceptances/LC creditors and guarantees extended to group entities, neither of which is separately quantified in the consolidated statements. Both should be obtained from the issuer; gearing and net debt/PBILDT stated above would be higher on the adjusted basis, and the figures here are labelled accordingly rather than presented as if they were the adjusted measure.
