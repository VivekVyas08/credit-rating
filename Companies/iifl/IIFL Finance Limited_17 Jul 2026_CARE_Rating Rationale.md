## Rationale and key rating drivers

The assigned ratings to the debt instruments of IIFL Finance Limited (**IIFL Finance / the Company**) factor in its established position as one of India's leading gold-loan non-banking finance companies (NBFCs) with a three-decade track record and a 4,829-branch network; its **strong capitalisation** (standalone CRAR of 17.84% with Tier-I of 12.24% as on March 31, 2026 against the 15% regulatory minimum, reinforced during FY2025–26 by a US$152 million rights issue and a US$325 million international bond); a **predominantly secured loan book** (92.1% of standalone gross advances secured, largely by gold); and a **sharp recovery in profitability and asset quality** following the lifting of the FY2025 RBI restriction on its gold-loan business. On a standalone basis the Company reported PAT of **₹1,153.5 crore in FY2026** (against a loss of ₹409.6 crore in FY2025), with RoTA of 2.82%, RoNW of 17.0% and NIM of 7.74%. Ratings also derive comfort from a diversified resource profile and a strengthened Board (chaired by a former RBI Deputy Governor), with nil promoter-share pledge.

Ratings are constrained by the Company's **high single-product concentration in gold loans** (~91% of the standalone book) — the exposure affected by the RBI's six-month embargo on fresh gold-loan disbursements in FY2025; by **regulatory / compliance event risk** (the embargo was resolved in September 2024 without penalty, but the RBI levied a ₹5.30 lakh monetary penalty in February 2026 for an NPA-classification lapse, and an income-tax assessment relating to the Company remains pending, with disputed income-tax demands of ₹337.80 crore); and by **rising leverage on a rapidly growing, partly unseasoned book** (standalone overall gearing rose to 5.26x as on March 31, 2026 from 3.95x a year earlier, as gross advances grew ~65%). The Company's ability to sustain profitable growth and asset quality while managing the RBI Gold Loan Directions, 2025 (tighter LTV / bullet-repayment norms) will be a key monitorable.

## Rating sensitivities: factors likely to lead to rating actions

**Positive factors**
- Sustained diversification of the loan book away from single-product (gold-loan) concentration while maintaining asset quality and strong capitalisation.
- A sustained track record of regulatory/compliance stability and favourable resolution of the pending income-tax matter.

**Negative factors**
- Any recurrence of adverse regulatory action or a material governance/compliance lapse.
- Overall gearing sustained above ~6.0x, or CRAR/Tier-I trending towards the regulatory minimum.
- GNPA sustained above ~3%, or RoTA sustained below ~2.0%.

## Analytical approach: **Standalone**

This rating is assigned on a **standalone** basis to IIFL Finance Limited, at the user's instruction. **This is a deliberate deviation from CARE's consolidation-methodology default:** the material subsidiaries — IIFL Home Finance Limited (HFC) and IIFL Samasta Finance Limited (NBFC-MFI) — operate in similar (lending) businesses with high integration, which would ordinarily warrant a consolidated view. Rating standalone assesses only IIFL Finance's own book (predominantly gold loans) and financials; the subsidiaries' credit strength (on a consolidated basis materially larger and more diversified — group AUM ₹1,08,180 crore, consolidated CRAR 25.3%) is **not** consolidated. Consistent with the standalone-exception treatment in CARE's consolidation criteria, the cash-flow impact of IIFL Finance's investments in its subsidiaries (HFC ₹825 crore; both subsidiaries profitable and self-sustaining) has been considered, and those investments assessed for impairment. No parent/promoter-group notching is applied (IIFL Finance is the parent; promoter and Fairfax treated as neutral for support-notching).

## Outlook: **Stable**

The Stable outlook reflects the expectation that IIFL Finance will sustain its post-recovery growth with capitalisation, secured-book asset quality and profitability at current strong levels, balanced against the concentration and regulatory-event overhang.

## Detailed description of key rating drivers

### Key strengths

**Established large-scale gold-loan franchise with a three-decade track record.** IIFL Finance is among India's largest gold-loan NBFCs, operating 4,829 branches across 28 states/UTs and serving over 4.6 million customers; gold-loan AUM reached ₹52,581 crore in FY2026 (an all-time high). The long vintage and pan-India last-mile network provide durable revenue visibility in the core secured product.

**Strong capitalisation with demonstrated ability to raise capital.** Standalone CRAR was 17.84% (Tier-I 12.24%; Tier-II 5.60%) as on March 31, 2026, above the 15% minimum. Capital was reinforced by a US$152 million rights issue (Fairfax participated) and a US$325 million international bond, and the Company issued ₹600 crore of Perpetual Debt Instruments (Tier-I) in FY2026. Standalone tangible net worth was ₹7,371 crore, with nil promoter-share pledge.

**Predominantly secured book underpinning healthy asset quality.** 92.1% of standalone gross advances (₹38,565 crore) are secured by tangible assets, with unsecured exposure reduced sharply to 7.7% (from 28.8% a year earlier). On a standalone basis (Note 38A.6, stage-wise EAD), Gross Stage-3 (GNPA) was **1.13%** and Net Stage-3 (NNPA) **0.58%** as on March 31, 2026 (FY25 GNPA 1.84%), with Stage-2 at 1.96%; the impairment allowance of ₹429.3 crore is 1.11% of gross loans. (The Company's group-headline figures are GNPA 1.5% / NNPA 0.7% / PCR 93%.) The collateralised (gold) core supports low loss-given-default.

**Sharp recovery in profitability.** Standalone PAT rebounded to ₹1,153.5 crore in FY2026 (FY2025: loss ₹409.6 crore; FY2024: ₹584.8 crore), with RoTA 2.82%, RoNW 17.0%, NIM 7.74%, interest spread ~10.5% and cost-income 46.8%; credit cost was contained at 1.73% of average assets.

**Diversified resource profile.** Funding spans banks (term loans/ECB/refinance), public and private NCDs, subordinated and perpetual debt, and securitisation/co-lending/direct-assignment partnerships (FY2026: securitisation ₹6,890 crore + direct assignment ₹24,202 crore). Standalone cost of borrowing was ~10.35%.

### Key weaknesses

**High single-product concentration in gold loans.** ~91% of the standalone book is gold loans. While secured and granular, this concentration exposes the standalone entity to gold-price movements and, as FY2025 demonstrated, to product-specific regulatory action more acutely than the diversified consolidated group.

**Regulatory and compliance event risk.** In March 2024 the RBI barred fresh gold-loan sanction/disbursal citing process-level deviations; the restriction was lifted in September 2024 after a special audit, without penalty. Subsequently the RBI imposed a ₹5.30 lakh monetary penalty (February 2026) for an NPA-classification lapse (FY24 position), and an income-tax search (January 2025) and pending assessment remain, with disputed income-tax demands of ₹337.80 crore (₹20.25 crore paid under protest). A ratings agency (ICRA) maintains a Negative outlook. These temper the management/governance assessment, partly mitigated by a strengthened Board (former RBI Deputy Governor as Chair; members from RBI, NABARD, HDFC, ICICI) and a clean FY2026 joint statutory audit with no qualifications. The **RBI Gold Loan Directions, 2025** (tighter LTV and bullet-repayment norms) may compress yields on large-ticket gold loans and are a forward monitorable.

**Rising leverage on rapid, partly unseasoned growth.** Standalone gross advances grew ~65% in FY2026 and overall gearing rose to 5.26x (from 3.95x). Rapid growth reduces portfolio seasoning; leverage remains moderate for a predominantly secured book but is a monitorable.

### Liquidity: **Adequate**
The descriptor is the conclusion of the ALM arithmetic (Note 38B). Contractual debt repayments falling due within 12 months total **₹15,590 crore** (debt securities ₹8,053 cr + other borrowings ₹7,311 cr + sub-debt ₹116 cr + lease ₹111 cr); against these, standalone cash and bank balances were ₹1,923 crore (immediate cover ≈ **1.48 months** of debt from liquid assets alone), supplemented by liquid investments. The gap is bridged by the **short-tenor secured advance book** — the ~91% gold-loan portfolio matures/rolls within a year, generating large 12-month inflows against liabilities that are **termed out** (only ~30% of borrowings fall due within a year; ₹14,444 crore sits in the 1–3-year bucket and ₹6,258 crore beyond 3 years) — plus undrawn bank lines and a diversified funding base; the group liquidity buffer was ₹6,638 crore. This positive asset-shorter-than-liability mismatch, and the demonstrated repayment of over ₹18,000 crore to lenders during the FY2025 embargo, support an Adequate descriptor; CP and short-term obligations are not assumed to be rolled over. A 5-year base-case projection shows PAT rising to ~₹3,173 crore by FY2031 with gearing de-levering to ~4.9x; a stress case (higher credit cost, lower margin, halved growth) compresses PAT but keeps it positive and gearing within tolerance.

### Environment, social, and governance (ESG) risks
**Environmental** (indirect for an NBFC): Scope-3 emissions 11,063.80 tCO₂e; renewable adoption via Tata Green Tariff at large offices; ~21 lakh litres water conserved (rainwater harvesting); LEED-Gold Gurugram office and three ISO-14001-certified branches — low direct exposure.
**Social:** fair gold-auction conduct (the subject of the FY2025 embargo) and microfinance conduct (via subsidiary) are the key monitorables; a financial-inclusion profile of 4.6 million largely underserved borrowers; CSR spend ₹40 crore (27.95 lakh beneficiaries). Under POSH, 13 complaints were received in FY2026, 11 disposed, 2 pending at year-end (none pending >90 days).
**Governance** — the most material ESG dimension here. Board of nine with **55.56% Independent Directors (five of nine)**, chaired by a former RBI Deputy Governor, nil promoter-share pledge, and a clean FY2026 joint statutory audit with no qualifications, no Emphasis of Matter and a confirmed going-concern basis; BRSR-Core disclosures were externally assured (BDO India). Offsetting flags: the FY2025 gold-loan embargo; a **₹5.30 lakh RBI monetary penalty (February 2026)** for an NPA-classification lapse — which the Company's own investor materials describe as "Zero Regulatory Penalties", a disclosure-consistency point; a January-2025 income-tax search with disputed demands of ₹337.80 crore pending; and a scheduled statutory-auditor rotation (Sharp & Tannan exiting, Shah Gupta & Co. proposed from FY2027). Related-party transactions (Note 44) are with subsidiaries and the Fairfax entity (FIH Mauritius) and are stated to be at arm's length; the Company confirms no term-free loans to promoters/directors/KMP.
