## **Rationale** 

The upgrade in the ratings for Cemindia Projects Limited (CPL, formerly ITD Cementation India Limited) factors in the demonstrated operational synergies from being a part of the Adani Group as reflected by sizeable order inflows, with around 65% of new orders (out of ~Rs. 25,000 crore) during the 15 months that ended in June 2026 originating from the Adani Group. Post acquisition of majority equity stake by Renew Exim DMCC (an Adani Group entity) in May 2025, CPL has emerged as the key engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) arm for the Adani Group, while continuing to pursue external orders. CPL reported a robust order book (OB) position of Rs. 31,306.8 crore as on June 30, 2026, providing medium-term revenue visibility, translating into an OB/OI ratio of 3.1 times based on the operating income (OI) in FY2026. The order book has recorded a five-year CAGR of ~21%, increasing by ~71% from Rs. 18,300 crore as on March 31, 2025. The order book is well diversified in terms of segments, clients and geography. CPL is expected to benefit from access to the Group’s sizeable infrastructure project pipeline across ports, roads, airports, data centres, water and energy infrastructure. Further, the company enjoys greater financial flexibility as reflected in reduction in bank rates/charges post the acquisition by the Adani Group as well as favourable terms for bank guarantee (BG) requirements on Group orders. This also enhances CPL’s ability to bid for external projects. The counterparty risk associated with Group orders is mitigated to an extent by CPL’s direct contractual exposure to established Adani Group entities. Moreover, CPL has shareholder approval for raising additional equity up to Rs. 5,000 crore through qualified institutional placement (QIP) route, to support its expansion plans. The proposed equity raise would limit the company’s reliance on external debt and help to maintain its borrowings at prudent levels for any inorganic expansion plans and purposes other than regular capex. 

The rating action also favourably considers CPL’s increasing scale of operations, with the consolidated OI rising by 8.4% to Rs. 10,130.4 crore in FY2026 and recording a five-year CAGR of 30.0% during FY2021-FY2026. This trend continued in Q1 FY2027 with YoY growth of 7% (OI of Rs. 2,720.9 crore). The revenue growth is expected to strengthen by around 20-25% p.a. in FY2027 and FY2028, supported by the ramp-up of order book execution. The operating margin rose to 10.7% in FY2026 from 9.8% in FY2025, aided by operating leverage, project mix and claim-related income, and remained at 10.1% in Q1 FY2027. The operating margin is projected to remain range-bound within 10.0-10.7% over the medium term. The scale up, along with stable profitability is likely to support its financial profile. The coverage metrics also posted an improvement, with interest coverage at 5.0 times in FY2026 (3.8 times in FY2025). The same is expected to improve further and remain over 6 times in near term.

The ratings draw comfort from CPL’s established track record of over four decades in executing complex infrastructure projects, including marine, tunnelling, and underground metro works, supported by a diversified order book across segments and geographies, a strong technical team, and a fleet of specialised equipment. CPL is expected to maintain a balanced mix between Adani Group and non-group orders to sustain diversification, while deriving benefits from operational synergies. 

The long-term rating is, however, constrained by the increasing working capital-intensive nature of CPL’s operations due to sizeable inventory (mainly work-in-progress), as most projects have milestone-based billing for certain types of work. The net working capital (NWC)/OI increased to 4.7% in FY2026 from 0.8% in FY2025 primarily due to increase in inventory days to 103 (March 2025: 77 days) and debtor days to 47 (March 2025: 39 days). The working capital requirements are largely supported by mobilisation advances of around Rs. 1,015 crore as on March 31, 2026 and extended credit from suppliers, leading to a Total Outside Liabilities/Tangible Net Worth (TOL/TNW) of 1.9 times as on March 31, 2026 (March 2025: 2.2 times). The company is also exposed to execution risk, with around 65% of the order book as on June 30, 2026 in the nascent stages of execution (less than 10% executed), including around 34% where execution was yet to commence. However, these mainly comprise orders received in Q4 FY2026 and Q1 FY2027 that were in the mobilisation, design or approval stages. Any delay in work-front availability, land access or design approvals could adversely affect the progress of these projects. The ratings also factor in the inherent exposure to sizeable contingent liabilities in the form of BGs, mainly towards contractual performance, mobilisation advances and retention money. Nonetheless, the risks are partly mitigated by CPL’s established execution track record and absence of BG invocation in the past.  The ratings note the heightened competition in the construction sector, along with any volatility in input costs (steel, cement, etc), which could exert pressure on CPL’s profitability, despite the presence of price variation clause for core raw materials in these contracts. Given that the company has funded a sizeable share of its working capital requirement through extended credit period and relatively longer term (~2-3 years) advance from customers, any decline in either of these would have a bearing on its liquidity position. In the backdrop of expected sizeable growth over the next three fiscals, which will entail increase in working capital requirement, the company’s ability to judiciously manage its working capital cycle and maintain adequate liquidity remains important from the credit perspective. 

The Stable outlook on the long-term rating reflects ICRA’s expectation that CPL will benefit from its strong and well-diversified order book position, established execution capabilities and demonstrated operational synergies with the Adani Group. 

# **Key rating drivers and their description** 

## **Credit strengths** 

**Being part of Adani Group drives growth synergies and strong financial flexibility** – Following the acquisition by Renew Exim 

DMCC in May 2025, CPL has emerged as the key EPC execution arm for the Adani Group, while continuing to pursue external orders. The sizeable order inflows from the Group and access to the Group’s infrastructure project pipeline support CPL’s growth prospects. Further, the company enjoys favourable terms for BG requirements on Group orders, enhancing its ability to bid for external projects. The counterparty risk associated with such orders is mitigated to an extent by CPL’s direct contractual exposure to established Adani Group entities. The association with the Group has also facilitated more favourable banking terms, thereby strengthening CPL’s financial flexibility. 

**Strong diversified order book position provides medium-term revenue visibility** – CPL’s unexecuted order book stood at Rs. 31,306.8 crore as on June 30, 2026, translating into an OB/OI of 3.1 times based on the OI in FY2026, providing healthy mediumterm revenue visibility. The order book recorded a five-year CAGR of ~21%, increasing by ~71% from Rs. 18,300 crore as on March 31, 2025, supported by sizeable order inflow from the Group. The order book is diversified across marine infrastructure (23.2%), urban infrastructure/MRTS (16.5%), data centres (13.3%), water and sewage (10.9%), buildings (8.8%), transportation (8.4%), industrial structures (8.1%), among other segments. The top five projects accounted for 32% and top three clients contributed to 26%. Geographically, the order book is spread across multiple states and overseas locations, although Maharashtra, Rajasthan and Delhi together occupy 46% of the share. CPL caters to established private-sector entities, government bodies and public-sector undertakings, thereby mitigating the counterparty risk to a large extent.

– **Large scale of operations and healthy financial profile** CPL’s ability to execute technically complex projects has supported the scale-up in OI to Rs. 10,130.4 crore in FY2026 from Rs. 2,728.0 crore in FY2021. To drive the next phase of growth, CPL has expanded its organisational structure, created dedicated divisions and strengthened its manpower, project-monitoring systems and digital capabilities. These measures are expected to support the simultaneous execution of multiple large projects and the ramp-up of the recently expanded order book. The scale up and enhancement in operating margin led to improved coverage metrics in FY2026, with a comfortable interest coverage of 5.0 times and 5.5 times in Q1 FY2027 (3.8 times in FY2025), while the DSCR also rose to 3.2 times in FY2026 from 2.0 times in FY2025. These are likely to improve further over the medium term, given the increasing scale of operations, stable profitability and moderation in finance costs, aided by more favourable banking terms. CPL’s plans to raise equity under the approved QIP would further strengthen its capitalisation and provide additional financial flexibility to support its growth plans. 

## **Credit challenges** 

**Relatively leveraged capital structure** – CPL’s operations are working capital intensive due to sizeable inventory (mainly workin-progress), as most projects have milestone-based billing for certain types of work. The NWC/OI increased to 4.7% in FY2026 from 0.8% in FY2025 due to increase in inventory days to 103 (March 2025: 77 days) and debtor days to 47 (March 2025: 39 days). The working capital requirements are largely supported by mobilisation advances of around Rs. 1,015 crore as on March 31, 2026 and extended credit from suppliers, leading to TOL/TNW of 1.9 times as on March 31, 2026 (2.2 times: March 2025). However, in absence of any material debt-funded capex and expected equity raising plans, the leverage is likely to steadily improve over the medium term and will remain a rating monitorable. 

– **Moderate execution risk, exposure to stiff competition and raw material price escalations** The company is exposed to execution risk, given that around 65% of the order book as on June 30, 2026 was in the nascent stages of execution (less than 10% executed), including around 34% where execution was yet to commence. However, these mainly comprise orders received in Q4 FY2026 and Q1 FY2027 that were in the mobilisation, design or approval stages. Any delay in work-front availability, land access or design approvals could adversely affect the progress of these projects. Further, there is intense competition in the domestic civil construction industry, with sizeable subcontracting and volatility in key input costs could constrain profitability, despite the expected increase in scale. The company also remains exposed to sizeable contingent liabilities in the form of BGs, mainly towards contractual performance, mobilisation advances and retention money. Nonetheless, these risks are partly mitigated by CPL’s established execution track record, absence of BG invocation in the past, price variation clauses, early procurement, back-to-back supplier arrangements and focus on specialised projects. 

# **Environmental and social risks** 

CPL operates across multiple project sites and geographies, which mitigates the risk of material business disruptions arising from location-specific physical climate events. Nevertheless, construction activities inherently carry environmental risks, including exposure to restrictions on operations in regions facing air quality concerns. The company has implemented various sustainability initiatives for adoption of renewable energy, waste management, water recycling and resource efficiency, supporting a reduction in its environmental footprint. From a social perspective, construction entities remain vulnerable to workforce health and safety risks that could result in regulatory action, reputational impact or execution disruptions. However, CPL has maintained a strong safety track record, while continuing to maintain healthy relationships with its employees and contractual workforce, with no material execution disruptions due to labour-related issues. 

# **Liquidity position: Adequate** 

CPL’s liquidity position is supported by free cash and bank balances of around Rs. 380 crore and a buffer of around Rs. 209 crore in fund-based working capital limits as on June 30, 2026. The annual capex is expected to be at around Rs. 350-400 crore, primarily towards sourcing of construction equipment and there are no material debt funding plans. The liquidity profile is likely to be supported by healthy net cash accruals of around Rs. 800-1,000 crore per annum against modest debt repayment obligations of Rs. 61.1 crore in FY2027 and Rs. 49.4 crore in FY2028.

# **Rating sensitivities** 

**Positive factors –** The ratings can be upgraded if there is a significant improvement in the company’s scale of operations and material improvement in operating margins while maintaining healthy and diversified order book along with improvement in leverage and coverage metrics as well as improvement in liquidity position on a sustained basis. 

– **Negative factors** Pressure on the ratings could arise in case of deterioration in operating profitability or elongation of working capital cycle, or if significantly high debt-funded capex impacts the company’s liquidity or the overall financial profile. A specific credit metric, which could result in a rating downgrade, is if interest coverage ratio is less than 5.0 times on a sustained basis.

# **About the company** 

Incorporated in June 1978, CPL is a public limited company involved in providing design, engineering, procurement and construction services for infrastructure projects in India. It has done a variety of work, which includes piling, foundations, ground improvement, geotechnical and specialist engineering, marine structures and ports. It has undertaken transportation projects including highways and bridges, hydroelectric projects such as tunnels and dams, industrial works and urban infrastructure projects. CPL was a part of the Thailand-based ITD Thai Group till May 2025. Subsequently, the promoter holding has been acquired by the Adani Group through its UAE-based entity – Renew Exim DMCC, making it a part of the Adani Group. Renew Exim DMCC holds 67.46% equity stake in CPL as on June 30, 2026.
