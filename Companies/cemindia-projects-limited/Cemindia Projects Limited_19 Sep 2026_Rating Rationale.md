## Rationale

The upgrade to [ICRA]AA (Stable) reflects the way membership of the Adani group has converted, over roughly fifteen months, from a change of control into a demonstrated operating advantage. Around 65% of the order inflow of about Rs. 25,000 crore that CPL booked over the fifteen months to June 2026 came from the group, and the company now functions as the group's principal EPC execution arm while continuing to win third-party work. That inflow, together with the group's own infrastructure pipeline across ports, roads, airports, data centres and water, took the **order book to Rs. 31,306.8 crore as on June 30, 2026 — 3.1 times FY2026 operating income** and up about 71% from Rs. 18,300 crore a year earlier. The association has also lowered CPL's bank charges and improved bank-guarantee terms on group orders, which strengthens financial flexibility and helps it bid for external projects; the counterparty risk on group orders is mitigated by direct contractual exposure to established Adani entities. Shareholder approval is in place to raise up to Rs. 5,000 crore through a qualified institutional placement, which would keep borrowings modest as the book executes.

The scale-up sits on a strengthening financial profile. **Consolidated operating income rose 8.4% to Rs. 10,060.58 crore in FY2026** (a five-year CAGR of about 30%), and Q1 FY2027 continued the trend with revenue up 7% year-on-year. The **OPBDIT margin improved to 10.66% from 9.32%**, aided by operating leverage, project mix and claim-related income, and **interest coverage rose to 4.99 times from 3.65 times**, with Total debt/OPBDIT at 0.93 times and TOL/TNW easing to 1.95 times from 2.19 times. Total debt of Rs. 1,001.23 crore is largely short-tenor working-capital funding against a self-liquidating contract book, with only Rs. 177.69 crore long-term. The rating also draws on an execution track record of over four decades in technically complex marine, tunnelling and underground-metro work, supported by a specialist equipment fleet and a diversified book across segments, clients and geographies.

The rating is constrained by the increasingly working-capital-intensive nature of the business. Most projects bill on milestones, so work-in-progress inventory is sizeable: **NWC/OI rose to 4.7% in FY2026 from 0.8% in FY2025**, as inventory days lengthened to 103 (from 77) and debtor days to 47 (from 39). Customer mobilisation advances — about Rs. 1,015 crore at March 2026 — together with extended credit from suppliers finance much of that cycle, and a withdrawal of either source would pressure liquidity. There is also execution risk: about 65% of the June-2026 book was in early stages (less than 10% executed) and roughly 34% had not commenced, though these are largely Q4 FY2026 and Q1 FY2027 wins still in mobilisation, design or approval. Sizeable bank-guarantee contingent liabilities, competition in civil construction and input-cost volatility temper the profile, partly offset by an established record with no past BG invocation and price-variation clauses on core inputs.

Over FY2027-FY2028 the order book supports revenue growth of about 20-25% per annum, with the margin range-bound within 10.0-10.7% and coverage improving further as scale builds and finance costs moderate. The monitorables are the working-capital cycle and contract-asset build, the pace of inflows relative to execution, the continuation of mobilisation advances and supplier credit, and the completion of the approved equity raise.

**The Stable outlook reflects ICRA's expectation that CPL will continue to benefit from its strong, well-diversified order book, established execution capability and demonstrated operating synergies with the Adani group.**

## Key rating drivers and their description

### Credit strengths

**Adani-group membership drives order inflow and financial flexibility** – Following RenewExim DMCC's acquisition of the controlling stake in May 2025, CPL has become the Adani group's key EPC execution arm while still pursuing external orders. Group inflows (about 65% of roughly Rs. 25,000 crore booked in the fifteen months to June 2026) and access to the group's infrastructure pipeline underpin growth, while more favourable bank-guarantee terms and bank charges strengthen financial flexibility and support external bidding. Direct contractual exposure to established group entities limits counterparty risk on those orders.

**Strong, well-diversified order book giving medium-term revenue visibility** – The unexecuted book stood at **Rs. 31,306.8 crore as on June 30, 2026, 3.1 times FY2026 operating income**, up about 71% from Rs. 18,300 crore at March 2025 (five-year CAGR about 21%). By segment, marine work is the largest single share at 23.2%; urban infrastructure/MRTS follows at 16.5% and data centres at 13.3%, with water/sewage (10.9%), buildings (8.8%), transportation (8.4%) and industrial structures (8.1%) making up most of the balance. Concentration is contained — the five largest projects are 32% of the book and the three largest clients 26%. Geographically it spans multiple states and overseas locations, with Maharashtra, Rajasthan and Delhi together about 46%. The client base of private entities, government bodies and PSUs mitigates concentration.

**Large scale of operations and a healthy financial profile** – Operating income scaled to **Rs. 10,060.58 crore in FY2026 from Rs. 2,728 crore in FY2021**. Coverage metrics improved with the scale-up and margin: **interest coverage of 4.99 times (5.5 times in Q1 FY2027, ICRA basis) against 3.65 times in FY2025, DSCR of 3.2 times (from 2.0 times), Total debt/OPBDIT of 0.93 times and TOL/TNW of 1.95 times**. These are expected to improve further on rising scale, stable profitability and lower finance costs; the approved QIP of up to Rs. 5,000 crore would strengthen capitalisation further.

**Established multi-decade execution record** – With an operating history of over four decades (incorporated June 1978, formerly ITD Cementation), CPL has a recognised franchise in technically complex marine, tunnelling and underground-metro work, backed by a strong technical team and a fleet of specialised equipment — a genuine competitive strength for the scale and complexity of work it targets.

### Credit challenges

**Working-capital-intensive operations and a relatively leveraged capital structure** – Milestone-based billing on most projects keeps work-in-progress inventory sizeable, and **NWC/OI rose to 4.7% in FY2026 from 0.8% in FY2025** (inventory days 103 from 77; debtor days 47 from 39). Funding for the cycle comes largely from customer advances (around Rs. 1,015 crore) and supplier credit, and TOL/TNW stood at 1.95 times (2.19 times in FY2025). Absent material debt-funded capex and with the equity raise planned, leverage should improve steadily, and remains a monitorable.

**Execution risk, competition and input-cost exposure** – About 65% of the June-2026 book was in early stages (under 10% executed) and roughly 34% had not commenced, mainly recent wins in mobilisation, design or approval; delays in work-front availability, land access or design approvals could slow progress. Intense competition in domestic civil construction, sizeable subcontracting and input-price volatility could constrain margins, and the company carries large bank-guarantee contingent liabilities. These are partly mitigated by the execution record, the absence of past BG invocation, price-variation clauses, early procurement and back-to-back supplier arrangements.

## Environmental and social risks

**Environmental considerations** – Heavy civil construction is energy- and resource-intensive, with the principal exposures being diesel and grid electricity at project sites, embodied carbon in construction materials, water use and waste; sites in areas with air-quality restrictions can face operating curbs. Operating across many sites and geographies limits location-specific climate disruption. On FY2026 disclosures, CPL deployed rooftop solar (about 39.12 tonnes CO2e annual reduction), reused 56,414 kL of water through sedimentation, RO and STP systems, recycled over 10,000 tonnes of material and reused over 3,600 tonnes, alongside renewable-energy adoption and resource-efficiency initiatives. ICRA assesses environmental risk as not currently a rating driver.

**Social considerations** – Construction is labour-intensive and site-safety-critical, with workforce health and safety, contract-labour management and community impact the principal exposures; safety lapses can bring regulatory action or execution disruption. CPL reports a strong safety record with no material labour-related disruptions, tracks Lost Time Injury Frequency Rate, and channels CSR through education, healthcare, skill development and community infrastructure. Governance sits within the Adani-group framework; the CARO clause 3(ii)(b) bank-returns observation noted below is the one open process item. ICRA assesses social and governance risk as not constraining the rating.

## Liquidity position: Adequate

As on June 30, 2026, the company held free cash and bank balances of roughly Rs. 380 crore, with a further headroom of about Rs. 209 crore undrawn in its fund-based working-capital limits, and generates net cash accruals estimated at Rs. 800-1,000 crore a year. Scheduled term-debt repayments against these are small — Rs. 61.1 crore falling due through FY2027 and Rs. 49.4 crore through FY2028. Annual capex is expected at around Rs. 350-400 crore, mainly for construction equipment, with no material debt-funding plans, and shareholder approval is in place for a QIP of up to Rs. 5,000 crore that would further limit reliance on external debt. The descriptor is **Adequate** rather than Strong because the operations are working-capital-intensive and are funded partly through mobilisation advances and extended supplier credit whose continuation matters; it is Adequate rather than Stretched because accruals cover the scheduled repayments many times over and there is no debt-maturity cliff.

## Rating sensitivities

**Positive factors** – The ratings could be upgraded on a significant improvement in scale and a material improvement in operating margins, while maintaining a healthy, diversified order book and improving leverage, coverage and the liquidity position on a sustained basis.

**Negative factors** – Pressure could arise from deterioration in operating profitability or an elongation of the working-capital cycle, or from significantly high debt-funded capex that impairs liquidity or the overall financial profile. The specific credit metric that could trigger a downgrade is **interest coverage falling below 5.0 times on a sustained basis**.

## About the company

Cemindia Projects Limited (CPL), formerly ITD Cementation India Limited, was **incorporated in June 1978** and is a public limited company providing design, engineering, procurement and construction services for infrastructure in India — piling and foundations, ground improvement, geotechnical and specialist engineering, marine structures and ports, transportation (highways and bridges), hydroelectric works such as tunnels and dams, and industrial and urban-infrastructure projects. CPL was part of the Thailand-based ITD (Italian-Thai Development) group until May 2025, when the Adani group acquired the promoter holding through its UAE-based entity RenewExim DMCC, which held **67.46% as on June 30, 2026**. For FY2026 the company reported consolidated operating income of Rs. 10,060.58 crore (up 8.4%) and profit after tax of Rs. 597.73 crore. It is listed on the NSE and the BSE (ISIN INE686A01026).

**Basis note — reconciliation to ICRA's published figures.** The table above is on the **company's own consolidated basis**, which every figure ties to its audited statements. ICRA's published action of September 03, 2026 is on a **"Consolidated plus 2 JVs" basis** (fully consolidating ITD ITD Cem JV and CEC-ITD Cem-TPL JV): on that basis ICRA reports FY2026 operating income Rs. 10,130.4 crore (FY2025 Rs. 9,342.9 crore), PAT Rs. 622.9 crore excluding JV profit share (Rs. 369.6 crore), OPBDIT/OI 10.7% (9.8%), PAT/OI 6.1% (4.0%), TOL/TNW 1.9 times (2.2 times), Total debt/OPBDIT 0.9 times (1.1 times) and interest coverage 5.0 times (3.8 times). The two bases differ by about 0.7% on operating income and do not change the leverage, coverage or rating conclusion; the residual gaps (chiefly FY2025 coverage and the JV profit share) are the two additional fully-consolidated JVs, whose standalone financials are not in the document set to rebuild.

**Computation basis.** OPBDIT is computed as PBT plus finance cost plus depreciation less other income, **retaining the core-related claim/settlement income** — the FY2026 "Interest on settlement/award" line of Rs. 50.46 crore (FY2025 Rs. 0.27 crore), which is earned in the ordinary course of executing and closing out contracts and which lifted the margin per ICRA's own narrative. Other income (note 25) was decomposed to its line items before use; bank-deposit interest, tax-refund interest, forex, PPE-disposal profit and miscellaneous income are non-operating and stripped. No forex loss appears in operating costs and no exceptional item is disclosed in any year. Total debt of Rs. 1,001.23 crore follows the company's own disclosed definition (non-current + current borrowings + supplier's credit) plus lease liabilities, and includes Rs. 96.32 crore of supplier-finance credit the company itself classifies as debt. A consolidation-basis restatement means FY2024 (old ITD basis) and FY2025-FY2026 (revised basis) are not perfectly like-for-like; FY2025 is taken from the newer annual report.
