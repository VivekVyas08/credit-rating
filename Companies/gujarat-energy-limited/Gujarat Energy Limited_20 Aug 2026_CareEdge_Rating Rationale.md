## **Rationale and key rating drivers**

The ratings assigned to Gujarat Energy Limited (GEL, formerly Gujarat Gas Limited) derive from an
exceptionally conservative capital structure, a dominant position in India's city gas distribution
(CGD) market, and ownership within the Gujarat State Petroleum Corporation (GSPC) group with the
Government of Gujarat behind it.

**The company carries no borrowings.** Non-current and current borrowings were **nil** in both
FY2024 and FY2025, with adjusted debt consisting solely of lease liabilities of ₹115.16 crore
against total equity of ₹8,453.65 crore — overall gearing of **0.014x** — and the company held net
cash of ₹244.24 crore. Interest coverage was 57.9 times. This is the dominant rating consideration;
no leverage or coverage threshold is remotely in play.

The ratings also factor in the **GSPC Group Composite Scheme of Arrangement, effective 1 May 2026**,
under which gas trading, exploration and production assets, wind power generation and investments
came into the company, the gas transmission business was demerged into GSPL Transmission Limited
(separate listing underway), and Gujarat Gas Limited was renamed Gujarat Energy Limited. The
company reported net worth of **₹18,517 crore** as on 31 March 2026 on the merged basis, more than
double the ₹8,453.65 crore standalone position a year earlier.

Operating performance is robust. On the merged basis, FY2026 EBITDA was **₹3,772 crore** against
₹3,677 crore, and profit after tax **₹2,299 crore**, on total sales volume of 13.63 mmscmd. Revenue
declined 12.7% to ₹24,198 crore — but this is a **mix effect, not a demand failure**: the low-margin
gas-trading segment contracted while the higher-margin CGD business grew, expanding EBITDA margin
232 basis points to 15.59%. CNG volume reached a record 3.60 mmscmd in Q4 FY2026, up 12% year on
year.

These strengths are tempered by exposure to natural gas price and availability volatility, a
regulated tariff environment, and the execution and structural uncertainty inherent in a
composite scheme that took effect only after the reporting year-end.

## **Rating sensitivities: Factors likely to lead to rating actions**

### **Positive factors**

- Not applicable. The long-term rating is at the highest level on CareEdge Ratings' scale.

### **Negative factors**

- Any material change in the shareholding or expected support of the GSPC group and the Government
  of Gujarat.
- A sustained departure from the debt-free capital structure — overall gearing rising above 0.50x
  or net debt to EBITDA above 1.0x on a sustained basis.
- A sustained decline in CGD volumes, or an adverse regulatory or gas-allocation change materially
  compressing CGD margins.
- Materially adverse outcomes from the composite scheme, including at the demerged transmission
  entity where residual obligations attach to GEL.

## **Analytical approach:** Standalone, with group and government linkage

The assessment is on GEL's standalone financials, factoring in linkages with the GSPC group and,
through it, the Government of Gujarat. The company is a state government company (CIN
L40200GJ2012SGC069118, "SGC") with IAS officers as Chairman and Managing Director.

_**Basis note — two bases are in play and are deliberately not mixed.** The Q4 FY2026 press release
reports the **merged entity** with the FY2025 comparative restated onto that basis. The FY2023–FY2025
annual reports report **Gujarat Gas standalone, pre-scheme**. FY2025 total income is ₹17,394.94
crore on the standalone basis and ₹27,718 crore on the merged basis — a gap of ₹10,323 crore, being
the incoming trading, E&P and wind businesses. Splicing the two would manufacture roughly 59% of
revenue growth that did not occur. Merged-basis figures are used for scale and profitability;
standalone figures for capital structure. **The absence of merged-basis capital structure is
evidenced:** searched the Q4 FY2026 press release on 20-Aug-2026 for `borrowings`, `debt`,
`gearing` and `lease` — zero hits. The release discloses revenue, EBITDA, PAT, net worth, EPS and
dividend only._

## **Outlook:** Stable

The Stable outlook reflects the expectation that GEL will maintain its debt-free capital structure
and leading CGD position, that the composite scheme will be absorbed without disruption, and that
GSPC group and Government of Gujarat support will continue.

## **Detailed description of key rating drivers:**

### **Key strengths**

**Debt-free capital structure with a substantial net cash position**

This is the defining feature of the credit. Borrowings — non-current and current — were **nil** as
on 31 March 2025 and 31 March 2024. Adjusted debt comprises lease liabilities alone: ₹115.16 crore
against ₹117.77 crore.

Net worth grew to ₹8,453.65 crore from ₹7,689.26 crore entirely through internal accrual — share
capital was unchanged at ₹137.68 crore. On the merged basis net worth stood at ₹18,517 crore as on
31 March 2026.

**Leading position in India's city gas distribution market**

GEL is the largest CGD company in India. FY2026 CGD volume was **8.69 mmscmd**, with total volume
including gas trading of 13.63 mmscmd:

| Sales volume (mmscmd) | Q4 FY2026 | FY2025-26 |
|---|---|---|
| **CGD segment (A)** | **8.88** | **8.69** |
| — Industrial | 4.19 | 4.29 |
| — CNG | 3.60 | 3.43 |
| — PNG domestic | 0.91 | 0.82 |
| — PNG commercial | 0.17 | 0.16 |
| **Gas trading segment (B)** | 4.63 | 4.94 |
| **Total (A+B)** | **13.51** | **13.63** |

The distribution network and customer base give the position durability: **839 CNG stations** (14
added in FY2026, seven under the FDODO scheme) and PNG supply to **24.18 lakh households**, with
35,400 domestic customers added in Q4 FY2026 alone.

**Margin expansion despite lower revenue — a favourable mix shift**

Revenue on the merged basis fell 12.7% to ₹24,198 crore from ₹27,718 crore, while EBITDA rose 2.6%
to ₹3,772 crore. EBITDA margin therefore expanded **232 basis points to 15.59%** from 13.27%.

The explanation is segment mix, not pricing. Gas trading is a high-revenue, low-margin
pass-through activity; its contraction mechanically lifts blended margin while barely affecting
profit. Reading the revenue decline as deterioration would invert the actual picture. CNG — among
the higher-margin CGD categories — grew 12% year on year to a record 3.60 mmscmd in Q4.

**Demonstrated volume growth in industrial clusters and LPG substitution**

The Morbi ceramic cluster illustrates the demand pull: gas-consuming units rose from 83 to 675 and
consumption from 0.36 to 7.8 MMSCMD between March and May 2026. In the domestic segment, 2,835
housing societies covering about 4.86 lakh households had been converted to piped natural gas and
made LPG-free as of May 2026 — a substitution trend that converts a one-off connection into an
annuity volume.

**State ownership and GSPC group linkage**

GEL is a Gujarat state government company. The Chairman and Managing Director are serving IAS
officers, and the board carries senior state government representation. The company banks with a
wide panel including State Bank of India, Bank of Baroda, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank and
Punjab National Bank.

### **Key weaknesses**

**Exposure to natural gas price and availability volatility**

Gas is the principal input and its cost is largely a pass-through, but with a lag. Sourcing spans
domestic allocation, long-term contracts and spot LNG, so periods of elevated international LNG
prices compress margins in the industrial segment where customers can switch to alternative fuels.
The company's response to the Middle East crisis — accelerating PNG conversion to displace LPG — is
a demand-side mitigant rather than a hedge against input cost.

**Regulated tariff environment**

CGD operations are regulated by the Petroleum and Natural Gas Regulatory Board, covering network
tariffs, marketing exclusivity periods and infrastructure obligations. Regulatory change in
allocation priority or tariff determination can affect margins independently of operating
performance.

**Composite scheme effective after the reporting year-end**

The GSPC Group Composite Scheme took effect on 1 May 2026 — after the 31 March 2026 balance sheet
date. Consequently the merged-entity capital structure, the residual obligations attaching to GEL
following the transmission demerger, and the debt profile of the incoming trading, E&P and wind
businesses are **not evidenced in the documents reviewed**. Searched for `borrowings` in the Q4
FY2026 press release: no debt disclosure. The debt-free position documented above is a
**standalone, pre-scheme** fact; whether it survives the scheme is a matter for issuer confirmation
and is the principal open question in this assessment.

## **Liquidity: Strong**

Liquidity is strong. The company had **no borrowings** and therefore no scheduled repayment
obligation beyond lease liabilities of ₹115.16 crore. Cash and bank balances stood at ₹359.40 crore
as on 31 March 2025, together with other current financial assets of ₹1,298.64 crore. Current
assets of ₹2,965.19 crore covered current liabilities comfortably.

Free cash generation is substantial: profit after tax of ₹1,145.51 crore plus depreciation of
₹510.64 crore gives gross cash accrual of approximately ₹1,656 crore against no debt service
obligation. The board recommended a final dividend of ₹8.90 per ₹2 share for FY2026 — 445% of face
value and the highest recommended to date — which is affordable on that accrual.

_A dated bank-facility utilisation statement was not among the documents reviewed; the assessment
rests on the balance-sheet and accrual evidence cited._

## **Environment, social, and governance (ESG) risks**

**Environmental:** The core business is a transition positive — piped natural gas and CNG displace
higher-emitting LPG, coal and liquid fuels in domestic, commercial, industrial and transport use,
and the LPG-substitution and Morbi conversion programmes are direct emission-reduction activity.
The company discloses quantified intensity metrics in its BRSR: Scope 1 and 2 emissions of 7.10
tCO2e per ₹ crore of turnover (6.62 in the prior year), energy intensity of 38.05 GJ per ₹ crore
(36.10), water intensity of 4.79 KL per ₹ crore (5.52) and waste intensity of 0.035 MT per ₹ crore
(0.025). Emissions, energy and waste intensity all rose year on year while water intensity
improved — a mixed record that warrants monitoring. Against this, natural gas remains a fossil fuel
and carries long-horizon transition risk as electrification advances in transport and cooking.

**Social:** Safety is the material exposure — gas distribution involves pipeline integrity, leak
management and public safety across a dense urban network. The social contribution is substantial
and measurable: connecting 4.86 lakh households to piped gas removes cylinder handling and improves
indoor air quality.

**Governance:** A listed state government company under SEBI LODR, with IAS officers as Chairman
and Managing Director and independent directors on the board. State ownership brings alignment with
public policy and support expectations, and the corresponding consideration that commercial and
policy objectives can diverge.

## **About the company and industry**

Gujarat Energy Limited (CIN L40200GJ2012SGC069118), incorporated in 2012 and listed on BSE and NSE,
is India's largest city gas distribution company. It was known as **Gujarat Gas Limited** until the
GSPC Group Composite Scheme of Arrangement took effect on 1 May 2026, under which it became an
integrated energy company: gas trading, exploration and production assets, wind power generation
and investments were brought in, while the gas transmission business was demerged into GSPL
Transmission Limited, whose separate listing is underway.

The company distributes natural gas to industrial, commercial, domestic and transport customers
across Gujarat and other states, operating 839 CNG stations and supplying more than 24.18 lakh
households.

India's CGD sector is regulated by the Petroleum and Natural Gas Regulatory Board, which awards
geographical areas with marketing and network exclusivity for defined periods. Demand is driven by
industrial fuel substitution, CNG penetration in transport and household piped-gas conversion, with
government policy supporting a higher share of natural gas in the primary energy mix.
