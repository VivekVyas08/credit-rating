## Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has upgraded its ratings on the bank facilities of Yasho Industries Limited (Yasho) to 'Crisil A-/Stable/Crisil A2+' from 'Crisil BBB+/Positive/Crisil A2'.

The upgrade reflects the material strengthening of Yasho's financial risk profile through fiscal 2026 and the pronounced step-up in operating performance in the first quarter of fiscal 2027. Adjusted gearing improved to 1.26 times as on March 31, 2026, from 1.97 times two years earlier, while adjusted debt to Ebitda moderated to 3.86 times from 5.43 times over the same period. Critically, fiscal 2026 marked the first year since the Pakhajan greenfield expansion in which the company generated positive free cash flow — cash accrual from operations of Rs 151 crore against capital expenditure (capex) of Rs 74 crore — permitting an absolute reduction in adjusted debt of Rs 25 crore.

Operating performance inflected sharply in the first quarter of fiscal 2027. Revenue of Rs 307.74 crore represents an annualised run rate of about Rs 1,231 crore against Rs 830 crore in fiscal 2026, supported by 42% year-on-year volume growth, while the Ebitda margin expanded to 24.2% from 17.4% for fiscal 2026 on improved product mix and operating leverage. Capacity utilisation at Pakhajan rose above 65% from about 60% in fiscal 2026, and the working capital cycle compressed to 143 days from 190 days. Company-reported debt to Ebitda stood at 1.86 times.

The ratings continue to reflect Yasho's established position in speciality chemicals with a diversified product portfolio of over 140 products across five verticals, longstanding customer relationships in regulated export markets, and the operating flexibility afforded by the substantially underutilised Pakhajan facility. These strengths are partially offset by a financial risk profile that remains leveraged in absolute terms, working capital intensity that is high and — on the receivables line — deteriorating, exposure to crude-linked raw material price volatility and export-market tariff actions, and the absence of disclosed customer-concentration metrics.

For arriving at the ratings, Crisil Ratings has consolidated the business and financial risk profiles of Yasho and its two wholly owned subsidiaries — Yasho Industries Europe B.V. (Netherlands) and Yasho Inc. (USA). Both operate in the same business as the parent, function as sales and distribution arms in Yasho's principal export markets, and are neither ring-fenced nor separately funded; full analytical consolidation therefore applies. Please refer to Annexure — List of entities consolidated.

Unsecured loans of Rs 47.00 crore extended by the directors and other key managerial personnel have been treated as debt rather than as equity. The supplied documents evidence neither formal subordination to external lenders nor a contractual undertaking of non-withdrawal, and interest of Rs 4.53 crore was serviced on these balances in fiscal 2026. Should subordination be formally documented, a part-equity treatment could be considered at the next review.

## Key Rating Drivers — Strengths

**Established position in speciality chemicals with a diversified portfolio.** Yasho manufactures over 140 products across five verticals — food antioxidants, aroma chemicals, rubber accelerators, lubricant additives and speciality chemicals — serving pharmaceuticals, food, fragrance, tyre and industrial machinery end-markets. Crisil Ratings' criteria for the chemical industry regards speciality chemicals more favourably than bulk, given proprietary process technology, the customer-qualification barrier that makes buying patterns sticky, and the low share of the customer's own cost base that reduces price sensitivity. Yasho's three-decade operating history, in-house research and development centre staffed by more than 30 chemists, and global quality accreditations support this positioning. Exports contributed about 62% of revenue in fiscal 2026 across the USA, Europe, Asia and the Middle East; the company entered Asian and African geographies during the first quarter of fiscal 2027.

**Improving financial risk profile with demonstrated deleveraging.** Adjusted gearing improved to 1.26 times as on March 31, 2026 (1.39 times and 1.97 times in the two preceding fiscals) and total outside liabilities to tangible networth to 1.63 times. Networth strengthened to Rs 441 crore, aided by a Rs 125 crore preferential equity allotment in fiscal 2025. Adjusted debt to Ebitda improved to 3.86 times in fiscal 2026 from 5.43 times in fiscal 2024, and the company prepaid Rs 23.30 crore of term debt, leaving only Rs 15.60 crore of scheduled repayment due in fiscal 2027. Cash accrual from operations of Rs 151 crore in fiscal 2026 more than tripled from Rs 45 crore in the prior fiscal.

**Substantial headroom for growth without proportionate capital outlay.** The Pakhajan facility operated at about 60% utilisation in fiscal 2026 and above 65% in the first quarter of fiscal 2027, against combined peak revenue capacity across Pakhajan and Vapi of Rs 1,450–1,550 crore — nearly double fiscal 2026 revenue. Only about 25% of the 42-acre Pakhajan parcel is built out. Management guides to utilisation above 75% in fiscal 2027 and to revenue exceeding Rs 1,600 crore by fiscal 2028. Incremental revenue at higher utilisation should carry materially better contribution margins, as evidenced by the first quarter of fiscal 2027.

**Customer-funded expansion limits incremental balance-sheet risk.** A 15-year long-term supply agreement for lubricant additives with a global multinational carries a project cost of Rs 85–90 crore funded entirely by the customer, of which Rs 98.12 crore of advances had been received by the first quarter of fiscal 2027 against Rs 70.89 crore paid to vendors. Commercial supply is expected from the fourth quarter of fiscal 2027, with an indicated annual revenue contribution of about Rs 150 crore. The structure secures both offtake and funding.

## Key Rating Drivers — Weaknesses

**Financial risk profile remains leveraged, with modest coverage.** Despite the improvement, adjusted debt of Rs 557.93 crore against Ebitda of Rs 144.46 crore for fiscal 2026 leaves leverage elevated for the rating category. Interest coverage was 2.60 times on a Crisil-adjusted basis (Ebitda to finance costs) and 1.61 times on an Ebit basis for fiscal 2026 — thin, and reflecting the full-year impact of interest that was previously capitalised during the Pakhajan build. Return on capital employed of 9.0% remains below the pre-expansion level. The first-quarter fiscal 2027 improvement, while marked, represents a single quarter and is not yet established through a full cycle.

**High and, on receivables, worsening working capital intensity.** Net working capital stood at about 149 days of revenue in fiscal 2026. Receivable days lengthened to 89 days from 76 days in fiscal 2024, a deterioration sustained across three fiscals, even as inventory days improved to 103 days from 133 days. The company reports a reduction in the overall cycle to 143 days in the first quarter of fiscal 2027; sustaining this will be a key monitorable, as working capital absorbed a substantial part of accrual during the growth phase. Exports carry inherently longer collection cycles and limited ability to stretch payables on imported inputs.

**Exposure to raw material volatility and export-market disruption.** The majority of inputs are crude oil derivatives, with aroma chemicals additionally exposed to clove oil prices. Management explicitly attributed below-optimal utilisation in the second and third quarters of fiscal 2026 to US tariff measures and trade restrictions — a demonstrated, not theoretical, vulnerability. With about 62% of revenue exported, tariff and trade-policy actions in destination markets remain a live risk, partially mitigated by a natural currency hedge.

**Undisclosed customer concentration and an unexplained segment shift.** No customer-concentration metric is disclosed in any supplied document. The reliance on a small number of large multinational counterparties implied by the long-term supply agreement, together with management's attribution of first-quarter fiscal 2027 growth to "additional off-take by existing customers" and "acquisition of marquee international customers," suggests concentration is material but unquantified. Separately, the consumer division has compressed to 11% of revenue in fiscal 2026 from 17% in fiscal 2025 and 16% in fiscal 2024, a shift not explained in management commentary.

## Liquidity: Adequate

Cash accrual from operations of Rs 151 crore in fiscal 2026 comfortably covers scheduled term debt repayment of Rs 15.60 crore falling due in fiscal 2027, following a prepayment of Rs 23.30 crore. Fund-based and non-fund-based limits sanctioned from Saraswat Co-operative Bank, HDFC Bank, Axis Bank, SVC Co-operative Bank and HSBC stood at Rs 365.50 crore as on March 31, 2026, up from Rs 355.00 crore a year earlier, providing headroom against working capital requirements.

Liquidity is assessed as adequate rather than strong for three reasons. Utilisation of working capital limits is high, consistent with a 149-day cycle. Capex guidance for fiscal 2027 was revised upward to Rs 250 crore from Rs 125 crore, of which management indicates about Rs 100 crore will be debt-funded, which will consume headroom. And the customer advances of Rs 98.12 crore under the long-term supply agreement, while a genuine funding source, are a contract liability rather than permanent capital, and Rs 70.89 crore has already been disbursed to vendors.

---

## Outlook: Stable

Crisil Ratings believes Yasho will sustain its improved operating performance over the medium term, supported by rising utilisation at Pakhajan, a richer product mix and contracted volumes under the long-term supply agreement. The financial risk profile should strengthen further as accrual improves, though the pace will be tempered by the enlarged fiscal 2027 capex programme and the associated incremental borrowing.

## Rating Sensitivity Factors

**Upward factors**
* Sustained revenue growth with Ebitda margin maintained above 20%, translating into annual cash accrual exceeding Rs 200 crore
* Adjusted debt to Ebitda sustained below 2.5 times and interest coverage above 4.0 times
* Net working capital cycle sustained at or below 140 days

**Downward factors**
* Ebitda margin declining below 15% or a material loss of export volumes to tariff or trade actions
* Adjusted debt to Ebitda rising above 4.0 times, or debt-funded capex exceeding the guided Rs 100 crore
* Stretch in the working capital cycle beyond 170 days, or any delay or cancellation in the long-term supply agreement

## About the Company

Incorporated in 1985 and listed on the BSE and NSE (CIN L74110MH1985PLC037900), Yasho Industries Limited manufactures speciality and performance chemicals across five verticals — food antioxidants, aroma chemicals, rubber accelerators, lubricant additives and speciality chemicals. Operations are conducted from two facilities in Gujarat: Vapi, the older multi-purpose plant located near key ports, and Pakhajan, a 42-acre greenfield site commissioned through fiscal 2024 which also houses the company's research and development centre, completed in October 2025. The company is promoted by the Jhaveri family, which held 67.91% as on March 31, 2026. Mr Parag Jhaveri serves as Chairman and Managing Director. Two wholly owned subsidiaries support export sales in Europe and the USA. Employee strength is about 830.

## Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) Profile

Crisil Ratings believes Yasho's ESG profile supports its credit risk profile, though environmental disclosure for the most recent fiscal is incomplete.

The chemicals sector carries inherent exposure to emissions, effluent discharge, hazardous waste handling and process safety, with the added consideration that Yasho's principal markets — the USA and Europe — apply stringent environmental compliance standards to imported chemicals.

**Environmental.** Renewable energy consumption rose fourfold to 2,147.76 GJ in fiscal 2026 from 534.00 GJ, while non-renewable consumption declined to 266,201.33 GJ from 285,730.49 GJ. Installed solar capacity stands at 500 KVA, with a further 500 kW planned at Pakhajan. Water withdrawal rose to 103,789 KL from 91,101 KL, with 11.6% recycled; 3,517 KL was discharged to a common effluent treatment plant in fiscal 2026 against nil in the prior fiscal. Waste generation increased sharply to 877.09 MT from 2.62 MT. **Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions and emission intensity are reported as "Under Process" in the company's fiscal 2026 Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report and are therefore unavailable for the latest fiscal**; the fiscal 2025 figures were 16,270 tCO2e (Scope 1), 15,801 tCO2e (Scope 2) and 1.78 tCO2e/MT (intensity). The absence of current-year emissions data is a disclosure weakness relative to peers.

**Social.** The lost time injury frequency rate was nil for both employees and workers in fiscal 2026, with no fatalities — a strong safety record for a chemicals manufacturer. Women constitute one of seven board members (14%) and one of two key managerial personnel (50%). Employee strength is stable at about 830. Corporate social responsibility spending was Rs 65.32 lakh against an obligation of Rs 228.27 lakh, with Rs 115.20 lakh transferred to the statutory unspent account; a comparable shortfall arose in fiscal 2025, making under-deployment a recurring pattern rather than a one-off timing difference.

**Governance.** Audit opinions were unmodified in each of the three fiscals reviewed, with no Emphasis of Matter and no reporting under Section 143(12). The board is majority independent, and the company has not been declared a wilful defaulter. Governance considerations that temper the assessment are the recurring director and key managerial personnel unsecured loans of Rs 47.00 crore carrying interest of Rs 4.53 crore in fiscal 2026, the promoter-family concentration of management alongside a 67.91% shareholding, and the auditor transition from V J Shah & Co to Gokhale & Sathe in fiscal 2025, which the disclosures indicate was routine rotation.
