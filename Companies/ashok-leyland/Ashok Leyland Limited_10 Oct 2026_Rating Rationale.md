## Rationale

The ratings of Ashok Leyland Limited (AL) rest on three things. First, its entrenched position in
India's commercial vehicle (CV) market: AL is the country's second-largest maker of medium and
heavy commercial vehicles (M&HCV), with a 30.3% domestic share in FY2026, and the leader in M&HCV
buses with 34.1%. Second, debt metrics that sit in the strongest band of ICRA's indicative
ranges: standalone Total debt/OPBDIT of 0.2 times, interest coverage of 33.8 times and a net cash
position of Rs. 5,755.8 crore as on March 31, 2026. Third, a superior liquidity profile. Two
factors keep the ratings from rising further. M&HCV demand is highly cyclical: AL's volumes
halved between FY2019 and FY2021 and it reported a loss in FY2021. And the wider group makes
recurring calls on AL's cash — equity for loss-making electric-vehicle (EV) subsidiaries, the
capital needs of a fast-growing captive non-banking financial company (NBFC), and short-term
inter-corporate deposits (ICDs) to promoter-group companies.

FY2026 was a record year. Vehicle volumes rose 13.0% to 2,20,437 units, 11.7% above the FY2019
peak. Standalone operating income grew 13.6% to Rs. 44,007.0 crore, and the OPBDIT margin
widened by 31 bps to 13.0% (8.1% in FY2023) on a richer mix, cost control and operating
leverage. Reported PAT of Rs. 3,565.5 crore is after exceptional charges of Rs. 348.5 crore
(Rs. 308.5 crore for the new Labour Codes and a Rs. 40.0 crore litigation provision). OPBDIT
excludes all other income (Rs. 315.2 crore, mostly treasury income) and these exceptional items;
foreign-exchange differences carry no separate line in the profit-and-loss notes (the cash-flow
statement shows a net non-cash gain of Rs. 4.1 crore), so no forex adjustment is made. Cash flow
from operations of Rs. 4,792.1 crore covered capital expenditure of Rs. 1,049.5 crore, dividends
of Rs. 1,835.4 crore and Rs. 386.3 crore of equity into subsidiaries, and standalone debt fell
to Rs. 1,195.0 crore. On ICRA's analytical basis — consolidated, excluding the financial-services
subsidiaries — leverage is higher but still comfortable: Total debt/OPBDIT was about 1.0 times
and interest coverage about 12 times in FY2026 on a conservative estimate of the business's
OPBDIT. The non-financial subsidiaries' borrowings rose to Rs. 3,538.6 crore from Rs. 1,607.5
crore in FY2024.

The risks are measurable against large buffers. AL's domestic truck share has slipped to 30.0%
from 32.3% in FY2023. In a downturn that cuts volumes by a quarter and the OPBDIT margin to 6%,
ICRA's stress case keeps interest coverage above 14 times and free liquidity above Rs. 4,800
crore without new borrowing. A repeat of the FY2019-FY2021 collapse, with the margin at 3%,
still leaves free liquidity of about Rs. 2,780 crore at the trough. On the group side, AL
invested Rs. 2,670.4 crore of equity in subsidiaries over FY2024-FY2026 (Rs. 2,069.3 crore of it
in Optare, the parent of the Switch EV business). It has also guaranteed Rs. 785.7 crore of
subsidiary loans. ICDs to promoter-group companies peaked at Rs. 670 crore during FY2026, though
they were nil at each year-end. All of these sit against free cash and liquid investments of
Rs. 6,950.8 crore. The captive NBFC, Hinduja Leyland Finance (HLF, 61.12%), grew its loan book
by 29.7% to Rs. 49,156.7 crore in FY2026 while its credit costs nearly doubled to Rs. 1,234.2
crore. Its capital needs are a monitorable; HLF's proposed merger into the listed NDL Ventures
Limited would give it independent access to equity.

ICRA expects the CV industry to see moderate, consolidation-led growth in FY2027. It expects AL's
volumes to grow around 5% with an OPBDIT margin of 12.5-13.5%, and AL to remain net-cash at the
standalone level after the Rs. 1,468.5 crore dividend declared in May 2026, capital expenditure
of about Rs. 1,200 crore and subsidiary support of about Rs. 500 crore. The monitorables are AL's
M&HCV market share, the progress of the EV subsidiaries towards self-sufficiency, HLF's capital
requirements, the group's use of AL's cash, and the state rules under the Labour Codes.

The Stable outlook on the long-term rating reflects ICRA's opinion that AL's net-cash balance
sheet and superior liquidity give it ample room to absorb a cyclical downturn, and that the
constraints from the group are unlikely to change materially over the outlook horizon.

## Key rating drivers and their description

### Credit strengths

**Second-largest M&HCV maker and bus-segment leader, with a widening base beyond trucks** – AL
sold 1,28,033 M&HCVs in the domestic market in FY2026 (+11.5%), a 30.3% share, and is the
leader in M&HCV buses with a 34.1% share. Its LCV volumes reached a record 74,322 units (+14.3%),
with a 12.7% VAHAN share of the 0-7.5 tonne cargo segment. Exports rose to 18,082 units, and
management targets 25,000 in the near term. Outside trucks, spare parts and other products
earned Rs. 4,450.4 crore and engines and gensets Rs. 1,414.3 crore in FY2026, up from Rs. 2,577.9
crore and Rs. 633.8 crore in FY2023; these businesses are less cyclical than vehicle sales. The
network has 2,104 sales and service touchpoints.

**Debt metrics in the strongest band, with a net-cash standalone balance sheet** – Standalone
Total debt/OPBDIT improved to 0.2 times in FY2026 from 1.1 times in FY2023. Interest coverage
rose to 33.8 times from 10.1 times, DSCR to 8.1 times and RoCE to 36.0%, all within the
strongest band of ICRA's indicative metrics. Debt of Rs. 1,195.0 crore compares with free cash
and liquid investments of Rs. 6,950.8 crore. Total outside liabilities/tangible net worth of 1.2
times is driven by trade payables of Rs. 8,329.3 crore, not borrowings. Net worth rose to
Rs. 13,113.4 crore even after dividends of Rs. 3,876.2 crore paid over FY2025-FY2026.

**Superior liquidity and strong financial flexibility** – Free cash and liquid investments of
Rs. 6,950.8 crore and undrawn bank lines of Rs. 2,854.3 crore compare with scheduled debt
repayments of Rs. 483.8 crore in FY2027, Rs. 100 crore of which is already set aside in a
Debenture Redemption Fund deposit. AL is listed, with a market capitalisation of about Rs. 88,000
crore in early October 2026, and has a Rs. 2,000 crore commercial paper programme that was
undrawn at March 31, 2026.

### Credit challenges

**Exposure to the cyclicality of M&HCV demand and to competition** – M&HCVs were 58% of AL's
FY2026 vehicle volumes, and the segment is the most cyclical part of the CV industry. AL's own
ten-year record shows volumes falling from 1,97,366 units in FY2019 to 1,00,725 in FY2021,
revenue falling from Rs. 29,055 crore to Rs. 15,301 crore, and a loss of Rs. 314 crore in FY2021.
The current upcycle is in its fifth year. AL's domestic M&HCV share has edged down to 30.3% from
31.8% in FY2023, and its truck share to 30.0% from 32.3%, in a market led by a larger competitor.

**Recurring support to loss-making subsidiaries and rising non-financial subsidiary debt** – AL
put Rs. 1,567.0 crore, Rs. 717.1 crore and Rs. 386.3 crore of equity into subsidiaries in
FY2024, FY2025 and FY2026, chiefly into Optare (the EV business; Rs. 2,069.3 crore in total)
and OHM Global Mobility (Rs. 300 crore). The Optare group lost Rs. 148.3 crore in FY2026 and its
net assets of Rs. 130.8 crore compare with AL's investment of Rs. 3,000.2 crore; the auditors
flagged the Rs. 3,129.8 crore carrying value as a key audit matter. Switch Mobility's UK unit has
stopped manufacturing. Hinduja Tech lost Rs. 199.6 crore in FY2026. Borrowings of the
non-financial subsidiaries more than doubled to Rs. 3,538.6 crore over FY2024-FY2026, and AL has
guaranteed Rs. 785.7 crore of subsidiary loans (Switch Mobility UK Rs. 633.1 crore; Albonair
GmbH Rs. 152.6 crore).

**Cash outflows to promoter-group entities and the captive NBFC's capital needs** – AL extended
ICDs to seven Hinduja promoter-group companies, with up to Rs. 670 crore outstanding at a time
in FY2026 (Rs. 490 crore in FY2025); all were repaid by each year-end. ICRA treats financial
support to group entities as a governance consideration. HLF's loan book grew 29.7% to
Rs. 49,156.7 crore, its expected-credit-loss provision rose to Rs. 1,279.9 crore from
Rs. 888.2 crore, and AL subscribed Rs. 200 crore to HLF's equity in FY2025. Across the group's
financing book (Rs. 60,525.1 crore of exposure, including housing finance), Stage 3 loans held at
3.5% in both FY2025 and FY2026, with 45.3% provision cover. That stability was bought with
write-offs: of the Rs. 1,234.2 crore FY2026 impairment charge, only Rs. 415.5 crore was an
increase in the ECL allowance. The remaining Rs. 818.7 crore reflects loans charged off directly,
as derived by ICRA. The mix is also shifting: loans against property rose 54% to Rs. 14,735.0
crore, faster than vehicle loans (+20.8% to Rs. 34,286.6 crore). Further capital
calls are possible until the NDL Ventures merger — cleared by the RBI, the Competition
Commission and the stock exchanges — is sanctioned by the National Company Law Tribunal.

**Regulatory and contingent exposures** – The new Labour Codes cost AL Rs. 308.5 crore in FY2026,
and state-level rules are still pending. The obligation under the end-of-life vehicle
extended-producer-responsibility rules could not be estimated at March 31, 2026. Contingent
liabilities (indirect-tax and other claims) were Rs. 342.9 crore. Each is manageable against
liquidity, but together they are a source of earnings volatility.

## Environmental and social risks

**Environmental considerations** – CV makers face tightening emission norms and the shift to
electric, hydrogen and gas powertrains, which will require continued investment. AL is building
battery-electric, hydrogen-ICE, fuel-cell, LNG and CNG products and spent Rs. 1,050 crore on
capital expenditure in FY2026, partly on alternative fuels. In its own operations, renewable
energy met 77% of consumption in FY2026 (69% in FY2025), with 85% targeted for FY2027. The
company is certified Water Positive with 68% water recycling, and its BRSR Core is assured by
DNV. ICRA assesses the environmental risk as moderate.

**Social considerations** – AL employed 9,891 people at March 31, 2026. Labour regulation has
already cost it Rs. 308.5 crore through the Labour Codes charge. Product quality and safety
matter for a CV maker: AL provides for expected warranty claims at the point of sale, and the
non-cash unwinding of discount on its provisions added Rs. 95.7 crore to finance costs in FY2026. ICRA assesses the social risk as moderate.

## Liquidity position: Superior

As on March 31, 2026, AL held free cash and liquid investments of Rs. 6,950.8 crore. This
excludes unclaimed-dividend and escrow balances of Rs. 46.0 crore and a Rs. 100 crore deposit
earmarked for the Debenture Redemption Fund. It also had undrawn bank lines of Rs. 2,854.3 crore.
ICRA expects FY2027 cash flow from operations of about Rs. 5,000 crore. Against these sources,
FY2027 calls on cash total about Rs. 4,240 crore:
- scheduled debt repayment of Rs. 483.8 crore, including the Rs. 200 crore NCD due on March 17,
  2027, partly pre-funded by the Rs. 100 crore deposit;
- capital expenditure of about Rs. 1,200 crore;
- dividends of about Rs. 2,056 crore, including the Rs. 1,468.5 crore declared in May 2026;
- equity support to subsidiaries of about Rs. 500 crore.

Operating cash flow alone covers these calls. Free cash and the earmarked deposit cover FY2027
debt servicing (repayments plus interest of Rs. 157 crore) about 11 times. AL would not need new
debt even in ICRA's stress case or in a repeat of the FY2019-FY2021 downturn.

## Rating sensitivities

**Positive factors** – A sustained gain in M&HCV market share and scale, with a materially larger
share of revenue from exports and non-CV businesses that reduces cyclicality, together with an
end to ICDs to promoter-group companies and self-funding subsidiaries, could lead to an upgrade.

**Negative factors** – Pressure on the ratings could arise from a sustained loss of M&HCV market
share, or an OPBDIT margin below 9% on a sustained basis. It could also arise from Total
debt/OPBDIT on ICRA's analytical basis (consolidated excluding the NBFC) rising above 1.5 times
on a sustained basis. Support to subsidiaries, HLF or promoter-group entities large enough to
weaken liquidity materially, or any deterioration in governance practices, would also be
negatives.

## About the company

Ashok Leyland Limited, incorporated in 1948 and headquartered in Chennai, is the flagship company
of the Hinduja Group. Hinduja Automotive Limited, UK held 50.85% of its share capital as on March
31, 2026. AL makes M&HCV trucks and buses, light commercial vehicles, defence vehicles, and engines
and gensets, and sells spare parts and services. In FY2026 commercial vehicles were 82% of its
gross revenue; 7.0% of revenue came from outside India. Its subsidiaries include Hinduja Leyland
Finance (61.12%, a vehicle-finance NBFC), Optare Plc (93.28%, the parent of the Switch Mobility EV
businesses), Hinduja Tech (72.93%, engineering services) and OHM Global Mobility (100%, EV mobility
services).
