## Rationale

The [ICRA]A1+ rating on the commercial-paper programme rests on a credit profile that is strong on every axis a short-term rating turns on. Solar Industries India Limited (SIIL) is the market leader in industrial (commercial) explosives and initiating systems in India, a major supplier to Coal India and its subsidiaries and to Singareni Collieries, and a large exporter across a presence in 40-plus countries — a base business with deep backward integration (emulsifiers, detonator shells, PETN, TNT, RDX) that has lifted the operating margin over successive years. On top of that base sits a defence franchise that is scaling fast: consolidated defence revenue nearly doubled in FY2026 to Rs. 2,634 crore (up 94%), and the group order book stood at around Rs. 21,300 crore, providing multi-year revenue visibility. The financial profile is robust and improving — consolidated revenue grew 30.5% to Rs. 9,837.74 crore in FY2026, the ICRA-convention operating margin rose to 26.65% (from 26.07% and 22.56%), interest coverage strengthened to 19.57 times, Total debt/OPBDIT was 0.58 times and gearing 0.24 times, and the base-case forward view shows the sizeable capex programme funded entirely from internal accruals with no incremental borrowing.

FY2026 combined scale-up with continued de-risking. Revenue rose Rs. 2,297 crore to Rs. 9,837.74 crore, profit after tax grew 34.8% to Rs. 1,736.64 crore, and net worth attributable to owners climbed to Rs. 6,277.20 crore from Rs. 4,413.22 crore. Leverage stayed low despite a step-up in capex to around Rs. 1,556 crore for explosives capacity and defence facilities: total debt of Rs. 1,524.02 crore (of which Rs. 1,219.06 crore long-term, the balance short-tenor working-capital funding) carries a TOL/TNW of just 0.70 times, and cash and bank balances stood at Rs. 584.14 crore (no current investments at year-end). The rating also draws on a diversified end-market mix — mining, infrastructure, exports and defence, domestic and international — that cushions the demand cyclicality inherent to any one of them.

What could pressure the credit is concentrated in execution and input costs rather than in the balance sheet. The multi-year capex and defence build-out consumes a large share of free cash flow and expands working capital as the defence order book executes; the base explosives business remains exposed to demand cyclicality in mining and infrastructure and to ammonium-nitrate price volatility; and the defence order book, though large, is at an early ramp where delivery timelines and indigenisation milestones carry execution risk. None of these is a near-term liquidity concern — interest cover of 19.6 times, gearing of 0.24 times and an internally-funded capex plan leave a wide buffer — which is exactly why the short-term rating sits at the highest grade.

The company's healthy revenue growth (up 30.5% in FY2026 and more than 60% over FY2024-FY2026), its improving margin mix as higher-value defence and export orders build, and its conservative capital structure are expected to sustain the credit profile. The monitorables are the pace of defence order-book execution and its working-capital intensity, the trajectory of ammonium-nitrate and other input costs, the scale and funding of the capex programme, and any large debt-funded acquisition.

## Key rating drivers and their description

### Credit strengths

**Market leadership in commercial explosives with deep backward integration** – SIIL is the largest manufacturer of industrial explosives and initiating systems in India, supplying Coal India and its subsidiaries, Singareni Collieries and infrastructure and mining customers, and exporting across 40-plus countries from 90-plus manufacturing facilities. Backward integration into emulsifiers, detonator shells and energetic materials (PETN, TNT, RDX) supports cost control and product breadth, and has underpinned the rise in operating margin to the mid-20s percent.

**Fast-growing defence franchise with a large order book** – Consolidated defence revenue nearly doubled to Rs. 2,634 crore in FY2026 (up 94%), and Solar Defence and Aerospace Limited became the first private company to dispatch the Pinaka Enhanced Mk-I rocket system to the Indian Army, with the Nagastra-1 loitering munition also validated. The group order book of around Rs. 21,300 crore, supported by the Government's indigenisation push and export opportunities, provides medium-term revenue visibility.

**Strong and improving financial profile** – Consolidated revenue grew 30.5% to Rs. 9,837.74 crore in FY2026 with an operating margin of 26.65% and PAT of Rs. 1,736.64 crore. Leverage is low — gearing 0.24 times, Total debt/OPBDIT 0.58 times, TOL/TNW 0.70 times — and interest coverage is high at 19.57 times, each stronger than a year earlier. In the base-case forward view the capex programme is funded entirely from internal accruals, with leverage falling further.

### Credit challenges

**Sizeable capex and rising working-capital intensity** – A large multi-year capex programme (about Rs. 1,556 crore in FY2026) for explosives capacity and defence facilities, together with the working-capital build that accompanies the defence order-book ramp, absorbs much of the free cash flow. The profile absorbs this comfortably at current leverage, but the scale and funding of the programme remain a monitorable.

**Demand cyclicality, input-cost volatility and defence execution risk** – The base explosives business is exposed to demand cycles in mining and infrastructure and to ammonium-nitrate price movements, which can compress margins. The defence order book, though large, is at an early execution stage where delivery timelines, testing and indigenisation milestones carry execution risk. Diversification across end-markets and geographies partly mitigates these.

## Environmental and social risks

**Environmental considerations** – Manufacturing and handling of explosives and energetic materials is inherently hazardous and energy- and resource-intensive, with the principal exposures being process safety, emissions and effluent management, and the regulatory-licence regime governing explosives. On its FY2026 disclosures the company sourced 16% of its energy from renewable sources, consumed 11,13,560 GJ of energy and 7,70,592 KL of water, and reports operating on a Zero Liquid Discharge basis with waste managed through reuse, recycling and compliant disposal. ICRA assesses environmental risk as managed and not currently a rating driver.

**Social considerations** – Given the hazardous nature of the products, workforce and site safety is the central social exposure; the company reports a structured safety-audit programme (798 safety audits in FY2026) and community initiatives. The defence business operates under strict statutory and security oversight. Governance sits within a promoter-managed framework (promoter holding 95.11%) with a professional senior team and an unmodified audit opinion. ICRA assesses social and governance risk as not constraining the rating.

## Liquidity position: Strong

Liquidity is supported by cash and bank balances of Rs. 584.14 crore as on March 31, 2026 (no current investments outstanding at year-end), FY2026 interest coverage (OPBDIT/interest) of 19.6 times on the Rs. 133.97 crore interest bill with operating cash generation well in excess of it, and modest total debt of Rs. 1,524.02 crore against strong internal accruals that fully fund the base-case capex programme with no new borrowing over FY2027-FY2031. The Rs. 500.00 crore commercial-paper programme is a small share of the group's funding and is backed by ample balance-sheet and accrual headroom. The descriptor is **Strong** rather than Superior because a sizeable multi-year capex and defence build-out consumes much of the free cash flow and working capital is expanding with the defence ramp.

## Rating sensitivities

**Positive factors** – Not applicable. [ICRA]A1+ is the highest short-term rating on ICRA's scale.

**Negative factors** – The rating could be downgraded if there is a sustained and material deterioration in the operating margin, leverage or coverage — for instance from a large debt-funded acquisition or capex step-up, a sharp and prolonged demand or input-cost shock, or a stretch in the working-capital cycle — such that the credit profile weakens materially and durably.

## About the company

Solar Industries India Limited (SIIL), incorporated in 1995 and headquartered in Nagpur (CIN L74999MH1995PLC085878), is the largest manufacturer of industrial (commercial) explosives and explosive initiating systems in India, producing packaged emulsion explosives, bulk explosives and initiating systems, and a fast-growing supplier of defence products (ammunition, rockets, propellants and loitering munitions) through its subsidiary Solar Defence and Aerospace Limited. It supplies mining majors (Coal India and subsidiaries, Singareni Collieries), infrastructure customers and export markets across 40-plus countries, and is listed on the NSE and the BSE. For FY2026 the group reported consolidated revenue from operations of Rs. 9,837.74 crore (up 30.5%) and profit after tax of Rs. 1,736.64 crore. The promoter and promoter group held 95.11% as on the FY2026 record date; Shri Satyanarayan Nuwal is Chairman and Shri Manish Nuwal is Managing Director & CEO.

**Computation basis.** OPBDIT is computed as PBT plus finance cost plus depreciation less other income; other income is non-operating (its line-item composition — interest, net foreign-exchange gain, profit on sale of investments and miscellaneous — is read from the standalone note 21; the consolidated other income of Rs. 127.98 crore is stripped in full as an aggregate) — none of it is core-related job-work/scrap/duty-drawback income, and the State-GST/sales-tax subsidy is booked within Revenue as "other operating revenues" rather than in other income. The forex line is a **gain** in FY2025 and FY2026 and is excluded with other income; **no forex loss appears in operating (other) expenses in any year**, so the failure-mode-36 asymmetry (a stripped gain against a retained loss) does not arise, and **no exceptional item is booked in the consolidated profit and loss in any year** (the "profit before exceptional item" caption appears only in an interim standalone presentation, not in the audited consolidated accounts). Total debt of Rs. 1,524.02 crore is non-current plus current borrowings plus lease liabilities; **acceptances (Rs. 285.23 crore) are excluded from Total Debt per ICRA's convention** and sit instead in Total Outside Liabilities, which the TOL/TNW ratio captures. Tangible net worth uses equity attributable to owners (excluding non-controlling interests of Rs. 271.70 crore).
