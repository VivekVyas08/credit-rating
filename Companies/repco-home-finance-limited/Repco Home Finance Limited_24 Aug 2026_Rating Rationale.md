## Rationale

The rating reflects Repco Home Finance Limited's (RHFL) exceptionally comfortable capitalisation — capital-to-risk-weighted-assets ratio (CRAR) of 37.09% against the National Housing Bank's (NHB) 15% minimum, with gearing on a declining trend from 3.94 times (FY2023) to 3.01 times (30-Jun-2026) — and an asset-quality trajectory that has improved in every one of the last four years, with gross NPA falling from 7.0% (FY2022) to 2.67% (30-Jun-2026, excluding interest accrued). These strengths are weighed against a resource profile concentrated in a single funding channel and a scale that remains modest relative to larger rated housing finance company (HFC) peers.

In FY2025, RHFL's loan book (AUM) grew 7.2% to Rs. 14,492 crore (FY2024: Rs. 13,513 crore), profit after tax (PAT) rose 11.4% to Rs. 439.44 crore, and CRAR improved to 37.09% from 33.97% even as the balance sheet grew, driven by retained-earnings accretion (dividend payout of just 5.7% of PAT) rather than any capital raise. Gross NPA fell to 3.26% (excluding interest accrued; 3.99% on the RBI sectoral-exposure format including it) from 4.08%, and net NPA to 1.32% from 1.46%. The company issued its first-ever secured NCD in FY2026 (Rs. 199.19 crore outstanding at 30-Jun-2026, ISIN INE612J07160, 8.40% coupon), a modest but structurally significant first step in diversifying beyond bank and NHB borrowing. In Q1FY2027, the loan book stood at Rs. 15,708.23 crore (per the Reg 54(3) security-cover certificate) and PAT grew 5.7% year-on-year to Rs. 114.15 crore.

**A one-off item requires explicit note.** FY2026 PAT of Rs. 452.77 crore was flat with FY2025's Rs. 587.49 crore at the pre-tax level (profit before tax actually declined marginally, Rs. 586.04 crore vs Rs. 587.49 crore) and rose only because the company reversed a Rs. 14.40 crore deferred-tax liability on its Section 36(1)(viii) special reserve in Q4FY2026 — a Board-approved accounting position carrying an Emphasis of Matter in every subsequent auditor's review report. Excluding this reversal, FY2026 pre-provision operating profit growth (Rs. 554.97 crore vs Rs. 549.92 crore, +0.9%) was materially slower than FY2025's (+4.9%), which ICRA notes as the more representative measure of underlying earnings momentum.

What could go wrong: the resource profile is the central vulnerability. Twelve counterparties funded 96.69% of total liabilities and the top ten borrowings 93.34% of total borrowings at 31-Mar-2025, with zero commercial paper, external commercial borrowing, secured NCD or public-deposit funding outstanding at that date — a single-channel exposure to bank and NHB relationships. Provision coverage of 43.48% (30-Jun-2026) is thin against a gross NPA of 3.33% (including interest accrued) if the improving asset-quality trend were to reverse, and a Rs. 253.49 crore RBI Resolution-Framework-2.0 restructured pool was still slipping into NPA in successive half-years through 30-Sep-2025 (Rs. 10.15 crore, then Rs. 15.89 crore). Under ICRA's own stress modelling of these variables together (credit cost of 1.00% of average AUM, 75 bps of spread compression and zero disbursement growth, sustained for five years), gearing remains comfortable at 2.4–2.9 times, but return on average assets falls to 1.66–1.72% — below the 2.00% sustained threshold that CareEdge itself has published as a downgrade trigger for this issuer.

ICRA expects RHFL to grow disbursements at approximately 9–10% annually over the medium term (against 4.8%–7.4% in the two years to FY2025), supported by the branch-network expansion already undertaken (159 to 189 branches, FY2023–FY2025) that has yet to be fully worked through, while gearing continues its declining trend toward approximately 2.5 times by FY2031 under conservative payout assumptions, and credit cost normalises upward from the write-back years of FY2024–FY2026 to 0.30–0.40% of average AUM as the book seasons. The key monitorables are: (i) diversification of the funding base beyond the current bank/NHB concentration, given the NCD channel is still just 1.6% of drawn debt; (ii) sustained resolution — not merely absolute-level improvement — of the restructured book; and (iii) whether provision coverage is rebuilt as growth resumes, given its current thinness relative to the buffer needed if the four-year improving trend in asset quality were to turn.

The Stable outlook reflects ICRA's opinion that RHFL will sustain its current capitalisation and improving asset-quality trajectory while its funding base diversifies only gradually, without a step-change in either scale or risk profile over the medium term.

## Key rating drivers and their description

### Credit strengths

**Comfortable capitalisation providing a large buffer over both the regulatory minimum and near-term growth requirements** – CRAR of 37.09% (FY2025; Tier I 36.41%, Tier II 0.68%) against NHB's 15% minimum and the 12-times-net-owned-funds HFC leverage ceiling; gearing has fallen every year from 3.94 times (FY2023) to 3.36 times (FY2025) to 3.01 times (30-Jun-2026); equity funds 24.74% of the balance sheet (30-Jun-2026), up from 20.09% (FY2023). Net worth of Rs. 4,018.65 crore (30-Jun-2026) supports the current book more than twice over at the regulatory leverage ceiling.

**Asset quality that has improved in every one of the last four years, across a full credit cycle** – Gross NPA (excluding interest accrued) 7.0% (FY2022) → 5.8% (FY2023) → 4.08% (FY2024) → 3.26% (FY2025) → 2.67% (30-Jun-2026); net NPA 1.46% → 1.32% → 1.23% over the same period. The book is entirely retail housing/loan-against-property lending secured by mortgaged property, with no corporate or wholesale exposure.

**Established, profitable niche franchise in south Indian tier-2/tier-3 housing finance, promoted by a scheduled bank** – A 25-year operating history (incorporated April 2000), 189 branches and 44 satellite centres across 12 states and one union territory as at FY2025, serving over 1.1 lakh active customers; return on average managed assets of 3.00% (FY2025), among the higher levels in the rated HFC peer set. Repco Bank Limited, a Government of India enterprise, holds 37.13% and has promoted the company since inception — a governance credential, though (per Analytical approach below) not a factor carrying an explicit support notch.

### Credit challenges

**Resource profile concentrated in a single funding channel, with diversification only just beginning** – Twelve significant counterparties funded 96.69% of total liabilities and the top ten borrowings 93.34% of total borrowings at 31-Mar-2025 (AR Note 34); commercial paper, external commercial borrowing, secured NCDs and public deposits were all NIL outstanding at every quarter-end from 31-Mar-2024 to 31-Mar-2025. The company's first secured NCD (Rs. 199.19 crore, 30-Jun-2026) is a start but represents only 1.6% of drawn debt.

**Small scale relative to larger rated HFC peers, limiting negotiating leverage and constraining growth in absolute terms** – AUM of Rs. 14,492 crore (FY2025) compares with Rs. 98,000 crore for PNB Housing Finance Limited and materially larger books at LIC Housing Finance Limited; AUM grew at a 7.2% CAGR over FY2022–FY2025 despite the branch network expanding 19% over the same period, indicating the added distribution capacity has not yet translated into faster growth.

**Provision coverage thin relative to the buffer needed should the improving trend reverse, with a restructured pool still slipping** – Provision coverage ratio of 43.48% (30-Jun-2026) against gross NPA of 3.33% (including interest accrued); the Rs. 253.49 crore RBI Resolution-Framework-2.0 restructured book (30-Sep-2025) saw Rs. 10.15 crore and then Rs. 15.89 crore of exposure slip into NPA in successive half-years, evidencing that the underlying stress has not fully resolved even as headline asset quality improves. **Regulatory/accounting monitorable:** the Section 36(1)(viii) deferred-tax-liability reversal of Rs. 14.40 crore (Q4FY2026), carrying an Emphasis of Matter in the auditor's subsequent limited review reports, is a disclosed Board-approved position rather than a going-concern issue, but it means FY2026 headline PAT growth is not representative of underlying earnings momentum (see Rationale).

## Environmental and social risks

**Environmental considerations** – As a housing-finance intermediary, RHFL's direct environmental footprint is low. The company's BRSR discloses NIL Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions for both FY2025 and FY2024, and states that "the emissions are considerably low and it is operationally challenging for the company to collate such information from all branches and SAT centre" — an evidenced absence, not an unexplained gap. Electricity consumption is disclosed at Rs. 2,02,02,843 (FY2025) against Rs. 1,73,94,335 (FY2024); note the BRSR's own table header is inconsistently labelled "in Joules or multiples" while the column values are stated "(in Rs.)" — an internal labelling inconsistency in the source disclosure, reproduced here as disclosed rather than resolved by assumption. Environmental risk is assessed as **low** and does not constrain the rating.

**Social considerations** – RHFL employed 1,256 permanent staff as at FY2025. The company's core business — extending housing finance to salaried and non-salaried borrowers in tier-2/tier-3 cities, many with limited formal income documentation — carries a positive financial-inclusion dimension but also concentrates credit risk in a segment the company's own risk register describes as having borrowers who "do not have proper documented income to support cash flows" and who are "vulnerable to any downward change in the economy." No governance controversies, labour disputes or social-license issues were identified in the materiality sweep of the document set. Social risk is assessed as **low to moderate**, reflected already in the credit-challenge discussion of borrower-segment vulnerability above, and does not separately constrain the rating.

## Liquidity position: Adequate

**Sources:** cash and liquid investments of Rs. 280.22 crore (30-Jun-2026: cash Rs. 136.83 crore + other bank balances Rs. 5.29 crore + investments Rs. 138.10 crore), a company-computed Liquidity Coverage Ratio of 202.13% (30-Jun-2026, Reg 52(4) disclosure), continuing operating collections from an amortising, fully-secured retail mortgage book, and an unblemished repayment record across every borrowing facility ("no default in repayment of instalments during the financial year"; "no delay in repayment of borrowings during the financial year" — AR Note 14(c), 14(f)) that evidences continued access to refinancing from the company's existing 12 bank/NHB relationships.

**Claims:** borrowings maturing within 12 months of Rs. 2,458.19 crore (as at 31-Mar-2025, per the AR's contractual maturity-analysis note), against cash and liquid investments at the same date of Rs. 347.52 crore — cash alone covers only around 14% of the near-term repayment book. A dividend of Rs. 25.02 crore was proposed for FY2025 (immaterial against the repayment quantum); no major capex programme is disclosed, consistent with an HFC where incremental branch investment is embedded in operating expenditure rather than a separate capex line.

The arithmetic supports **Adequate**, not Strong: on-balance-sheet cash cover of near-term maturities is thin in isolation, and while the LCR and the unblemished repayment record both point to genuine refinancing capacity, that capacity is concentrated in the same twelve counterparties that fund 96.69% of the balance sheet — the same concentration flagged as the central credit challenge above. No undrawn bank-line or committed-facility headroom is disclosed anywhere in the document set (searched: "undrawn", "unavailed", "un-availed", "sanctioned but not", "unutilised"; the only hits are for "undrawn LOAN commitments" — an asset-side disclosure of loans the company has committed to disburse to ITS OWN customers, not a funding-side facility available to it, and not conflated with liquidity sources here).

## Rating sensitivities

**Positive factors** – Sustained AUM growth of 10%+ accompanied by tangible diversification of the funding base beyond the current bank/NHB concentration (e.g., NCD/CP issuance reaching a double-digit share of total debt on a sustained basis), while maintaining gross NPA below 2.5% and CRAR above 25%, could trigger a rating upgrade.

**Negative factors** – Gross NPA sustained above 5%, gearing sustained above 5 times, or return on average assets sustained below 2.00% could trigger a rating downgrade — thresholds chosen for direct comparability with CareEdge's own published sensitivities for this issuer (press release, 30-Sep-2025). A material deterioration in funding access or a further narrowing of the counterparty base could also pressure the rating, independent of the financial-ratio triggers above.

## About the company

Repco Home Finance Limited (RHFL), incorporated in April 2000 and headquartered in Chennai, Tamil Nadu, is a housing finance company registered with the National Housing Bank, promoted by Repco Bank Limited (a Government of India enterprise), which held 37.13% as at 31-Mar-2025. RHFL's equity shares are listed on the National Stock Exchange of India Limited and BSE Limited. The company primarily extends housing loans and loans against property to individual retail borrowers, concentrated in tier-2 and tier-3 cities across south India — Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Kerala, Maharashtra, Odisha, West Bengal, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Jharkhand, Rajasthan and the Union Territory of Puducherry — through 189 branches and 44 satellite centres as at 31-Mar-2025 (up from 159 branches and 33 satellite centres at 31-Mar-2023), serving over 1.1 lakh active customers with a reported AUM of Rs. 14,492 crore. Approximately 70% of the book is housing loans and 30% loan against property (non-housing); the customer base splits roughly 48% salaried and 52% non-salaried. RHFL's only related entity is Repco Micro Finance Limited, a microfinance-focused associate accounted for under the equity method (see Analytical approach).

## Any other information

**The reference-library ICRA file for this issuer is a securitisation rationale, not a corporate rating, and has not been used as one.** Dated 25-Feb-2026 and titled *"Repco Home Finance Limited: Ratings confirmed as final for SNs backed by a pool of loan against property (LAP) receivables issued by Pratham 12 2025,"* it rates securitisation notes (SNs) issued by the PRATHAM 12 2025 trust: [ICRA]AAA(SO) on Rs. 92.80 crore of Series A1 SNs (initial Rs. 93.25 crore) and [ICRA]AA-(SO) on Rs. 4.99 crore of Equity Tranche SNs (initial Rs. 5.01 crore), backed by an RHFL-originated LAP pool of Rs. 99.79 crore principal outstanding. RHFL is the Originator and Servicer of this transaction — not the rated obligor — and the (SO) structured-obligation rating on the trust's paper has been deliberately excluded from every figure and conclusion in this rationale.

**Contingent liabilities** (FY2025, immaterial against net worth of Rs. 3,313.55 crore): claims against the company not acknowledged as debts, Rs. 15.88 crore (0.48% of net worth); income tax and GST liabilities, Rs. 5.80 crore (0.18%).

**Auditor's opinion:** unmodified/unqualified in every annual and quarterly filing reviewed (FY2023–Q1FY2027), carrying one Emphasis of Matter from Q4FY2026 onward regarding the Section 36(1)(viii) deferred-tax-liability reversal discussed in Credit challenges above — a disclosed accounting judgement, not a qualification or going-concern flag.

**Regulatory restructured book:** Rs. 253.49 crore under the RBI Resolution Framework 2.0 (as at 30-Sep-2025), down from Rs. 281.44 crore (31-Mar-2025) but still shedding exposure into NPA (Rs. 10.15 crore and Rs. 15.89 crore in the two successive half-years to that date) — flagged as a live, not yet fully resolved, credit challenge.
