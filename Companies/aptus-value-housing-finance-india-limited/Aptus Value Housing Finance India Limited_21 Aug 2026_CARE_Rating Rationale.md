## **Rationale and key rating drivers**

The rating on Aptus Value Housing Finance India Limited (Aptus) is driven overwhelmingly by **capitalisation that is extreme rather than merely adequate**. Capital adequacy stood at **71%** against the RBI's 15% minimum for housing finance companies — more than four times over — with on-book gearing of **1.56x** and AUM to tangible net worth of **2.59x**. For a lender compounding its book at 20%+ a year, that is an unusual combination, and it gives Aptus both substantial growth headroom and a deep buffer against asset-quality stress.

Tangible net worth of **₹5,058.67 crore** has been built almost entirely through accrual. Return on net worth rose from 14.5% in FY2021 to **20.2%** in FY2026 *while* the equity base more than doubled — improving returns on a rapidly enlarging denominator, which is the harder version of that achievement.

Profitability is strong and well-explained rather than opportunistic. Profit after tax was **₹942.94 crore** on total income of **₹2,245.48 crore** — a PAT margin of **42.0%** and return on managed assets of **7.87%**. Net interest margin on average AUM was **10.81%**, reflecting affordable-housing yields against a *falling* cost of borrowing (8.3% in FY2026, down from 8.7%). Cost-to-income of **20.2%**, with operating expenses stable at 2.7% of AUM across six years, indicates genuine operating efficiency rather than a scale artefact.

Scale has compounded steadily: AUM grew **20.6% to ₹13,107 crore**, a six-year CAGR of ~26% from ₹4,068 crore in FY2021, across **339 branches in 6 states and 1 Union Territory** serving **1.88 lakh customers**. Q1FY2027 continued the trend, with income up 15.2% and profit up 19.0% year on year.

Against this, **asset quality is drifting adversely from a low base**. GNPA rose to **1.52%** from 1.19% and NNPA to **1.15%** from 0.89%, while credit cost on average AUM doubled to **0.51%**. The absolute levels remain comfortable and an LTV of ~40% provides real collateral protection, but the direction is uniformly negative, the book is only moderately seasoned, and provision coverage on stage 3 assets of 25.50% is thin — defensible only because of that low LTV.

The structural constraint is concentration. The portfolio remains **concentrated in southern India**, improving only gradually, and lends to an **underbanked borrower segment** with limited formal income documentation. The same characteristics that produce a 10.81% margin are those most exposed to local economic shocks and to any tightening of affordable-housing regulation.

One earnings item warrants attention: **assignment income rose to ₹172.44 crore from ₹16.31 crore**, as the off-book share of AUM stepped up from 2.2% to 9.0%. This is a real and legitimate contribution, but it is transaction-dependent and will not repeat automatically.

## **Rating sensitivities: Factors likely to lead to rating actions**

### **Positive factors**
- Meaningful geographic diversification away from the southern concentration, sustained alongside current profitability.
- Continued AUM growth above 20% with GNPA held at or below current levels through a full seasoning cycle.
- Demonstrated stability of earnings without reliance on assignment gains.

### **Negative factors**
- GNPA sustained above ~2.5%, or a further material rise in credit cost on average AUM.
- On-book gearing sustained above ~3.5x, or capital adequacy falling materially towards the regulatory minimum.
- Deterioration in profitability, with return on managed assets falling below ~4%.
- Any material weakening in the funding profile, or a rise in reliance on short-term borrowing from the current nil.

## **Analytical approach:** Consolidated

Aptus Value Housing Finance India Limited is consolidated with **Aptus Finance India Private Limited (AFIPL)**, its wholly-owned NBFC subsidiary, given common management, shared underwriting and operational integration. This is not optional: AFIPL carries **₹3,801 crore, or 29%, of the ₹13,107 crore managed book**, and Aptus has extended a corporate guarantee of **₹269.63 crore** for AFIPL's borrowings.

The distinction is material to every figure in this report. On a standalone basis Aptus reports total assets of ₹9,553.31 crore and total income of ₹1,564.15 crore; consolidated, those become ₹13,047.66 crore and ₹2,245.48 crore. **The two bases are never spliced.**

## **Detailed description of key rating drivers:**

### **Key strengths**

**Exceptional capitalisation with substantial growth headroom.** CRAR of **71%** against the RBI's 15% minimum leaves **56 percentage points of headroom**. On-book gearing of **1.56x** (FY2025: 1.59x) and AUM/TNW of **2.59x** (FY2025: 2.53x) are low for an HFC of this growth rate. Tangible net worth reached **₹5,058.67 crore** at 31 March 2026, from ₹4,293.58 crore a year earlier — growth funded by retained earnings rather than fresh capital.

**Healthy and durable profitability.** PAT of **₹942.94 crore** (FY2025: ₹751.25 crore), a **42.0%** PAT margin, **7.87%** return on managed assets and **20.2%** return on net worth. Net interest income rose 15.5% to **₹1,295.35 crore**. The margin is structural, not cyclical: affordable-housing lending yields against an improving cost of funds, with a spread of ~8.9%.

**Consistent operating efficiency.** Cost-to-income of **20.2%** and operating expenses steady at **2.7% of AUM** across six years, through a period in which AUM more than tripled. Efficiency has been maintained during expansion rather than sacrificed to it.

**Established track record and steady scale-up.** AUM compounded ~26% over six years — ₹4,068 → ₹5,180 → ₹6,738 → ₹8,722 → ₹10,865 → **₹13,107 crore** — with the book split ₹9,306 crore housing (71%) and ₹3,801 crore through AFIPL (29%). The network reached **339 branches** (FY2025: 300) across 6 states and 1 UT, serving **1.88 lakh customers** (FY2025: 1.62 lakh) with 3,807 employees.

**Comfortable funding profile with no short-term refinancing risk.** Total debt of ₹7,873.67 crore comprises debt securities of ₹1,350.49 crore and other borrowings of ₹6,523.18 crore. The annual report states **zero commercial paper and zero short-term unsecured borrowing** — unusual for a lender of this size and a meaningful mitigant against refinancing risk. Reported liquidity was ₹2,061 crore.

**Stable promoter and governance continuity.** Founder-promoter Mr M. Anandan, with the promoter group, holds **23.9%** and continues as Executive Chairman, alongside a private-equity-backed institutional shareholder base.

### **Key weaknesses**

**Asset quality deteriorating from a low base, on a moderately seasoned book.** GNPA rose to **1.52%** from 1.19% and NNPA to **1.15%** from 0.89% (annual report, consolidated). Credit cost on average AUM **doubled to 0.51%**. Given that AUM grew 20.6% in the same year — enlarging the denominator and mechanically suppressing the ratio — the underlying deterioration is somewhat greater than the headline suggests. Provision coverage on stage 3 assets of **25.50%** (standalone) is thin in isolation and depends on the ~40% LTV holding through a downturn.

**Geographical concentration.** The portfolio remains concentrated in southern India, with diversification improving only gradually across 6 states and 1 UT. Regional economic shocks, monsoon-linked rural income volatility, or state-level policy change would transmit directly to portfolio performance.

**Exposure to an underbanked borrower segment.** Aptus lends to self-employed and informal-income borrowers with limited documented income history, assessed through in-house appraisal. This supports the wide margin but concentrates credit risk in the segment most sensitive to local income disruption, and the through-cycle loss experience of such books is not yet fully observable at Aptus's stage of seasoning.

**Rising reliance on assignment income.** Income from derecognition rose more than tenfold to **₹172.44 crore**, taking the off-book share of AUM from 2.2% to 9.0%. This flatters FY2026 earnings by a margin that is transaction-dependent; stripped out, total income growth would have been materially lower than the reported 24.9%.

## **Liquidity: Adequate**

Aptus reported a liquidity buffer of **₹2,061 crore** at 31 March 2026 — but the annual report describes this as *"liquid assets **and undrawn sanctions**"*, so it is a combined figure, not cash. Cash and cash equivalents on the consolidated balance sheet were **₹563.32 crore** (FY2025: ₹302.59 crore), a cash-to-total-debt ratio of 0.07x, up from 0.04x; the balance of the buffer is undrawn sanctioned credit lines from banking partners. The company projects a liquidity surplus of ₹2,236 crore through September 2026. Liquidity coverage ratio stood at **211.79%** (standalone), comfortably above the regulatory requirement.

The funding structure is the main support. With **no commercial paper and no short-term unsecured borrowing**, Aptus carries no near-term refinancing wall; borrowings are term loans and NCDs with maturities extending to 2034 (term loan) and 2028–2031 (NCDs). Monthly collections from a granular retail book of 1.88 lakh accounts provide a predictable inflow, and CRAR of 71% supports continued access to bank funding.

The descriptor is **Adequate** rather than Strong for two reasons. First, on-balance-sheet cash alone covers a small fraction of debt, so the assessment leans on scheduled collections, undrawn sanctions and refinancing capacity rather than held liquidity. Second, under CareEdge's criteria only **unencumbered** cash counts toward liquidity — balances earmarked against facilities or held in a DSRA do not — and Aptus does not disclose that split. A grep for `unencumber` across all eight documents returns **three hits, every one of them the boilerplate LCR definition of high-quality liquid assets**, not a statement of the company's own encumbered position. Treating the full ₹2,061 crore as available would therefore overstate liquidity, and it is not treated that way here.

## **Environment, social, and governance (ESG) risks**

As a retail housing lender, Aptus's material ESG exposures are social and governance rather than environmental.

- **Social — the business model itself:** lending to underbanked, informal-income households for affordable housing is directly financial-inclusion positive; **1.88 lakh customers** are served across 6 states and 1 UT, at an average LTV of ~40% that protects the borrower's equity as well as the lender's.
- **Governance:** founder-promoter and group holding of **23.9%**, with Mr M. Anandan as Executive Chairman, alongside institutional and private-equity shareholders. The company is listed and subject to SEBI LODR disclosure.
- **Responsible lending and conduct:** the annual report identifies financial inclusion, responsible lending, data privacy, employee welfare and CSR as material topics through a double-materiality assessment, with SASB and GRI linkages disclosed.
- **Environmental:** limited direct exposure; the annual report addresses green operations and paper reduction as operational matters rather than credit-material ones.

Conduct risk in the underbanked segment — collection practices, fair pricing and grievance redressal — is the ESG factor most capable of becoming credit-material, given regulatory attention to microfinance-adjacent lending.

## **About the company and industry**

### Industry classification

| Macroeconomic indicator | Sector | Industry | Basic industry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Financial services | Financial services | Finance | Housing finance company |

**Aptus Value Housing Finance India Limited** is a listed affordable housing finance company registered with the National Housing Bank, headquartered in Chennai. It provides home loans, loans against property and small business loans to self-employed and informal-income borrowers in semi-urban and rural markets, operating through **339 branches across 6 states and 1 Union Territory** with **3,807 employees**. Its wholly-owned subsidiary, **Aptus Finance India Private Limited**, is an NBFC carrying the non-housing portion of the book. Founder-promoter Mr M. Anandan and the promoter group hold **23.9%**.

Consolidated AUM reached **₹13,107 crore** at 31 March 2026 — ₹9,306 crore (71%) in the housing finance company and ₹3,801 crore (29%) in the NBFC subsidiary.

The affordable housing finance sector has grown steadily on a structural housing shortage, government support through PMAY and rising formalisation of informal-income lending. Competition has intensified from banks moving down-market and from well-capitalised HFC peers, compressing yields at the larger end. The segment's defining risk is that its borrowers — self-employed, without documented income — are the least resilient to local income shocks, so underwriting quality and collections infrastructure separate performers from the rest more sharply than in prime housing finance.

## **Annexure III — Basis and evidence notes**

**Documents used (8).** Three annual reports (FY2024, FY2025, FY2026) and five quarterly results (Q1FY2026 through Q1FY2027). The **FY2026 annual report** and the **Q1FY2027 results (quarter ended 30 June 2026)** are the newest and were read first.

**Rated amounts — five-condition amendment satisfied.** (1) The CareEdge press release of 02-Jul-2026 for **this issuer** is held in the reference library and was extracted **before** drafting, per failure mode 29. (2) The rated-amount table is reproduced in full with the publication date stamped. (3) It is labelled CRA-published, not issuer-confirmed. (4) **Line integrity verified**: bank facilities 2,058.75 + 30.00 + 11.25 = ₹2,100.00 crore; NCDs 45.00 + 240.00 + 200.00 + 280.00 + 80.00 + 300.00 = ₹1,145.00 crore — both exact. (5) The rated-limits-versus-drawn-debt distinction is stated explicitly.

**Conventions verified by reproduction, not assertion.** Sixteen checks in `compute_ratios.py` and nine in the workbook's TieOut tab reconcile against CareEdge's published figures: TNW ₹5,058.67 crore against published ₹5,059 crore; total income ₹2,245.48 crore against ₹2,245; PAT ₹942.94 crore against ₹943; on-book gearing 1.56x against 1.56x; AUM/TNW 2.53x against 2.53x for FY2025. Two figures land 0.01 apart — FY2025 gearing (computed 1.59x, published 1.60x) and FY2026 AUM/TNW (computed 2.59x, published 2.58x) — and are reported **as computed**, not adjusted to force a tie.

**Asset quality — two different measures, both reported.** The annual report states GNPA of **1.52%** (FY2025: 1.19%) and explicitly labels it *consolidated*; CareEdge publishes gross stage 3 of **1.45%** (FY2025: 1.18%). These are different classifications of the same book — RBI IRAC norms against Ind AS staging — and the gap is not reconcilable from the documents held. Both are stated rather than one being silently selected. On a standalone basis the FY2026 results report GNPA of 1.29%, NNPA of 0.96% and provision coverage on stage 3 of 25.50%.

**Peer selection — corrected manually.** The pre-flight peer probe ranked **Jana Small Finance Bank** first, a *bank* rather than an HFC, and **HFCL Limited** third, a telecom cable manufacturer matching on the token "hfc". The probe flagged that the top peers disagreed on the row set. The metric set was therefore taken from **PNB Housing Finance Limited (03-Jul-2026)**, the true HFC peer, and `peer_rows.json` was overwritten accordingly.

**Basis discipline.** Every figure in this report is **consolidated**. The annual report carries both bases and they differ materially (standalone total income ₹1,564.15 crore against consolidated ₹2,245.48 crore); splicing them would misstate the report by ₹681 crore.

**Data cutoff.** Financial data as on **30 June 2026** (latest quarterly) and **31 March 2026** (latest audited). Prepared **21 August 2026**.

**Limitations, each searched rather than assumed.** No management interaction and no rating committee. Instrument term sheets and sanction letters were flagged as known gaps in the rating request itself and are absent: a grep for `term sheet|sanction letter` across the eight documents returns two hits, both in older annual reports and neither a term sheet, so covenant detail, security structure and lender-wise facility splits are not reproduced.
