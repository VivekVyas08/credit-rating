### **Detailed Rationale**

This independent assessment arrives at **'Crisil AAA/Stable'** on the long-term facilities and debentures of Reliance Industries Limited (RIL) and **'Crisil A1+'** on its short-term facilities and commercial paper, on a consolidated basis.

The rating reflects three structural anchors: **leadership in four large and unrelated businesses** — oil-to-chemicals (O2C), digital services (Jio), retail and oil and gas — none of which contributed more than 39.5% of fiscal 2026 segment EBITDA; **superior liquidity**, with cash and marketable securities of Rs 2,49,704 crore at March 31, 2026, 4.85 times the debt falling due within a year plus commercial paper outstanding; and **financial flexibility of an unusual order**, with a market capitalisation of about Rs 15.8 lakh crore (screener.in, October 10, 2026), 3.2 times adjusted debt.

The earnings mix has shifted towards the steadier consumer businesses. Retail and digital services together rose from 46.7% of segment EBITDA in fiscal 2024 to 53.4% in fiscal 2026, while O2C and oil and gas fell from 48.3% to 41.0%. That diversity was tested in fiscal 2025, when O2C EBITDA fell by Rs 7,401 crore (to Rs 54,988 crore) on weak refining and petrochemical margins: digital services and retail added Rs 10,312 crore in the same year, and total segment EBITDA still rose. In fiscal 2026 consolidated PBDIT grew 8.5% to Rs 1,98,987 crore — after excluding a one-time Rs 8,924 crore profit on the sale of listed investments booked in other income — with digital services up 17.8% to Rs 76,560 crore and O2C up 10.1% to Rs 60,546 crore.

Leverage is moderate on Crisil Ratings' wide debt definition. Adjusted debt — borrowings of Rs 3,74,421 crore, lease liabilities of Rs 23,579 crore and deferred spectrum liabilities to the Department of Telecommunications (DoT) of Rs 1,04,514 crore — was Rs 5,02,514 crore at March 31, 2026: 2.53 times PBDIT and 0.46 time adjusted networth. Net of cash and marketable securities it was 1.27 times PBDIT, against 1.71 times in fiscal 2023. Adding supplier-finance arrangements of Rs 76,377 crore takes adjusted debt to 2.91 times PBDIT. Interest coverage, with Rs 16,091 crore of capitalised interest included in the denominator, was 4.61 times.

The ratings are constrained by the scale of the investment programme. Capital expenditure (excluding spectrum) was Rs 1,31,769 crore, Rs 1,31,107 crore and Rs 1,44,271 crore in fiscals 2024-2026, capital commitments doubled to Rs 66,517 crore, and the company has announced a plan to invest Rs 10 lakh crore in multi-gigawatt AI-ready data centres, with no phasing or funding plan yet disclosed. Every Rs 1 lakh crore of debt-funded spending would add about 0.46 time to fiscal 2027 adjusted debt to PBDIT. O2C earnings remain cyclical, and fiscal 2026 operating cash flow of Rs 1,92,113 crore was helped by an Rs 86,609 crore rise in trade and other payables, within which contract liabilities rose by Rs 1,09,196 crore.

**The 'Stable' outlook reflects this analysis's opinion that RIL will sustain its leadership across businesses and fund capital expenditure largely from cash flow, keeping adjusted debt below 3.5 times PBDIT through the investment cycle.** In the base case, adjusted debt to PBDIT eases to 2.30 times in fiscal 2027 without new borrowing.

# **Analytical Approach**

Analytical approach: **Consolidated**. The analysis is on a **consolidated** basis. Under Crisil Ratings' criteria for consolidation, all subsidiaries of the rated entity are generally consolidated through the pooling of interests method; the exceptions — a subsidiary in a different sector or one that is explicitly ring-fenced — do not apply. RIL's material subsidiaries (Jio Platforms Limited, Reliance Jio Infocomm Limited, Reliance Retail Ventures Limited, Reliance Retail Limited and Reliance International Limited) are operating businesses under RIL's management control, share its name and are funded within its stated capital framework; there is no significant financial-sector subsidiary after the fiscal 2023 demerger of Jio Financial Services. Non-controlling interest is included in adjusted networth. Entities consolidated are listed in Annexure - List of entities consolidated.

**Ratio definitions (Crisil Ratings).** PBDIT includes recurring non-operating income (interest, dividend and treasury gains on a cash and marketable-securities book of about Rs 2.5 lakh crore) and excludes one-time items: the Rs 8,924 crore profit on the sale of listed investments in fiscal 2026 is removed, which the company itself excludes when comparing EBITDA. The foreign exchange (forex) loss that the company classifies within finance costs (Rs 2,788 crore in fiscal 2026) is kept in interest; the exchange difference within other expenses is small and retained. Interest includes capitalised interest. Adjusted debt includes lease liabilities and the deferred spectrum liabilities to DoT; supplier-finance arrangements, presented by the company within trade payables and other financial liabilities with due dates up to 1,083 days, are added in a wider measure that is shown alongside. Non-fund-based facilities (letters of credit, guarantees) are not added to debt: the drawn obligations they support appear either in trade payables (including supplier finance) or in contingent liabilities (guarantees of Rs 7,117 crore at March 31, 2026, assessed as unlikely to devolve).

**Parent / group.** RIL is the group's flagship; no support uplift is applied and no parent with a weaker credit profile draws on RIL's cash flows on the evidence of the documents, so there is no downward adjustment either.

# **Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description**

# **Key Rating Drivers — Strengths**

**Leadership and diversity across four large businesses.** Jio had 524.4 million subscribers at March 31, 2026 (533.3 million at June 30, 2026), with fourth-quarter average revenue per user rising from Rs 181.7 (fiscal 2024) to Rs 214.0 (fiscal 2026); digital services EBITDA was Rs 76,560 crore at a 43.5% margin. Reliance Retail operated 20,160 stores across 78.3 million sq ft and 387 million registered customers, with EBITDA of Rs 27,034 crore. The O2C business processed 80.0 million tonnes (78.2 and 80.5 million tonnes in fiscals 2024 and 2025) and earned Rs 60,546 crore of EBITDA. RIL does not publish a numeric operating rate (capacity utilisation) for O2C — searched 'operating rate' and 'utilisation' across the case documents; the fiscal 2026 annual report says only that the business "maximised capacity utilisation" — so throughput is used as the volume measure. Feedstock security rests on global crude sourcing; the fourth-quarter fiscal 2026 release cites efficient feedstock sourcing, while sharply higher crude premiums on physical barrels squeezed margins in that quarter. Oil and gas, from the KG D6 block and coal-bed methane, earned Rs 19,050 crore. No segment exceeded 39.5% of fiscal 2026 segment EBITDA.

**A rising share of stable consumer earnings.** Retail and digital services moved from 46.7% to 53.4% of segment EBITDA over fiscals 2024-2026. Digital services revenue grew at a 15.1% compound annual rate over the period (Rs 1,32,938 crore to Rs 1,76,164 crore) and retail at 10.0% (Rs 3,06,848 crore to Rs 3,71,085 crore). The fiscal 2025 O2C downturn showed the mix at work: O2C EBITDA fell 11.9% while total segment EBITDA rose 2.3%.

**Superior liquidity and financial flexibility.** Cash and marketable securities were Rs 2,49,704 crore at March 31, 2026 and Rs 2,46,791 crore at June 30, 2026. Market capitalisation was about Rs 15.8 lakh crore (October 2026), 3.2 times adjusted debt. Access to capital markets is long established: the company issued Rs 20,000 crore of 7.79% secured debentures maturing in 2032-34, its notes include foreign-currency bonds of Rs 57,274 crore, and Jio Platforms filed its draft red herring prospectus with SEBI in the first quarter of fiscal 2027.

**Moderating leverage with internal accrual funding capex.** Net adjusted debt to PBDIT eased from 1.71 times in fiscal 2023 to 1.27 times in fiscal 2026. Free cash flow after capex, spectrum and lease payments turned from a deficit of Rs 27,362 crore in fiscal 2023 to a surplus of Rs 57,953 crore in fiscal 2026 as capex fell from 0.92 time to 0.62 time PBDIT. Cash accrual to adjusted debt (NCATD) improved from 0.24 to 0.29 time.

# **Key Rating Drivers — Weaknesses**

**Large and expanding investment programme.** Capex has run at Rs 1.31-1.44 lakh crore a year and was Rs 38,682 crore in the first quarter of fiscal 2027. Capital commitments rose to Rs 66,517 crore from Rs 31,629 crore. Beyond the Rs 75,000 crore clean-energy commitment (a 40 GWh battery giga-factory commissioning from 2026, 100 GW of renewable capacity targeted by 2030), the company has announced a Rs 10 lakh crore plan for AI-ready data centres — about five times fiscal 2026 PBDIT — without a disclosed timeline or funding structure. Projects large relative to existing operations are viewed unfavourably; a debt-funded acceleration is the main downside risk to the rating.

**Cyclicality in O2C and decline in oil and gas.** O2C EBITDA moved from Rs 62,389 crore to Rs 54,988 crore to Rs 60,546 crore over fiscals 2024-2026, and the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 was hit by higher crude premiums, freight and fuel costs during the Middle East disruption. KG D6 output fell 8.2% to 248.8 BCFe in fiscal 2026 on natural decline, and oil and gas EBITDA fell 10.1%.

**Working capital supports reported cash flow, and adjusted leverage is higher than headline net debt.** Fiscal 2026 operating cash flow included an Rs 86,609 crore rise in trade and other payables; contract liabilities rose to Rs 1,72,201 crore from Rs 63,005 crore, mostly outside the standalone company, and the reason is not explained in the documents. Supplier finance stood at Rs 76,377 crore. On Crisil Ratings' definitions adjusted debt is Rs 5,02,514 crore — four times the company's own net debt of Rs 1,24,717 crore, which nets cash and excludes leases and DoT liabilities. Return on capital employed was modest at 8.67% in fiscal 2026, held down by capital work in progress of Rs 1,92,287 crore.

**Legal and regulatory matters.** The Government of India has demanded USD 2.81 billion (RIL share USD 1.87 billion) from the KG D6 contractors over gas allegedly migrated from neighbouring blocks; RIL has filed a special leave petition in the Supreme Court and considers the demand remote, and the amount is not included in contingent liabilities. In the SEBI matter relating to trades in Reliance Petroleum shares in 2007, the disgorgement of Rs 447.27 crore with interest at 12% a year is under appeal; the Supreme Court reserved judgment on January 30, 2026.

# **Liquidity: Superior**

Unencumbered cash and marketable securities were Rs 2,49,704 crore at March 31, 2026 (cash and cash equivalents Rs 1,45,977 crore, current investments Rs 97,431 crore and other marketable securities Rs 6,296 crore, as the company defines them in its capital-management note); no part of this is disclosed as earmarked or held as margin. Debt payable within fiscal 2027 is Rs 38,308 crore (current maturities of long-term borrowings Rs 25,965 crore, DoT instalment Rs 4,962 crore and lease liabilities Rs 7,381 crore), and commercial paper outstanding is Rs 13,186 crore: cash covers these 4.85 times. Operating cash flow is expected at about Rs 1.7 lakh crore in fiscal 2027 in the base case against capex of about Rs 1.5 lakh crore, and no new borrowing is needed to keep cash above Rs 2 lakh crore in any year of the base case.

# **ESG Profile**

Crisil Ratings believes RIL's environment, social and governance (ESG) profile supports its credit risk profile. ESG is material for RIL on each limb of the test: its O2C business is emissions-intensive, institutions and depository-receipt holders own 40.29% of its shares, and it borrows in international markets (foreign-currency bonds of Rs 57,274 crore).

**ESG highlights:**
- RIL targets Net Carbon Zero by 2035 and has committed Rs 75,000 crore to clean energy, including 100 GW of renewable capacity by 2030, a battery giga-factory of 40 GWh (scalable to 100 GWh) commissioning from 2026, and 3 MMTPA of green-hydrogen-equivalent capacity by 2032.
- Energy-efficiency programmes saved about 10 million GJ over the past three years.
- Lost-time injury frequency rate (per million man-hours) was 0.06 for O2C and E&P, 0.06 for retail and 0.60 for digital services in fiscal 2026.
- CSR spending was Rs 2,248 crore in fiscal 2026 (Rs 2,156 crore in fiscal 2025).
- Statutory auditors gave unmodified opinions on the fiscal 2026 standalone and consolidated statements; key audit matters relate to litigation, a Level-3 valuation, revenue recognition at Reliance Jio Infocomm and IT controls.

# **Outlook: Stable**

Crisil Ratings believes RIL will maintain its leadership in each business and fund its capital expenditure largely from cash flow, so that adjusted debt stays below 3.5 times PBDIT through the investment cycle.

# **Rating sensitivity factors**

**Upward factors:** Not applicable (the long-term rating is at the highest level of the scale).

**Downward factors**
- Adjusted debt (including DoT liabilities and leases) above 3.5 times PBDIT, or net adjusted debt above 2.0 times PBDIT, **on a sustained basis**.
- Debt-funded capital expenditure or inorganic investments, including the AI data-centre programme, materially above the current Rs 1.3-1.5 lakh crore annual run-rate.
- A sustained downturn in O2C margins reducing consolidated PBDIT below about Rs 1.75 lakh crore.
- An adverse outcome on the KG D6 demand of a size that weakens liquidity materially.

# **About the Company**

RIL, incorporated in 1973 and promoted by Mukesh D. Ambani (Chairman and Managing Director), reported value of sales and services of Rs 11,75,919 crore (US$ 124.0 billion) in fiscal 2026. It operates an integrated refining and petrochemicals complex (O2C), oil and gas production (KG D6 and coal-bed methane), the Jio digital services business through Jio Platforms Limited (66.43%), retail through Reliance Retail Ventures Limited (83.56%), media through Jiostar India Private Limited (63.16%) and a new-energy business. The promoter group held 49.11% at March 31, 2026. For fiscal 2026 RIL reported consolidated revenue from operations of Rs 10,75,675 crore and profit of Rs 95,754 crore.

For the first quarter of fiscal 2027, revenue was Rs 3,40,257 crore and profit Rs 23,196 crore, against Rs 2,73,252 crore and Rs 21,859 crore a year earlier (the earlier quarter's profit excludes the one-time gain).
