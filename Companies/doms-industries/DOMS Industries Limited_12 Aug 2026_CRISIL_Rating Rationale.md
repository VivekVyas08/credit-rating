### **Detailed Rationale**

This independent assessment arrives at **'Crisil AA-/Stable'** for the long-term bank facilities of DOMS Industries Limited (DOMS).

The rating reflects DOMS's leading position in India's branded stationery and art-materials market, an exceptionally deep distribution network, and a very strong financial risk profile marked by minimal leverage and high debt-protection metrics. Consolidated revenue grew 21.6% to Rs 2,326 crore in fiscal 2026 (from Rs 1,913 crore), extending a 23% two-year compound growth rate, while the balance sheet strengthened materially: gearing improved to **0.11 time** (from 0.20 time), total debt including lease liabilities fell to Rs 141 crore, total outside liabilities to tangible networth improved to 0.31 time, and interest coverage rose to **37.3 times**. Return on capital employed held at 21.8% through a heavy investment year.

These strengths are set against a **clear and recent deterioration in operating profitability**. Operating margin has turned down over three consecutive periods — 18.2% in fiscal 2025, 17.3% in fiscal 2026, and **12.1-12.3% in the quarter ended June 30, 2026** — a fall of roughly 525-530 basis points year-on-year in the latest quarter. Revenue in that quarter grew 19.2% to Rs 671 crore while profit after tax **declined 23.4%** to Rs 45 crore. The compression is driven by sharp raw-material inflation that the company has chosen not to pass through in full, prioritising volume and market share; gross margin fell about 400 basis points to 38.2%. A structural element also exists: the faster-growing hygiene business (subsidiary Uniclan Healthcare Private Limited, 8.7% of fiscal 2026 revenue, up from 5.9%) earns a materially lower margin than the stationery core — 6.7% against 13.4% at the latest quarter — so mix shift dilutes group profitability even before input costs.

Crucially, **the deterioration is an earnings-quality issue, not a solvency one**. Holding the June-quarter margin flat across a full year would still imply operating profit of roughly Rs 330 crore against finance costs of Rs 11 crore — coverage near 30 times, with net debt to operating profit inside 0.5 time. The capital structure has ample capacity to absorb a multi-year margin reset without stress on debt service.

The rating also factors in a materially thinner liquidity cushion than the leverage ratios alone suggest. Cash and bank deposits fell from Rs 306 crore (March 2024) to Rs 225 crore (March 2025) to **Rs 62 crore (March 2026)** — an 80% reduction over two years — as internal resources funded capacity expansion (capital work-in-progress rose to Rs 162 crore) and successive acquisitions, moving the group from a net cash position to modest net debt of Rs 79 crore. Annual cash accrual of around Rs 328 crore remains several times the identified near-term claims, so the position is comfortable; but the buffer is no longer the outsized one it was.

In **June 2026** the company signed an Asset Purchase Agreement to acquire worldwide, perpetual ownership of the **Reynolds** brand and related assets — intellectual property, business assets, employees, contracts and associated liabilities across pens, markers, highlighters and school supplies — from Reynolds Pens India Private Limited and its global affiliates. The consideration is **not disclosed** in any document reviewed for this assessment. The transaction is strategically significant, adding an established writing-instruments brand aimed at young adults and professionals alongside the school-focused DOMS portfolio, but both its funding and the liabilities assumed are unquantified here and are named as a monitorable rather than modelled.

# **Key Rating Drivers — Strengths**

**Leading position in branded stationery underpinned by distribution depth that is difficult to replicate:** DOMS reaches the market through more than 130 super stockists, over 6,250 distributors, **more than 145,000 retail outlets** and a sales force exceeding 1,000, with the hygiene arm adding a further 95+ super stockists, 1,300+ distributors and 55,000+ outlets. In a category sold through fragmented general trade to price-sensitive consumers, this reach is the durable competitive asset — it is what allows new SKUs (the count rose by roughly 400 in the past year) and new brands to be monetised quickly. Revenue compounded 23% over fiscals 2024-2026, ahead of category growth, and the company has repeatedly used the network to absorb acquired businesses.

**Very strong capital structure with substantial headroom:** total debt including lease liabilities stood at Rs 141 crore as on March 31, 2026 against a networth of Rs 1,302 crore — gearing of 0.11 time, improved from 0.20 time a year earlier, with total outside liabilities to tangible networth of 0.31 time. Non-current borrowings were reduced to Rs 38 crore from Rs 106 crore during the year. Total debt to operating profit was 0.35 time and interest coverage 37.3 times. Return on capital employed of 21.8% has been stable across three years despite a rising asset base, indicating the growth capital deployed is earning its cost.

**Consistent, disciplined portfolio expansion:** the group has integrated six acquisitions across adjacent categories — paper stationery (Pioneer Stationery Private Limited, stake raised from 51% to 64% during fiscal 2026; Super Treads Private Limited, acquired June 1, 2025), packaging (Micro Wood Private Limited), school bags (Skido Industries Private Limited), toys (Clapjoy Innovations Private Limited, 30% associate) and baby hygiene (Uniclan Healthcare Private Limited) — while keeping leverage minimal throughout. Uniclan commissioned a third diaper line during fiscal 2026, taking capacity to about 65 crore pieces annually, alongside a new wet-wipes line.

# **Key Rating Drivers — Weaknesses**

**Sharp and continuing compression in operating profitability:** operating margin fell from 18.2% (fiscal 2025) to 17.3% (fiscal 2026) and then to 12.1-12.3% in the June 2026 quarter, with gross margin down about 400 basis points to 38.2%. Raw-material inflation is the proximate cause and the company's stated response is to protect market share rather than margin — a defensible commercial choice that nonetheless defers recovery to the pace of price revision and input-cost normalisation. Profit after tax fell 23.4% year-on-year in the June quarter despite 19.2% revenue growth. Sustained failure to recover toward mid-teens margins would erode the cash generation on which the current rating headroom depends.

**Margin-dilutive mix shift as hygiene scales:** the hygiene business grew to 8.7% of consolidated revenue in fiscal 2026 from 5.9%, and grew 23.7% year-on-year in the June quarter — faster than the stationery core. At a 6.7% operating margin against stationery's 13.4%, continued outperformance by hygiene mechanically lowers blended group profitability. The company itself attributes part of the fiscal 2026 margin moderation to Uniclan's higher contribution.

**Reduced liquidity buffer alongside an unquantified acquisition and continuing capital expenditure:** cash and bank deposits fell 80% over two years to Rs 62 crore as of March 31, 2026, while capital commitments stood at Rs 94 crore and capital work-in-progress at Rs 162 crore. The Reynolds acquisition signed in June 2026 carries an undisclosed consideration and assumed liabilities. Cash accrual remains ample relative to identified claims, but the combination of a spent buffer, a live capital programme and an unpriced acquisition reduces the margin for error relative to two years ago.

# **Liquidity: Adequate**

Cash and bank deposits stood at Rs 62 crore as on March 31, 2026, against gross cash accrual of about Rs 328 crore in fiscal 2026. Identified near-term claims — capital commitments of Rs 94 crore, non-current borrowing repayment of Rs 38 crore and current lease obligations of Rs 15 crore — are covered several times over by accrual, and gearing of 0.11 time leaves very substantial untapped debt capacity at a listed entity with a market capitalisation of roughly Rs 13,700 crore. The descriptor is nonetheless held at Adequate rather than Strong for three evidenced reasons: the on-balance-sheet liquid buffer has fallen 80% in two years; the Reynolds consideration is undisclosed and cannot be set against these sources; and **average bank-limit utilisation — CRISIL's own named liquidity test — is not available in the documents reviewed** and would be required for a complete assessment.

# **ESG Profile**

The ESG profile of DOMS does not constrain its credit risk profile.

The stationery and writing-instruments sector has a **moderate environmental impact** — wood, polymer, pigment and paper inputs, with associated resource-sourcing, emissions and waste considerations across manufacturing and packaging — while the baby-hygiene business (disposable diapers and wet wipes) carries **higher product-lifecycle and waste intensity**. **Social impact is moderate**, reflecting a manufacturing workforce and a product base sold substantially to children and schools, where product-safety standards are a direct commercial as well as ethical requirement.

**ESG highlights:**

- **Renewable energy:** the company operates a **1 MW solar plant at its Umbergaon facility** and states an intention to expand captive solar capacity in coming years.
- **Emissions disclosure:** DOMS reports under the **Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report (BRSR)** framework, disclosing Scope 1 and Scope 2 GHG emissions in absolute terms and as intensity per rupee of turnover (including a PPP-adjusted intensity measure), with the company noting that emission and waste metrics depend on emission factors and engineering estimates.
- **Governance:** the company is professionally governed with an independent-director-chaired audit committee and a Big-4-network statutory auditor (Price Waterhouse Chartered Accountants LLP) issuing an **unqualified opinion with a single standard key audit matter** (timing of revenue recognition). IPO net proceeds of Rs 333 crore were **fully utilised in accordance with the stated objects**, with no deviation reported — a positive disclosure-discipline signal for a recently listed issuer.

**Disclosed limitation:** quantified ESG targets (net-zero commitment year, Scope 1/2/3 reduction percentages, LTIFR, water and gender-diversity metrics) were **not extractable from the documents supplied** at the granularity CRISIL's format contemplates. The assessment above is therefore based on disclosed practice rather than on a full target-and-progress dataset, and is stated as such rather than inferred.

*Source: DOMS Industries Limited Annual Report FY2025-26 (BRSR and governance sections) and the Q1 FY2027 results filing.*

# **Outlook: Stable**

DOMS is expected to sustain high-teens revenue growth on distribution depth, new capacity commissioning from the second quarter of fiscal 2027, and the addition of the Reynolds portfolio, while its minimal leverage absorbs the current margin pressure without strain on debt servicing. The outlook assumes progressive margin recovery as calibrated price revisions take effect and input costs stabilise.

# **Rating sensitivity factors**

**Upward factors**
- Operating margin recovering to and sustaining above 16-17% while maintaining high-teens revenue growth
- Successful integration of the Reynolds portfolio without material leverage increase, with net debt to operating profit sustained below 0.5 time and the liquidity buffer rebuilt

**Downward factors**
- Operating margin sustaining below 13-14% beyond fiscal 2027, materially reducing cash accrual
- Debt-funded acquisition or capital expenditure taking gearing above 0.5 time or net debt to operating profit above 1.5 times
- A material stretch in working capital, or an unfavourable outcome or funding structure on the Reynolds transaction

# **About the Company**

Incorporated in 2006 and headquartered at Umbergaon, Gujarat, DOMS Industries Limited manufactures and markets branded stationery and art materials — pencils, pens, mathematical instruments, scholastic art products and school supplies — under the DOMS brand, alongside a baby-hygiene business through subsidiary Uniclan Healthcare Private Limited. The company listed on BSE and NSE on December 20, 2023; net IPO proceeds of Rs 333 crore stand fully utilised as at June 30, 2026 in accordance with the stated objects. F.I.L.A. — Fabbrica Italiana Lapis ed Affini S.p.A. of Italy holds 26.01% within the promoter group.
