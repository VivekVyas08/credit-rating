### **Entity note (read first)**

This is a new rating engagement. Oil India Limited (OIL) is a **Maharatna Central Public Sector Enterprise**, **56.66%-owned by the Government of India** (unchanged FY2025→FY2026), with the balance held by public shareholders including ~10% cross-held by Indian Oil Corporation, Bharat Petroleum and Hindustan Petroleum. OIL's material subsidiary, **Numaligarh Refinery Limited (NRL, 69.63%-owned)**, is midway through a large refinery-capacity expansion that is the dominant driver of the consolidated (though not the standalone) financial risk profile — see Key Rating Drivers.

### **Detailed Rationale**

This independent assessment arrives at **'Crisil AAA/Stable'** for Oil India Limited's long-term facilities, with a short-term rating of **'Crisil A1+'**.

The rating rests on three structural anchors: a **standalone balance sheet carrying negligible leverage** (gearing 0.30 time, interest coverage 9.06 times, standalone FY2026), a **government linkage that is both a policy-role and moral-obligation factor** (56.66% GoI ownership, Maharatna status, and international ratings — Moody's Baa3/Fitch BBB-Stable — that the company's own annual report states are "at par with India's sovereign rating"), and an **improving reserve-replacement position** (Reserve Replacement Ratio 1.02 in fiscal 2026 against 0.94 the year before, back above the unity threshold CRISIL's own oil-and-gas criteria treat as the key indicator of sustainable resource replenishment).

Consolidated revenue was broadly flat over the review period — Rs 41,026 crore (fiscal 2023) to Rs 37,050 crore (fiscal 2026) — while **consolidated PBDIT margin compressed every year, from 38.97% to 33.41%**, and **consolidated gearing rose from 0.49 time to 0.65 time**. Both movements trace to a single, well-disclosed cause rather than to a deterioration in the core upstream business: Numaligarh Refinery's capacity-expansion project, whose SBI-led consortium financing has been enhanced twice within the review period — most recently to **Rs 21,678 crore plus a Rs 4,113 crore standby facility** (September 2025) — with repayment beginning only in **44 quarterly instalments from 30 September 2029**. Consolidated capital-work-in-progress nearly tripled over the same period (Rs 11,953 crore to Rs 37,644 crore). The parent's own standalone balance sheet did not participate in this leverage build: standalone gearing actually fell slightly, from 0.33 time to 0.30 time.

**The central question for this rating is therefore not whether OIL's upstream business remains investment-grade — its standalone metrics answer that comfortably — but whether the NRL project's financing structure insulates the parent from execution risk at the subsidiary.** Three features support insulation: the facility is co-funded by government-linked lenders (Rs 2,200 crore drawn from the Oil Industry Development Board, a further Rs 5,482 crore tied up with the National Bank for Financing Infrastructure and Development), it carries its own project-style repayment schedule with a multi-year construction moratorium rather than relying on near-term parent cash flow, and NRL is 69.63%-owned rather than a wholly captive vehicle. Against this, the facility has already been rescoped and enhanced once within the review period (from Rs 15,504 crore in May 2025 to Rs 21,678 crore plus standby in September 2025) — a demonstrated pattern of scope growth that is the primary forward monitorable for this rating, not a historical footnote.

A quantitative anchor computed for this case (screening scorecard, see the workpaper) reads one notch lower, at 'Crisil AA+'. That reading is driven almost entirely by the same consolidated leverage metrics discussed above, which the scorecard cannot decompose by entity or by financing structure; it is recorded and explained in the workpaper (§10a) rather than silently overridden — the calibration's own back-testing documentation states it systematically under-reads issuers at the AA+/AAA boundary absent a fuller support-notch and qualitative assessment than a screening tool can apply.

**The 'Stable' outlook reflects this analysis's view that OIL's standalone credit strength, government linkage and improving reserve position are sufficient to absorb the NRL project's construction-phase execution risk at its current, disclosed scope** — with any further scope or cost enhancement at NRL, or a stall in the reserve-replacement improvement, treated as the events most likely to pressure the rating from here.

# **Analytical Approach**

Assessment is on **both standalone and consolidated bases**; consolidated figures are used for the headline ratios and the rating view, consistent with CRISIL's own reference reports for integrated oil and gas issuers in this corpus (Indian Oil Corporation, Bharat Petroleum).

**Methodology.** CRISIL Ratings methodology for manufacturing and corporate service sector companies — Section I (general approach), **Section IV (financial ratios)**, and **Section XII (oil and gas sector — upstream E&P)**; `basics-of-ratings`; `criteria-for-consolidation`; `criteria-for-factoring-parent-group-government-linkages`. No dedicated CRISIL Oil & Gas E&P criteria paper exists — confirmed against `Knowledge Base/02_Sector_Routing_Map.md`, which routes E&P/refining to the manufacturing master's Section XII, exactly as applied here.

**Government linkage — assessed, notch applied conservatively.** Under CRISIL's four-category GRE framework, OIL sits closer to Category II/III (limited-to-moderate policy exclusivity — the E&P sector permits private and foreign participation under HELP, unlike a pure-monopoly utility — but with material moral-obligation weight from GoI's 56.66% stake, Maharatna status, and the energy-security framing repeated throughout the company's own disclosures). A single conservative support notch is applied in the quantitative screening exercise; the qualitative rating view above does not additionally uplift beyond what the standalone metrics and international-agency corroboration already support.

**Parent/subsidiary treatment for NRL.** NRL's debt is fully consolidated (69.63% ownership, control per Ind AS 110); it is **not** treated as a contingent or off-balance-sheet exposure, and is analysed as a driver of the consolidated financial risk profile rather than notched separately, consistent with `criteria-for-consolidation`.

# **Key Rating Drivers — Strengths**

**Government-linked upstream PSU with a sovereign-equivalent international credit profile:** Government of India holds 56.66% (unchanged year-on-year), OIL carries Maharatna CPSE status, and its Moody's (Baa3, Stable) and Fitch (BBB-, Stable) ratings are stated by the company itself to be "at par with India's sovereign rating" — a corroboration this analysis treats as meaningful precisely because it comes from agencies with no domestic-scale bias.

**Materially unlevered standalone balance sheet:** standalone gearing of 0.30 time and interest coverage of 9.06 times (fiscal 2026), essentially flat to modestly improving over the review period — the parent entity's own credit profile has not absorbed the subsidiary's expansion financing.

**Improving reserve-replacement position, the sector's defining metric:** Reserve Replacement Ratio rose to 1.02 in fiscal 2026 from 0.94 the year before — above the unity threshold that CRISIL's own oil-and-gas criteria identify as the central determinant of sustainable E&P credit quality — supported by a record 74 wells drilled (22 exploratory, 52 development) and 6.232 MMTOE of 2P reserves added during the year. 2P reserves stood at 68.68 MMT of crude oil and 50.58 BCM of natural gas as at 31 March 2026.

**Operational execution track record:** terminal crude-oil production rate of 10,566 MTPD as at fiscal year-end 2026 — the highest in fourteen years — against natural decline in mature Assam fields and operational disruptions during the year, evidencing an ability to execute through operating headwinds.

# **Key Rating Drivers — Weaknesses**

**Rising consolidated leverage concentrated in a single, still-incomplete capital project:** consolidated gearing rose from 0.49 time (fiscal 2023) to 0.65 time (fiscal 2026) and consolidated PBDIT interest coverage nearly halved, from 17.75 times to 10.28 times, driven by NRL's refinery-expansion financing — a Rs 21,678 crore consortium facility plus a Rs 4,113 crore standby, enhanced twice within the review period, repayable only from 30 September 2029. **The facility's demonstrated pattern of scope enhancement (two upward revisions in under a year) is the primary forward risk**, since a further enhancement would extend both the leverage build and the timeline before deleveraging begins.

**Consolidated PBDIT margin has compressed in every year of the review period** — 38.97% to 33.41% — against broadly flat consolidated revenue, reflecting the cost structure of the expanding refining/midstream segment layering onto the upstream business, alongside a recurring GST-on-royalty provisioning cost (Rs 865.12 crore in fiscal 2026) embedded in operating expenses. This is a multi-year direction, not a single-year dip, and its trajectory into fiscal 2027 is a monitorable in its own right.

**Contingent liabilities and litigation, quantified against the balance sheet's own cushion:** standalone claims not acknowledged as debt of Rs 4,150.44 crore (fiscal 2026) — the largest single item, Rs 2,484.81 crore, relates to a demand under the Assam Taxation (on Specified Lands) Amendment Act, 2004, partly covered by a Rs 702.02 crore bank guarantee — plus Rs 4,028.06 crore of guarantees, predominantly regulatory/operational in nature (Minimum Work Programme, OALP and DSF block commitments) rather than third-party credit support. Combined, these represent roughly 17% of standalone net worth of Rs 48,526 crore — assessed as manageable given the largely operational, non-financial character of the guarantee book, but the Assam Taxation matter in particular remains an open, uncapped exposure.

# **Liquidity: Strong**

**Sources (standalone, ~12 months):** cash, bank balances and current investments of Rs 3,693 crore (fiscal 2026); net cash generated from operating activities of Rs 7,576 crore (fiscal 2026); an undrawn Rs 4,113 crore standby facility at the NRL project level not yet called upon as at the annual-report date.

**Claims:** current maturities of long-term debt of approximately Rs 474 crore (standalone, fiscal 2026, embedded within current borrowings), largely undrawn working-capital facilities, and standalone capital commitments of Rs 461.24 crore — the NRL expansion capex itself is funded through the subsidiary's own dedicated project facility and does not draw on OIL's standalone liquidity.

The parent's operating cash flow alone covers its near-term maturities several times over, and standalone gearing leaves substantial headroom to raise further debt if required. This descriptor applies to the standalone entity; construction-phase risk at NRL is assessed separately as a leverage and execution factor, not a parent liquidity item, consistent with the facility's non-recourse, project-financing structure as disclosed.

# **ESG Profile**

The ESG profile of Oil India Limited supports its credit risk profile, and does not constrain the rating at its current level.

Upstream exploration and production carries inherent environmental exposure — land use, produced-water management, methane and flaring emissions, and well-integrity/blowout risk — alongside social exposure tied to a large regional (predominantly Assam-based) workforce and community footprint. OIL's own disclosures show active mitigation of both.

**ESG highlights:**
- **Climate target:** Net Zero by 2040; GHG emissions of 1.190 million tCO2e in fiscal 2026, down 17.93% against the fiscal 2024 baseline.
- **Flaring reduction:** gas flaring fell 32%, from 82 MMSCM (fiscal 2025) to 56 MMSCM (fiscal 2026), attributed to augmented in-field pipeline networks and improved gas utilisation.
- **Renewable/decarbonisation:** captive solar power consumption up 45%; methane-abatement technology (AUSEA) deployed across 24 installations; a dedicated wholly-owned subsidiary (OIL Green Energy Limited, incorporated during fiscal 2026) leads new-energy investment, with approximately Rs 20,000 crore of planned energy-transition and net-zero investment through 2040.
- **Third-party recognition:** S&P Global ESG score more than doubled, from 22 to 46.
- **Governance:** the standalone and consolidated financial statements each carry an unmodified audit opinion with Key Audit Matters limited to valuation judgements (investment valuation, contingent-liability estimation) rather than any qualification or Emphasis of Matter drawing attention to a going-concern or fraud-related item.

ESG considerations carry increasing weight for a government-linked energy issuer's funding access, both domestically and through its international-agency ratings; OIL's improving disclosure trajectory and its explicit 2040 net-zero commitment support continued access to both bank and capital-market funding on favourable terms.

# **Outlook: Stable**

The Stable outlook reflects this analysis's opinion that Oil India Limited's standalone financial strength, government linkage, and improving reserve-replacement position are sufficient to absorb the construction-phase execution risk of the Numaligarh Refinery expansion at its current, disclosed scope, without near-term pressure on the rating.

# **Rating sensitivity factors**

**Positive factors** — Not applicable at the AAA level.

**Negative factors** — Downward pressure could arise **on a sustained basis** from: (a) a further scope or cost enhancement of the NRL expansion facility beyond the Rs 21,678 crore plus Rs 4,113 crore standby currently sanctioned, extending the deleveraging timeline; (b) consolidated gearing sustained materially above the current 0.65 time or interest coverage falling below 8 times; (c) Reserve Replacement Ratio falling back below 1.0 time for two consecutive years, reversing the fiscal 2026 improvement; (d) any dilution of the Government of India's ownership or a change in GoI's stated posture toward the entity's policy role.

# **About the Company**

Oil India Limited, incorporated in 1959 and headquartered in Duliajan, Assam, is India's second-largest national oil company engaged in exploration, development and production of crude oil and natural gas, with midstream (crude oil and product pipelines) and, through its 69.63%-owned subsidiary Numaligarh Refinery Limited, downstream refining operations. The Government of India held 56.66% of the company's paid-up equity as at 31 March 2026 (unchanged from 31 March 2025); the balance is held by public shareholders, including approximately 10% cross-held by Indian Oil Corporation, Bharat Petroleum and Hindustan Petroleum. The company reported consolidated total income of Rs 38,981 crore and standalone total income of Rs 24,039 crore for fiscal 2026.
