## **Rationale and key rating drivers**

CARE Ratings Limited (CareEdge Ratings) has assigned ratings of 'CARE BBB; Stable / CARE A3+' to the bank facilities of Cochin Minerals and Rutile Limited (CMRL).

The ratings derive their principal strength from **a balance sheet that is effectively unlevered**. Total debt was **₹6.21 crore** at 31 March 2026 against a tangible net worth of **₹172.03 crore**, giving overall gearing of **0.04 times**; long-term borrowings have been **nil since FY2025**. Against that debt the Company held **₹38.17 crore of unencumbered cash**, leaving it in a **net cash position of ₹31.95 crore**. TOL/TNW was 0.44 times and the current ratio 2.93 times. On any conventional measure the capacity to service the rated facilities is not in question.

That strength is set against **a materially weaker business profile than the balance sheet alone suggests, and FY2026 was a difficult year**. Total operating income fell **7.6% to ₹294.92 crore**, and the PBILDT margin compressed for a second consecutive year to **7.96%, from 9.67% in FY2025 and 12.61% in FY2024**. Profit after tax **halved to ₹12.51 crore** and return on capital employed fell to **12.28% from 24.02%** over two years. The cause is disclosed by the Company itself and is structural rather than cyclical: CMRL manufactures **synthetic rutile by upgrading ilmenite**, and states that domestic ilmenite availability has "declined significantly", obliging it to rely increasingly on **imported** ilmenite and to modify its process to run different imported grades. It further states that synthetic rutile prices fell **without a proportional reduction in ilmenite** — a squeeze from both ends of the conversion spread.

The clearest evidence of that constraint is the FY2026 **exceptional item of ₹5.05 crore**, being the impairment of capital work-in-progress on the **metallisation project**, which the Company records as "long suspended due to short supply of main raw material, ilmenite from the domestic market" and from which it expects no future economic benefit. **The Company could not source its way into growth, and wrote off the attempt.** That is a more informative fact about this credit than any leverage ratio.

**The trough appears to have passed.** In the quarter ended **30 June 2026**, revenue rose **72.2% year on year to ₹131.26 crore** (Q1 FY2026: ₹76.21 crore) and profit before exceptional items rose **231% to ₹16.80 crore** (Q1 FY2026: ₹5.07 crore). Profit after tax of **₹12.38 crore in that single quarter** is within 1% of the **entire** FY2026 figure of ₹12.51 crore, and the implied PBILDT margin of about **11.6%** is well above the 7.96% full-year print. One unaudited quarter is not a run-rate and is not seasonally adjusted, so no ratio in this report is struck on it — but it is the newest evidence available and it points one way.

The ratings are constrained by **scale and concentration**: ₹294.92 crore of revenue from a single product at a single site, with no segmental, geographic or product diversification of the kind CareEdge's manufacturing criteria treat as explicit business-risk factors.

## **Rating sensitivities: Factors likely to lead to rating actions**

### **Positive factors**

- PBILDT margin sustained above **11%** with revenue sustained above **₹400 crore**, demonstrating that the Q1 FY2027 recovery is durable rather than a restocking effect.
- A demonstrated, contracted **security of ilmenite supply** — long-term supply arrangements or backward integration — that removes the constraint which caused the metallisation write-off.

### **Negative factors**

- PBILDT margin falling below **6%** on a sustained basis, or a return to the FY2026 revenue level.
- Overall gearing rising above **0.50 times**, or erosion of the net cash position, whether through debt-funded capital expenditure or a sustained working-capital build.
- Any interruption to ilmenite availability, domestic or imported, that curtails capacity utilisation.

## **Analytical approach:** Standalone

CMRL is assessed on a **standalone** basis. This is not an election: the Company's corporate-governance report states that **"The Company has no subsidiary"**, so there is no entity to consolidate. Annexure-6 is accordingly Not Applicable.

**Business risk bounds the rating; it is not averaged into the financial score (failure mode
53).** CareEdge's manufacturing criteria band the Financial Risk factors numerically and assess
the Business Risk factors — scale, customer and geographic concentration, product diversification
— in descriptive language only, with no formula converting them into notches. This exercise's
scorecard originally averaged a business-risk score against the financial ratios, and the result
was that an effectively unlevered balance sheet (gearing 0.04 times, interest coverage 121.75
times) carried the screening anchor to **AA-**, five notches above where the business profile can
support it. Business risk is therefore applied as a **ceiling**: on scale (total operating income
₹294.92 crore), single-product and single-site concentration, customer concentration, and the
Company's own disclosure of declining domestic ilmenite availability forcing import dependence,
the achievable grade is capped at **BBB** regardless of the balance sheet. The ceiling and its
reasoning are recorded on the face of the scorecard at `Inputs!C69`/`D69` and bind the anchor at
`Rating!C15`; the assigned rating is reached with **no committee overlay notches at all**.

## **Outlook: Stable**

The Stable outlook balances an effectively unlevered balance sheet and a visible earnings recovery in Q1 FY2027 against an unresolved raw-material constraint and a small, undiversified operating base. It reflects the expectation that CMRL will sustain a net cash position and fund its maintenance capital expenditure from internal accruals.

## **Detailed description of key rating drivers:**

### **Key strengths**

**Effectively unlevered balance sheet with a net cash position** – Long-term borrowings have been nil since FY2025 and total debt was ₹6.21 crore at 31 March 2026 against a tangible net worth of ₹172.03 crore. Unencumbered cash of ₹38.17 crore leaves the Company in net cash of ₹31.95 crore. TOL/TNW was 0.44 times against 0.62 times two years earlier. Interest cover is arithmetically about 122 times, though on a balance sheet with this little debt that ratio measures the absence of borrowing rather than the capacity to service it, and it is not a discriminator in this rating.

**Established position in the titanium feedstock chain** – CMRL operates 50,000 MT of installed synthetic rutile capacity, upgrading ilmenite with hydrochloric acid to produce a feedstock for the titanium dioxide pigment industry, and has adapted its process to run multiple imported ilmenite grades while holding product quality.

**Comfortable liquidity and self-funded capital expenditure** – The current ratio was 2.93 times. Capital expenditure has been funded from internal accruals throughout, and the Company has no announced project requiring external funding.

### **Key weaknesses**

**Raw material availability is the binding constraint, and it is disclosed by the Company** – Domestic ilmenite supply has "declined significantly" on the Company's own account, forcing a shift to imports with the associated cost, logistics and geopolitical exposure. The FY2026 impairment of ₹5.05 crore on the long-suspended metallisation project — abandoned expressly for want of domestic ilmenite — is the concrete demonstration that this constraint has already cost the Company a growth avenue.

**Small scale and single-product concentration** – Revenue of ₹294.92 crore from one product at one site. There is no segmental or geographic diversification to absorb a shock to the single conversion spread on which earnings depend.

**Margin compression through FY2026, on an unfavourable price relationship** – The PBILDT margin fell to 7.96% from 12.61% two years earlier and PAT halved, because synthetic rutile realisations fell while ilmenite costs did not follow. The relationship between input and output prices is not within the Company's control.

## **Liquidity:** Strong

Liquidity is strong. **Unencumbered cash was ₹38.17 crore at 31 March 2026** — cash and cash equivalents of ₹14.14 crore plus deposits of 3 to 12 months' maturity of ₹24.03 crore. A further **₹5.57 crore is excluded** as encumbered: ₹5.07 crore of margin money pledged against bank guarantees and letters of credit, and ₹0.50 crore in the unpaid-dividend account, neither of which can be applied to debt service. Against that, total debt is ₹6.21 crore, all short-term, and gross cash accruals were ₹14.10 crore in FY2026 against maintenance capital expenditure of about ₹12 crore. The Company is in net cash, has no long-term repayment obligation, and funds capital expenditure internally. The descriptor reflects a genuine cash surplus rather than merely adequate cover.

## **Environment, social, and governance (ESG) risks**

**Environmental** – Synthetic rutile production is a chemical upgrading process using hydrochloric acid, generating iron chloride by-products and requiring effluent treatment; the Company reports on pollution-control compliance and by-product handling in its annual report. Reliance on imported ilmenite adds a transport-emissions element to the input chain.

**Social** – Operations are concentrated at a single site in Kerala, so community relations and workforce continuity at that location carry weight disproportionate to the Company's size. The annual report carries CSR disclosures under Rule 8 of the Companies (CSR) Rules, 2014, with CSR expense of ₹0.82 crore in FY2025.

**Governance** – The audit report on the FY2025-26 standalone financial statements carries **no qualification**. No governance qualification, related-party concern or auditor emphasis of matter was identified. The Company discloses no credit rating in any of its three annual reports, consistent with the absence of any prior CRA action.

## **About the company and industry**

Cochin Minerals and Rutile Limited is a listed company manufacturing **synthetic rutile**, produced by upgrading ilmenite to remove its iron content using hydrochloric acid, together with associated by-products. Installed synthetic rutile capacity is **50,000 MT**. Synthetic rutile is a feedstock for the titanium dioxide pigment industry. The Company has no subsidiary.

***Operating-profit normalisation — the treatment named (failure mode 36).*** *PBILDT is struck on revenue from operations, starting from **profit before exceptional items** and deducting **other income in full**. Consequently: (i) the **₹5.05 crore metallisation impairment is excluded by construction**, being an exceptional item — it is non-cash and non-recurring, and its significance to this rating is as evidence of the raw-material constraint, not as a charge against operating performance; (ii) other income of ₹2.27 crore in FY2026 is removed entirely, taking with it interest and dividend income and an investment subsidy of ₹0.15 crore; (iii) **forex:** a forex gain of ₹0.35 crore appears in the FY2026 notes — it sits within other income and is therefore already stripped, so the convention is not asymmetric in this case; (iv) **CSR expense** of ₹0.82 crore (FY2025) was a separate P&L line in the FY2024-25 annual report and is folded into other expenses in the FY2025-26 annual report; it is retained within PBILDT on both bases.*

***Restatements between annual-report vintages, disclosed (failure mode 26).*** *Three reclassifications affect the FY2025 comparative and the newer annual report governs throughout: CSR of ₹0.82 crore folded into other expenses; ₹0.15 crore moved from other income into revenue; and ₹2.14 crore removed from other current assets, matched by ₹1.07 crore of non-current and ₹1.07 crore of current liabilities. Each nets to zero, and total income and the balance-sheet total are unchanged on both vintages.*
