## Rationale

The rating reaffirmed for Vodafone Idea Limited (VIL) rests on a proposition that the company's own financial ratios do not support and that its shareholders' conduct does. On the numbers alone the credit is weak without ambiguity: **interest coverage of 0.88 times in FY2026**, meaning VIL did not earn its own interest bill; **total debt of Rs. 1,92,528 crore against OPBDIT of Rs. 19,003 crore, or 10.13 times**; and a **negative net worth of Rs. 35,758 crore**. All three sit beyond the weakest indicative band ICRA's Telecom Services methodology publishes. What changes the conclusion is the composition of that debt and the identity of the creditor: **Rs. 1,45,454 crore of it is owed to the Government of India, which is also VIL's largest shareholder at 49.00%, and of which Rs. 1,144 crore falls due over the first ten years.** Against a rated facility of Rs. 727 crore, VIL held **Rs. 3,608 crore of unencumbered cash and deposits** and generated **Rs. 19,411 crore of operating cash flow** in FY2026.

FY2026 was the year the existential question was answered. Following a reassessment directed by the Supreme Court, the DoT **finalised VIL's adjusted gross revenue (AGR) dues at Rs. 64,046 crore against a provisional Rs. 87,695 crore** — a reduction of 27% — and restructured the repayment so that a minimum of Rs. 100 crore a year falls due between March 2032 and March 2035, with Rs. 10,608 crore a year thereafter to March 2041. Under Ind AS 109 the carried liability of Rs. 80,502 crore was derecognised and re-recognised at a present value of Rs. 24,880 crore, producing a credit of **Rs. 55,622 crore** to the statement of profit and loss. Separately, the Government converted **Rs. 36,950 crore of spectrum auction dues into equity**, allotted on April 8, 2025, following an earlier conversion of Rs. 16,133 crore of deferred interest in February 2023. The consequence is visible in the auditor's report: the **Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern paragraph that qualified the FY2025 accounts does not appear in FY2026.**

The operating business improved through the year, and the mix improved faster than the headline. **Operating income rose to Rs. 44,873 crore from Rs. 43,572 crore**, with the OPBDIT margin widening for a third consecutive year to **42.35% from 41.60% and 40.15%**. The subscriber base nonetheless **fell to 192.8 million from 198.2 million** over the four quarters, and VLR subscribers to 169.3 million from 175.3 million. Underneath that decline, **4G/5G subscribers grew to 128.9 million from 126.4 million, postpaid subscribers by 17.6% to 30.1 million, customer ARPU to Rs. 190 from Rs. 175 — an increase the company reports as 8.3% on its unrounded figures — and data usage per 4G/5G subscriber by 27% to 20.2 GB.** VIL is shedding low-value voice-only subscribers and retaining data users, which is why revenue grew 2.9% year-on-year in Q4 FY2026 on a base that shrank 2.7%. The company states monthly subscriber additions turned positive from February 2026; one quarter is not a trend, and ICRA treats it as a monitorable rather than a base case.

Three things could go wrong, and they are of increasing severity. **First, the capex funding is not secured.** VIL plans **Rs. 45,000 crore of capital expenditure over FY2027-FY2029** to extend 4G coverage and roll out 5G, against FY2026 capex of Rs. 8,217 crore and FY2024 capex of just Rs. 1,614 crore — the years of under-investment being precisely what cost the company its subscribers. ICRA's projections put the external funding requirement at approximately **Rs. 36,500 crore over the three years**, and the company's own annual report states that discussions with lenders for long-term funding are continuing. **Second, the company does not yet cover its interest bill.** On ICRA's base case, interest coverage crosses 1.0 times in FY2027 and then stalls near 1.3 times through FY2031; total debt continues to *rise* in every projected year because capex exceeds cash generation, and leverage improves only because OPBDIT grows. In a stress case in which ARPU growth stalls after FY2027 and the subscriber base declines 2-3% annually, **interest coverage falls back below 1.0 times by FY2029** and total debt/OPBDIT deteriorates to 16.96 times. The margin for error is one bad year. **Third, and largest, the AGR obligation has not been forgiven — it has been deferred.** From FY2036 VIL must pay Rs. 10,608 crore a year for six years. Against ICRA's base-case FY2031 OPBDIT of Rs. 28,743 crore, a single instalment is 37% of operating profit, before interest. That obligation sits beyond any reasonable forecasting horizon, which is what makes it tolerable today and what makes the intervening decade's earnings build the decisive question.

ICRA expects operating income to grow in the mid-to-high single digits over FY2027-FY2028 and the OPBDIT margin to widen towards 44-45% as ARPU improvement drops through a high-operating-leverage cost base, with the subscriber base stabilising during FY2028. Total debt is expected to rise as the capex programme is funded, with total debt/OPBDIT remaining near 10 times through FY2029. The monitorables are the tie-up of the term debt for the capex programme, receipt of the contracted Contingent Liability Adjustment Mechanism (CLAM) inflows and the Aditya Birla Group (ABG) equity commitment, the durability of the subscriber turn, and any change in the Government's shareholding or in the policy stance underlying the telecom reforms package.

**The Stable outlook reflects ICRA's opinion that the AGR resolution and the equity conversions have removed the near-term threat to VIL's ability to service its rated obligations, while the unfunded capex programme, an interest cover that has only just reached parity, and the FY2036 repayment schedule together preclude an improvement in the credit profile over the outlook horizon.**

## Key rating drivers and their description

### Credit strengths

**Demonstrated and repeated extraordinary support from the Government of India, now the largest shareholder at 49.00%** – Support here is evidenced by conduct rather than asserted from ownership. The Government has intervened three times in three years, each time on a larger scale: **Rs. 16,133 crore of deferred interest converted to equity in February 2023**; **Rs. 36,950 crore of spectrum auction dues converted to equity, allotted April 8, 2025**; and in FY2026 the **reassessment of AGR dues from Rs. 87,695 crore to Rs. 64,046 crore with repayment deferred to FY2032-FY2041**. Together the two conversions removed Rs. 53,083 crore of claims from the balance sheet and are the reason negative net worth has narrowed from Rs. 1,04,167 crore in FY2024 to Rs. 35,758 crore. ICRA notes that VIL is not a Government company: the 49.00% holding is deliberately below majority, no management control has been taken, and no debt is guaranteed. The uplift reflects a track record, not a legal obligation.

**Committed promoter support from both partners, quantified and contracted** – The **Aditya Birla Group has committed US$500 million (approximately Rs. 4,730 crore)** through fully convertible instruments, and Mr. Kumar Mangalam Birla has been re-appointed Chairman of the Board. **Vodafone Group has settled the CLAM receivable of Rs. 6,394 crore**, under an amendment executed on December 31, 2025 providing for **Rs. 2,307 crore of cash over twelve months** with the balance secured on **328 crore earmarked equity shares** of VIL held by Vodafone Group entities over five years, valued at Rs. 3,529 crore at the amendment date. Lender confidence is separately evidenced: Vodafone Idea Telecom Infrastructure Limited (VITIL) **raised Rs. 3,300 crore through non-convertible debentures in December 2025**, before AGR clarity emerged.

**A large, spectrum-rich national franchise with an improving subscriber mix** – VIL operates across **all 22 service areas** with **8,030.4 MHz of spectrum, of which 8,012.8 MHz is liberalised**, including mid-band 5G in all 17 priority circles and mmWave in 16. 5G services have been extended to over 80 cities across the 17 priority circles, which contribute approximately 99% of revenue. The network comprises over 2,02,000 unique broadband towers after more than 17,300 additions during FY2026, over 3,50,000 km of optical fibre, and 4G population coverage of **86.3%, up from 82.7%**. Within a declining base, the paying core is growing: **4G/5G subscribers at 128.9 million, postpaid at 30.1 million (up 17.6%), customer ARPU at Rs. 190 from Rs. 175** and data usage at 20.2 GB per subscriber (up 27%).

**A covenant-driven reclassification cured during the year** – At March 31, 2025 the company had reclassified **Rs. 726 crore from non-current borrowings to current maturities of long-term debt**, while stating its belief that no acceleration of payment would follow. **At March 31, 2026 that reclassification is nil.** ICRA reads this alongside the removal of the going-concern paragraph as evidence that the lender-facing position normalised through FY2026 rather than merely being deferred.

**Operating profitability improving on high operating leverage** – OPBDIT rose to **Rs. 19,003 crore from Rs. 18,126 crore and Rs. 17,126 crore**, with the margin widening in each of the three years to **42.35%**. Quarterly EBITDA rose in each of the last three quarters of FY2026 to Rs. 4,889 crore. Operating cash flow more than doubled to **Rs. 19,411 crore from Rs. 9,290 crore**.

### Credit challenges

**Interest coverage below 1.0 times, and a negative net worth** – VIL's OPBDIT of Rs. 19,003 crore did not cover its finance cost of Rs. 21,495 crore in FY2026, giving **interest coverage of 0.88 times** — improved from 0.74 and 0.66 times in the two preceding years, but still short of parity. **Total debt/OPBDIT stands at 10.13 times** and **net worth is negative at Rs. 35,758 crore**. Against ICRA's Telecom Services methodology, whose weakest indicative bands are interest coverage below 2.5 times and debt-to-profit above 3.8 times, all of VIL's financial metrics fall outside the published range. Gearing and total outside liabilities/tangible net worth are **not meaningful** on a negative net worth and are reported as such rather than as negative multiples.

**A Rs. 45,000 crore capital expenditure programme whose funding is not yet committed** – The FY2027-FY2029 programme compares with FY2026 capex of Rs. 8,217 crore and FY2024 capex of Rs. 1,614 crore. ICRA's projections indicate an external funding requirement of approximately **Rs. 36,500 crore over the three years**; VIL has indicated a funding structure of approximately Rs. 25,000 crore of term debt and Rs. 10,000 crore of non-fund-based facilities alongside internal accruals and non-operating inflows *(this split is attributed to ICRA's published rating action of June 02, 2026; the issuer's own documents were searched for "25,000", "25000", "non-fund based facility" and "term debt" across the FY2025-26 annual report and all four quarterly presentations, and the split does not appear — the only "25,000" hit is an unrelated handset-insurance coverage limit)*. The annual report states discussions with lenders are continuing. **Execution here is the principal near-term risk**: the programme is what arrests the subscriber decline, and under-investment is what caused it.

**Continuing subscriber attrition against two better-capitalised competitors** – The base declined to **192.8 million from 198.2 million** and VLR subscribers to 169.3 million from 175.3 million over the four quarters of FY2026. VIL remains the third of three private operators. While the company reports that monthly additions turned positive from February 2026, a single quarter does not establish a trend, and the improvement in ARPU to date has come substantially from upgrading existing subscribers to 4G/5G — a pool that necessarily shrinks as the 4G/5G share passes two-thirds of the base.

**A deferred Government obligation of a size that dominates the balance sheet, with a repayment cliff in FY2036** – Deferred payment obligations of **Rs. 1,45,454 crore** — spectrum of Rs. 1,20,200 crore and AGR of Rs. 25,254 crore as carried — represent 76% of total debt. The relief obtained in FY2026 is a deferral, not a discharge: **Rs. 10,608 crore a year falls due from FY2036 to FY2041**, equal to 37% of ICRA's base-case FY2031 operating profit. VIL must build materially greater earnings power over the intervening decade, and the capex programme is the mechanism by which it proposes to do so.

**Contingent liabilities and disputed demands outside the AGR settlement** – Regulatory, tax and legal disputes disclosed as contingent liabilities stood at **Rs. 12,743 crore** at March 31, 2026, and VIL carries a further **Rs. 8,780 crore of provisions** relating to one-time spectrum charges above 6.2 MHz and spectrum usage charge demands after FY2019. Taxation, regulatory and litigation exposures, and borrowings, were both identified as Key Audit Matters.

## Environmental and social risks

**Environmental considerations** – Telecom networks are energy-intensive, and the principal environmental exposure is grid electricity consumed at tower and data-centre sites, with diesel back-up at unreliable-grid locations. Regulatory and cost pressure on both is rising. VIL's own Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report shows the direction improving on every disclosed measure: **Scope 1 emissions fell to 7,934.24 tCO2e from 8,537.88 tCO2e and Scope 2 emissions to 3,01,210.40 tCO2e from 3,36,218.27 tCO2e**, a combined reduction of 10.3%. **Total energy consumption fell to 18,19,108 GJ from 19,47,515 GJ**, while **energy from renewable sources rose to 1,85,127 GJ from 1,69,092 GJ, taking the renewable share to 10.2% from 8.7%.** The company reports solar-based Renewable Energy Technology generation at over 2,000 sites and has pursued network-equipment efficiency and site-level power management. ICRA assesses environmental risk as moderate and not currently a rating driver, noting that the Rs. 45,000 crore network programme will raise absolute consumption even as intensity falls.

**Social considerations** – The sector's social exposures are service continuity and quality for a 192.8 million subscriber base, data privacy and cybersecurity, tariff affordability in a price-sensitive market, and workforce safety across a distributed field network. On the company's own disclosures, the **Lost Time Injury Frequency Rate improved to 0.050 from 0.120 per million person-hours**, with **nil fatalities in both FY2026 and FY2025** and five total recordable work-related injuries. ICRA notes that affordability is a live commercial as well as social question for this issuer: the ARPU improvement on which the forward view depends must be achieved in one of the lowest-priced telecom markets globally, and the company's capacity to raise tariffs is constrained by its position as the third operator. On governance, the FY2026 audit opinion is unmodified on both bases and carries no material uncertainty relating to going concern, against a Material Uncertainty paragraph in the FY2025 report.

## Liquidity position: Adequate

Sources over the next twelve months comprise **unencumbered cash and cash equivalents of Rs. 2,106 crore and fixed deposits of three to twelve months of Rs. 1,502 crore — Rs. 3,608 crore in total, on the company's own stated basis**; FY2026 net cash from operating activities of **Rs. 19,411 crore**; the contracted CLAM inflow of **Rs. 2,307 crore**; and the ABG equity commitment of **Rs. 4,730 crore**.

Contractual claims over the same period comprise **bank and other debt repayment of Rs. 726 crore**, **deferred payment obligation instalments of Rs. 7,076 crore** scheduled by March 2027, and a **spectrum usage charge instalment of Rs. 124 crore** — Rs. 7,926 crore in total. Discretionary capital expenditure of approximately Rs. 15,000 crore sits above that and is not yet funded.

Unencumbered liquidity covers the FY2027 bank repayment **5.0 times**, and operating cash flow covers all contractual claims **2.4 times**. The descriptor is **Adequate** rather than Strong because the term debt for the capex programme is not committed, net worth is negative and interest coverage is below 1.0 times; and Adequate rather than Stretched because every contractual claim falling due within twelve months is covered by operating cash flow with substantial headroom, and the Government obligation that dominates the balance sheet is not payable in bulk until FY2036.

**ICRA excludes Rs. 3,625 crore of "bank balance other than cash and cash equivalents" from the liquidity figure**, of which the company discloses only Rs. 1,502 crore as fixed deposits of three to twelve months. The residual is not shown to be unencumbered and the group carries **Rs. 7,534 crore of non-fund-based facilities** — bank guarantees and letters of credit — against which margin would be held. Including it would overstate liquidity by 62%.

## Rating sensitivities

**Positive factors** – ICRA could upgrade the rating if VIL ties up the full funding for its capital expenditure programme and executes it such that the subscriber base returns to sustained growth, with **interest coverage improving above 1.5 times and total debt/OPBDIT below 8.0 times on a sustained basis**; or if the Government or the promoter groups provide support of a more formal character than has been extended to date, such as a guarantee of the rated facilities.

**Negative factors** – The rating could be downgraded if VIL is unable to raise the term debt required for its capital expenditure programme within FY2027; if subscriber attrition resumes or ARPU improvement stalls such that **interest coverage falls back below 1.0 times on a sustained basis**; if the contracted CLAM inflows or the ABG equity commitment are not received; if there is any reduction in the Government's 49.00% shareholding or a change in the policy stance underlying the telecom reforms package; or if the contingent liabilities of Rs. 12,743 crore crystallise to a material extent.

## About the company

Vodafone Idea Limited was formed by the merger of Vodafone India Limited into Idea Cellular Limited on August 31, 2018, and is a partnership between the Aditya Birla Group and Vodafone Group. The company offers voice and data services on 2G, 4G and 5G technologies across **all 22 service areas in India**, serving **192.8 million subscribers as on March 31, 2026**, of which approximately 67% are 4G/5G subscribers. It holds 8,030.4 MHz of spectrum, of which 8,012.8 MHz is liberalised, including mid-band 5G spectrum in 17 priority circles and mmWave spectrum in 16 circles. The network comprises over 2,02,000 unique broadband towers, presence at nearly 2,07,000 unique locations, and a portfolio of over 3,50,000 km of optical fibre cable. The company is listed on the National Stock Exchange and the BSE.

**As on March 31, 2026 the Government of India held 49.00% and the promoter groups 25.64%**, following the allotment on April 8, 2025 of 3,695 crore equity shares at Rs. 10 each to the Department of Investment and Public Asset Management in discharge of Rs. 36,950 crore of spectrum auction dues. Issued and paid-up capital stands at 10,834.30 crore equity shares of Rs. 10 each. VIL has not paid a dividend since FY2015-16.
