## Rationale

The rating assigned to Balkrishna Industries Limited (BKT) rests on three things the company's own financial statements demonstrate rather than assert: a specialised export franchise that earns a materially above-average operating margin, a balance sheet that has absorbed a very large capital programme without leverage moving, and cash generation sufficient to fund most of that programme internally.

The margin is the starting point. OPBDITA margin was 23.93% in FY2026, against 23.52% in FY2025 and 24.07% in FY2024 — a band of roughly 23.5–24.1% held across three years, on operating income that grew from Rs. 9,368.87 crore to Rs. 10,823.08 crore over the same period. For a manufacturer selling a physical, freight-heavy, commodity-input product, sustaining a margin in that range for three consecutive years is itself the evidence of pricing power; it is not a claim requiring separate proof.

The leverage record is the second anchor, and it is unusually clean. Gearing was 0.44 times in FY2023, 0.35 in FY2024, 0.31 in FY2025 and 0.38 in FY2026 — a 13-basis-point-wide band in a period during which the company spent Rs. 3,003.29 crore on fixed assets in FY2026 alone (consolidated cash-flow statement; the MD&A states Rs. 2,984 crore for the same year on its own capitalisation basis, a Rs. 19 crore presentation difference, not a discrepancy of substance). Total debt rose only Rs. 843.37 crore across FY2026, to Rs. 4,110.82 crore from Rs. 3,267.45 crore, because operating cash flow of Rs. 2,249.32 crore — itself up 27.5% year-on-year — funded roughly three-quarters of the year's capital spend. TOL/TNW peaked at 0.62 times and interest coverage was 19.39 times, having not fallen below 19 times in any of the last three years.

**A basis point that governs how FY2026 must be read.** Reported profit after tax fell 24.9% in FY2026, to Rs. 1,243.10 crore from Rs. 1,654.96 crore. That fall is almost entirely non-operating. BKT recorded a net foreign-currency **loss of Rs. 290.01 crore in FY2026** against a net **gain of Rs. 267.36 crore in FY2025** — an adverse pre-tax swing of Rs. 557.37 crore, which by itself exceeds the Rs. 411.86 crore decline in PAT. Critically, the company reports this item **inside Other Income when it is a gain and inside Other Expenses when it is a loss**. The conventional operating-profit formula, which nets off other income, therefore strips the FY2025 gain while leaving the FY2026 loss embedded in costs — and manufactures a margin collapse out of an item that is not operating in either direction. Handled symmetrically, as it is here, FY2026 margin *rose* 41 basis points; handled asymmetrically it appears to *fall* 227 basis points. ICRA has added the Rs. 290.01 crore back for this reason, and states the treatment explicitly under Key financial indicators rather than leaving it implicit in a formula.

**What could go wrong.** The capital programme is the principal exposure. BKT has announced a cumulative outlay of Rs. 6,800 crore since August 2024, to be deployed through FY2028-29, directed at expanding off-highway tyre capacity to 4,25,000 MTPA, raising captive carbon black capacity to 3,60,000 MTPA, and entering India's on-highway tyre market with two-wheeler and commercial vehicle radial products (annual report, Strategic growth levers). Two of those three are genuinely new competences: BKT has not manufactured on-highway radial tyres at scale, and it will be entering a domestic market against incumbents with established distribution. The commitment is now substantially contracted rather than merely announced — capital commitments not yet paid nearly tripled to Rs. 2,711.84 crore in FY2026 from Rs. 923.98 crore — which reduces abandonment risk but equally reduces the company's freedom to slow spending if demand disappoints. The working-capital counterpart is already visible: current ratio fell to 1.01 times in FY2026 from 1.32 times, and to 0.97 times at 30-Jun-2026, while the company's own long-term-debt-to-working-capital ratio moved from 2.47 times to 7.22 times over the same quarter. Separately, currency remains a live earnings variable: FY2026 demonstrated that a single year's movement can shift pre-tax profit by an amount equal to about 5% of revenue, and the company both imports raw materials and carries part of its borrowing in foreign currency.

**What ICRA expects.** The most recent evidence available — Q1 FY2026-27, consolidated, quarter ended 30-Jun-2026 — is encouraging on both lines of the income statement. Revenue grew 25.2% year-on-year to Rs. 3,455.27 crore (Q1 FY2026: Rs. 2,760.02 crore) and PAT grew 56.4% to Rs. 450.77 crore. The company's own consistently-defined Reg 52(4) operating margin rose to 15.57% from 11.52% in the year-ago quarter and 15.04% in the immediately preceding quarter — a like-for-like comparison, since the company computes it identically in both filings. ICRA notes that the quarterly filing does not decompose Other Expenses, so the forex normalisation applied to the annual figures cannot be reproduced at quarterly frequency; the unadjusted quarterly OPBDITA margin of 21.54% is therefore not directly comparable to the annual 23.93%, and no conclusion is drawn from that gap in either direction. Management has confirmed the announced capital expenditure remains on schedule and within budget (Q1 FY2026-27 filing, note 4). ICRA's base case assumes revenue growth of 8.0% in FY2027 moving to 9–10.5% thereafter and an OPBDITA margin held broadly in the 22–23.5% range, with the mix shift toward lower-margin on-highway products offset over time by scale and by the widening carbon black contribution. On that path the funding-gap arithmetic in the accompanying five-year model brings the company to a net-debt-free position around FY2028. The monitorables are the ramp-up of the on-highway and carbon black capacity to commercial utilisation without margin dilution beyond what is assumed, whether the current ratio recovers as the programme completes, and the durability of export demand.

The Stable outlook reflects ICRA's opinion that BKT's capital structure and coverage indicators carry sufficient headroom to absorb both the remaining capital expenditure and a period of weaker export demand without migrating from the assigned rating, and that its established off-highway franchise will continue to generate the operating cash flow on which that headroom depends.

## Key rating drivers and their description

### Credit strengths

**Specialised global off-highway franchise with product breadth that a generalist tyre maker cannot easily replicate** – BKT sells a catalogue of over 3,600 specialised SKUs into more than 160 countries through its own network of subsidiaries, distributors and commercial partners (annual report). The relevant point for the rating is not the count but its economics: an off-highway catalogue of that width is expensive to build, is sold in low volumes per size, and is what allows the company to hold a 23.5–24.1% operating margin across FY2024–FY2026. The company operates a direct-sales model with less than 3% of volume routed through overseas warehouses, and maintains finished-goods inventory at a stated 12–15 days of production, giving an annualised inventory turnover above 24 times — an inventory discipline that materially limits the working-capital cost of serving a long-tail catalogue.

**Leverage held within a narrow band through the largest capital programme in the company's history** – Gearing has moved only between 0.31 and 0.44 times across FY2023–FY2026 (FY2023 0.44x; FY2024 0.35x; FY2025 0.31x; FY2026 0.38x), and TOL/TNW peaked at 0.62 times. Interest coverage was 19.98x, 19.15x and 19.39x in FY2024, FY2025 and FY2026 respectively, and Total debt/OPBDITA has stayed between 1.33 and 1.59 times. Net worth stood at Rs. 10,955.29 crore at 31-Mar-2026 and Rs. 11,583.84 crore at 30-Jun-2026 (company-disclosed, Reg 52(4)). These are the metrics of a company that has chosen to grow within its cash generation rather than ahead of it.

**Capital expenditure funded predominantly from internal accrual, with backward integration and logistics economics that are quantified rather than asserted** – FY2026 operating cash flow of Rs. 2,249.32 crore covered roughly three-quarters of the Rs. 3,003.29 crore spent on fixed assets, and total debt rose only Rs. 843.37 crore against that spend. Structurally, BKT manufactures its own carbon black — the principal filler in tyre compound — with capacity moving from 2,65,000 MTPA to 3,60,000 MTPA and a dedicated 64 MW captive power plant completed, which converts a bought-in commodity exposure into an internal cost. Logistics cost was 4.40% of revenue in FY2026, and export consolidation at the Bhuj facility, 95 km from Mundra Port, now covers approximately 70% of exports with a stated roadmap to 80%. These are disclosed, measurable operating advantages, not narrative ones.

### Credit challenges

**Earnings exposed to currency and to imported input costs, demonstrated at scale in FY2026** – The Rs. 290.01 crore net foreign-currency loss booked in FY2026, against a Rs. 267.36 crore gain the prior year, moved pre-tax profit by Rs. 557.37 crore across two years — close to 5% of a single year's revenue. The company carries a structural natural hedge, since the great majority of its revenue is earned in foreign currency, but it also imports a substantial share of raw material and carries borrowings denominated in foreign currency, so the hedge is partial and the residual exposure is real. Operating profitability absorbed the FY2026 episode without impairment, which is the reason this is a challenge rather than a rating constraint, but the quantum establishes the sensitivity.

**A large capital programme executing into two segments the company has not previously served at scale, with commitments now largely contracted** – Of the Rs. 6,800 crore announced through FY2028-29, the on-highway component (two-wheeler and commercial vehicle radial tyres for the Indian market) and the expanded specialty carbon black capacity both require BKT to compete on terms it has not previously tested: a domestic market with entrenched incumbents, and a customer set to whom it has historically sold as a captive supplier. Management's own FY2030 framework targets on-highway tyres at roughly 20% of revenue and carbon black at roughly 10%, against an overall revenue ambition of about Rs. 23,000 crore by 2030 from Rs. 10,412 crore in 2025 — a 2.2x expansion in five years. Contracted-but-unpaid capital commitments of Rs. 2,711.84 crore at 31-Mar-2026, nearly triple the prior year's Rs. 923.98 crore, confirm the next phase is locked in. On-highway tyres typically carry lower margins than the specialised off-highway range, so a successful ramp-up is expected to dilute blended margin somewhat even in the favourable case; an unsuccessful one would consume capital without the offsetting earnings.

**Working capital has tightened materially alongside the capital draw** – Current ratio fell from 1.27 times (FY2024) to 1.32 times (FY2025) and then to 1.01 times (FY2026), and stood at 0.97 times at 30-Jun-2026. The company's own long-term-debt-to-working-capital ratio deteriorated from 2.47 times at 31-Mar-2026 to 7.22 times at 30-Jun-2026. The absolute liquidity position remains comfortable (see Liquidity position below) and the movement is the arithmetic consequence of funding capital expenditure partly from working capital rather than a sign of operational stress — but it is a genuine, quantified reduction in cushion and it maps directly to the negative sensitivity below.

## Environmental and social risks

**Environmental considerations** – Tyre and carbon black manufacture is energy- and emissions-intensive, and BKT's footprint is correspondingly material rather than nominal. The company's BRSR discloses Scope 1 emissions of 7,27,419 MtCO2e in FY2026 against 6,75,773 MtCO2e in FY2025, a 7.6% increase broadly consistent with the combination of 3.6% revenue growth and commissioning of new capacity; Scope 2 emissions fell 2.6% to 81,487 MtCO2e from 83,684 MtCO2e. Emission intensity per rupee of turnover was unchanged at 0.73 MtCO2e per lakh of revenue between the two years — that is, the absolute increase reflects growth rather than deteriorating efficiency. The company identifies evolving deforestation-linked sourcing requirements and potential carbon pricing as forward regulatory exposures in its own risk disclosure, and reports mitigations including membership of the Global Platform for Sustainable Natural Rubber, a Natural Rubber Procurement Policy, renewable energy projects, a completed 64 MW captive power plant, and displacement of 90% of warehouse diesel forklifts with electric equivalents. ICRA assesses environmental risk as **moderate**: it does not constrain the rating at the assigned level, but the absolute quantum is rising and its regulatory treatment in BKT's principal export markets is not settled, so it remains a monitorable as new capacity reaches full utilisation.

**Social considerations** – BKT's principal social exposures are workforce safety in a heavy manufacturing setting and product quality/liability in a safety-critical product. The FY2025-26 annual report and BRSR disclose that 22.72% of R&D and capital expenditure in FY2026 (FY2025: 19.20%) was directed at energy efficiency, emission and water reduction, environmental monitoring, safety infrastructure and electric/renewable equipment. The company reports in-house R&D and testing infrastructure including the NATRAX facility at Indore and digital production traceability. The materiality sweep across the document set identified no labour dispute, safety controversy, product recall or community objection. ICRA assesses social risk as **low**; it does not constrain the rating.

## Liquidity position: Strong

**Sources.** Cash and liquid investments of Rs. 1,391.99 crore at 31-Mar-2026 (cash and cash equivalents Rs. 60.92 crore, other bank balances Rs. 3.59 crore, current investments Rs. 1,327.48 crore, consolidated). Operating cash flow of Rs. 2,249.32 crore in FY2026, up 27.5% year-on-year, against which the company's three-year record shows no year of negative operating cash generation. Company-disclosed debt service coverage of 4.31 times and interest service coverage of 70.76 times for FY2026, and 4.87 times and 32.20 times respectively for Q1 FY2026-27 (Reg 52(4), consolidated). No delay or default on any borrowing facility is reported in any document reviewed, and the auditor's opinion is unmodified in every year examined.

**Claims.** Current liabilities of Rs. 5,089.00 crore at 31-Mar-2026 against current assets of Rs. 5,154.47 crore. Contracted capital commitments not yet paid of Rs. 2,711.84 crore, the bulk of which falls due over FY2027–FY2029. Dividend outflow of Rs. 309.19 crore in FY2026 (FY2025: Rs. 309.51 crore), with an interim dividend of Rs. 4 per share declared in Q1 FY2026-27 costing Rs. 77.32 crore.

**Conclusion.** The arithmetic supports **Strong** rather than Superior. Coverage indicators are exceptionally comfortable and internal accrual has demonstrably carried the majority of a very large capital programme; the base-case funding-gap analysis brings the company to net-debt-free around FY2028. Against that, on-balance-sheet cash of Rs. 1,391.99 crore is moderate relative to Rs. 2,711.84 crore of contracted commitments, and the current ratio has genuinely compressed over five consecutive quarters. Superior would require a cash buffer that covers the committed programme outright; BKT's does not, and relies instead on continuing cash generation — which is strong, but is a flow rather than a stock.

## Rating sensitivities

**Positive factors** – ICRA could upgrade the rating if BKT achieves a material and sustained increase in scale and earnings — specifically, commercial ramp-up of the on-highway tyre and expanded carbon black capacities to meaningful utilisation while OPBDITA margin is sustained at or above 23.5% — accompanied by gearing sustained below 0.30 times and restoration of the current ratio above 1.25 times on a sustained basis.

**Negative factors** – Pressure on the rating could arise if OPBDITA margin falls below 21% on a sustained basis, if gearing rises above 0.75 times because the remaining capital expenditure is funded more heavily by debt than the FY2026 pattern implies, if the current ratio remains below 1.0 times beyond the completion of the present capital programme, or if delays in commissioning the new on-highway or carbon black capacity defer the earnings contribution on which the debt-reduction path depends. A structural rather than cyclical contraction in export demand, evidenced by sustained volume decline across successive years, would have the same effect.

## About the company

Balkrishna Industries Limited (BKT) is a listed Indian manufacturer of specialised off-highway tyres — serving agricultural, construction, earthmoving, industrial, mining and all-terrain applications — and a backward-integrated producer of carbon black, the principal filler used in tyre compound. The company is registered at Waluj, Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar, Maharashtra, and is promoted by the Poddar family, which held 58.29% of the equity as disclosed in the FY2025-26 annual report.

BKT operates four tyre manufacturing facilities — at Bhuj (Gujarat), Waluj (Maharashtra), Bhiwadi and Chopanki (both Rajasthan) — with off-highway tyre capacity being expanded to 4,25,000 MTPA through continuous debottlenecking, alongside captive carbon black capacity moving to 3,60,000 MTPA. Bhuj, situated 95 km from Mundra Port, is the flagship export plant and currently accounts for approximately 70% of exports, with a stated objective of raising this to 80%. The company sells over 3,600 SKUs into more than 160 countries through wholly-owned subsidiaries, distributors and commercial partners, and reports a single business segment, Tyres, under Ind AS 108.

During FY2025-26 BKT entered India's on-highway tyre market, launching two-wheeler tyres under the ZENOVA and THYROS brands and a commercial vehicle radial range, with passenger car radial products identified as a subsequent step. Management's stated FY2030 framework targets group revenue of approximately Rs. 23,000 crore against Rs. 10,412 crore in 2025, with off-highway tyres contributing about 70% of revenue, on-highway tyres about 20% and carbon black about 10%.

**Operating-profit normalisation (stated, not implied).** OPBDITA = profit before tax + finance cost + depreciation − other income **+ non-operating charges reported within Other Expenses**. The final term is load-bearing, not housekeeping. BKT reports its net foreign-currency result inside **Other Income when it is a gain** (FY2025: Rs. 267.36 crore) and inside **Other Expenses when it is a loss** (FY2026: Rs. 290.01 crore). A formula that nets off other income alone therefore removes the gain and retains the loss, biasing the series against loss years: on this issuer that error understated FY2026 margin by 268 basis points and inverted the year-on-year direction from a 41-basis-point rise to a 227-basis-point fall. No exceptional item is reported in any year in the audited statements; that statement is made on the basis of a line-item read of the Other Income and Other Expenses notes, not on the absence of the word "exceptional".

**Other computation bases.** Total debt includes lease liabilities. Net worth is total equity excluding revaluation reserve, consistent with the company's own Reg 52(4) presentation. The Reg 52(4) filing's own "Operating Margin" (14.09% for FY2026) is an EBIT-basis measure struck after depreciation and is a different, smaller convention than OPBDITA margin; both are genuine and neither has been adjusted to reproduce the other. FY2024 figures are cross-validated across two annual reports which report in different units (lakh and crore) and reconcile exactly; a Rs. 102.04 crore reclassification between Trade Payables and Other Financial Liabilities occurs between the two reports' FY2024 comparative columns with both totals unaffected, and is recorded in `compute_ratios.py`'s `DISCLOSED_DIFFERENCES` rather than silently resolved.

**Disclosed divergence against ICRA's published figures.** ICRA's published rationale of 25-Aug-2026 states OPBDIT/OI of 23.9% (FY2025) and 24.3% (FY2026) and interest coverage of 22.3x and 23.7x, against 23.52%/23.93% and 19.15x/19.39x computed here. The margin gap is a constant ~0.38 point in both years, which points to a standing normalisation rather than a year-specific error — CSR expenditure of Rs. 34.04 crore (FY2026) and Rs. 32.94 crore (FY2025) is the closest single candidate but cannot be confirmed from a published summary. The coverage gap implies roughly Rs. 18–24 crore netted out of the reported finance-cost line, consistent with excluding lease-liability interest and discount unwinding. Both are disclosed per failure mode 6 rather than reconciled by adjusting inputs, and neither published figure has been used as an input to any computation in this report.
