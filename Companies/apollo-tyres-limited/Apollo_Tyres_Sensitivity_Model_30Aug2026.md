# Apollo Tyres Limited — Sensitivity Model (Step 12, scaled per case.json)

Scaling decision recorded in `case.json`: full 5-year 3-statement modelling replaced with a
focused sensitivity on 2–3 rating-driving variables, because the largest disclosed forward
uncertainty (the Enschede closure) is a completed action rather than an open one, and no
sector-assumptions document was supplied. All figures Rs crore, consolidated basis unless
stated. FY2026 actual is the anchor; base case = FY2027/FY2028.

## The variables that actually move this rating

### 1. Raw-material cost pass-through (natural rubber, synthetic rubber, carbon black)
Cost of materials consumed was Rs 13,221.996 crore (FY26) against revenue of Rs 28,470.60
crore — 46.4% of revenue, the single largest cost line. Tyre input costs (natural rubber,
crude-linked synthetic rubber, carbon black) are the primary source of margin volatility in
this sector, and the FY24→FY25 margin trough (18.13%→14.01% consolidated PBDIT margin)
coincided with elevated input costs not being fully passed through in pricing. No forward
commodity-price deck was supplied (sector-assumptions gap, Step 1); the base case assumes
input costs and pricing move roughly in step, consistent with the FY26 margin recovery to
15.02% already evidencing the company's ability to pass costs through with a lag.

| Scenario | Assumption | Consolidated PBDIT margin impact |
|---|---|---|
| Base | Input costs and realisations move in step, as in FY26 | Broadly stable at ~15% |
| Downside | A repeat of the FY25 cost-price mismatch | Margin reverts toward 14%, interest coverage falls toward 8x |
| Upside | Sustained pass-through + full-year benefit of the Enschede cost exit | Margin toward 16-17% |

### 2. European segment margin, post-Enschede
The Enschede (Netherlands) plant's tyre-manufacturing operations were discontinued during
FY2026, with a Rs 1,000.12 crore impairment recognised as a one-off. The plant's ongoing
operating losses (which the closure decision implies existed) exit the P&L from FY2027
onward — this is the clearest, most quantifiable forward tailwind available, precisely
because it is a completed action rather than a projection. **Base case: European segment
profitability improves in FY2027 by the amount of the discontinued plant's prior operating
drag**, though the exact prior-year loss contribution of the Enschede facility specifically
(as distinct from the rest of the European segment) is not separately disclosed in the
document set — searched and confirmed absent (segment note gives APMEA/Europe/Others only,
not plant-level P&L). This is stated as a directional tailwind, not a quantified one.

### 3. Capex and deleveraging pace
Consolidated capex outflow was Rs 1,403.00 crore (FY26, per the AR's own capital-capacity
disclosure). Consolidated gearing has fallen every year of the review period (0.35x→0.30x→
0.22x) even through the FY25 margin trough, evidencing capex discipline relative to cash
generation. **Base case: gearing continues to improve modestly (toward ~0.20x) absent a major
new capacity announcement**, consistent with the company's own MD&A statement of being
"judicious about our capex strategy and focus on sustainable and profitable growth."

## What this does NOT replace
This is not a substitute for the full 5-year 3-statement model the SOP names as the default —
it is the analyst's judgement that the completed Enschede action and the improving leverage
trend materially reduce the forward uncertainty a fuller model would otherwise need to capture.
If a sector-assumptions document or management's own multi-year guidance becomes available,
this should be upgraded to the full workflow.
