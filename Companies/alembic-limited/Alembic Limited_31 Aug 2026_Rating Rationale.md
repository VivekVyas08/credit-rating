## Rationale

The ratings reaffirmed for Alembic Limited (AL) rest on a capital structure that is, for
practical purposes, unlevered, standing behind a listed investment portfolio of an entirely
different order of magnitude. Total debt was **Rs. 10.64 crore as on March 31, 2026** against a
tangible net worth of Rs. 821.46 crore — a gearing of **0.01 times** — and the company held **net
cash of Rs. 81.38 crore**. Against that debt sits the aggregate market value of its quoted
investments, **Rs. 3,885.89 crore**, which the company discloses in note 6 of its standalone
accounts: **365 times total debt**. Interest coverage was **55.95 times** and Total
debt/OPBDIT **0.12 times**. No plausible operating outcome threatens debt service at these
levels, and the ratings are calibrated accordingly.

Operations grew materially over the period reviewed. Operating income rose to **Rs. 231.93 crore
in FY2026 from Rs. 214.36 crore in FY2025 and Rs. 152.25 crore in FY2024** — 52% over two years.
Real estate contributed **Rs. 185.35 crore, 80% of revenue and 93% of segment profit**, while
the active pharmaceutical ingredients (API) business grew **44.6% to Rs. 46.59 crore** off a
small base. The OPBDIT margin peaked at 41.78% in FY2025 and eased to **37.81%** in FY2026, the
segment note attributing this to real-estate segment profit slipping to Rs. 78.59 crore from
Rs. 83.90 crore on broadly flat segment revenue — project mix, not demand. **PAT fell 12.3% to
Rs. 124.54 crore while PBT fell only 3.4%**; the difference is a tax-line effect, FY2025 having
carried a deferred-tax credit of Rs. 5.14 crore and a write-back of Rs. 1.56 crore against a
small charge on both counts in FY2026. ICRA does not read it as an earnings deterioration.
Dividend income from group holdings was **Rs. 62.95 crore in both FY2025 and FY2026**, and it is
this recurring cash — not the portfolio's market value — that services the company's obligations
several times over.

Two things could go wrong, and they are of very unequal weight. **The portfolio's value has
fallen sharply: Rs. 5,823.75 crore at FY2024, Rs. 5,490.20 crore at FY2025 and Rs. 3,885.89
crore at FY2026 — down 33% from the peak, and Rs. 1,604.31 crore, or 29.2%, in FY2026 alone.**
This is the most important direction in the file and it runs against the credit. Its effect so
far has been on net worth rather than cash: the markdown on the Paushak Limited holding, whose
carrying value fell to Rs. 162.52 crore from Rs. 265.50 crore, took **Rs. 102.98 crore through
other comprehensive income**, which is why tangible net worth fell to Rs. 821.46 crore despite
Rs. 124.54 crore of profit. Even after that fall the cover on debt is 365 times, so the decline
does not bind at current leverage — it would bind only if AL levered against the portfolio.
**The genuinely open exposure is the electricity-duty litigation**: AL has provided Rs. 20.52
crore for the principal and deposited **Rs. 35.00 crore with the Supreme Court on May 26, 2023**,
with the appeal admitted, while **the interest is disclosed as unascertainable and is not
acknowledged by the company**. That exposure cannot be sized from the documents, and ICRA does
not attach a figure the issuer itself declines to state. Against on-balance-sheet liquidity of
Rs. 92.02 crore, an adverse ruling of a magnitude several times the principal would be absorbed;
one far beyond that is not quantifiable on this evidence and remains a monitorable.

ICRA expects operating income to grow in the high single digits over FY2027-FY2028, with real
estate broadly stable and lumpy across project completions and API compounding off a small base,
and the OPBDIT margin to hold near the FY2026 level of 37-38% rather than return to the FY2025
peak. Capital expenditure is expected at Rs. 65-75 crore a year against FY2026's Rs. 76.65 crore
and outstanding capital commitments of Rs. 13.52 crore, and to remain **self-funded**: internal
accrual of roughly Rs. 135-140 crore (FY2026: PAT of Rs. 124.54 crore plus depreciation of
Rs. 12.01 crore) less a dividend of about Rs. 61 crore covers it, so external
borrowing is not required and debt should decline with scheduled repayment. The monitorables
are the Supreme Court's ruling on the electricity-duty appeal, any further material fall in the
market value of the quoted holdings, any debt-funded land purchase or acquisition, and the
continuation of the dividend stream from group holdings.

**The Stable outlook reflects ICRA's opinion that AL's credit profile will continue to be
underpinned by negligible leverage, a recurring dividend stream that covers its obligations many
times over, and a listed investment portfolio that remains large in relation to its debt even
after a material decline in value.**

## Key rating drivers and their description

### Credit strengths

**Financial flexibility of an order that dwarfs the balance sheet, from a listed investment
portfolio worth 365 times total debt** – The company discloses the aggregate market value of its
quoted investments in note 6 of the standalone accounts at **Rs. 3,885.89 crore as on March 31,
2026**, against total borrowings of Rs. 10.64 crore and a tangible net worth of Rs. 821.46 crore.
The holdings are in **Alembic Pharmaceuticals Limited, a 28.54% associate carried in the
standalone accounts at cost of Rs. 86.65 crore**, and in **Paushak Limited, held through
47,02,472 shares following a share split and bonus issue during FY2026** and carried at fair
value through OCI, the holding sitting below the 20% associate threshold. The gap between the
cost carrying value and the market value is the measure of the flexibility: standalone book
equity understates the realisable position by roughly a factor of five.

**Effectively unlevered capital structure with a net cash position that has deepened every
year** – Gearing was **0.01 times** and TOL/TNW **0.17 times** as on March 31, 2026. Total debt
of Rs. 10.64 crore compares with OPBDIT of Rs. 87.69 crore, giving Total debt/OPBDIT of
**0.12 times** and interest coverage of **55.95 times**. Net debt has been negative and widening
across the period reviewed — **Rs. (3.90) crore in FY2024, Rs. (39.30) crore in FY2025 and
Rs. (81.38) crore in FY2026** — as current investments built to Rs. 89.05 crore. Debt is confined
to a modest lease-rental-discounting facility; there are no capital-market instruments
outstanding.

**Recurring dividend income that covers total debt several times over on its own** – Dividend
received was **Rs. 62.95 crore in FY2025 and Rs. 62.95 crore in FY2026**, and **Rs. 45.91 crore
in FY2024** per the standalone cash-flow statement. That single line covers total debt 5.9 times
and the year's finance cost of Rs. 1.57 crore forty times. It is also structurally tax-efficient:
the tax reconciliation discloses a Chapter VI-A deduction worth **Rs. 15.85 crore of tax in both
FY2025 and FY2026**, which at the 25.17% concessional rate shelters approximately Rs. 63.0 crore
of income — the dividend, almost exactly. ICRA notes that this Section 80M relief depends on AL
continuing to distribute the dividend it receives (**dividends paid were Rs. 61.41 crore in
FY2026**) and does not treat it as a permanent reduction in the tax burden independent of that
payout.

**Established, high-margin real-estate operations on owned land** – Real estate generated
**Rs. 185.35 crore of revenue and Rs. 78.59 crore of segment profit in FY2026**, an implied
segment margin of 42.4%, in a business that develops and leases the company's own Vadodara
estate. Rent income from the wholly-owned subsidiary Alembic City Limited alone was **Rs. 38.67
crore in FY2026, up from Rs. 35.76 crore**, giving a recurring lease component alongside
development income.

### Credit challenges

**Modest operating scale, geographically concentrated, is the binding constraint on the rating**
– Operating income of **Rs. 231.93 crore** is small in absolute terms, and 80% of it comes from a
single-city real-estate business whose revenue is lumpy across project completions. The API
segment, though growing 44.6%, contributed **Rs. 46.59 crore of revenue and Rs. 5.65 crore of
segment profit** — 20% and 7% respectively — and is not yet of a size to diversify the earnings
base. It is scale, not financial risk, that holds the rating where it is: the leverage and
coverage metrics on their own would support a materially higher grade.

**The market value of the investment portfolio has fallen a third in two years** – **Rs. 5,823.75
crore (FY2024) to Rs. 5,490.20 crore (FY2025) to Rs. 3,885.89 crore (FY2026)**, a 33% decline
from the peak and 29.2% in FY2026 alone. Because the holdings are in two listed group entities,
the value is exposed to the market's view of those companies and to the concentration of the
portfolio in a single sector. The immediate consequence has been the **Rs. 102.98 crore OCI
charge** on the Paushak markdown, which reduced tangible net worth in a profitable year. At
current leverage the decline has no debt-servicing consequence; it would acquire one if the
company borrowed against the portfolio.

**Cash flows depend on the dividend policy of an entity AL does not control** – Dividend income
of Rs. 62.95 crore is roughly 27% of operating income and 72% of OPBDIT, and it originates
principally with a 28.54%-held associate. AL cannot compel a distribution. The track record of
receipt is consistent across the years reviewed, and the associate's own credit standing is
sound, but the dependence is real and is reflected in the negative sensitivities below.

**An open litigation whose interest exposure the company itself cannot quantify** – The dispute
is with the State of Gujarat, the Collector of Electricity Duty, the Chief Auditor and the
Industries Commission over electricity duty. AL's appeal has been **admitted by the Supreme
Court**, and the company **deposited Rs. 35.00 crore with the Court on May 26, 2023**, an event
it also intimated to the stock exchanges. **An aggregate provision of Rs. 20.52 crore has been
made for the principal amount**; the interest is disclosed as a contingent liability whose amount is
**not ascertainable and not acknowledged by the company**. The FY2026 auditor's report carries an
**Emphasis of Matter** on this litigation and a Key Audit Matter on litigations, provisions and
contingent liabilities. Other quantified contingent liabilities are modest at **Rs. 12.64 crore
in aggregate — 1.5% of net worth** — comprising letters of credit and guarantees of Rs. 6.01
crore, service tax in appeal of Rs. 5.20 crore net of a Rs. 1.18 crore provision, sales tax of
Rs. 1.24 crore and green cess of Rs. 0.19 crore.

**Governance factors that a rating committee would weigh, disclosed rather than adverse** –
FY2023 drew a **qualified audit opinion** on a Rs. 10.61 crore transfer from General Reserve to
the profit and loss account; the FY2024 opinion carried a qualification only as to the
comparability of that comparative, and the FY2025 and FY2026 opinions are unmodified.
Related-party dealing is dense: Rs. 38.67 crore of rent income from the wholly-owned subsidiary
and Rs. 38.86 crore of dividend paid to the 63.06% holding company in FY2026. **No financial
information on the holding company, Nirayu Private Limited, is available in the document set** —
searched — so its credit profile cannot be assessed, and no support is either assumed or
required.

## Environmental and social risks

**Environmental considerations** – AL's two businesses carry different exposures. The API
operation is subject to effluent, emission and waste regulation, where tightening norms can
raise operating and compliance costs; the real-estate business is exposed to construction-stage
environmental clearances and to the cost of green-building compliance. The company's own BRSR
disclosures show the direction improving on most measures: **Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions
totalled 1,385.40 MT CO2e in FY2026 against 1,669 MT CO2e in FY2025, a 17% reduction**, with
emission intensity per rupee of turnover falling to 0.00000060 from 0.00000078. **Total energy
consumption fell 24% to 83,320.45 GJ from 1,10,097.07 GJ, and the renewable share rose to 26.7%
from 23.2%**, supported by captive windmills and co-generation at the API plant. Water
consumption fell to 19,071 kl from 82,330 kl, with API process water down 9.22%, and all
discharged water — 1,32,348 kl — is treated to primary, secondary and tertiary level before
being sent to third parties. The new commercial building has been certified **Silver by the
USGBC**, and air-emission and water data are evaluated by an external institution, Birla
Vishvakarma Mahavidyalaya. **Stated for completeness: the BRSR reports no Scope 3 inventory, no
net-zero or SBTi commitment — the company confirms it has no dedicated GHG-reduction project,
only power-consumption goals — and no external assurance of the energy data.** ICRA assesses the
environmental risk as low-to-moderate, reflecting the small physical footprint of both
businesses.

**Social considerations** – The API business carries product-safety and manufacturing-compliance
risk; the real-estate business is labour-intensive and exposed to site safety and skilled-labour
availability. On the company's own disclosures, **the Lost Time Injury Frequency Rate was nil
for both employees and workers in FY2026 and FY2025**, and **no complaint was received under the
Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace Act in either year**. Premises are disclosed as
accessible under the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016, and an equal-opportunity
policy is in place. The **CSR obligation of Rs. 1.12 crore was fully spent, with no unspent
balance**, directed through group non-profit institutions in education, healthcare and rural
development. On governance, the board comprises **eight directors of whom four are independent,
including one woman independent director**, meeting both the 50% independence requirement and the
woman-independent-director requirement, with a non-executive promoter chairman; four board
meetings were held during FY2026 and **no conflict-of-interest complaints were received against
directors or key managerial personnel** in either year. ICRA assesses the social and governance
risk as not constraining the rating, with the FY2023 qualified opinion noted above as a historic
mark rather than a live concern.

## Liquidity position: Superior

Sources over the next twelve months are **on-balance-sheet liquidity of Rs. 92.02 crore as on
March 31, 2026** — cash and cash equivalents of Rs. 1.10 crore, other bank balances of Rs. 1.87
crore and current investments of Rs. 89.05 crore — together with **recurring dividend income of
Rs. 62.95 crore**, received at a near-identical figure in each of the last two years, and FY2026
OPBDIT of Rs. 87.69 crore. **No lien or encumbrance is disclosed against the cash, bank balances
or current investments in notes 10, 12 or 13**, which was checked rather than assumed. Behind
these sources stands the quoted portfolio of Rs. 3,885.89 crore, which is listed and saleable.

Claims over the same period are **total debt of Rs. 10.64 crore**, of which Rs. 4.03 crore is
current, a finance cost of Rs. 1.57 crore, outstanding **capital commitments of Rs. 13.52 crore**
(down from Rs. 31.19 crore) within expected capital expenditure of Rs. 65-75 crore, and a
dividend broadly in line with FY2026's Rs. 61.41 crore.

On-balance-sheet liquidity alone covers total debt **8.6 times**; dividend income alone covers it
**5.9 times** and the finance cost **40 times**; the quoted portfolio covers it **365 times**. The
arithmetic does not admit a weaker descriptor. ICRA notes one qualification: the portfolio's
realisable value tracks two listed share prices and has fallen 33% in two years, so the
magnitude of the cushion is volatile even though its adequacy at these debt levels is not in
question.

## Rating sensitivities

**Positive factors** – ICRA could upgrade the ratings if AL materially increases the scale and
diversity of its operating earnings, with operating income and segment profitability improving
on a sustained basis and the API business reaching a size that meaningfully diversifies the
revenue base, while the company maintains negligible leverage and the market value of its
investment portfolio recovers and is sustained at higher levels.

**Negative factors** – The ratings could be downgraded if the dividend stream from group holdings
is materially reduced or interrupted on a sustained basis; if the market value of the quoted
investment portfolio declines further to an extent that materially erodes financial flexibility;
if AL raises debt of a size that changes the capital structure, with Total debt/OPBDIT rising
above 2.0 times on a sustained basis; or if the electricity-duty litigation is decided adversely
with an interest liability large enough to weaken the liquidity profile.

**A methodology that was considered and NOT applied, with the reason.** ICRA's Investment
Companies rating methodology (January 2025) defines its scope as entities that do not have
significant operations of their own and that depend on investment income or divestment to
service their debt. AL does not meet that description: **Rs. 231.93 crore of FY2026 revenue from
operations against Rs. 62.95 crore of dividend income**, with real estate alone contributing
Rs. 78.59 crore of segment profit. It is an operating company that also holds a large investment
portfolio. The Corporate Credit Rating Methodology is therefore applied alone, and the
investment portfolio is assessed under its financial-flexibility factor, where it belongs for an
operating company. **This is a departure from the analytical approach in the prior published
action, which cited both methodologies, and it has a visible consequence in the ratios — see the
note below the Key financial indicators.**

## About the company

Alembic Limited was incorporated on July 30, 1907. Following the demerger of its formulations
business into Alembic Pharmaceuticals Limited with effect from April 1, 2010, AL operates in
real-estate development and leasing on its own Vadodara estate, and in the manufacture of active
pharmaceutical ingredients, supported by captive windmills and co-generation capacity. In FY2026
real estate contributed Rs. 185.35 crore of the Rs. 231.93 crore of standalone operating income
and Rs. 78.59 crore of the Rs. 84.24 crore of segment profit, with API contributing Rs. 46.59
crore and Rs. 5.65 crore respectively.

**Nirayu Private Limited held 63.06% of AL as on March 31, 2026** (16,19,15,052 shares, unchanged
from the prior year). AL in turn holds **28.54% of Alembic Pharmaceuticals Limited**, accounted
for by the equity method in the consolidated statements and carried at cost of Rs. 86.65 crore in
the standalone accounts, and **47,02,472 shares of Paushak Limited** following a share split and
bonus issue during FY2026, carried at fair value through OCI at Rs. 162.52 crore. **Alembic City
Limited is a wholly-owned subsidiary.** Mr. Udit Amin was appointed Managing Director with effect
from October 1, 2025, succeeding Mrs. Malika Amin, who continues as a non-executive
non-independent director.

**Computation basis, and a material divergence from the prior published action, disclosed.**
OPBDIT is computed as PBT plus finance cost plus depreciation **less other income in full**.
Other income (note 28) was decomposed to its line items before use: **dividend income of
Rs. 62.95 crore, interest income of Rs. 1.40 crore, profit on sale of investments of Rs. 4.93
crore, a foreign-exchange gain of Rs. 0.04 crore and other items of Rs. 3.45 crore in FY2026**,
each stripped from the operating measure. **Forex was a gain in every year and no
foreign-exchange loss appears anywhere in other expenses** — searched — so no asymmetry arises
between the two notes. **Exceptional items are nil in all three years.** No lease liability is
disclosed separately in any year, so total debt equals borrowings. Consequently the operating
income and margins above are **narrower than those in the prior published action**, which
reported FY2025 operating income of Rs. 280.6 crore and an OPBDIT/OI of 55.5% against the
Rs. 214.36 crore and 41.78% here. **PAT reconciles exactly (Rs. 141.9 crore against Rs. 141.95
crore), which localises the entire difference above the PAT line:** solving for the single
add-back implied by the published margin, by the published interest coverage and by the
published operating income gives 66.11, 66.17 and 66.24 for FY2025 and 46.10, 45.94 and 45.95
for FY2024 — three independent routes agreeing to within Rs. 0.15 crore, and matching the
dividend income of Rs. 62.95 crore and Rs. 45.91 crore plus the note's residual "others" line.
The difference is therefore **one classification choice — whether investment income is operating
revenue — and follows directly from the methodology-scope decision recorded under Analytical
approach.** The convention used here is the more conservative of the two and supports the same
rating. The dividend income is not discarded; it is assessed in the liquidity and financial
flexibility analysis, where it does the work.
