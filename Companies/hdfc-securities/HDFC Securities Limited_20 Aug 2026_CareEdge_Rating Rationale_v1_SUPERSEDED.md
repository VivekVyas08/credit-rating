## **Rationale and key rating drivers**

The rating derives principally from **HDFC Bank Limited's 94.01% ownership** of HDFC Securities Limited (HSL), the shared HDFC brand, and HSL's role as the broking arm of the parent's retail franchise — client acquisition, the three-in-one account and distribution all run through the bank. Under CareEdge's parent-linkage framework this is a strategically important, near-wholly-owned subsidiary of a highest-credit-quality parent, and a short-term rating at the top of the scale follows directly from that relationship.

The standalone profile supports rather than strains that conclusion, though it weakened over FY2026. Profit after tax fell **17.3% to ₹929.9 crore** on total income down **4.7% to ₹3,110.2 crore**, not on weak volumes — turnover actually rose 82% — but on a roughly 50% collapse in revenue earned per unit of turnover, meeting a 5.4% rise in operating costs. PBILDT margin contracted 550bps to 42.4%. In the same year **total debt grew 90.3%**, taking gearing from **2.37x to 4.20x**. Debt roughly doubling while profit falls is the central tension in this file and is flagged as such.

Three things resolve it. First, the incremental borrowing **funded asset growth, not losses**: the margin funding book rose 29.4% to ₹7,132.66 crore and investments rose 163% to ₹2,761.99 crore. Second, although funding is **100% short-term**, the asset side is short-dated to match — financial assets maturing within 12 months of ₹18,229.85 crore exceed total financial liabilities of ₹18,065.10 crore, and cash cover on commercial paper held **flat at ~0.74x** across both years. Third, and most importantly, **the FY2026 decline has already reversed**: in the quarter ended 30 June 2026 total income rose **30.3% year-on-year to ₹950.03 crore** and profit after tax rose **28.3% to ₹297.06 crore**, an annualised run-rate above even FY2025's result.

The constraints are a **dividend payout held near 80% in both years**, which left internal capital generation at just 5.3% and equity growth at 7% against a far faster-growing book; a liquidity cushion that, while still positive, **narrowed 92% from ₹1,939.73 crore to ₹164.75 crore**; and complete dependence on rolling short-term funding, which is tolerable only because of the parent standing behind it.

## **Rating sensitivities: Factors likely to lead to rating actions**

### **Positive factors**
- Not applicable. CARE A1+ is the highest rating on the short-term scale.

### **Negative factors**
- Any dilution of HDFC Bank's shareholding below a clear majority, or any signal that HSL is no longer strategically important to the parent — this is the dominant driver and its weakening would matter more than any standalone metric.
- Gearing sustained above ~5.0x, or a further sharp rise in debt not matched by earnings recovery.
- The ALM gap turning **negative**, or liquid-asset cover on commercial paper falling durably below ~0.60x.
- Reversal of the Q1FY2027 recovery, with income and profitability resuming the FY2026 decline over consecutive quarters.
- Continued ~80% dividend payout alongside book growth, further suppressing internal capital generation.
- Further compression in revenue yield on turnover, such that rising volumes continue to produce flat or falling income over successive quarters.

## **Detailed description of key rating drivers:**

### **Key strengths**

**Near-wholly-owned subsidiary of HDFC Bank Limited (94.01%).** This is the single most important factor. HSL shares the HDFC brand, sources clients through the bank's branch and digital channels, and operates the integrated three-in-one broking-bank-demat proposition. The ownership level leaves no meaningful minority interest and is well above the threshold at which CareEdge's linkage framework treats a subsidiary as strategically important.

**Funding entirely short-term, but matched by short-dated assets.** Financial assets due within 12 months of **₹18,229.85 crore** cover total financial liabilities of **₹18,065.10 crore**. Cash and bank balances stood at **₹9,622.71 crore** at 31-Mar-2026, and the ₹7,132.66 crore margin funding book is secured against listed securities with margin and is self-liquidating. The commercial paper carried tenors of 7–180 days at 6.15%–8.05%, against 28–182 days at 7.43%–8.05% a year earlier.

**Strong absolute profitability and a decisive Q1FY2027 recovery.** Even in a down year HSL earned a **29.9% net margin and 25.9% return on equity**. The quarter ended 30 June 2026 then reversed the trend outright: total income **₹950.03 crore, up 30.3%** year-on-year and 11.7% sequentially; profit before tax **₹391.63 crore, up 28.6%**; profit after tax **₹297.06 crore, up 28.3%**. Operating margin held at 41%.

**Strong volume and market-share momentum.** Average daily turnover rose **82%** to approximately ₹3 trillion, from ₹1.6 trillion a year earlier. Derivative volumes grew **83%** year-on-year, market share in derivatives increased **1.7 times**, and retail equity delivery market share was retained in the same range as FY2025. Over **1 million customers** access the company's services digitally, and brokerage and fee income was **₹1,516 crore**, 48.7% of total income. On volume and share, FY2026 was a strong year — which makes the revenue outcome, addressed below, the more striking.

### **Key weaknesses**

**Debt nearly doubled while profit fell.** Total debt rose **90.3% to ₹15,113.88 crore** in FY2026 and a further **19.9% to ₹18,120.67 crore** by 30 June 2026, while FY2026 profit after tax fell 17.3%. Gearing moved from **2.37x to 4.20x**. The borrowing funded asset growth rather than losses, which is the mitigant, but the pace is rapid and warrants monitoring.

**High dividend payout suppresses capital generation.** Payout was **79.9% in FY2025 and 79.6% in FY2026** — four-fifths of a falling profit. Internal capital generation was consequently only **5.3%**, and equity grew just 7% to ₹3,595.92 crore against a balance sheet growing far faster. This is the weakest element of the file, tempered by the fact that the upstreaming goes to the same parent that supports the credit.

**Liquidity cushion materially thinner.** The excess of sub-12-month assets over financial liabilities fell from **₹1,939.73 crore to ₹164.75 crore**, a 92% reduction. The gap remains positive, but the margin for error is far smaller than a year ago.

**Severe yield compression — volume growth is not reaching revenue.** This is the defining operating fact of FY2026 and it is easy to miss. Turnover rose 82% and derivative volumes 83%, yet total income *fell* 4.7%. Revenue earned per unit of turnover therefore **halved**, from roughly 2,040 to 1,037 on the reported endpoints (about a 48% fall on the unrounded turnover figure). The mix has shifted toward derivative turnover, which earns far less per rupee traded than cash delivery, and zero-brokerage discount platforms continue to pressure pricing. Growth in activity can no longer be assumed to translate into growth in earnings.

**Branch network being consolidated, not expanded.** Branches fell from **184 across 139 cities** at 31-Mar-2024 to **134 across 106 cities** at 31-Mar-2025 and **128 across more than 100 towns and cities** at 31-Mar-2026 — a **30% reduction in two years**. Read alongside 1 million+ digital customers this is a deliberate shift to digital delivery and a cost response to yield compression, rather than distress. It does, however, reduce physical reach in smaller centres.

**Complete refinancing dependence.** With long-term debt at nil, the entire ₹18,120.67 crore funding stack must be rolled continuously in tenors under a year.

## **Liquidity: Strong**

Liquidity is assessed as **Strong**. Cash and bank balances of **₹9,622.71 crore** at 31-Mar-2026 cover **0.74x** of the ₹12,931.33 crore commercial paper outstanding — a ratio essentially unchanged from 0.74x a year earlier, indicating liquidity was scaled deliberately alongside the borrowing rather than allowed to lag. Adding the self-liquidating margin book of ₹7,132.66 crore and trade receivables of ₹1,425.74 crore, financial assets maturing within 12 months of **₹18,229.85 crore** exceed total financial liabilities of **₹18,065.10 crore**.

The descriptor is **Strong** rather than **Superior** for two reasons: the sub-12-month surplus narrowed 92% year-on-year to ₹164.75 crore, and cash alone does not fully cover commercial paper. It is not lower than Strong because access to the parent's funding and the demonstrated ability to raise commercial paper at 6.15%–8.05% through a volatile year both remain intact.

## **About the company and industry**

**HDFC Securities Limited** (CIN **U67120MH2000PLC152193**) is a retail and institutional **stock broking** and distribution company, incorporated in 2000 and headquartered in Mumbai. It is a **94.01% subsidiary of HDFC Bank Limited**. The company offers equity and derivative broking, margin funding, depository participant services, and distribution of mutual funds and other financial products, through **128 branches across more than 100 towns and cities** as on 31 March 2026, and digital channels. It has one wholly-owned subsidiary, **HDFC Securities IFSC Limited**, operating from GIFT City.

The Indian retail broking industry is cyclical, with revenue tied to market turnover, retail participation and the regulated economics of each segment. FY2026 was **not** a volume downturn — industry and HSL turnover both rose sharply, with HSL's average daily turnover up 82% and derivative volumes up 83%. It was a **yield** downturn: a mix shift toward lower-yielding derivative turnover, regulatory tightening of derivative-segment rules, and sustained zero-brokerage competition together compressed revenue earned per rupee traded by roughly half. Incumbents have responded by leaning on margin funding, distribution and advisory income, and by consolidating physical branch networks in favour of digital delivery — both visible in HSL's FY2026 numbers. The June 2026 quarter showed the revenue effect beginning to reverse.

## **Annexure III — Basis and evidence notes**

**Documents used (8).** Three annual reports (FY2024, FY2025, FY2026) and five quarterly results (Q1–Q4 FY2026 and the quarter ended 30 June 2026). The **FY2026 annual report dated 04-08-2026** and the **30 June 2026 results** are the newest and were read first; all headline figures are drawn from them.

**Entity confirmation.** All eight documents were confirmed to relate to HDFC Securities Limited, CIN U67120MH2000PLC152193.

**Evidenced absence — rated programme size and existing ratings.** These are recorded as absent only because the searches below were run and returned nothing:

1. A grep for `credit rating` across the FY2026 annual report returned **2 hits**, both generic investment-policy references to the ratings of instruments the company may invest in — neither states a rating on HSL's own debt.
2. A grep for rating-scale strings (`CARE A1|CRISIL A1|ICRA A1|A1\+|AAA|AA\+`) against the issuer's documents returned **no hit** that assigns a rating to HSL.
3. A pre-flight search of the reference library of published rationales returned **no same-issuer document**. It surfaced **HDFC Bank Limited** — the *parent*, with a rated amount of ₹1,50,000 crore — and unrelated broking peers including Almondz. Both were flagged by the entity-confirmation guard as matching on only part of the name and were therefore **not imported**. The parent's rated amount is expressly **not** carried across to this issuer.

**Evidenced presence — the long-term / short-term split.** The split is not an inference. The contractual maturity tables in the FY2026 annual report were read for both years and place the full carrying amount of *both* debt securities and other borrowings in the **"less than 1 year"** column, with nil in the 1–2 year, 2–3 year and beyond-3-year columns, in **FY2025 and FY2026 alike**. Long-term debt is therefore **NIL** in both years, and this is the issuer's own disclosure.

**Tie-outs.** Fourteen reconciliation checks were run in `compute_ratios.py` and six in the workbook's TieOut tab; all reconcile. Debt securities of ₹12,931.33 crore tie to commercial paper of ₹12,827.38 crore plus accrued interest of ₹103.96 crore; total debt of ₹15,113.88 crore ties to debt securities plus the secured loan of ₹2,182.55 crore, exactly.

**Year-attribution check on sector metrics — a correction made before delivery.** The first extraction pass drew the operating metrics from whichever annual report matched the search string, without confirming which year each belonged to. That produced three errors: average daily turnover of "+34% off ₹1.7 trillion" (an **FY2025** report figure, and partly an NSE market statistic), "1.09 million active transacting clients" (an **FY2024** figure), and a branch trend stated as *growth from 128 to 134* when the true sequence is **184 → 134 → 128**, i.e. a 30% contraction. Every operating metric was then re-extracted per document and tied to its source year: FY2024 AR 184 branches / 139 cities; FY2025 AR 134 / 106; FY2026 AR 128 / 100+ and turnover +82% to ~₹3 trillion. This also corrected the industry narrative, which had wrongly described FY2026 as a volume downturn when it was a yield downturn. A grep for the stated "82%" against the FY2026 report confirms the issuer's own figure; note the rounded endpoints (₹1.6 Tr to ₹3 Tr) imply 87.5%, so the issuer's 82% is used and the unrounded turnover is about ₹2.91 trillion.

**Sector metric set.** Broking metrics — average daily turnover, active clients, branch and city count — are used. Lending-company metrics (Stage III, CRAR, AUM) and bank metrics (NIM, GNPA, CASA) are **not** applicable to a broking intermediary and are deliberately not reported.
