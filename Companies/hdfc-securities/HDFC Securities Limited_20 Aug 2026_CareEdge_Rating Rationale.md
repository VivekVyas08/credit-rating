## **Rationale and key rating drivers**

The ratings derive principally from **HDFC Bank Limited's 93.99% ownership** (as on 30-Jun-2026) and the expectation of continued need-based support. HSL is the broking arm of the parent's retail franchise: HBL's network and clientele drive lead generation, while HSL's broking customers strengthen the bank's retail liability franchise. Linkages are managerial as well as operational — HBL has a nominee director on the board, and the MD & CEO, Dhiraj Relli, previously headed branch banking at HBL. The shared brand and the financial flexibility that comes with it are integral to the rating.

The standalone profile supports that conclusion. HSL is the **sixth-largest retail stockbroker by active clients** (~10.05 lakh as on 31-Jul-2026), and earned **₹930 crore** on total income of **₹3,110 crore** in FY2026 — a PAT margin of **29.90%**, ROTA of **5.22%** and RONW of **27.54%**. Revenue is meaningfully diversified: fees and commission income contributed **48%** of FY2026 income (brokerage alone **33%**) and interest income on the margin trading facility book a further **31%**.

The central development, and the one that shapes the risk profile, is the **margin trading facility**. The MTF book has grown from **₹5,512 crore** (31-Mar-2025) to **₹7,133 crore** (31-Mar-2026) to **₹10,081 crore** (30-Jun-2026) — up 41% in a single quarter, and now **2.76x tangible net worth**. Borrowings have been deployed to fund MTF since FY20, so the book explains the leverage: **overall gearing rose from 2.65x to 4.64x and further to 5.28x**, with about **86%** of fund-based borrowing in commercial paper. Management intends to hold *fund-based* gearing below 5x, subject to the regulatory leverage cap.

That growth is the rating's principal monitorable rather than a straightforward strength. MTF is secured against approved securities, but adverse movement in the underlying collateral transmits credit and market risk directly, and the ability to expand the book while maintaining an adequate capitalisation buffer and healthy asset quality is what will determine whether the profile holds. **Tangible net worth has grown only gradually** — ₹3,257 crore to ₹3,496 crore to ₹3,647 crore — because roughly **80% of profit is paid out as dividend**, leaving little internal accretion against a fast-growing book.

Earnings moderated in FY2026: total income fell 4.7% and PAT 17.3% on lower brokerage income in unfavourable market conditions, with cost-to-income rising from 39.42% to 45.54% and RONW compressing from **51.63% (FY2024) → 43.28% (FY2025) → 27.54% (FY2026)**. The June 2026 quarter reversed the trend — income **₹950 crore**, PAT **₹297 crore**, PAT margin **31.27%**, cost-to-income back to 39.70% and RONW of **34.0% annualised**.

The forward-looking constraint is regulatory. **RBI's amended directions of 13-Feb-2026, effective 01-Jul-2026**, govern bank credit to capital-market intermediaries and require fully collateralised lending, higher-quality collateral, minimum haircuts and MTF backed by high-quality liquid collateral. These are expected to raise funding costs precisely where HSL is growing.

## **Rating sensitivities: Factors likely to lead to rating actions**

### **Positive factors**
- Not applicable. The ratings are at the highest levels on both the long-term and short-term scales.

### **Negative factors**
- Weakening in the credit profile of HDFC Bank Limited.
- Dilution of the parent support philosophy or of linkages with HBL, or a significant change in HBL's shareholding in HSL.
- Material impact on profitability, and/or deterioration in the asset quality of the MTF book.
- Overall gearing sustained materially above management's stated fund-based threshold of 5x without a commensurate strengthening of the capital base.

## **Detailed description of key rating drivers:**

### **Key strengths**

**Strong parentage and linkages with HDFC Bank Limited.** HBL (CARE AAA; Stable / CARE A1+) held **93.99%** of HSL as on 30-Jun-2026, having taken a controlling 55% interest in FY06 and increased it since. Board representation runs through HBL's nominee director, and several key personnel — including the MD & CEO — came from the bank. HBL's scale, network and diverse clientele aid lead generation, while HSL's broking customers strengthen the bank's retail liability franchise. Given these linkages, brand sharing and a financially strong parent, continued need-based support is expected. HSL is strategically important to the group, giving the bank's customers access to a broader suite of capital market and investment products.

**Strong retail broking franchise.** Ranked **sixth by active clients**, with ~**10.05 lakh** active clients as on 31-Jul-2026 and ~**95%** of Q1FY27 brokerage from retail clients. The company offers equity and derivatives broking to retail and institutional clients and distributes mutual funds, insurance, fixed deposits and IPOs. In line with the industry shift to digital, the branch network has been deliberately **consolidated from 184 (31-Mar-2024) to 134, then 128, and 127 as on 30-Jun-2026**, with the in-house discount app **"HDFC Sky"** targeting new-age investors.

**Healthy earnings profile despite recent moderation.** FY2026 total income ₹3,110 crore and PAT ₹930 crore — PAT margin **29.90%**, ROTA **5.22%**, RONW **27.54%**. Q1FY27 PAT ₹297 crore on income of ₹950 crore, PAT margin **31.27%** and annualised ROTA **6.01%**. Revenue is diversified across brokerage (33%), total fees and commission (48%) and MTF interest income (31%). Interest income rose to **₹1,498 crore** in FY2026 from ₹1,418 crore, on higher margin requirements and investment income.

**Comfortable capital position and financial flexibility.** Tangible net worth of **₹3,647 crore** as on 30-Jun-2026 (₹3,496 crore at 31-Mar-2026; ₹3,257 crore at 31-Mar-2025). As part of the HDFC group, HSL raises commercial paper at competitive rates, predominantly at three-month tenor, and maintains access to bank lines for working capital.

### **Key weaknesses**

**Presence in an inherently volatile and competitive broking business.** Earnings depend significantly on capital market activity and trading volumes. Brokerage contributed 33% of FY2026 income and MTF 31% — both directly a function of market performance. Low-cost and zero-brokerage firms continue to gain share; HSL has retained its customer base with parental support, but the ability to enhance market share and profitability remains a key monitorable.

**MTF expansion concentrates credit and market risk against a slow-growing capital base.** The book reached **₹10,081 crore** at 30-Jun-2026, **2.76x tangible net worth**, from 1.69x two years earlier. Adverse deviation in the valuation of underlying securities can significantly affect the risk profile. Compounding this, TNW grows slowly because **dividend payout has been ~80% in both FY2025 and FY2026** — CareEdge's own framing is that net worth increased only gradually "despite healthy earnings, considering sizeable dividend payouts". **The ability to grow the MTF book while maintaining an adequate capitalisation buffer and healthy asset quality is the key monitorable for these ratings.**

**Rising leverage, concentrated in short-tenor commercial paper.** Overall gearing (including non-fund-based limits utilised) rose **2.65x → 4.64x → 5.28x**. About **86%** of fund-based borrowings were commercial paper at both 30-Jun-2026 and 31-Mar-2026 (97% at 30-Jun-2025), so the funding stack must be rolled continuously at three-month tenors.

**Susceptibility to regulatory change.** Two live items:
- **RBI amended directions dated 13-Feb-2026, effective 01-Jul-2026** — a comprehensive framework for bank credit to capital-market intermediaries including stockbrokers: fully collateralised lending, higher collateral quality with significant cash components, minimum haircuts particularly on equity securities, restrictions on funding proprietary trading, MTF required to be backed by high-quality liquid collateral, and stricter monitoring and concentration limits. **Expected to increase funding costs and necessitate stronger capitalisation and liquidity management.**
- **SEBI's October 2024 measures**, effective in phases between November 2024 and April 2025: fewer weekly expiries in derivatives (previously the largest F&O contributor), larger lot sizes, a 2% increase in extreme loss margin on short options on expiry day, upfront collection of option premiums, and removal of calendar-spread treatment on expiry day.

## **Liquidity: Strong**

As on **30 June 2026**, total fund-based borrowings were **₹18,286 crore**, comprising **commercial paper ₹15,765 crore**, **TREPS borrowings ₹2,183 crore** and **loan against deposits ₹338 crore**. Against these, the company held **unencumbered cash and cash equivalents of ₹422 crore** and **unutilised sanctioned lines of credit of ₹2,698 crore**.

Cover therefore does not come primarily from cash. It comes from the **short-term, self-liquidating MTF book** of ₹10,081 crore, whose inflows are used to meet debt obligations; from **excess margins maintained with exchanges**, deployable in exigencies; from the undrawn lines above; and from the financial flexibility of being an HDFC Bank subsidiary, including demonstrated access to CP at competitive rates through a volatile year. The company also avails bank guarantees to meet exchange margin requirements — ₹1,100 crore outstanding at 31-Mar-2026, which is counted in overall gearing.

> **Basis note.** The balance-sheet line "bank balance other than cash and cash equivalents" stood at ₹7,838.73 crore at 31-Mar-2026. That is **not** company liquidity: for a broker it is overwhelmingly client funds and exchange margin, segregated and unavailable to service company debt. Only the **unencumbered** figure is used above.

## **About the company and industry**

### Industry classification

| Macroeconomic indicator | Sector | Industry | Basic industry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Financial services | Financial services | Capital markets | Stockbroking and allied |

**HDFC Securities Limited** (CIN **U67120MH2000PLC152193**) was incorporated in **April 2000** as a joint venture of HDFC Bank Limited, HDFC Limited and Indocean Securities Holdings Limited. It is engaged in a single line of business — providing broking services in the capital market — and is a **public limited company listed in the debt market on the BSE**. HBL obtained a controlling interest of 55% in FY06 and held **93.99%** as on 30-Jun-2026. HSL is registered as a **Stockbroker** with SEBI and as a **Corporate Agent** with IRDA. It began with equity trading, later added derivatives, and now offers a broad suite of savings and investment products alongside core broking, distributing third-party products and cross-selling group products. **In FY19 it began offering MTF**, extending short-term loans to broking customers fully secured against cash and/or collateral of approved securities. It has one wholly-owned subsidiary, **HDFC Securities IFSC Limited**, at GIFT City.

The Indian retail broking industry is cyclical, with revenue tied to market turnover, retail participation and the regulated economics of each segment. FY2026 saw brokerage income decline on unfavourable market conditions before recovering in the June 2026 quarter on improved trading volumes. Structural pressure on yield persists from low-cost and zero-brokerage platforms, pushing incumbents toward margin funding, distribution and advisory income — a diversification that brings its own credit and market risk. Regulatory intervention, from SEBI on derivatives and from RBI on bank credit to intermediaries, remains the principal forward-looking variable.
