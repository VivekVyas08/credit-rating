Rationale and key rating drivers 
Reaffirmation of ratings to bank facilities and commercial paper (CP) of HDFC Securities Limited (HSL) considers strong franchise 
with its status as the sixth-largest retail stockbroker in terms of active client base as on June 30, 2026, its comfortable 
capitalisation, and healthy earnings profile. Ratings continue to derive strength from HSL’s strong parentage, with HDFC Bank 
Limited (HBL- rated ‘CARE AAA; Stable/ CARE A1+’) holding 93.99% (As on June 30, 2026) stake in HSL, and expectation of 
continued need-based support from HBL. Ratings also factor in HSL’s strategic importance to the group as it provides access to a 
broader suite of capital market and investment products to the bank’s customers. Ratings also consider managerial and operational 
linkages with the parent and broader benefits, including strong financial flexibility, and shared brand identity and client base.  
 
However, ratings also factor in risks inherent in broking business due to high dependence on capital markets, resulting in volatility 
in income and profitability, increasing competition in the sector, and susceptibility towards regulatory changes. With the rising 
share of margin trading facility (MTF) in the revenue mix, overall indebtedness, and associated asset quality also remain key 
monitorable.

Analytical approach: Standalone 
CARE Ratings Limited (CareEdge Ratings) has analysed HSL’s standalone credit profile, and has factored in linkages with its 
parent, HBL, owing to its strong operational and managerial linkages and shared brand name. 
Outlook: Stable 
The stable outlook factors in the expectation that HSL will continue to have a healthy and diversified earnings profile, considering 
its strong retail broking franchise while maintaining comfortable capitalisation levels. 
Detailed description of key rating drivers 
 
Strong parentage and expected support from HBL  
HBL, the largest private sector bank in India designated as a Domestic Systemically Important Bank (D-SIB), holds 93.99% stake 
in HSL as on June 30, 2026. HSL benefits from the sharing of HDFC brand and linkages in the form of access to established 
customer base and franchise of the bank, and business synergies, which allows the HDFC group to offer a full bouquet of financial 
services. HSL benefits from the widespread presence of HBL across the nation with diverse clientele, which helps HSL in getting 
leads for its business. Over 85-90% of the company’s customers are HBL’s customers.

HSL has Board representation from HBL, where Arvind Vohra (Country Head of Retail Assets at HDFC Bank) is HBL’s Nominee 
Director. Several personnel serving in key roles at HSL have been previously associated with HBL. Dhiraj Relli, serving as the 
Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer (CEO), has worked as a Senior Executive Vice President and Head of Branch banking 
at HBL.  
From an operational perspective, HBL’s scale, widespread network, and the group’s overall diverse clientele aid HSL in lead 
generation for its broking business. Likewise, HSL’s broking customers serve in further strengthening the bank’s retail liability 
franchise. Given these significant managerial and operational linkages, name and brand sharing and a financially strong parent, 
CareEdge Ratings expects HBL to extend need-based support to HSL. 
 
Strong retail broking franchise  
HSL is ranked sixth in terms of active clients (~10.05 lakh clients as on July 31, 2026). It offers broking services across equity 
and derivatives market segments for retail and institutional clients and distributes third-party products, such as mutual funds, 
insurance policies, fixed deposits, and initial public offers (IPOs), among others. Along with these, the company also provides 
MTF, a short-term loan to customers, which stood at ₹10,081 crore as on June 30, 2026.  
In line with the industry trend toward digitalisation and a greater focus on digital offerings, the company streamlined its distribution 
network by consolidating branches, reducing the count to 127 as on June 30, 2026, from 184 as on March 31, 2024, with ~95% 
of total brokerage in Q1FY27, coming from retail clients.  
The company has its own discount broking application “HDFC Sky”, through which the company is targeting new-age investors. 
Seamless integration with HBL and benefits of common branding will continue to play a key role in growing and managing the 
overall broking franchise. 
 
Healthy earnings profile, despite the recent moderation in broking business 
In FY26, the company reported a total income of ₹3,110 crore and a profit after tax (PAT) of ₹930 crore, translating into a PAT 
margin of 29.90% and a return on total assets (ROTA) of 5.22%. In Q1FY27, the company reported a PAT of ₹297 crore, with a 
PAT margin and annualised ROTA of 31.27% and 6.01%, respectively. 
HSL’s income is primarily derived from brokerage and fee income, and interest income on its MTF book, which contributed 48% 
and 31%, respectively, of total income in FY26. The company’s total income witnessed moderation in FY26 over FY25 due to 
decline in brokerage income, considering unfavourable market conditions. Brokerage income has grown in Q1FY27, on a year-
over-year (y-o-y) basis due to improvement in trading volumes. Given that brokerage income is entirely capital market dependent, 
HSL has sought to diversify its revenue base by generating fee income from cross-selling financial products and interest income 
from MTF loans. 
Interest income rose slightly to ₹1,498 crore in FY26 from ₹1,418 crore in FY25, primarily considering increased margin 
requirements and investment income, despite subdued MTF growth. However, the MTF book increased sharply to ₹10,081 crore 
as of June 2026 from ₹7,133 crore as of March 2026 (₹5,512 crore as of March 2025). Alongside a moderation in ROTA and a 
reduction in gearing, the return on net worth (RONW) also declined, though it remained healthy at 27.54% in FY26, down from 
43.28% in FY25 and 51.63% in FY24. RONW improved to 34.0% in Q1FY27, mainly due to improvement in the market volumes. 
CareEdge Ratings expects profitability, particularly RONW, to improve over the coming quarters, contingent on market conditions, 
with MTF-linked interest income continuing to support diversification and growth. 
 
Comfortable capital position  
The company’s tangible net worth (TNW) increased gradually to ₹3,647 crore as on June 30, 2026, from ₹3,496 crore as on March 
31, 2026 (₹3,257 crore as on March 31, 2025), despite healthy earnings, considering sizeable dividend payouts.  
Until FY20, fund-based borrowings were primarily utilised to meet exchange obligations arising from the broking business. Since 
FY20, these borrowings have also been deployed to fund the MTF portfolio, leading to growth in borrowings in line with the 
expansion of the MTF book, which stood at ₹10,081 crore as on June 30, 2026. In addition to fund-based borrowings, the company 
avails bank guarantees to meet margin requirements with exchanges. 
Overall gearing increased to 4.64x as on March 31, 2026, from 2.65x as on March 31, 2025, and further to 5.28x as on June 30, 
2026, considering increased margin requirements, investments, and growth in MTF book. Nearly 86% of the total fund-based

Key weaknesses 
Presence in inherently volatile and competitive broking business 
HSL’s earnings profile significantly depend on capital market activity and trading volumes, which are inherently volatile. Brokerage 
income accounted for 33% of the total income in FY26, while income from the MTF portfolio contributed 31%, which is directly a 
function of the market performance. As HSL expands its MTF book to support its growth, it exposes the company to credit and 
market risk as adverse deviation in valuation of underlying securities can significantly affect the HSLs risk profile. Hence, the ability 
to grow MTF book while maintaining adequate capitalisation buffer and healthy asset quality shall continue to remain a key 
monitorable. In addition, income from the distribution of third-party products, such as mutual funds, insurance, bonds, fixed 
deposits, and IPOs provides diversification to the overall revenue base. 
The company continues to face competitive pressures in the brokerage industry, with low-cost and zero-brokerage firms gaining 
market share. However, with continued support from its parent bank, HSL has been able to retain its customer base and sustain 
operations in a highly competitive environment. In addition to diversifying revenues, the company remains focused on 
strengthening client relationships and continues to provide traditional (offline) and digital investment services. Given these 
competitive dynamics, the company’s ability to enhance its market share and profitability remains a key monitorable.  
Susceptibility to regulatory changes  
Capital market industry witnessed continuous regulatory revisions. To further enhance transparency levels and limit misuse, 
Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) introduced a few regulations in the last few years. In October 2024, SEBI introduced 
multiple measures to curb retail participation, which became effective in a phase-wise manner between November 2024 and April 
2025. These measures include reduction in derivatives with weekly expiry, which was highest Futures & Options (F&O) contributor, 
increase in lot size and increase in extreme loss margin by 2% on short options contracts on their expiry day. Other measures 
included upfront collection of option premiums and removal of Calendar Spread Treatment on expiry day.  
Through its amended directions dated February 13, 2026 (effective July 01, 2026), the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) introduced a 
comprehensive framework governing bank credit to capital market intermediaries, including stockbrokers. Revised norms mandate 
fully collateralised lending, prescribe higher collateral quality (with significant cash components), and impose minimum haircuts, 
particularly on equity securities. The bank funding for proprietary trading has been restricted, while margin trading facilities are 
required to be backed by high-quality liquid collateral. The framework also brings such exposures under stricter monitoring and 
concentration limits. These measures are expected to increase funding costs and necessitate stronger capitalisation and liquidity 
management across brokers to varying levels.  
CareEdge Ratings will continue to monitor HSL’s ability to adapt technology, systems, and risk management processes in response 
to the constantly evolving regulatory landscape without adverse impact on its overall business profile. 
Liquidity: Strong 
As on June 30, 2026, the company had a total fund-based borrowings of ₹18,286 crore which included CP of ₹15,765 crores, 
Treps borrowings of ₹2,183 crores and loan against deposits of ₹338 crores. Against these, it maintained an unencumbered cash 
and cash equivalent of ₹422 crore and unutilised sanctioned lines of credit of ₹2,698 crore. The company’s MTF book is a short-
term book, and the inflows from this book are also utilised to meet debt obligations. HSL also maintains excess margins with 
exchanges that can be deployed in case of exigencies. The company benefits from strong financial flexibility as a subsidiary of 
HBL.

Stockbroking and allied  
 
HSL was incorporated in April 2000 as a joint venture of HBL, HDFC Limited, and Indocean Securities Holdings Limited. It is 
engaged in a single line of business as a provider of broking services to its clients in the capital market. The company is a public 
limited company listed in debt market in Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE). HBL has been gradually increasing its stake in HSL. It 
obtained a controlling interest of 55% in HSL in FY06, and as on June 30, 2026, the bank’s overall shareholding in HSL stood at 
93.99%. The company is registered as a “Stockbroker” with SEBI and as a “Corporate Agent” with the Insurance Regulatory and 
Development Authority (IRDA). The company started with equity trading and later rolled out derivatives trading. Currently, it 
offers a broad suite of savings and investment products and core broking product. It also distributes third-party products and 
plays a key role in cross-selling products of its group companies. In FY19, HSL started offering MTF, as part of which the company 
extends short-term loans to its broking customers fully secured against cash and/ or collateral of approved securities.
