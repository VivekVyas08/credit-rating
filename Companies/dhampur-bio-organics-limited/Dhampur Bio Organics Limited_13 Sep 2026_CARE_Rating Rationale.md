## **Rationale and key rating drivers**

CARE Ratings Limited (CareEdge Ratings) has reaffirmed the ratings on the bank facilities of Dhampur Bio Organics Limited (DBOL) at 'CARE BBB+ / CARE A2' and **revised the outlook to Positive from Stable**.

The outlook revision reflects a **deleveraging that is contracted and substantially executed, but which the FY2026 balance sheet does not yet show**. DBOL entered a business transfer agreement in April 2026 to sell its Meerganj undertaking on a slump-sale basis for ₹305.00 crore, and the unit transferred in **June 2026 — the first quarter of FY2026-27**. Consequently **none of those proceeds are reflected in the 31 March 2026 figures presented below**, which still carry the full pre-sale debt of ₹1,060.46 crore. Long-term debt including current maturities stood at **₹289.66 crore** at that date; the proceeds are being applied against it. Because the event falls after the balance-sheet date, it is a forward catalyst rather than a completed improvement, and it is the principal reason the outlook moves rather than the rating.

The operating base under that transaction is also improving. Gross sugar recovery rose to **10.98% in FY2026 from 10.56%**, and net recovery to **10.74% from 9.80%**, as red-rot-vulnerable cane varieties were replaced across the western and central Uttar Pradesh command areas. The effect is visible in output rather than throughput: **cane crushed fell to 32.92 lakh tonne from 34.98 lakh tonne** (and 41.44 lakh tonne in FY2024, a 20.6% decline over two years), yet **sugar produced rose to 3.54 lakh tonne from 3.10 lakh tonne**, helped by the cessation of syrup diversion to ethanol (**3.37 lakh tonne of cane diverted in FY2025, nil in FY2026**). Inventory fell to **₹994.45 crore from ₹1,051.39 crore**, easing the working-capital cycle to 174 days of net operating income from 204.

Earnings have not yet followed. The PBILDT margin declined for a second year, to **6.29% from 7.43% and 7.53%**, as the state advised price for cane rose ₹30 per quintal to ₹400 while ethanol prices remained administered and unchanged. Interest coverage was **2.08 times** (FY2025: 2.09 times) and RoCE **3.46%**. Net debt/PBILDT stood at **8.06 times** against the 4.20 times CareEdge Ratings identifies as its positive trigger, and total debt/gross cash accruals at **12.67 times** on accruals of ₹83.73 crore. The balance sheet remains leveraged and is serviced by thin accruals; that is what holds the rating at BBB+ while the outlook moves.

The ratings continue to draw strength from the promoter group's long record in sugar and from forward integration — 20,500 TCD of residual crushing capacity, **76.5 MW of co-generation** and **312.5 KLPD** of biofuels and spirits capacity including 100 KLPD of dual feed (molasses and grain), which lets the distillery run on grain when cane is short. They remain constrained by reduced scale and single-state concentration following the divestment, and by the cyclical, administered nature of the industry.

CareEdge Ratings notes that the **Income Tax Department conducted a search under Section 132 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 at the Company's head office and all other premises from 29 October 2025 to 04 November 2025**. The Company states it extended full co-operation, that **uncertainty exists regarding the outcome**, and that it has identified no need for any adjustment to the financial statements; cash of ₹0.16 crore was held with the Department at 31 March 2026 (previous year: nil). No demand is quantified in the audited financials. A material adverse outcome would be negative for the ratings.

## **Rating sensitivities: Factors likely to lead to rating actions**

### **Positive factors**

- PBILDT margin sustained above **8.0%** backed by recovery sustained at or above 11%, together with net debt/PBILDT below **4.20 times** on a sustained basis.
- Completion of the announced debt prepayment from the Meerganj proceeds, with interest coverage sustained above **3.00 times**.

### **Negative factors**

- Overall gearing rising above **1.30 times** on a sustained basis.
- PBILDT margin declining below **6.0%**, or interest coverage below 1.50 times, on a sustained basis.
- Any material adverse outcome of the Income-tax proceedings, or any crystallised demand, that weakens liquidity or the capital structure.
- Adverse change in government policy on cane pricing, ethanol procurement or sugar release quotas.

## **Analytical approach:** Standalone

DBOL is assessed on a **standalone** basis. Its two wholly owned subsidiaries (Annexure-6) are not operationally integrated with the rated entity's sugar and distillery value chain: Dhampur International Pte. Ltd. reported **nil revenue** in FY2026, and Sonitron Bio Organics Private Limited is a separately managed trading operation. Their combined effect on the rated entity's credit profile is immaterial — consolidating them moves tangible net worth by ₹3.04 crore and leaves total debt unchanged, since neither carries borrowings. **This basis matches the one CareEdge Ratings applied to this issuer in its action of 13 January 2026.**

## **Outlook: Positive**

The Positive outlook reflects the expectation that the Meerganj proceeds will materially reduce long-term debt during FY2027, and that improving recovery and firm sugar realisations will lift accruals from the FY2026 base. An upgrade would require that deleveraging to be evidenced in the balance sheet alongside a sustained margin recovery.

## **Detailed description of key rating drivers:**

### **Key strengths**

**Forward-integrated operations moderate exposure to the sugar cycle** – The Company operates 20,500 TCD of residual crushing capacity across Asmoli and Mansurpur, 76.5 MW of bagasse-based co-generation, and 312.5 KLPD of biofuels and spirits capacity on B-heavy molasses, of which 100 KLPD is **dual feed**, capable of running on molasses or grain. Dual feed is the material protection: it allows the distillery to operate on grain in a short cane year, which is precisely the exposure the FY2024-25 red-rot episode created.

**Cane recovery has turned, and output rose on falling throughput** – Gross recovery improved to 10.98% from 10.56% and net recovery to 10.74% from 9.80%. Cane crushed nonetheless fell to 32.92 lakh tonne from 34.98 lakh tonne, while sugar produced **rose** to 3.54 lakh tonne from 3.10 lakh tonne — the combined effect of better recovery and the cessation of syrup diversion to ethanol (3.37 lakh tonne of cane in FY2025, nil in FY2026). Inventory days eased to 174 from 204.

**Contracted deleveraging from the Meerganj slump sale** – The undertaking was transferred in June 2026 for ₹305.00 crore against long-term debt of ₹289.66 crore at 31 March 2026. The transaction removes a unit in the catchment worst affected by red rot, so its disposal should be accretive to blended recovery as well as to leverage.

### **Key weaknesses**

**Thin and declining profitability with modest coverage** – The PBILDT margin fell for a second year, to 6.29% from 7.43% and 7.53%, and interest coverage was 2.08 times. Total debt/gross cash accruals remained elevated at 12.67 times and RoCE was 3.46%. Yield recovered; cost rose faster.

**Working-capital-intensive operations on a low unencumbered cash base** – Production is concentrated in the October–April crushing season against year-round demand. Working-capital borrowings were **₹764.51 crore** at 31 March 2026 — that is current borrowings of ₹847.19 crore **less ₹82.68 crore of current maturities of long-term debt**, a distinction that materially changes both the working-capital and the term-debt picture. Drawing power is a function of inventory valuation, exposing the Company to sugar price declines.

**Reduced scale and single-state concentration in a regulated industry** – Post-divestment the Company operates two plants in one western/central UP command area. Cane price is set by the state advised price without reference to sugar realisations; ethanol prices are administered; release quotas are regulated.

**Open Income-tax proceedings** – The Section 132 search of October–November 2025 remains without a determined outcome, and the Company itself records that uncertainty exists.

## **Liquidity:** Adequate

Liquidity is adequate. **Unencumbered cash was ₹4.50 crore at 31 March 2026** — cash and cash equivalents only. A further ₹6.19 crore of "bank balances other than cash and cash equivalents" is **excluded** because the Company's own notes show it comprises deposits held as security or margin against guarantees and deposits earmarked for the Molasses Storage Fund; it cannot be applied to debt service. Against that, gross cash accruals were ₹83.73 crore in FY2026, set against scheduled long-term repayments and maintenance capital expenditure of about ₹45 crore. The principal internal source is the seasonal unwinding of the ₹994.45 crore inventory, and the principal external source is the ₹305.00 crore of Meerganj proceeds received in Q1 FY2027. The current ratio was 1.14 times. The descriptor reflects accruals that cover scheduled obligations with limited headroom on a very small free-cash base, mitigated by a large self-liquidating inventory and by sanctioned limits. Month-wise utilisation of working-capital limits is to be confirmed by the issuer — see Annexure-1.

## **Environment, social, and governance (ESG) risks**

**Environmental** – Sugar and distillery operations are water- and effluent-intensive. The Company reports water intensity of 0.00008 ml per rupee of turnover and combined Scope 1 and Scope 2 emission intensity of 0.47 gCO2e per rupee of turnover in FY2025-26 (0.11 in the prior year), with waste intensity of 0.00000441 gm per rupee. Bagasse co-generation and molasses/grain ethanol place the Company on the supply side of India's renewable-fuel transition.

**Social** – Operations depend on a large farmer catchment in western and central Uttar Pradesh; cane price is administered and timely payment to farmers is a licence-to-operate issue.

**Governance** – The audit report on the FY2025-26 standalone financial statements carries no qualification. The Section 132 Income-tax search is the single open governance-relevant matter, disclosed by the Company in note 36(c).

## **About the company and industry**

Dhampur Bio Organics Limited was demerged from Dhampur Sugar Mills Limited under a Scheme of Arrangement effective 03 May 2022. It manufactures sugar, ethanol and other biofuels, extra neutral alcohol and country liquor, generates renewable power from bagasse, and produces organic manure and carbon dioxide. It operated three integrated units at Asmoli, Mansurpur and Meerganj until March 2026; **the Meerganj unit (9,000 TCD of crushing and 19 MW of co-generation) was sold in June 2026**, leaving 20,500 TCD across two plants.

***Operating-profit normalisation — the treatment named (failure mode 36).*** *PBILDT is struck on total operating income net of excise, starting from **profit before exceptional items** and deducting **other income in full**. Consequently: exceptional items are excluded by construction (FY2025: ₹4.96 crore; nil in FY2024 and FY2026); other income of ₹30.97 crore in FY2026 is removed entirely; the **loss on sale of property, plant and equipment (₹2.32 crore) is retained** in PBILDT because it sits within other expenses — the convention is asymmetric and this is stated rather than assumed; and **no foreign-exchange line appears** in the FY2025-26 standalone notes, the only forex amounts anywhere in the document set being ≤₹1.42 crore in the FY2023-24 annual report.*

***Divergence against CareEdge's published figures, disclosed not forced (failure mode 32).*** *On the convention above, PBILDT is 137.88 / 139.93 / 131.04. CareEdge publishes 143 / 140 / 134. FY2025 reproduces almost exactly; FY2024 and FY2026 are 5.12 and 2.96 short, most likely because CareEdge adds back the loss on asset sale and small non-operating items inside other expenses. **The inputs are not adjusted to close the gap** — a definition that will not reproduce is a definition to disclose, not to force. Ratios struck on PBILDT here therefore differ slightly from theirs: net debt/PBILDT 8.06 times against their 7.9 times, and interest coverage 2.08 against their 2.13.*
