## **Rationale and key rating drivers**

CARE Ratings Limited (CareEdge Ratings) has reaffirmed the ratings on the bank facilities of Dhampur Bio Organics Limited (DBOL) at 'CARE BBB+; Stable / CARE A2'.

The reaffirmation reflects a **stabilising operating profile set against still-weak accrual coverage**. The cane-yield problem that drove the January 2026 downgrade has begun to reverse: gross sugar recovery improved to **10.98% in FY2025-26 from 10.56%**, and net recovery to **10.74% from 9.80%**, as the replacement of red-rot-vulnerable cane varieties took effect in the Company's western and central Uttar Pradesh command areas. Working-capital intensity eased materially, with inventory of **₹1,013.17 crore** at March 31, 2026 equal to **175 days** of net operating income against **215 days** a year earlier. Deleveraging was real: total debt including leases fell to **₹1,060.46 crore from ₹1,162.34 crore**, overall gearing to **1.03 times from 1.15 times**, and total debt/gross cash accruals to **12.63 times from 16.95 times**, assisted by the receipt of **₹305.00 crore** from the Meerganj divestment applied towards term-loan prepayment.

Those gains have **not yet reached the profit and loss account**. The PBILDT margin fell for a second year, to **6.20% in FY2026 from 7.24% in FY2025 and 7.32% in FY2024**, as the state advised price for cane rose ₹30 per quintal while ethanol prices remained administered and unchanged, compressing distillery realisations. Interest coverage was **2.08 times** (FY2025: 2.01 times; FY2024: 3.01 times) and return on capital employed **3.45%**. Net debt/PBILDT stood at **7.83 times**, well outside the 4.20 times that CareEdge Ratings has identified as its positive trigger for this issuer. The Company therefore retains a leveraged balance sheet serviced by thin accruals, and the rating remains constrained accordingly.

The ratings continue to draw strength from the promoter group's long operating record in sugar and from **forward integration** — 20,500 TCD of crushing across two plants and **312.5 KLPD** of biofuels and spirits capacity, including 100 KLPD of dual-feed (molasses and grain) capacity — which moderates, without removing, exposure to the sugar cycle. They remain constrained by the reduced scale and single-state concentration that followed the Meerganj divestment, by the cyclical and administered nature of the industry, and by agro-climatic risk.

CareEdge Ratings notes that the **Income Tax Department conducted a search under Section 132 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 at the Company's head office and all other premises from October 29, 2025 to November 04, 2025**. The Company has stated that it extended full co-operation, that **uncertainty exists regarding the outcome**, and that it has not identified the need for any adjustment to the financial statements; cash of **₹0.16 crore** was held with the Department at March 31, 2026 (previous year: nil). No demand has been quantified in the audited financial statements. A material adverse outcome would be negative for the ratings.

## **Rating sensitivities: Factors likely to lead to rating actions**

### **Positive factors**

- PBILDT margin sustained above **8.0%** on the back of sustained gross recovery at or above 11%, together with net debt/PBILDT reducing below **4.20 times** on a sustained basis.
- Interest coverage sustained above **3.00 times**.

### **Negative factors**

- Overall gearing rising above **1.30 times** on a sustained basis.
- Interest coverage falling below **1.50 times**, or PBILDT margin below 5.0%, on a sustained basis.
- Any material adverse outcome of the Income-tax proceedings, or any crystallised demand, that weakens liquidity or the capital structure.

## **Outlook:** Stable

The Stable outlook reflects CareEdge Ratings' expectation that improving cane recovery and the eased working-capital cycle will hold the credit profile broadly where it is over the next 12 months, with leverage contained near current levels, while thin margins and modest coverage prevent any near-term strengthening.

## **Detailed description of key rating drivers:**

### **Key strengths**

**Forward-integrated operations across sugar, distillery and co-generation reduce single-product exposure** – The Company operates 20,500 TCD of cane crushing across Asmoli and Mansurpur and 312.5 KLPD of biofuels and spirits capacity on B-heavy molasses, of which 100 KLPD is dual-feed, capable of running on either molasses or grain. Dual feed matters in a year when cane is short: it lets the distillery run on grain when molasses availability is constrained, which is precisely the exposure that the FY2024-25 red-rot episode created. Ethanol production was 15 million bulk litres in Q1 FY2026-27 with 6 million bulk litres of closing stock at June 30, 2026.

**Cane recovery and the working-capital cycle both improved in FY2026** – Gross recovery rose to 10.98% from 10.56% and net recovery to 10.74% from 9.80%, following the replacement of disease-vulnerable cane varieties and improved pest management. Inventory days fell to 175 from 215 and receivable days to 11.04 from 10.74 on a materially larger revenue base. This is the operating evidence behind the Stable outlook.

**Deleveraging following the Meerganj divestment** – Proceeds of ₹305.00 crore were applied towards term-loan prepayment. Total debt including lease liabilities reduced to ₹1,060.46 crore at March 31, 2026 from ₹1,162.34 crore, overall gearing to 1.03 times from 1.15 times and TOL/TNW to 1.26 times from 1.37 times, against a tangible net worth of ₹1,026.64 crore.

### **Key weaknesses**

**Thin and declining profitability with modest debt coverage** – The PBILDT margin declined for a second consecutive year, to 6.20% in FY2026 from 7.24% and 7.32% in the two preceding years, and interest coverage remained modest at 2.08 times. Total debt/gross cash accruals, while improved, remained elevated at 12.63 times on gross cash accruals of ₹83.99 crore, and RoCE was 3.45%. The direction of the margin — not its level alone — is the binding constraint on the rating: yield has recovered but cost has risen faster.

**Reduced scale and single-state concentration in a cyclical, administered industry** – Crushing capacity reduced to 20,500 TCD from 29,500 TCD following the Meerganj divestment, concentrating operations in two plants within western and central Uttar Pradesh. Revenue and profitability remain exposed to the state advised price mechanism, which is set without reference to sugar realisations, to monthly release quotas, and to administered ethanol pricing which has remained unchanged. Agro-climatic and infestation risk is concentrated in a single command area, as the red-rot episode demonstrated.

**Working-capital-intensive operations dependent on seasonal borrowings** – Production is concentrated in the October-April crushing season against year-round demand, requiring high inventory funded through working-capital facilities; current borrowings were ₹847.19 crore at March 31, 2026. Drawing power is a function of inventory valuation, exposing the Company to sugar price declines.

**Open Income-tax proceedings** – The Section 132 search of October-November 2025 remains without a determined outcome, and the Company itself records that uncertainty exists.

## **Liquidity:** Adequate

Liquidity is adequate. Against scheduled long-term repayments of approximately ₹26 crore per annum on the reduced term-debt base, maintenance capital expenditure of about ₹45 crore and dividends, the Company generated gross cash accruals of ₹83.99 crore in FY2026 and held cash and bank balances of ₹36.16 crore at March 31, 2026 (cash and cash equivalents ₹8.71 crore plus other bank balances ₹27.45 crore). Seasonal unwinding of the ₹1,013.17 crore inventory is the principal internal source. The current ratio was 1.16 times. The descriptor reflects accruals that cover scheduled obligations with limited headroom, against a high but seasonally self-liquidating working-capital position funded by sanctioned bank limits. Month-wise utilisation of working-capital limits is to be confirmed by the issuer and is not available in the documents reviewed — see the searched-and-absent note in Annexure-1.

## **Environment, social, and governance (ESG) risks**

**Environmental** – Sugar and distillery operations are water- and effluent-intensive. The Company reports water intensity of 0.00008 ml per rupee of turnover and combined Scope 1 and Scope 2 emission intensity of 0.47 gCO2e per rupee of turnover in FY2025-26 (0.11 in the prior year), with waste intensity of 0.00000441 gm per rupee of turnover. Co-generation from bagasse and ethanol from molasses and grain place the Company on the supply side of India's renewable-fuel transition, which is a structural mitigant.

**Social** – Operations depend on a large farmer catchment in western and central Uttar Pradesh; cane price is administered and payment timeliness to farmers is a licence-to-operate issue for the sector.

**Governance** – The audit report on the FY2025-26 consolidated financial statements carries no qualification. The Section 132 Income-tax search described above is the single open governance-relevant matter and is disclosed by the Company in note 39(c) to the consolidated financial statements.

## **About the company and industry**

Dhampur Bio Organics Limited, incorporated following the 2021 demerger of Dhampur Sugar Mills Limited, manufactures sugar, ethanol and other biofuels, extra neutral alcohol and country liquor, generates renewable power from bagasse, and produces organic manure and carbon dioxide. It operates two integrated units at Asmoli and Mansurpur in Uttar Pradesh with aggregate crushing capacity of 20,500 TCD and 312.5 KLPD of biofuels and spirits capacity. The Meerganj unit was divested during FY2026-27's opening quarter for consideration of ₹305.00 crore.

Consequently: (i) exceptional items are excluded by construction (FY2025: ₹1.76 crore; nil in FY2024 and FY2026); (ii) other income of ₹31.83 crore in FY2026 is removed entirely, taking with it interest income of ₹1.72 crore and provision/balance write-backs of ₹6.35 crore and ₹5.12 crore that sit inside it; (iii) on the expense side the **loss on sale of property, plant and equipment of ₹2.32 crore is retained** in PBILDT, because it sits within other expenses — the convention is asymmetric and this is stated rather than assumed; at 0.11% of net operating income it does not change the margin conclusion; (iv) **forex:** no foreign-exchange line appears in the FY2025-26 annual report's other income or other expenses notes.
