## **Rationale and key rating drivers**

The ratings assigned to the bank facilities of NABKISAN Finance Limited (NABKISAN) derive their
principal strength from the company's **ownership by the National Bank for Agriculture and Rural
Development (NABARD), which holds 87.77%**. NABKISAN operates as NABARD's lending vehicle to farmer
producer organisations, primary agricultural cooperative societies, microfinance institutions and
allied rural enterprises — a role that is central to NABARD's own developmental mandate rather than
incidental to it. The ratings factor in an expectation of ongoing and, if required, extraordinary
support, reflected in the funding structure: borrowings from related parties dominate the liability
side.

The ratings also factor in a materially enlarged and better-capitalised entity following the
**amalgamation of NABSAMRUDDHI Finance Limited into NABKISAN**, sanctioned by the National Company
Law Tribunal, Chennai by order dated 30 October 2025. Both entities were NABARD subsidiaries, so
the combination is under common control and has been accounted by pooling of interests, with the
FY2025 comparative restated. Total assets rose to ₹7,238.23 crore as on March 31, 2026 and the loan
book to ₹6,571.24 crore, up 36.06% on a like-for-like basis.

Profitability is sound. Net interest income rose to ₹207.19 crore from ₹182.45 crore, return on
average assets was 2.09% and return on average equity 13.81%, with an efficient cost structure —
cost-to-income improved to 16.27% from 22.38%.

These strengths are tempered by three factors. **Leverage has risen sharply**, with borrowings to
net worth at 6.04x as on March 31, 2026 against 4.88x a year earlier. **Capital quality has
weakened even as headline adequacy improved** — CRAR rose to 22.26% from 18.12%, but entirely
through a Tier II raise; the Tier I ratio *fell* to 14.73% from 17.61%. And **reported earnings in
both years are flattered by provision write-backs**, without which growth looks different in
direction and magnitude (see below). The credit remains concentrated in agriculture and rural
lending, a segment exposed to monsoon, crop-cycle and policy risk.

## **Rating sensitivities: Factors likely to lead to rating actions**

### **Positive factors**

- A demonstrable strengthening of NABARD's support framework — an explicit guarantee, a committed
  capital-support undertaking, or a formal statement of strategic importance.
- Tier I ratio restored above 18% alongside gearing sustained below 5.0x.

### **Negative factors**

- Any dilution of NABARD's shareholding below a controlling threshold, or evidence of reduced
  operational or funding linkage with the parent.
- Gearing rising above 7.0x, or the Tier I ratio falling below 12%.
- A material rise in credit costs, particularly any reversal of the provision write-backs of
  FY2025 and FY2026, or deterioration in asset quality in the FPO and microfinance portfolios.

## **Analytical approach:** Standalone, with parent-linkage notching

The ratings are based on NABKISAN's standalone financials, notched up for linkages with NABARD per
CareEdge's *Notching by Factoring Linkages with Parent* criteria. The company has no subsidiaries,
joint ventures or associates of its own; NABSAMRUDDHI Finance Limited was amalgamated into it and
no longer exists as a separate entity.

_The notch-up reflects majority ownership (87.77%), strategic fit with NABARD's developmental
mandate, shared brand, and demonstrated funding support. It is **not** sized against a quantified
committed-support undertaking — no guarantee or letter of comfort was located in the documents
reviewed. Under CareEdge's criteria, where support is formally committed the uplift is restricted
to the committed amount; here the assessment rests on the strength of linkage rather than a
quantified commitment, and that distinction is stated rather than assumed away._

## **Outlook:** Stable

The Stable outlook reflects an expectation that NABARD's ownership and funding support will
continue, that the enlarged post-merger platform will be absorbed without disruption to asset
quality, and that leverage will stabilise at current levels rather than continuing to rise.

## **Detailed description of key rating drivers:**

### **Key strengths**

**Majority ownership by NABARD and a strategic role in its developmental mandate**

NABARD holds 87.77% of NABKISAN. The company lends to farmer producer organisations, primary
agricultural cooperative societies, microfinance institutions, NGOs and rural enterprises —
channels through which NABARD executes its statutory developmental role. This is materially
stronger than a purely financial holding: the subsidiary is an instrument of the parent's own
mandate, which raises both the incentive and the reputational cost of non-support.

Support is visible in the funding structure rather than only asserted. Borrowings from related
parties stood at ₹310.21 crore against ₹22.28 crore from unrelated parties in the comparative
disclosure reviewed, and the registered office is located within NABARD's Tamil Nadu Regional
Office building.

**Materially enlarged platform following the NABSAMRUDDHI amalgamation**

The scheme sanctioned by the NCLT Chennai on 30 October 2025 vested all assets, liabilities and
undertakings of NABSAMRUDDHI Finance Limited into NABKISAN. Consideration was 11,74,17,402 equity
shares of ₹10 each, at an exchange ratio of 55 NABKISAN shares for every 58 NABSAMRUDDHI shares,
with the ₹640.46 lakh difference between transferor paid-up capital and consideration taken to
Capital Reserve.

Because both entities were under NABARD's common control, the combination is accounted by pooling
of interests — book values combined line by line, intra-group balances eliminated, uniform
accounting policies applied — and **the FY2025 comparative is restated onto the merged basis**. The
36.06% loan-book growth reported below is therefore organic and like-for-like, not an artefact of
the merger.

**Sound profitability with an efficient cost structure**

Net interest income rose 13.6% to ₹207.19 crore, equivalent to 3.30% of average total assets.
Return on average assets was 2.09% and return on average equity 13.81%. The cost structure is the
standout: cost-to-income improved to 16.27% from 22.38%, reflecting a wholesale lending model with
few branches and low origination cost per rupee lent — operating expenses of ₹34.46 crore support a
₹6,571 crore book.

**Comfortable headline capital adequacy**

CRAR stood at 22.26% as on March 31, 2026 against 18.12% a year earlier, comfortably above the
regulatory minimum. Total regulatory capital rose to ₹1,528.64 crore from ₹906.15 crore. The
company reports compliance with all RBI capital requirements, and single-borrower and group
exposures in excess of the prescribed Tier I thresholds are reported as nil.

### **Key weaknesses**

**Sharply higher leverage**

Borrowings rose 41.7% to ₹6,137.15 crore as on March 31, 2026 from ₹4,330.04 crore, against net
worth growth of 14.4% to ₹1,016.03 crore. Gearing consequently rose to **6.04x from 4.88x**. The
loan book grew faster than internal accrual could fund, and the gap was met with debt. For an NBFC
this is the metric that most directly constrains the rating in the absence of fresh equity, and a
continuation of this trajectory would be a negative.

**Capital quality weakened even as headline adequacy improved**

This deserves emphasis because the headline figure points the other way. CRAR improved 414 basis
points to 22.26%, but the entire improvement — and more — came from Tier II, which rose to ₹517.22
crore from ₹25.39 crore. The **Tier I ratio fell to 14.73% from 17.61%**, a decline of 288 basis
points.

Tier II is subordinated debt, not loss-absorbing equity in the sense Tier I is, and it amortises
toward maturity. A reader looking only at CRAR would conclude capitalisation strengthened; on the
quality of that capital the direction is the opposite. Risk-weighted assets grew 37.3% to ₹6,866.29
crore while Tier I capital grew only 14.8%.

**Reported earnings are flattered by provision write-backs in both years**

The impairment line is a **credit** in both FY2025 and FY2026 — a write-back of previously held
provisions, not a charge. Normalising it out changes both the level and the growth rate:

| ₹ crore | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Profit before tax, as reported | 160.69 | 179.81 |
| Less: provision write-back credit | (15.73) | (2.50) |
| **Profit before tax, normalised** | **144.97** | **177.31** |
| Reported PBT growth | — | **+11.90%** |
| **Normalised PBT growth** | — | **+22.31%** |

The direction of this adjustment is favourable — FY2025 carried the larger write-back, so
normalising *improves* the growth comparison from 11.9% to 22.3%. But the level of profit in both
years is lower than reported, and the sustainability question stands: a write-back is a
non-recurring credit, and the impairment allowance on loans has fallen to 0.88% of the book from
1.26%. Whether that reflects genuine asset-quality improvement or a thinning cushion is the key
monitorable.

**Sectoral concentration in agriculture and rural lending**

The portfolio is concentrated in farmer producer organisations, cooperatives and microfinance
institutions. These borrowers are exposed to monsoon performance, crop cycles, commodity prices and
farm-loan-waiver policy — risks that are correlated across the book rather than diversifiable
within it. The developmental character of the mandate that makes parent support likely is the same
characteristic that limits portfolio diversification.

## **Liquidity: Adequate**

Liquidity is adequate, supported by access to NABARD funding. Cash and cash equivalents stood at
₹156.80 crore and other bank balances at ₹54.22 crore as on March 31, 2026, with investments of
₹437.15 crore providing a further cushion — ₹648.17 crore of liquid resources in aggregate against
borrowings of ₹6,137.15 crore. Cash reduced from ₹242.20 crore a year earlier as the loan book
expanded, though other bank balances and investments both rose materially.

The decisive liquidity consideration is not the balance-sheet cushion but the funding relationship:
as a majority-owned NABARD subsidiary funded substantially by related-party borrowings, refinancing
access is a function of the parent relationship rather than of market appetite.

_A dated asset-liability maturity ladder and a facility-wise utilisation statement were not located
in the documents reviewed; the assessment rests on the balance-sheet and funding-structure evidence
cited. Recorded rather than presented as a complete ALM review._

## **Environment, social, and governance (ESG) risks**

**Environmental:** Direct environmental exposure is minimal — the company is a wholesale lender
without industrial operations. Indirect exposure arises through the agricultural portfolio, which
is materially exposed to physical climate risk: monsoon variability, drought and flood affect
borrower repayment capacity directly and across the book simultaneously. This is a credit
transmission channel rather than a compliance matter.

**Social:** The social profile is unusually favourable and is the substance of the business rather
than an overlay. Lending to farmer producer organisations, primary agricultural cooperative
societies and microfinance institutions extends formal credit to rural borrowers who are otherwise
underserved — the financial-inclusion purpose for which NABARD established the company. The
counterpart is conduct risk: microfinance and rural lending are subject to close RBI supervision on
fair practices, pricing and recovery conduct, where a lapse carries regulatory and reputational
consequences disproportionate to its financial size.

**Governance:** The company is a majority-owned subsidiary of a statutory development finance
institution, and is board-managed with a Chairperson and a Managing Director & CEO in office. The
FY2026 financial statements were audited by L.U. Krishnan & Co., Chartered Accountants. The company
reports compliance with all RBI capital and exposure requirements, no loan account restructured
during the year, and nil single-borrower and group exposures above the prescribed Tier I
thresholds. Quantified ESG metrics beyond CSR disclosure were **not located** in the documents
reviewed — searched for `Scope 1`, `Scope 2`, `emission intensity` and `energy consumption` with no
quantified disclosure returned, consistent with an unlisted NBFC not subject to BRSR reporting.

## **About the company and industry**

NABKISAN Finance Limited (CIN U65191TN1997PLC037525), incorporated in 1997 and headquartered in
Chennai, is a non-banking financial company 87.77% owned by the National Bank for Agriculture and
Rural Development. It lends to farmer producer organisations, primary agricultural cooperative
societies, microfinance institutions, NGOs and rural enterprises across India. Effective under the
NCLT scheme of 30 October 2025, NABSAMRUDDHI Finance Limited — a fellow NABARD subsidiary — was
amalgamated into the company.

_A second CIN, U65191TN1997PLC003752, appears once in the FY2025 annual report. It is a
transposition of the digits in U65191TN1997PLC037525, which appears five times across three
documents including the FY2026 financial statements and is the correct identifier._

The Indian agricultural and rural finance sector is characterised by developmental policy support,
priority-sector lending obligations on banks, and borrower cash flows tied to crop cycles and
monsoon performance. Institutions in this space typically operate with concentrated sectoral
exposure offset by strong sponsor or government linkage.
