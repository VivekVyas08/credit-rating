## **Rationale and key rating drivers**

The rating on the Tier II bonds of NABKISAN Finance Limited (NABKISAN) is driven by the company's
**strong parentage of the National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD)**, which
holds **89.12%**, and by its strategic role in extending credit to farmer producer organisations
(FPOs), agri-startups, non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) and microfinance institutions (MFIs)
in furtherance of NABARD's own developmental mandate.

Support is structural rather than discretionary, and is visible across four channels: equity
ownership; **on-call refinance facilities that constitute 87.70% of the borrowing profile**;
managerial deputation, with the Managing Director & CEO and the Chief Financial Officer both
NABARD officers; and board representation through a NABARD nominee director. NABARD itself is
India's apex development finance institution for the rural sector, with advances of ₹9,32,549
crore, total assets of ₹10,98,318 crore and tangible net worth of ₹88,793 crore as on March 31,
2026.

The **completed merger of NABSAMRUDDHI Finance Limited into NABKISAN**, effective 16 December 2025
under a scheme sanctioned by the NCLT Chennai (order dated 30 October 2025), has enlarged the
platform and diversified the product set into housing finance, renewable energy and water,
sanitation and hygiene (WASH) financing alongside the traditional agriculture book. Post-merger
assets under management stood at **₹7,066 crore** with tangible net worth of **₹1,011 crore** as on
March 31, 2026.

The rating further factors in adequate profitability and capitalisation and comfortable asset
quality — return on total assets of 2.09%, capital adequacy of 22.26% and gross stage III assets of
0.85%. Support was demonstrated again after the year end through a **rights issue of ₹301.46 crore
completed in Q2FY27**.

These strengths are tempered by a moderate scale of operations and a concentrated exposure profile,
with the top 25 exposures accounting for 55.65% of the book.

## **Rating sensitivities: Factors likely to lead to rating actions**

### **Positive factors**

- Not applicable — the rating is at the highest level on CareEdge Ratings' scale.

### **Negative factors**

- Deterioration in the credit profile of the parent, NABARD.
- Dilution in NABARD's stake below 51%, or dilution in expected support from, and strategic
  importance to, NABARD.

## **Analytical approach:** Standalone

CareEdge Ratings has adopted a standalone approach for analysing NABKISAN, while factoring in
linkages with NABARD for its strong parentage and operational, financial and managerial support.
NABSAMRUDDHI Finance Limited was amalgamated into the company and no longer exists as a separate
entity; the company has no subsidiaries, joint ventures or associates of its own.

## **Outlook: Stable**

The Stable outlook factors in the expectation that NABKISAN will continue to receive need-based
support from NABARD, and will profitably scale up its portfolio while maintaining comfortable
capitalisation and asset quality.

## **Detailed description of key rating drivers**

### **Key strengths**

**Strong parentage and a strategic role in developing the agriculture and rural sector**

Established in 1997, NABKISAN is a subsidiary of NABARD and executes a defined part of the parent's
statutory mandate: wholesale lending to FPOs, agri-startups, agri-based corporates, NBFCs and MFIs.
Its lending to NBFCs and MFIs serving rural and micro borrowers reaches segments NABARD does not
serve directly, complementing rather than duplicating the parent.

The support structure is multi-layered and specific:

- **Equity** — NABARD holds 89.12%, alongside the Government of Tamil Nadu and institutional
  shareholders including Indian Bank, Indian Overseas Bank, Tamilnad Mercantile Bank, Canara Bank,
  ICICI Bank, Federal Bank and DBS Bank India.
- **Funding** — on-call refinance facilities from NABARD constitute **87.70% of the borrowing
  profile**, giving both cost and availability advantages no market-funded peer can replicate.
- **Capital** — catalytic capital funds for agri-startups and innovation, and the ₹301.46 crore
  rights issue completed in Q2FY27.
- **Credit protection** — guarantee cover through **NABSanrakshan Trustee Private Limited**, a
  fellow NABARD subsidiary, providing government-backed coverage of up to **85% of the loan amount
  subject to a cap of ₹2 crore per eligible FPO loan**, supplemented by guarantee funds from the
  Governments of Tamil Nadu and Odisha.
- **Management and governance** — the Managing Director & CEO and Chief Financial Officer are
  officers deputed from NABARD; a NABARD nominee director sits on the board.

Given this integration, support is expected to be forthcoming in a timely manner, and NABARD is
expected to retain strategic control and oversight.

**Adequate capitalisation metrics**

Tangible net worth rose to **₹1,011 crore** as on March 31, 2026 from ₹881 crore a year earlier,
and further to ₹1,042 crore as on June 30, 2026. Capital adequacy stood at **22.26%** as on
March 31, 2026 (18.11% a year earlier) and 22.80% as on June 30, 2026.

Gearing rose to **6.07x** as on March 31, 2026 from 4.92x, before moderating to 5.53x as on
June 30, 2026. The ₹301.46 crore rights issue completed in Q2FY27 further strengthens the capital
base; leverage is expected to remain in the 5–6x range.

The company has begun diversifying its resource base beyond NABARD refinance, securing a working
capital line from a public sector bank in April 2025 and issuing the Tier II non-convertible
debentures rated here in February 2026.

**Adequate profitability metrics**

Profit after tax was **₹132 crore** in FY2026 on total income of ₹545 crore, against ₹126 crore and
₹472 crore respectively in FY2025. Return on total assets was **2.09%** and return on net worth
**13.90%**, against 2.57% and 15.34% in FY2025.

The moderation reflects **net interest margin compression as the company reduced exposure to
high-yield MFI loans** — a deliberate mix shift rather than a pricing failure. Operating efficiency
improved, with operating expenses at **0.45% of average total assets** against 0.52%, reflecting
economies of scale post-merger. Credit costs were stable at **0.05%** (FY2025: −0.04%), the
negative prior-year figure arising from reversal of expected credit loss provisions created in
earlier years.

In Q1FY27 the company reported profit after tax of ₹31 crore, with annualised ROTA of 1.74% and
RONW of 11.95%, affected by higher provisions against incremental non-performing assets.

**Comfortable asset quality**

Gross stage III assets improved to **0.85%** as on March 31, 2026 from 0.86% (pre-merger NABKISAN:
1.31% as on March 31, 2025 and 2.00% as on March 31, 2024). Net stage III assets stood at **0.27%**
against 0.25%. As on June 30, 2026, gross stage III was 0.94% and net stage III 0.37%.

Asset quality is materially de-risked by the NABSanrakshan guarantee mechanism and the Tamil Nadu
and Odisha state guarantee funds described above.

### **Key weaknesses**

**Moderate scale of operations with concentrated exposures**

Assets under management were **₹7,066 crore** as on March 31, 2026 (₹6,852 crore as on June 30,
2026), with AUM to tangible net worth of 6.99x. Growth was driven partly by the merger, through
higher disbursements, an expanded product portfolio and wider geographic reach.

Concentration is the more material constraint, and it is **counterparty rather than sectoral**. The
top 25 exposures accounted for **55.65%** of total exposures as on March 31, 2026, up from 49.71% a
year earlier; the single largest exposure was 3.04% of the loan book and **19.95% of tangible net
worth**. The portfolio is skewed toward bulk lending, with refinance to NBFCs and MFIs at **87.18%**,
followed by pass-through certificates (6.17%), direct lending (2.85%), direct assignment (2.62%),
agri-corporates (0.65%), on-lending to producer organisations (0.28%) and activity-based financing
through NGOs, trusts and Section 8 companies (0.25%).

Concentration risk is partly mitigated by the lower credit risk of an on-lending portfolio relative
to direct lending to FPOs or agri-startups. The company intends to rebalance gradually toward
direct lending through co-lending partnerships and greater FPO and agri value-chain financing.

**Exposure to the agriculture and FPO segment**

Exposure to agriculture and FPOs remains susceptible to sectoral cyclicality, limited borrower cash
flow buffers and the early-stage nature of many FPOs. **This exposure is currently limited, at
2.85% of the overall portfolio**, which mitigates the associated risk to a large extent. As the FPO
book is deliberately expanded, maintaining asset quality at scale becomes a key monitorable.

## **Liquidity: Strong**

Liquidity is strong, supported by the structure of the book and by parental funding access. A
significant share of the loan portfolio is refinance lending with an average tenure of around three
years, funded through corresponding refinance lines from NABARD, which constitutes **87.63% of
total company debt** — producing a **well-matched asset-liability profile by construction**.

The asset-liability management statement as on March 31, 2026 showed **no cumulative mismatches**.
Cash and bank balances, including fixed deposits, stood at **₹211 crore**. Scheduled collections
for the following six months were **₹1,469 crore** against scheduled debt obligations of **₹1,451
crore** over the same period. On-call refinance access from NABARD provides additional financial
flexibility beyond these balances.

## **Environment, social, and governance (ESG) risks**

**Environmental:** Direct exposure is minimal — a wholesale lender without industrial operations.
Indirect exposure runs through the agricultural portfolio, where monsoon variability, drought and
flood affect borrower repayment capacity across the book simultaneously. Post-merger the company
also finances renewable energy and WASH projects, a positive-impact exposure.

**Social:** The social profile is the substance of the business. Lending to FPOs, cooperatives and
MFIs extends formal credit to rural borrowers otherwise underserved — the financial-inclusion
purpose for which NABARD established the company. The counterpart is conduct risk: rural and
microfinance lending is subject to close RBI supervision on fair practices, pricing and recovery.

**Governance:** A majority-owned subsidiary of a statutory development finance institution, with a
NABARD nominee director and NABARD-deputed MD & CEO and CFO ensuring policy alignment and
governance continuity. FY2026 financial statements were audited by L.U. Krishnan & Co., Chartered
Accountants. The company reports compliance with all RBI capital and exposure requirements, no loan
account restructured during the year, and nil single-borrower and group exposures above the
prescribed Tier I thresholds.

## **About the company and industry**

**Industry classification**

| Macroeconomic indicator | Sector | Industry | Basic industry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Financial services | Financial services | Finance | Non-banking financial company (NBFC) |

NABKISAN Finance Limited (formerly Agri Development Finance (Tamil Nadu) Limited, CIN
U65191TN1997PLC037525) was incorporated under the Companies Act, 1956 on February 14, 1997. It is a
subsidiary of NABARD with additional equity participation from the Government of Tamil Nadu and
institutions including Indian Bank, Indian Overseas Bank, Tamilnad Mercantile Bank, Canara Bank,
ICICI Bank, Federal Bank and DBS Bank India Limited. NABSAMRUDDHI Finance Limited was merged into
NABKISAN on December 16, 2025, forming a single entity with expanded presence in rural and
developmental finance.

Its core mandate is to provide credit for the promotion, expansion and commercialisation of
enterprises engaged in agriculture, allied activities and rural non-farm sectors. Within bulk
lending, it focuses on NBFCs lending in rural areas, FPOs, agriculture value-chain financing and
MSMEs engaged in income-generating activities.
