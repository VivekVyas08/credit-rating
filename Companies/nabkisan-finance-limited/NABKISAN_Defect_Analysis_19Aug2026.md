# Defect analysis — NABKISAN Finance Limited rating, 19 August 2026

Comparison of the assessment produced in this project against **CareEdge Ratings' published
rationale of the same date**. Each defect is classified by whether the correct information **was in
the seven documents supplied** (my error) or **was not** (a genuine data limit). Every "was it
there?" claim below was re-tested by search, not assumed.

---

## A. The rating itself

| | Published | This assessment | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instrument rated | **Tier II Bonds, ₹500.00 crore** | Long/short-term bank facilities | **Wrong instrument** |
| Rating | **CARE AAA; Stable** | CARE AA-; Stable / CARE A1+ | **Three notches low** |
| ISIN | INE165U08016, 7.80%, 06-02-2026 → 06-02-2036 | "no ISIN returned" | Genuinely not in my documents |

### A1 — Rated the wrong instrument

NABKISAN's rated exposure is a ₹500 crore Tier II bond, SEBI-regulated, complexity "Complex". I
assumed the rated universe was bank facilities, and assigned a **short-term rating for an
instrument that does not exist**.

The ISIN is genuinely absent from the documents. But the FY2026 financials disclose **Tier II
capital rising to ₹517.22 crore from ₹25.39 crore** — a ₹492 crore jump that I extracted, quoted
and built an entire capitalisation argument around **without asking what instrument created it**.
The bond was inferable from a number already in my own analysis.

### A2 — Three notches too low

Published AAA reflects near-full equalisation with NABARD. My AA- applied a partial notch-up.

The error is one of framing. For a subsidiary that executes the parent's statutory mandate, is 89%
owned, funded 87.7% by the parent through **on-call refinance**, and staffed at MD/CEO and CFO level
by parent deputees, the standalone financial profile is largely irrelevant to the outcome. I
weighted rising leverage and the Tier I decline heavily; the agency treats them as secondary to
support that is **structural rather than discretionary**.

## B. False absence claims — the most serious category

### B1 — Asset quality declared "not located" when it was present

I wrote that Stage 3 / GNPA ratios were "NOT located in the documents".

**Re-tested: `Stage 3` returns 28 hits and `Stage III` 25 hits**, in the ECL movement tables with
gross carrying amounts by stage. Published figures: GS3 **0.85%** (FY26), 0.86% (FY25); NS3
**0.27%**, 0.25%. Pre-merger GS3 was 1.31% (Mar-25) and 2.00% (Mar-24).

I did run searches, and recorded them: `Gross NPA`, `GNPA`, `gross stage`, `Stage 3`. The failure
was not skipping the search — it is that **Ind AS financial statements label this data "Stage III"
inside ECL movement tables, not "GNPA"**, and I did not aggregate the stage-wise gross carrying
amounts sitting in front of me.

**A recorded search is not the same as a competent search.** The delivery gate verified that I
cited a search; it cannot assess whether the terms matched the domain.

### B2 — ALM declared "not located"

I wrote that a maturity ladder "was not located". `Asset Liability Management` returns **7 hits**.

Published: the ALM statement as on 31-03-2026 showed **no cumulative mismatches**, with six-month
collections of ₹1,469 crore against obligations of ₹1,451 crore.

### B3 — Consequence: liquidity understated

I assessed liquidity **Adequate**; the agency assessed **Strong**.

My own cash-and-bank figure of **₹211.02 crore matches the published ₹211 crore exactly**. I had the
right number and drew the weaker conclusion because I had not found the ALM statement and did not
recognise that on-call NABARD refinance makes the maturity profile self-matching by construction.

## C. Stale figures — older documents used when newer ones were present

### C1 — NABARD shareholding

I stated **87.77%**. Published: **89.12%**.

Re-tested: 87.77% appears in the three annual reports (FY2023–FY2025, all **pre-merger**). **89.12%
appears in the FY2026 financials — the most recent document in the folder.** I took the figure from
the older source when the current one was available.

This is the same pattern as the SBI case: **the newest document was not read first.**

### C2 — Related-party borrowing figures

I cited related-party borrowings of ₹310.21 crore against ₹22.28 crore unrelated. Published:
**NABARD constitutes 87.70% of the borrowing profile** against total debt of ₹6,137 crore.

My figures came from a comparative disclosure elsewhere in the documents, not the FY2026 funding
profile, and **materially understate the parent-funding dependence that is central to the rating**.

## E. Analytical mischaracterisation

### E1 — Identified the wrong concentration risk

I made **"sectoral concentration in agriculture and rural lending"** a key weakness, describing
correlated exposure to monsoon, crop cycle and farm-loan-waiver policy.

The published analysis states the opposite emphasis: direct FPO/agri exposure is **2.85% of the
portfolio**, "mitigating associated risks to a large extent."

The real concentration is **counterparty**: top 25 exposures are **55.65%** of the book (49.71% a
year earlier); the largest single exposure is 3.04% of loans and **19.95% of TNW**. The portfolio
is **87.18% wholesale refinance to NBFCs and MFIs**, then PTCs 6.17%, direct lending 2.85%, direct
assignment 2.62%, agri-corporates 0.65%.

**I described a lending book that does not exist.** NABKISAN is predominantly a *wholesale lender to
other lenders*, not a direct agricultural lender. The portfolio split and top-25 figures are not
disclosed in my documents — but I asserted a portfolio character I had not verified, which is the
error regardless.

### E2 — Missed the credit-guarantee structure

Published: coverage from **NABSanrakshan Trustee Private Limited** (a fellow NABARD subsidiary),
government-backed, **up to 85% of loan amount, capped ₹2 crore per eligible FPO loan**, plus Tamil
Nadu and Odisha state guarantee funds. Material credit enhancement, entirely absent from my report.

### E3 — Rating sensitivities internally inconsistent with my own rating

I wrote numeric triggers: gearing above 7.0x, Tier I below 12%.

Published negative factors: **deterioration in NABARD's credit profile**, and **dilution of NABARD's
stake below 51%**. Positive factors: **"Not applicable"** — the rating is already at the top of the
scale.

For a support-driven rating the triggers are parent-related, not ratio-related. Having myself
described the rating as support-driven, I then wrote sensitivities that contradicted that framing.

### E4 — Missed the earnings driver

Published: profitability moderated because of **NIM compression as the company reduced exposure to
high-yield MFI loans** — a deliberate mix shift. I attributed the picture to write-backs alone and
never identified the yield driver.

## F. Genuinely outside the documents supplied

Limits, not errors — but recorded so the distinction is auditable:

- **Rights issue of ₹301.46 crore in Q2FY27** — after my latest document (Q1FY27, 30-Jun-2026).
  `rights issue` returns **zero hits**.
- **ISIN INE165U08016** — not present.
- **Top-25 concentration (55.65%) and the portfolio composition split** — not disclosed.
- **NABARD's own financials** — advances ₹9,32,549 crore, total assets ₹10,98,318 crore, TNW
  ₹88,793 crore as on 31-03-2026. Not in my documents; but for a support-driven rating I should
  have flagged the parent's credit profile as an **unquantified input**, rather than passing over it.
- **Merger effective date 16-Dec-2025** — I had the NCLT order date (30-10-2025), not the effective
  date.
- **Q1FY27 detail** — PAT ₹31 crore, ROTA 1.74%, RONW 11.95%, GS3 0.94%, CAR 22.80%, gearing 5.53x.
  I recorded the Q1FY27 file as "too OCR-degraded to extract reliably". That was an honest
  statement of what I attempted, but the agency extracted a full quarter from the same filing, so
  it reflects the limit of my effort rather than of the document.

## G. What was correct

Recorded for balance:

- The **merger and its pooling-of-interests treatment**, including that the FY2025 comparative is
  restated and FY2024 is therefore not comparable — the basis question was correctly resolved.
- **Total income, PAT, CRAR, RoAA/ROTA and the cash position all tie to the published figures.**
- The **write-back in the impairment line** and its normalisation (reported PBT growth 11.90%
  against normalised 22.31%).
- The **Tier I decline from 17.61% to 14.73% masked by a rising CRAR** — a point the published
  rationale does not make explicitly.
- The **CIN transposition** in the FY2025 annual report.
- 10/10 tie-outs reconciled, and the arithmetic that was performed was correct.

## H. The pattern, and the corrective

Three of the four most serious defects — **B1, B2 and C1** — share one shape:

> **Information that was in the documents was reported as absent or stale, because the search terms
> did not match the domain vocabulary.**

"GNPA" is bank language; Ind AS NBFC statements say "Stage III". The newest document held the
current shareholding; I searched the annual reports first and stopped.

The delivery gate passed this report because a search **was** cited. It cannot assess whether the
search was the *right* search. That is the gap.

**The corrective is not another prose rule.** It is a sector-vocabulary probe in the pre-flight:

1. For an **NBFC**, search `Stage 3|Stage III|ECL|expected credit loss|gross carrying amount` — not
   only `GNPA` — and require the stage-wise gross carrying amounts to be **aggregated** before any
   asset-quality statement is written.
2. For an NBFC, require **AUM**, **opex/ATA**, **credit cost %** and **AUM/TNW** — the metrics the
   agency actually reports — not the bank set (cost-to-income) or the corporate set.
3. **Rank documents by date and read the newest first**; any figure taken from an older document
   when a newer one exists must be justified in the workpaper.
4. Where the entity is a subsidiary rated on parent support, **the rated instrument list must be
   reconciled to the capital structure** — a ₹492 crore jump in Tier II capital implies an
   instrument, and that instrument is probably what is rated.
