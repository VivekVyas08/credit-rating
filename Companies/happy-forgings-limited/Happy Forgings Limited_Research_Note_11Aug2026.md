# Happy Forgings Limited — Research Note (Fact Pack)
**No rating, no outlook — this is a research/fact-pack deliverable only.** Data cutoff: Q1 FY2026-27 (30-Jun-2026); audited base FY2025-26. Prepared from the company's own annual reports, investor presentations and two broker notes — see Document Read Log and Sources & Caveats at the end.

---

## TL;DR

Happy Forgings Limited (HFL) is a Ludhiana-based, 47-year-old forging-and-precision-machining manufacturer — India's 2nd-largest crankshaft producer for CV/high-HP industrial applications — supplying CV, PV, farm-equipment, off-highway and industrial customers domestically and via export (26% of FY26 revenue). FY26 revenue was Rs 1,546 crore (+9.8% YoY) with EBITDA margin at a record 30.4% and PAT margin 19.5%, both up YoY on richer product mix (machining-content growth) and operating leverage. The balance sheet carries **zero non-current debt** in every one of the last three fiscals — the entire Rs 330 crore of FY26 debt is short-term, revolving working-capital financing — funded overwhelmingly by internal accruals even through a large ongoing capex cycle (capital commitments of Rs 423 crore as on 31-Mar-2026, roughly double the year before). Both covering brokers (ICICI Securities, Motilal Oswal) carry BUY ratings with 15-21% upside, citing a Rs 950 crore incremental order book (~60% export-linked) and margin-expansion levers from a captive-solar project and new higher-margin industrial programmes. Key monitorables: execution of the heavy-forging capacity expansion (commercial revenue only from FY28), rising-but-still-moderate working-capital intensity, an EPCG export obligation whose exact current quantum could not be reliably read from a corrupted source table, and export/forex exposure as the export share of revenue grows.

## Company & segments

Incorporated 1979; listed on BSE/NSE following its IPO. Three manufacturing facilities in Ludhiana, Punjab — installed forging capacity 148,000 MT and machining capacity 68,000 MT as on 31-Mar-2026 (75,200 MT machining as on 30-Jun-2026 per the Q1FY27 deck). Products: crankshafts, front axle carriers, steering knuckles, differential housings, transmission parts, suspension products, valve bodies — serving CV, PV, farm equipment, off-highway, and industrial/power/rail end markets. *(Source: Q1FY27 investor deck "About Us"/"Fully Integrated Capabilities" slides.)*

**Single reportable segment** — the entire business is classified as "Forging" under Ind AS 108; management (CODM = Chairman & Managing Director) does not maintain separate segment financials. *(Source: AR FY26, Note on operating segments, L9842; consolidated version at L13434-13438.)* Within this single segment, the company discloses **product-mix and geography-mix trend data**, used below as the closest available proxy for diversification:

## Broker-view synthesis

**Where the two brokers agree:** both reiterate BUY on the back of a stronger-than-expected Q1FY27 (revenue +27% YoY, EBITDA +39% YoY, margin >30% for a 4th consecutive quarter); both cite the Rs 950 crore order book (~60% export-linked, diversified Industrial 35-40%/PV 25-30%/CV 25-30%) as the primary growth driver; both name the captive solar project (SOP ~Jan-2027, Motilal Oswal quantifies a 1.0-1.5% EBITDA-margin uplift once fully operational) and new higher-margin industrial programmes (Motilal Oswal: 80-85% gross margin on machined crankshafts, 60-65% on forged) as forward margin levers; both flag raw-material/other-cost pass-through arrangements (1-3 month lag) as the mechanism protecting margin against near-term input-cost inflation.

**Where they diverge:** Motilal Oswal is more aggressive on medium-term growth (30% standalone revenue CAGR FY26-28, 41% earnings CAGR, margin expansion to 33% by FY28) than ICICI Securities (implied ~24-27% revenue growth from its FY27E/FY28E figures, margin held near 31-32%) — the gap hinges on how much of the order book converts to revenue on ICICI's more conservative execution-timing assumption versus Motilal Oswal's fuller ramp-up case. ICICI's target multiple (40x) is set on a later (Sep-2028E) EPS base than Motilal Oswal's (33x on FY28E), a methodological rather than a fundamentals divergence.

**Verification:** both notes' company-attributed figures (Q1FY27 revenue/EBITDA/PAT, order-book size, capacity-utilisation ~59%) were checked against the company's own Q1FY27 investor deck and matched — no broker paraphrase was carried into this note without that cross-check.

## What to watch (named monitorables)

- **Heavy-forging/machining capacity commissioning** — equipment installation targeted for completion by end-FY27 (per Q1FY27 MD commentary), commercial revenue only from FY28; execution delay is the single largest company-specific risk both brokers name.
- **Captive solar project** — SOP guided for January 2027 (Motilal Oswal); ~1.0-1.5% EBITDA-margin uplift once fully ramped.
- **EPCG obligation quantum** — needs issuer confirmation given the source-document inconsistency flagged above; watch for resolution in the FY27 annual report.
- **Working-capital intensity** — GCA days have risen from 199 (FY24) to 253 (FY26); still moderate vs. peers but the trend, if it continues, would erode some of the company's liquidity/coverage cushion.
- **Export mix and forex exposure** — direct+deemed exports rose from 26% (FY26) to ~28% (Q1FY27) and are guided higher (60% of the new order book is export-linked); Q4FY26 PAT growth already lagged EBITDA growth once due to adverse FX movement.
- **Order-book conversion** — Rs 950cr of incremental revenue visibility is management-guided, not yet booked/executed; the pace of conversion is the key swing factor behind the two brokers' differing FY27-28 growth assumptions.
