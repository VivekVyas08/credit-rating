### **Detailed Rationale**

This independent assessment arrives at **'Crisil AA/Stable'** for Happy Forgings Limited's (HFL) bank facilities, with a short-term rating of **'Crisil A1+'**.

The rating reflects HFL's strong business risk profile as a high-complexity, precision auto-forging and machining supplier — the second-largest producer of crankshafts for commercial-vehicle and high-horsepower industrial applications in India — combined with an exceptionally strong financial risk profile: near-zero leverage, very high interest coverage, and industry-leading operating margins. Revenue grew 9.8% to Rs 1,546 crore in fiscal 2026 (from Rs 1,409 crore), with EBITDA margin expanding to 30.4% (from 28.9%), driven by an improving product mix (machining share 89-90% of revenue), operating leverage and steady realisation growth. The momentum has continued into fiscal 2027, with Q1 revenue up 27.0% year-on-year to Rs 449 crore and EBITDA margin at 31.3%.

The company carries **zero non-current (term) borrowings** in each of the last three fiscals — the entirety of its Rs 330 crore of FY26 debt is short-term, revolving working-capital financing (cash credit, vendor/customer bill discounting), renewable annually. Gearing was 0.15 time and interest coverage 45 times in fiscal 2026, both materially stronger than similarly-rated sector peers. The company is funding a large, multi-year capex programme (capital commitments of Rs 423 crore as on March 31, 2026, up from Rs 285 crore a year earlier, for heavy-forging capacity, machining expansion and a captive solar project) predominantly from internal accruals — operating cash flow of Rs 445 crore in fiscal 2026 comfortably covered the year's capex of Rs 461 crore, and free cash generation is expected to strengthen as the new capacity ramps up from fiscal 2028.

These strengths are partially offset by HFL's exposure to cyclicality in the automotive and industrial capital-goods end markets, single-segment concentration (the entire business is the Forging segment), rising — though still moderate — working-capital intensity as the capex cycle and export mix build out, and a large export-linked order book (Rs 950 crore of incremental revenue visibility over 2-3 years, ~60% export-linked) that carries execution and forex-translation risk.

# **Key Rating Drivers — Strengths**

**Strong market position in a high-entry-barrier niche of the auto-component industry:** HFL is the second-largest producer of crankshafts for commercial-vehicle and high-horsepower industrial applications in India, and the third-largest heavy forging-and-machining company in the country, with 47 years of manufacturing track record and the second 14,000-tonne forging press installed in India. The business requires tight machining tolerances (0.005-0.2mm), lengthy customer/product validation cycles, and capital-intensive, vertically-integrated (forging + machining + metallurgical + heat-treatment) facilities — factors that limit new entrants and price erosion. This translated into consolidated revenue growth from Rs 860 crore (fiscal 2022) to Rs 1,546 crore (fiscal 2026), a 21% CAGR ahead of underlying industry volume growth, with a diversified end-market mix (Commercial Vehicles 37%, Farm Equipment 32%, Industrials 14%, Off-Highway 11% and Passenger Vehicles 6% of fiscal 2026 revenue) and growing export contribution (26% of FY26 revenue, rising to ~28% in Q1FY27) that together reduce dependence on any single demand pocket.

**Structural diversification away from the cyclical core is the direction of travel, not just the current mix:** the two segments least tied to the commercial-vehicle cycle — Industrials and Passenger Vehicles — moved from a combined 19% of revenue in fiscal 2026 to 24% in Q1 fiscal 2027 (Industrials 14%→16%, PV 6%→8%), while Commercial Vehicles fell from 37% to 33%. The forward evidence points the same way and more sharply: the disclosed Rs 950 crore incremental order book is weighted **35-40% to Industrials and 25-30% to Passenger Vehicles** against those segments' current 14% and 6% revenue shares — an order-book mix materially richer in non-CV applications than the installed revenue base, which mechanically raises the non-cyclical share as it converts. Management has separately guided to the industrial business doubling over three to four years, and the heavy-forging capex is explicitly aimed at industrial end-uses (power generation, wind, railways, oil and gas, heavy engineering). This progressive re-weighting is the single most credit-positive structural trend in the profile: it directly attenuates the cyclicality that is otherwise this issuer's principal business-risk exposure. *(Segment shares: company investor presentation, corroborated against both broker notes; order-book mix: management commentary as reported by ICICI Securities and Motilal Oswal, 05-Aug-2026 — attributed, not company-filed.)*

**Industry-leading operating efficiency and profitability:** EBITDA margin expanded from 26.8% (fiscal 2022) to 30.4% (fiscal 2026) — well above the auto-forging peer set (comparable-rated peers in the 14-16% range) — driven by a shift toward higher-value-added machined products (machining share of revenue 89-90%, up from a lower base), raw-material pass-through arrangements with a 1-3 month lag, and operating leverage from a capacity-utilisation ramp (forging ~59%, machining ~78-82% in fiscal 2026, leaving headroom before further large capex is needed). Return on capital employed, while moderated to 16.5-17.4% in fiscals 2025-26 (from ~19% in fiscal 2024) by the ongoing capacity build-out that has not yet reached full utilisation, remains healthy for the sector.

**Very strong financial risk profile with negligible leverage:** the company has carried zero non-current borrowings in each of the past three fiscals; its entire debt book (Rs 330 crore as on March 31, 2026) is short-term, on-demand working-capital financing, annually renewable rather than a scheduled repayment obligation. Gearing was 0.09-0.15 time and interest coverage 33-54 times over fiscals 2024-26 — both metrics several multiples stronger than the CRISIL AA-/Positive and AA-/Negative-rated peers referenced for sector calibration (adjusted debt/adjusted net worth 0.45-1.17 times; interest coverage 2.7-5.3 times, per those peers' own published Key Financial Indicators). Net debt/EBITDA is at or near a net-cash position on every convention examined (0.0-0.1x on this analysis; the company's own broader liquid-asset convention shows net cash).

**Track record of funding growth predominantly through internal accruals:** cumulative operating cash flow of Rs 1,277 crore over fiscals 2021-26 funded 92% of cumulative capex of Rs 1,393 crore over the same period, without material recourse to external debt or equity dilution beyond the original listing. This capital-allocation discipline, combined with a promoter holding of 78.48% (unpledged) and a board with 50% independent directors, supports the financial-flexibility assessment.

# **Key Rating Drivers — Weaknesses**

**Susceptibility to cyclicality in the automotive and capital-goods end markets:** as an auto-component supplier serving CV, PV, farm-equipment and industrial customers, HFL's revenue is linked to the capex and replacement cycles of those end markets and to macroeconomic conditions in its export geographies. The Ramkrishna Forgings and Craftsman Automation reference cases illustrate how a sector-wide demand slowdown or forex/tariff shock (e.g., US/Mexico export duties) can compress margins and stretch coverage metrics at otherwise well-positioned sector peers; HFL's own management has flagged recent geopolitical developments as a source of raw-material and manufacturing cost inflation, assessed as manageable given existing pass-through arrangements.

**Single-segment concentration:** the entire business is reported as one segment (Forging) under Ind AS 108 — there is no diversification cushion from an unrelated line of business if the forging/machining end-markets face a structural downturn, though the customer, product (CV/PV/Farm/Off-Highway/Industrial) and geography mix within that segment is reasonably diversified.

**Rising working-capital intensity and a large, execution-dependent capex/order-book pipeline:** gross current assets rose from 199 days (fiscal 2024) to 253 days (fiscal 2026) as the export mix and capex cycle built out — still moderate relative to the sector-calibration peers (130-160 days) but a clear rising trend to monitor. Capital commitments of Rs 423 crore (more than double two years earlier) fund heavy-forging and machining capacity that only reaches commercial revenue from fiscal 2028. The company also carries an EPCG (Export Promotion Capital Goods) export obligation linked to concessional import duties on capital goods, which grew materially year-on-year per the Annual Report — the exact current quantum could not be reliably established from the source document (a garbled table conversion; see workpaper), so it is named as a monitorable to confirm with the issuer rather than quantified here. Realisation of the Rs 950 crore incremental order book (~60% export-linked) depends on timely capacity commissioning and continued export demand.

# **Liquidity: Strong**

Cash and current investments stood at Rs 290 crore as on March 31, 2026 (up to Rs 421 crore on the company's own broader liquid-asset convention). Operating cash flow of Rs 445 crore in fiscal 2026 comfortably covered the year's capex of Rs 461 crore and is expected to remain adequate against broker-guided FY27 capex of Rs 350-400 crore and the company's FY26 dividend run-rate of Rs 28 crore. All outstanding debt is short-term working-capital financing, renewable annually rather than a scheduled term obligation — there is no debt-maturity wall to fund. Average monthly bank-limit utilisation over the trailing 12 months was not available in the documents reviewed for this assessment and would need to be confirmed with the issuer/lenders for a complete Crisil-methodology liquidity sign-off; based on the disclosed cash generation and balance-sheet cushion, liquidity is assessed as Strong.

# **ESG Profile**

The ESG profile of HFL supports its credit risk profile.

The forging and precision-machining sector has a **moderate-to-significant environmental impact**, being energy-intensive (heating, forging presses, heat treatment) with associated Scope 1 and 2 emissions, and material in its resource and water use. **Social impact is material** given a manufacturing workforce exposed to high-temperature and heavy-machinery hazards. HFL discloses targets and progress against both.

**ESG highlights:**

- **Emissions and energy:** total Scope 1 and 2 GHG emissions of **98,300 tCO2e, a 7% year-on-year reduction**, with energy intensity of **9.4 GJ/MT, down 4% year-on-year**. The company targets a **50% reduction in Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions by 2030** against its baseline.
- **Renewable energy:** a **20 MW captive solar plant is under construction, targeted for 2028**, which is expected to reduce both emissions and operating cost — the company and its brokers estimate a meaningful margin benefit once operational.
- **Water and waste:** targets **100% Zero Liquid Discharge across all sites** and a **35% reduction in freshwater consumption by 2031-32**.
- **Social:** lost-time injury frequency rate (**LTIFR) of 0.64**; approximately **22,160 cumulative employee training hours**; 100% workforce medical coverage; cumulative CSR expenditure of **Rs 23.7 crore over FY2021-26**.
- **Governance:** a six-member board with **50% independent directors (three of six) and two women directors**; three of five committees (Audit, Nomination & Remuneration, and Stakeholders' Relationship) chaired by independent directors; approximately 100% board attendance in fiscal 2026; and no promoter share pledge. One governance blemish is disclosed: a Rs 0.28 crore fine paid to the exchanges for an inadvertent, since-remediated delay in constituting the Nomination & Remuneration Committee.

ESG considerations matter increasingly to lenders and to the global OEM customers HFL supplies, several of which impose supply-chain sustainability requirements; the company's disclosure and target-setting support its positioning with both.

*Source: Happy Forgings Limited investor presentation Q1 FY2027 (ESG targets and commitments) and Annual Report FY2025-26 (governance disclosures).*

# **Outlook: Stable**

This assessment believes HFL will sustain its strong market position and industry-leading operating margin, supported by its diversified end-market/geography mix and disciplined, largely internally-funded capital allocation. The ongoing capex cycle, the rising-but-moderate working-capital intensity, and the unresolved EPCG-obligation quantum (see Weaknesses) are real monitorables but are assessed as manageable against a balance sheet carrying negligible leverage and very high interest-coverage headroom — nothing in the documents reviewed points to a directional change in the credit profile over the next 12-18 months.

# **Rating sensitivity factors**

**Upward factors**
- Successful ramp-up of the heavy-forging/machining capacity addition translating into sustained revenue growth and EBITDA margin at or above 30%, with continued negligible leverage
- Diversification of the customer/end-segment base further reducing cyclicality exposure, while sustaining current profitability and coverage metrics

**Downward factors**
- A sustained demand slowdown in the automotive/industrial end markets, or a sharp adverse forex/tariff shock in key export markets, compressing EBITDA margin below ~25% on a sustained basis
- Debt-funded capex or acquisitions materially increasing gearing beyond 0.5 time, or a sustained stretch in working-capital intensity (GCA days) weakening interest coverage below 20 times
- Delay in commissioning the heavy-forging/machining capacity impairing the realisation of the disclosed order book

# **About the Company**

Incorporated in 1979 and headquartered in Ludhiana, Punjab, Happy Forgings Limited designs and manufactures heavy forgings and high-precision machined components — crankshafts, front axle carriers, steering knuckles, differential housings, transmission parts and other critical components — for commercial vehicle, passenger vehicle, farm equipment, off-highway and industrial customers in India and overseas. It operates three manufacturing facilities in Ludhiana with installed forging capacity of 148,000 MT and machining capacity of 68,000 MT (as on June 30, 2026). The company listed on the BSE and NSE following its IPO; promoters hold 78.48% of equity (unpledged).
