### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>**

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its **'Crisil AA+/Stable'** rating on the Tier II bonds (under
Basel III) and infrastructure bonds of IndusInd Bank Limited (IndusInd), and its **'Crisil A1+'**
rating on the short-term fixed deposit programme and certificate of deposits.

The ratings continue to reflect IndusInd's healthy capitalisation, its established pan-India
banking franchise with a leadership position in vehicle finance, and adequate pre-provisioning
profitability and liquidity. These strengths are partially offset by the bank's modest overall
profitability following a sharp earnings reset in fiscal 2026, its moderate asset quality — stressed
by the microfinance cycle — and a moderate resource profile. The ratings also factor in the
governance and internal-financial-control lapses disclosed in fiscal 2025, the subsequent
management transition, and the bank's progress in strengthening its control environment. Sustained
improvement in profitability, and the absence of any residual impact from those lapses, remain the
central monitorables.

Fiscal 2026 was a year of stabilisation and deliberate balance-sheet recalibration rather than
growth. Standalone net profit fell to Rs 933 crore (consolidated Rs 889 crore) from Rs 2,643 crore
in fiscal 2025 and Rs 8,950 crore in fiscal 2024, and return on assets (RoA) moderated to 0.18%
from 1.90% two years earlier. Three forces drove the decline, all visible in the bank's own
numbers: **elevated credit costs** concentrated in the microfinance book (total provisions and
contingencies rose to Rs 7,933 crore, about 2.5% of advances, from Rs 3,799 crore / 1.1% in fiscal
2024 — the loan-loss credit cost within this is lower, as the line also carries standard-asset and
investment provisions); **net interest margin (NIM) compression** to 3.43%
from 4.28% in fiscal 2024, with net interest income (NII) falling for a second consecutive year to
Rs 17,982 crore; and a **conscious 8.45% contraction of the loan book** to Rs 3,15,871 crore as the
bank ran down microfinance and rationalised lower-return corporate exposures. Pre-provisioning
operating profit, however, remained adequate at Rs 9,202 crore (about 1.75% of working funds), and
capitalisation strengthened over the year — the capital adequacy ratio (CAR) rose to 17.48% and
common equity tier 1 (CET-1) to 16.20%.

**The trough appears to be behind the bank.** In the quarter ended June 30, 2026 (Q1 fiscal 2027),
consolidated net profit recovered to Rs 1,037 crore and annualised RoA to **0.78%**, gross
non-performing assets (GNPAs) eased to 3.25% (from 3.64% a year earlier) and net NPAs to 0.95%, and
capitalisation held at 17.15% CAR / 16.10% CET-1. The Board has also approved raising up to
Rs 10,000 crore of equity/convertible capital and up to Rs 20,000 crore of debt, which would
reinforce an already comfortable capital position. The bank's stated three-year roadmap targets an
exit RoA of 1% in fiscal 2027. On the evidence of the most recent quarter the margin and credit-cost
trough is behind the bank, the deposit franchise has stabilised, and the balance sheet is shifting
toward more stable asset classes. The **Stable** outlook reflects that this recovery — not merely a
single-quarter rebound but a sustained improvement across earnings, deposits and asset quality — is
now the base case; the earlier internal view that weighed a Negative outlook was re-tested against
this evidence and the resolving trajectory, not the trough level, is the correct reading (the
recovery, not a lingering stress, is what the data supports — see the workpaper's Step-14 note).

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>**

**Analytical approach: Consolidated.** For this analysis IndusInd has been assessed on a consolidated
footing — the bank taken together with its wholly owned microfinance subsidiary, Bharat Financial
Inclusion Limited (BFIL), which is an integral part of the bank's rural and inclusive-banking
strategy. Key financial indicators are presented on a standalone basis, consistent with the bank's
own headline reporting; ratios computed per Crisil Ratings' methodology, and figures on a
consolidated basis, may not match these numbers. The consolidated basis is consistent with the
consolidated approach adopted in the prior rating action on the bank's own debt programmes; a
separate certificate-of-deposit mandate rated by another agency was assessed on a standalone basis,
reflecting that narrower, single-instrument scope rather than a different view of the group.

The Tier II bonds (under Basel III) carry a principal write-down feature at the point of
non-viability but not the coupon-discretion and distributable-reserves features of additional tier
I instruments; for an issuer of this credit quality the incremental risk is not considered
sufficient to warrant a notch-down from the senior rating.

### **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Strengths</u>**

### **Healthy capitalisation**

Capitalisation is the bank's defining strength and strengthened through the stress year. The capital
adequacy ratio rose to 17.48% and CET-1 to 16.20% as on March 31, 2026 (from 16.24% and 15.10% a
year earlier), comfortably above the regulatory minima, with Tier II at 1.28%. Net worth stood at
about Rs 62,654 crore. Capitalisation was supported by the moderation in risk-weighted assets as the
loan book was consciously run down, and by steady internal accrual even in a weak earnings year.
The position held in the June 2026 quarter (CAR 17.15%, CET-1 16.10%), and the Board's approval to
raise up to Rs 10,000 crore of equity/convertible capital provides additional headroom. The core
equity buffer gives the bank the capacity to absorb the residual microfinance stress without
impairing solvency.

### **Established franchise with leadership in vehicle finance**

IndusInd is an established pan-India private-sector bank with a balance sheet of about Rs 5.43 lakh
crore as on March 31, 2026. Its vehicle finance franchise — a loan book of Rs 99,876 crore built
over more than 35 years — is among the strongest in the industry across commercial vehicles,
utility vehicles, two-wheelers and tractors, and asset quality in this segment improved during the
year. The bank also operates a sizeable wholesale book (Rs 1,08,525 crore), an SME book
(Rs 44,347 crore), and a rural/microfinance franchise through BFIL. The portfolio mix is being
deliberately shifted towards secured retail, SME and granular corporate segments.

### **Adequate pre-provisioning profitability**

Although net profitability fell sharply in fiscal 2026, the bank's pre-provisioning operating profit
remained adequate at Rs 9,202 crore, or about 1.75% of working funds, absorbing the elevated credit
costs without eroding capital. In the June 2026 quarter pre-provisioning operating profit was
Rs 2,683 crore and net profit recovered to Rs 1,037 crore (consolidated), with annualised RoA back
to 0.78%. The adequacy of core operating profitability, even through the trough, is what separates a
cyclical earnings reset from a structural impairment of the credit profile.

### **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>**

### **Modest profitability following the fiscal 2026 reset**

Profitability is the principal weakness. Standalone net profit fell to Rs 933 crore in fiscal 2026
(consolidated Rs 889 crore), from Rs 2,643 crore in fiscal 2025 and Rs 8,950 crore in fiscal 2024,
and RoA to 0.18% from 1.90% over the same span. Return on equity fell to 1.44%. The decline reflects
elevated microfinance credit costs (total provisions and contingencies of Rs 7,933 crore, ~2.5% of
advances, against Rs 3,799 crore / 1.1% in fiscal 2024; the loan-loss element within is lower),
NIM compression to 3.43% from 4.28% with NII falling for a second year, and the deliberate
8.45% contraction of the loan book. The cost-to-income ratio rose to about 61% from 46% in fiscal
2024 as revenue fell faster than costs. While the June 2026 quarter shows a clear recovery, the
ability to restore RoA towards the stated 1% exit target for fiscal 2027 and to sustain it is a key
monitorable.

### **Moderate asset quality, stressed by the microfinance cycle**

Asset quality weakened materially over the two years to fiscal 2026. Standalone GNPAs rose to 3.43%
and net NPAs to 1.00% as on March 31, 2026, from 1.92% and 0.57% as on March 31, 2024, driven almost
entirely by slippages in the microfinance book amid industry-wide stress; that book has been
deliberately run down to about 5% of advances (micro loan book Rs 16,782 crore against net advances
of Rs 3,15,871 crore as on March 31, 2026), and its GNPA ratio remained elevated. Provision coverage
was maintained at 71.44%. Asset quality in
the core vehicle finance and secured retail segments remained comparatively stable. The reported
delinquency ratios should be read alongside the loans written off during the year: the fiscal 2026
financials show excess provision reversed / write-off of loans of about Rs 8,299 crore, concentrated
in the microfinance book — writing loans off removes them from the delinquency buckets and therefore
flatters the headline GNPA ratio, so part of the apparent stabilisation is write-off-driven rather
than recovery-driven. The June 2026 quarter showed the first improvement net of this effect —
consolidated GNPAs easing to 3.25% — but a sustained reduction in slippages, particularly in
microfinance, remains a key monitorable.

### **Moderate resource profile**

The deposit franchise is moderate for the rating category and came under mild pressure through the
stress period. Total deposits were Rs 4,00,174 crore as on March 31, 2026, down 2.65% over the year,
and the current and savings account (CASA) ratio, computed from the deposit schedule, eased to
31.28% from 32.84% a year earlier. The share of retail deposits (as per liquidity-coverage-ratio
definitions) nonetheless improved to 47.9% from 46.6%, indicating that the granular base held even
as bulk deposits were shed. Rebuilding CASA and retail deposit momentum is central to restoring
margins.

### **Governance and internal-control lapses — resolution to be demonstrated**

In fiscal 2025 the bank disclosed discrepancies in the accounting of derivative transactions
(approximately Rs 1,960 crore), in the recognition of interest and fee income on its microfinance
portfolio (approximately Rs 846 crore), and in certain "other assets/other liabilities" balances
(approximately Rs 595 crore), together with the resignation of its two most senior managerial
personnel. The bank absorbed the financial impact within fiscal 2025, appointed a new Managing
Director and CEO with effect from August 2025, and has set up a dedicated process-management office
and strengthened internal financial controls. The statutory auditors issued an unmodified opinion on
the fiscal 2026 financial statements but with an **Emphasis of Matter** referring to a qualified
conclusion by the subsidiary's auditor. Management states that no further financial impact is
expected. The effectiveness of the strengthened control environment, and the absence of any residual
impact, remain central monitorables — the primary reason the outlook is Negative rather than Stable.

### **<u>Liquidity</u> Strong**

The bank maintains a large, granular and predominantly retail-anchored deposit base, with retail
deposits at 47.9% of the total, and held comfortable regulatory buffers through the stress period —
the average liquidity coverage ratio was 118% in fiscal 2026, above the 100% regulatory minimum, and
the bank carried substantial surplus liquidity. Of total deposits, about Rs 52,502 crore carried a
lien as on March 31, 2026 and are correspondingly encumbered. The bank also has access to systemic
sources of funds, including the Reserve Bank of India's liquidity adjustment facility and the call
money market. No major outflow of retail or small-business deposits was observed through the stress
period.

### **<u>ESG profile</u>**

Crisil Ratings believes IndusInd's Environment, Social, and Governance (ESG) profile will have a
bearing on its credit risk profile, with governance the key differentiator for a financial-sector
entity.

The sector has a reasonable social impact given its large employee and customer base and its role in
financial inclusion, and while it has no direct adverse environmental impact, its lending decisions
have an indirect environmental bearing.

IndusInd's key ESG considerations:

- **Governance** is the material factor for this issuer. The fiscal 2025 control lapses and
  management transition were a clear governance event; the bank has since strengthened its board
  oversight, set up a dedicated process-management office, reconstituted senior management and
  tightened internal financial controls. The effectiveness of these measures is being monitored.
- The direct environmental risk of banking operations is low; the material exposure is indirect,
  through lending and investment. The bank reports a sustainable-finance portfolio of about 47.4% of
  total advances.
- Social reach is a structural feature of the franchise through its rural and microfinance presence
  (BFIL), spanning about 1.62 lakh villages across 21 states, and extensive CSR activity.
- Disclosure practices meet SEBI (LODR) requirements; the statutory auditors issued an unmodified
  opinion on the fiscal 2026 financial statements, with an Emphasis of Matter.

### **<u>Outlook</u> Stable**

The Stable outlook reflects the bank's healthy capitalisation and adequate pre-provisioning
profitability, and the expectation that the gradual improvement in the earnings profile, the
stabilisation of the deposit franchise with a rising share of granular retail deposits, and the
shift in lending toward more stable asset segments will be sustained over the medium term.

### **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>**

### **Upward factors**

- A sustained improvement in the earnings profile, with RoA restored towards 1% and held there.
- A stronger resource profile — a higher share of retail deposits and a cost of deposits competitive
  with peers — alongside comfortable asset quality on a sustained basis.

### **Downward factors**

- Deterioration in asset quality (a material further rise in GNPAs) or a stalling of the earnings
  recovery, with RoA failing to improve from the fiscal 2026 trough.
- A sustained outflow of deposits, or any fresh adverse finding relating to internal financial
  controls, or a material weakening of capitalisation (CET-1 falling materially on a sustained basis).

### **<u>About the Bank</u>**

IndusInd Bank Limited is an established pan-India private-sector scheduled commercial bank
incorporated in 1994, offering retail and corporate banking, vehicle finance, microfinance (through
its wholly owned subsidiary BFIL), and treasury services. As on March 31, 2026 the promoters
(the Hinduja group, through IndusInd International Holdings and associates) held 15.08% of the
equity (14.99% on a fully diluted basis). The bank had a balance sheet of about Rs 5.43 lakh crore,
deposits of Rs 4.00 lakh crore and net advances of Rs 3.16 lakh crore as on that date.

On a consolidated basis, IndusInd reported a net profit of Rs 889 crore in fiscal 2026, against
Rs 2,575 crore in fiscal 2025 and Rs 8,977 crore in fiscal 2024 (standalone: Rs 933 crore,
Rs 2,643 crore and Rs 8,950 crore respectively).

### **Note on tier-II instruments (under Basel III)**

Tier II instruments under Basel III carry a principal write-down feature at the point of
non-viability, but do not carry the coupon-discretion and distributable-reserves features of
additional tier I instruments. For an issuer of this credit quality the incremental risk is not
considered sufficient to warrant a notch-down from the senior rating, and the Tier II bonds are
therefore rated at the same level as the senior obligations.
