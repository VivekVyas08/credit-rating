## 1. What the bank is

Equitas Small Finance Bank Limited (ESFBL) began as Equitas Finance Limited and turned into a
small finance bank in 2016. Its former holding company, Equitas Holdings Limited, was amalgamated
into the bank in FY2023 (FY2023-24 AR). It has no promoter, subsidiary, associate or joint venture
(Q1 FY2027 results, note 13). It is listed on the NSE and BSE (EQUITASBNK). At 31 March 2026 it
had 1,060 branches plus 53 other offices, and 28,072 employees (FY2025-26 AR, BRSR).

## 2. Book mix — from microfinance to secured retail

The microfinance book was 5,224.69 in FY2023 and 6,265.45 in FY2024, then cut 28% to 4,527 in
FY2025 (12% of advances). By June 2025 it was about 10% (FY2024-25 AR). The FY2025-26 AR
highlights repeat "~10%" and state that 71% of the microfinance book, and 99% of FY2026
microfinance disbursements, are covered by the CGFMU guarantee scheme. That is why "advances
covered by bank/government guarantees" rose to 4,246.92 from 445.39 (Schedule 9). Unsecured
advances are 3,827.35, or 8.95% of net advances.

## 3. What went wrong in FY2025-26 — and what has turned

- **Profit collapsed.** PAT (Rs crore) went 573.59 (FY2023) → 798.96 (FY2024) → 147.05 (FY2025)
  → 103.08 (FY2026). ROTA on CareEdge's basis fell to 0.30% and then 0.18%.
- **The microfinance cycle drove it.** Slippages were 2,031.62 (FY2025) and 2,125.40 (FY2026),
  about 6% of opening standard advances. Write-offs were 826.35 and 919.97, and pre-tax credit
  cost was Rs 1,135.42 crore and Rs 1,136.84 crore.
- **The turn.** Quarterly PAT went from −223.78 (Q1 FY2026) to 90.03 (Q3) and 212.68 (Q4).
  Q1 FY2027 PBT was Rs 244.07 crore, against a Rs 297.48 crore loss a year earlier (segment note).

## 4. Funding and liquidity

**Funding has weakened.**
- CASA fell from 42.2% (FY2023) to 26.2% (FY2026).
- Term deposits from banks are Rs 8,448.13 crore (18% of deposits).
- The top-20 depositors hold 20.61% of deposits (17.60% in FY2025).
- Borrowings rose to Rs 5,772.55 crore. They include two Rs 500 crore Lower Tier II bonds, and
  a Rs 2,000 crore IBPC was also issued.

**Liquidity ratios are comfortable.** LCR was 138.45% (Q4 FY2026 average) and NSFR 114.01%. But the
FY2026 ALM statement shows negative cumulative gaps within one year (workpaper §11).

## 5. Management and governance

- **People:** the bank is founder-led, with P N Vasudevan as MD and CEO (Q1 FY2027 results).
- **Board:** 11 directors, 9 of them independent (82%), including one woman, plus 2 whole-time
  directors (FY2025-26 AR).
- **Audit and regulators:** joint statutory auditors ASA & Associates LLP and Suri & Co. gave
  unmodified opinions. RBI imposed no penalty in FY2026; the only penalties were small
  tax-department ones.
- **Frauds:** 488 cases involving Rs 18.33 crore in FY2026 (1,891 cases, Rs 20.54 crore in FY2025).

## 6. Risks (credit lens)

| # | Risk | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Persistent credit losses | Slippages 4.5-6.6% of opening standard advances every year FY2023-26; write-offs ~2% of gross advances |
| 2 | Weak profitability | ROTA 0.30% / 0.18% (FY2025/26); cost-to-income 71.7%; pre-provision profit/ATA ~2.2% vs credit cost 2.0% |
| 3 | Funding profile | CASA 26.2%; top-20 depositors 20.6%; bank term deposits 18%; IBPC Rs 2,000 crore |
| 4 | Capital consumption | CRAR 23.80% → 20.31% (FY2023-26); leverage ratio 13.21% → 8.84%; base-case growth takes CRAR to ~16.5% by FY2031 without new equity |
| 5 | Segment cyclicality | microfinance, small business and used vehicles are sensitive to borrower cash flows |
| 6 | Regulatory | SFB norms (CRAR 15%, priority-sector targets); PSLC purchases Rs 1,000 crore and sales Rs 1,800 crore in FY2026 |

The market prices the bank above book, which keeps equity raising open, a material
financial-flexibility point given the capital consumption in §6. The bank's capital-raising
record supports it: two Lower Tier II raises of Rs 500 crore each (FY2025 and FY2026, capital
note). No DCF is used: for a bank, book value and the access to capital are the credit-relevant
measures.
