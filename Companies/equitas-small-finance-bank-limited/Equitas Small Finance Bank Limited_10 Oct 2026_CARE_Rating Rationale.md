## **Rationale and key rating drivers**

The ratings of Equitas Small Finance Bank Limited (Equitas) rest on three things:
- its established, diversified and now largely secured retail lending franchise, with total
  assets of ₹60,610 crore as on March 31, 2026;
- capitalisation that remains comfortable, with a capital adequacy ratio (CAR) of 20.31% and a
  Tier-I CAR of 16.68%, well above the 15% and 7.5% minima for small finance banks;
- an adequate liquidity profile.

The bank has cut microfinance from about 19% of advances in FY23 to roughly 10%. Most of what
remains is covered by the Credit Guarantee Fund for Micro Units (CGFMU), and the book is now led
by small business, vehicle and housing loans.

Against these strengths, profitability has been weak for two years. Return on total assets
(ROTA) fell to 0.30% in FY25 and 0.18% in FY26, from 1.99% in FY24. Credit costs of 2.0-2.3% of
average total assets absorbed most of the pre-provision operating profit of about 2.2%. The
underlying loss rate remains high: fresh slippages equalled 5.93% of opening standard advances
in FY26, and the bank wrote off loans amounting to 2.11% of gross advances. The headline gross
non-performing assets (GNPA) ratio of 2.60% is therefore held down by write-offs; it is not
evidence of a low rate of default.

The ratings are also constrained by a weakening resource profile:
- the current account savings account (CASA) ratio has fallen to 26.21% from 42.23% in FY23;
- the twenty largest depositors hold 20.61% of deposits;
- the bank relies increasingly on term deposits from banks and on inter-bank participation
  certificates (IBPC).

Earnings have turned since the first quarter of FY26, when the bank lost ₹224 crore. It earned
₹90 crore in Q3FY26 and ₹213 crore in Q4FY26, and reported a profit before tax of ₹244 crore in
Q1FY27. CareEdge Ratings expects ROTA to recover towards 0.8-1.0% in FY27 as credit costs
normalise. However, growth of 15-18% at that level of return will consume capital, and the bank
is likely to need fresh equity, or slower growth, to hold CAR comfortably above 17% over the
medium term.

## **Rating sensitivities: Factors likely to lead to rating actions**

### **Positive factors: Factors that could individually or collectively lead to positive rating action/upgrade**

- ROTA sustained above 1.5%, with credit costs below 1.2% of average total assets.
- CASA ratio improving above 35%, with lower depositor concentration.
- Tier-I CAR above 18% on a sustained basis, supported by fresh equity capital.

### **Negative factors: Factors that could individually or collectively lead to negative rating action/downgrade**

- ROTA below 0.8% in FY27, or below 1% on a sustained basis thereafter.
- GNPA above 3.5% on a sustained basis, or a renewed rise in slippages above 6%.
- CAR below 17% on a sustained basis without a credible capital plan.
- Material weakening of the liquidity profile or a further rise in depositor concentration.

## **Analytical approach:** Standalone

## **Outlook:** Stable

The Stable outlook reflects CareEdge Ratings' expectation that profitability will recover from
the FY26 trough as microfinance stress recedes, while capital remains adequate to absorb credit
costs. The bank could absorb a FY27 credit cost of about 4.0% of average total assets, roughly
twice the FY26 level, before CAR fell to the regulatory 15%.

# **Key strengths**

## **Comfortable capitalisation with a buffer for one more stress year**

CAR stood at 20.31% and Tier-I CAR at 16.68% as on March 31, 2026 (March 31, 2025: 20.60% and
17.84%), against regulatory minima of 15% and 7.5%. The leverage ratio was 8.84% against a 4.5%
requirement. Tier-I capital of ₹5,342 crore supports risk-weighted assets of ₹32,028 crore.

The bank raised two Basel II Lower Tier-II bonds of ₹500 crore each, in December 2024 and July
2025. These lifted Tier-II capital to ₹1,161 crore from ₹244 crore two years earlier.

Capital ratios have, however, declined steadily: CAR from 23.80% in FY23, Tier-I CAR from 23.08%,
and the leverage ratio from 13.21%. Tangible net worth was flat at ₹5,582 crore in FY26 because
earnings were too thin to keep pace with 18% loan growth. CareEdge Ratings' stress analysis finds
CAR would still be about 16.6% after a FY27 credit cost of 3.0% of average assets, but would fall
below 15% if such costs lasted two years. The bank's ability to raise equity, supported by a
market valuation of about 1.2 times book, is therefore a key monitorable.

## **Diversified, granular and largely secured loan book after de-risking microfinance**

Gross advances grew 18.0% to ₹43,580 crore in FY26 (FY25: 17.5%). Equitas cut its microfinance
portfolio by 28% to ₹4,527 crore in FY25, from ₹6,265 crore in FY24, and the share fell further
to about 10% of advances in FY26. The CGFMU scheme covers 71% of the microfinance book and 99%
of FY26 microfinance disbursements, so advances covered by bank or government guarantees rose to
₹4,247 crore. Unsecured advances fell to ₹3,827 crore, 8.95% of net advances.

The book is led by small business loans, vehicle finance and housing finance; loans against
property (₹4,955 crore) and gold loans (₹886 crore) are growing. It is granular: the twenty
largest borrowers account for 3.93% of advances. Priority-sector advances were ₹29,698 crore, 68%
of gross advances.

## **Established retail franchise**

Equitas operates 1,060 branches and 53 other offices across 16 states and two Union Territories,
with 28,072 employees. Deposits were ₹46,533 crore as on March 31, 2026.

The state-wise mix of advances is not disclosed in the documents reviewed: searches of the
annual reports and quarterly results for state-wise or geographic concentration of advances
returned no hits. CareEdge Ratings treats geographic concentration as a monitorable and has
requested the data from the bank.

# **Key weaknesses**

## **Weak profitability for two years, recovery under way**

PAT fell to ₹147 crore in FY25 and ₹103 crore in FY26, from ₹799 crore in FY24. ROTA declined to
0.18% and return on tangible net worth to 1.84%.

Net interest margin (NIM) on average total assets narrowed to 5.98% in FY26 from 6.63% in FY25
and 7.67% in FY24, as the high-yield microfinance book shrank and the cost of funds stayed near
7%. Operating costs remained high at 5.64% of average total assets, a cost-to-income ratio of
71.67%. Pre-tax credit cost was ₹1,137 crore in FY26 (FY25: ₹1,135 crore), 2.00% of average total
assets.

Quarterly results show a turn: a loss of ₹224 crore in Q1FY26, then a profit of ₹90 crore in
Q3FY26 and ₹213 crore in Q4FY26, and a profit before tax of ₹244 crore in Q1FY27.

## **High slippages; asset quality held by write-offs**

The bank lends mainly to self-employed and small-business borrowers. Fresh NPAs of ₹2,125 crore
in FY26 (FY25: ₹2,032 crore) were 5.93% of opening standard advances (FY25: 6.64%).

The bank wrote off ₹920 crore in FY26 (₹630 crore technical and ₹290 crore other), equal to
2.11% of gross advances. In FY25 write-offs were ₹826 crore (2.24%). It also sold NPAs with a
principal of ₹565 crore to asset reconstruction companies for a consideration of ₹184 crore.

As a result, GNPA fell to 2.60% and net NPA (NNPA) to 0.72% as on March 31, 2026 (FY25: 2.89%
and 0.98%). Provision coverage was 73.03%, or 86.81% including technical write-offs. Security
receipts of ₹175 crore are 97% provided, and the net stressed assets to tangible net worth ratio
was 6.85%. Recoveries from previously written-off accounts were ₹346 crore in FY26.

## **Moderate and weakening resource profile**

Deposits grew 8.0% in FY26 after 19.3% in FY25, slower than advances, and the credit/deposit
ratio rose to 91.87% from 84.01%. CASA has fallen every year: 42.23% (FY23), 31.98%, 28.78% and
26.21% (FY26).

Term deposits from banks were ₹8,448 crore (18% of deposits), and the twenty largest depositors
held 20.61% (FY25: 17.60%). Borrowings rose to ₹5,773 crore from ₹2,137 crore, including the
Lower Tier-II bonds. During the year the bank also issued ₹2,000 crore of inter-bank
participation certificates, all maturing within two months.

The cost of deposits was 7.11% in FY26 (FY25: 7.40%), as reported by the bank.

# **Liquidity**: Adequate

The liquidity coverage ratio (LCR) averaged 138.45% in Q4FY26 (Q4FY25: 156.52%). The net stable
funding ratio (NSFR) was 114.01% as on March 31, 2026; both are well above the 100% requirement.
LCR declined through the year from 179.64% in Q1FY26.

Per the asset-liability maturity statement as on March 31, 2026, cumulative mismatches were
negative from the 2-7 day bucket onwards, reaching ₹6,411 crore (29% of cumulative outflows) in
the up-to-one-year buckets. Cash and balances with the Reserve Bank of India and other banks of
₹5,666 crore, which the statement excludes, cover most of that gap. Part of the RBI balance is
the cash reserve requirement and is not unencumbered, so CareEdge Ratings relies on the LCR's
high-quality liquid assets (₹10,222 crore in Q4FY26) as the measure of free liquidity. The bank also held government
securities of ₹9,260 crore and has access to the RBI's liquidity adjustment facility and marginal
standing facility.

Liquidity therefore depends on the renewal of term deposits, including bank deposits and IBPC.

# **Environment, social, and governance (ESG) risks**

Equitas's service-based business has limited direct environmental exposure; environmental risk
arises mainly through its borrowers.

Social risk centres on its customer base of self-employed and low-income borrowers. Priority-sector
advances are 68% of gross advances, which supports financial inclusion but leaves the book
sensitive to borrower income shocks. Customer complaints rose 24% to 29,533 in FY26, and 488
frauds involving ₹18.33 crore were reported. These are areas for continued operational
attention.

On governance:
- the Board has 11 directors, nine of them independent, including a woman director;
- the joint statutory auditors issued unmodified opinions;
- no penalty was imposed by the RBI in FY26;
- the bank spent ₹14.67 crore on CSR in FY26.

# **About the company and industry**

# **Industry classification**

| Macroeconomic indicator | Sector | Industry | Basic industry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Financial services | Financial services | Banks | Other bank |

Equitas is a Chennai-headquartered small finance bank that has operated as a bank since 2016,
having previously been Equitas Finance Limited. It is listed on the National Stock Exchange and
BSE. It has no promoter: its former holding company, Equitas Holdings Limited, was amalgamated
into the bank in FY23. The bank has no subsidiary, associate or joint venture.

It lends to small businesses, vehicle buyers, home buyers, micro and small enterprises and
microfinance borrowers, and offers deposit and transaction banking. As on March 31, 2026, it had
total assets of ₹60,610 crore, deposits of ₹46,533 crore and gross advances of ₹43,580 crore,
through 1,060 branches.
