### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>**

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its **'Crisil AAA/Stable'** ratings on the fixed deposits,
infrastructure bonds and Tier II bonds (under Basel III) of State Bank of India (SBI; part of the
SBI group), its **'Crisil AA+/Stable'** rating on the Tier I bonds (under Basel III) and its
**'Crisil A1+'** rating on the certificate of deposits.

The ratings continue to centrally factor in the SBI group's dominant market position in the Indian
banking industry, strong resource profile and adequate capitalisation. The ratings also factor in
the continued strong support that the bank is likely to receive from its majority owner, the
Government of India (GoI), both on an ongoing basis and in the event of distress. These strengths
are partially offset by the improving yet moderate profitability of the group.

Fiscal 2026 was a year of broad-based strengthening. The balance sheet crossed Rs 76 lakh crore and
total business Rs 109 lakh crore, with deposits up 11.03% to Rs 59.76 lakh crore and advances up
16.87% to Rs 49.33 lakh crore. Standalone net profit grew 12.88% to Rs 80,032 crore and operating
profit 11.25% to Rs 1.23 lakh crore. Asset quality reached the best level in the bank's ten-year
disclosed history — gross non-performing assets (GNPAs) at 1.49% (down 33 basis points [bps]) and
net NPAs at 0.39% (down 8 bps) — while capitalisation strengthened materially, with the overall
capital adequacy ratio (CAR) at 15.40% and common equity tier I (CET-1) at a record 12.29%.

The one qualification to an otherwise improving picture in fiscal 2026 was the revenue line. Net
interest income (NII) grew 4.08% to Rs 1.73 lakh crore against advances growth of 16.87% — a
divergence of roughly 1,280 bps that points to margin compression, and the reason profitability is
characterised as improving yet moderate notwithstanding a return on assets (RoA) of 1.12%. Earnings
growth in fiscal 2026 was carried more by operating leverage (cost-to-income improved to 50.11%
from 51.64%) and by benign credit costs than by core spread income.

**That compression has since reversed.** In the quarter ended June 30, 2026 (Q1 fiscal 2027), NII
grew **14.88%** year on year, domestic net interest margin (NIM) recovered to **3.00%** — up 7 basis
points sequentially over the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 — and whole-bank NIM improved 5 bps to
2.86%. Net profit for the quarter was Rs 21,121 crore (up 10.23%) and operating profit Rs 33,529
crore (up 9.77%), on advances growth of 18.63% and deposit growth of 9.73%. Deposits crossed
Rs 60 trillion and advances Rs 50 trillion. Asset quality improved further, with gross NPAs at 1.47%
(down 36 bps year on year) and net NPAs at 0.38% (down 9 bps), the slippage ratio at 0.57% (better
by 18 bps) and credit cost at 0.27%. Capital adequacy strengthened again to **15.67%**. On the
evidence of the most recent quarter the margin trough is behind the bank, and the moderate-
profitability weakness is a statement about the level of returns rather than their direction.

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>**

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of SBI and its subsidiaries,
collectively referred to as the SBI group, as the subsidiaries are an integral part of SBI's growth
strategy. The ratings also factor in the strong support that the bank is expected to receive from
GoI, both on an ongoing basis and in the event of distress.

The Tier I bonds (under Basel III) are rated one notch below the bank's senior obligations to
reflect their loss-absorption features — coupon discretion, the coupon-payment link to the
availability of distributable reserves and to the maintenance of the regulatory capital-conservation
buffer, and the principal write-down trigger at the point of non-viability.

### **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Strengths</u>**

### **Strong expectation of support from GoI**

The ratings continue to factor in the strong support expected from GoI, both on an ongoing basis and
in the event of distress, given that GoI is the majority shareholder in public sector banks (PSBs)
and the guardian of India's financial system. GoI held **55.03%** of the bank's equity capital as on
March 31, 2026 (56.92% as on March 31, 2025). Stability of the banking sector is of prime importance
to GoI, considering its criticality to the economy, the strong public perception of sovereign
backing for PSBs and the adverse implications of a PSB failure in terms of political fallout,
systemic stability and investor confidence. Crisil Ratings believes the majority ownership creates a
moral obligation on GoI to support PSBs, including SBI.

Support expectation is reinforced by the bank's regulatory status: SBI is designated a Domestic
Systemically Important Bank (D-SIB) by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and is accordingly required
to maintain an additional CET-1 buffer of 0.80% of risk-weighted assets with effect from April 1,
2025. The bank remains comfortably placed against the mandated leverage ratio requirement of 4%.

### **Dominant market position in the Indian banking industry**

SBI is the largest bank in India on every principal measure. Total business surpassed Rs 109 lakh
crore in fiscal 2026 and the balance sheet crossed Rs 76 lakh crore. Deposits stood at Rs 59.76 lakh
crore and gross advances at Rs 49.33 lakh crore, the latter growing 16.87% with broad-based
expansion across retail, agriculture, SME and corporate segments. Retail advances crossed Rs 17 lakh
crore, the housing loan portfolio approached Rs 10 lakh crore, agriculture lending crossed Rs 4 lakh
crore — the highest for any bank in India — the SME portfolio exceeded Rs 6 lakh crore and the
corporate book reached over Rs 14 lakh crore.

Distribution is unmatched in scale: 23,265 domestic branches and 245 foreign branches/offices as on
March 31, 2026. Through its non-banking subsidiaries and joint ventures the group offers investment
banking, credit cards, life and general insurance, fund management, primary dealership, broking and
factoring.

### **Strong resource profile**

The deposit franchise is the group's most durable competitive advantage. CASA deposits grew 9.53% to
Rs 22.62 lakh crore and the CASA ratio stood at **39.46%** as on March 31, 2026, with savings bank
deposits recording their highest growth in four years. Retail banking accounted for 88.27% of total
deposits (Rs 52.75 trillion), 95.09% of CASA deposits and 90% of term deposits, underlining the
granularity of the liability base. The total investment book increased to Rs 18.09 lakh crore, of
which 95.48% is domestic and 73.95% of domestic investments are held to maturity; yield on
investments was 7.01%.

_The CASA ratio of 39.46% is computed on the domestic deposit base. On whole-bank deposits of
Rs 59.76 lakh crore — which the bank states grew across "both domestic and overseas operations" —
the ratio computes to 37.85%. The basis is stated here because the two are not interchangeable._

### **Adequate capitalisation**

Capitalisation strengthened materially in fiscal 2026. Overall CAR rose to **15.40%** from 14.25%,
and Tier I capital to 13.33% from 12.11%, with CET-1 at a record **12.29%**. Against the D-SIB
requirement of 12.30% (11.5% including the capital-conservation buffer, plus the 0.80% D-SIB
surcharge), the reported CAR provides a buffer of approximately 310 bps. Tier I capital rose to
Rs 5,27,992 crore from Rs 4,41,970 crore, an accretion of Rs 86,022 crore achieved substantially
through internal generation while the loan book grew 16.87%.

### **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>**

### **Improving, yet moderate profitability**

Profitability was impacted in the past owing to elevated GNPAs, leading to higher credit costs. With
the improvement in asset quality, credit costs have fallen materially and the earnings profile has
strengthened: standalone RoA has been above 1.0% since fiscal 2024 and reached 1.12% in fiscal 2026,
against 1.10% in fiscal 2025. Net profit rose to Rs 80,032 crore from Rs 70,901 crore.

Nonetheless, profitability remains moderate for the rating category. In fiscal 2026 NII grew only
4.08% while advances grew 16.87%, so earnings growth was driven by cost control (cost-to-income at
50.11%, from 51.64%) and low credit costs rather than by spread income. Return on equity eased to
18.57% from 20.32% in fiscal 2024, partly reflecting the larger equity base.

The margin trajectory has since turned. Domestic NIM moved 3.02% (Q1 fiscal 2026) → 3.05% (H1) →
3.08% (9M) → 3.03% (full year, the fourth quarter dragging the average down) → **3.00% in Q1 fiscal
2027, up 7 bps sequentially**, with NII growth reaccelerating to 14.88%. The compression was
therefore concentrated in the second half of fiscal 2026, consistent with lending rates repricing
downward faster than deposit costs, and is unwinding as deposits reprice. Return on assets held at
1.11% in the June 2026 quarter.

Even so, RoA in the 1.1% range remains modest against higher-rated private-sector peers, and the
bank's ability to sustain the margin recovery while containing credit costs — 0.27% in Q1 fiscal
2027 — remains a key monitorable.

### **<u>Liquidity</u> Superior**

The bank has a sizeable and granular retail deposit base, with retail banking supplying 88.27% of
total deposits and a CASA ratio of 39.46% on domestic deposits. The liquidity coverage ratio was
reported in a range of 124.32% to 143.05% across the quarterly averages disclosed for fiscal 2026,
and the solo net stable funding ratio in a range of 120.05% to 129.55% across the periods disclosed
— each comfortably above the 100% regulatory minimum. The bank also has access to systemic sources
of funds, including the liquidity adjustment facility from the RBI, the call money market and
refinance limits from apex bodies such as the National Housing Bank and the National Bank for
Agriculture and Rural Development.

### **<u>ESG profile</u>**

Crisil Ratings believes that SBI's Environment, Social, and Governance (ESG) profile supports its
already strong credit risk profile.

The ESG profile for financial sector entities typically factors in governance as a key differentiator
between them. The sector has reasonable social impact because of its substantial employee and
customer base and can play a key role in promoting financial inclusion. While the sector does not
have a direct adverse environmental impact, lending decisions may have a bearing on the environment.

SBI's key ESG highlights:

- The direct environmental risks associated with the bank are low given the nature of its business;
  the material environmental exposure is indirect, through lending and investment activities.

- The bank targets **carbon neutrality in its own operations by 2030** and **net zero, including
  financed emissions, by 2055**.

- The green advances portfolio **more than doubled during fiscal 2026 to Rs 1,18,723 crore** as on
  March 31, 2026, funded in part through green lines of credit and green bonds.

- Social reach is a structural strength of the franchise rather than an overlay: the bank operates
  23,265 domestic branches with a long-standing rural and semi-urban presence, and agriculture
  lending crossed Rs 4 lakh crore in fiscal 2026 — the highest for any bank in India.

- Governance is characterised by a board with a majority of independent directors, disclosure
  practices meeting SEBI (LODR) requirements including Regulation 25(8) certifications, a dedicated
  investor-grievance redressal mechanism and extensive financial disclosure. Statutory auditors
  expressed an **unmodified opinion**.

### **<u>Outlook</u> Stable**

Crisil Ratings believes the SBI group will maintain its dominant position in the financial services
sector in India and its strong resource profile over the medium term. Furthermore, GoI's ownership
will ensure continued need-based support, given the bank's significance to the domestic economy and
financial system.

### **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>**

### **Downward Factors**

- Material change in expectation of support from GoI.
- Significant and continuous increase in delinquencies (GNPAs crossing 10%), leading to a decline in

### **<u>About the Bank</u>**

SBI is the oldest and largest bank in India, and **is a scheduled commercial bank** designated a
Domestic Systemically Important Bank by the RBI. As on March 31, 2026, GoI owned 55.03% of the
bank's equity capital. The SBI group offers a wide range of banking and non-banking products and
services to corporate and retail customers. It had 23,265 domestic branches and 245 foreign
branches/offices as on March 31, 2026, and a presence across the globe. Through its non-banking
subsidiaries and joint venture companies, it offers investment banking, credit cards, life
insurance, general insurance, fund management, primary dealership, broking and factoring.

On a standalone basis, SBI reported a net profit of Rs 80,032 crore in fiscal 2026, against
Rs 70,901 crore in fiscal 2025 and Rs 61,077 crore in fiscal 2024.

### **Note on tier I instruments (under Basel III)**

Tier I bonds under Basel III are hybrid instruments carrying features of both equity and debt. Their
distinguishing features are the discretion available to the issuer on coupon payment, the link
between coupon payment and both the availability of distributable reserves and the maintenance of
the regulatory capital-conservation buffer, and the write-down of principal at the point of
non-viability as determined by the regulator. These features expose the instruments to a higher
level of risk than the bank's senior obligations and the rating is accordingly notched down.

### **Note on tier-II instruments (under Basel III)**

Tier II instruments under Basel III carry a principal write-down feature at the point of
non-viability, but do not carry the coupon-discretion and distributable-reserves features of Tier I
instruments. For an issuer of this credit quality the incremental risk is not considered sufficient
to warrant a notch-down from the senior rating.
