# **Rationale**

The ratings assigned to State Bank of India (SBI) factor in its dominant position in the Indian banking system as the country's largest bank, with total assets of Rs. 76.23 lakh crore, gross advances of Rs. 49.33 lakh crore and total deposits of Rs. 59.76 lakh crore as on March 31, 2026, and its unmatched systemic importance, reflected in the highest domestic systemically important bank (D-SIB) buffer requirement of 0.80% prescribed by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). The ratings also factor in the majority sovereign ownership — the Government of India (GoI) held a 55.03% stake as on March 31, 2026 — and SBI's role as the principal banker to the Government and to over 53 crore customers, which underpins the expectation of timely sovereign support, should there be a need.

SBI's asset quality has improved to its best levels in over a decade, with the gross non-performing asset (NPA) ratio at 1.49% and the net NPA ratio at 0.39% as on March 31, 2026 (2.24% and 0.57%, respectively, as on March 31, 2024), supported by a low fresh slippage ratio of 0.54% in FY2026 and a provision coverage ratio of 74.36% (91.97% including accounts under collection accounts/AUCA). The bank's solvency position is strong, with net NPAs at ~3.6% of its common equity tier 1 (CET-1) capital. The capitalisation profile strengthened considerably during FY2026, aided by the Rs. 25,000-crore equity raise through a qualified institutional placement (QIP) in July 2025 — the largest ever in the Indian capital markets — and healthy internal accruals. The CET-1 ratio improved to 12.29% as on March 31, 2026 (10.81% a year earlier) against SBI's minimum requirement of 8.80% (including the capital conservation buffer and the D-SIB buffer), while the total capital ratio stood at 15.40% against the requirement of 12.30%, providing a comfortable cushion of over 3% across capital tiers.

The bank's earnings profile remains healthy, with a net profit of Rs. 80,032 crore in FY2026 (+12.9% YoY), translating into a return on assets (RoA) of 1.12% and a return on equity (RoE) of 18.57%. ICRA, however, notes the compression in the domestic net interest margin (NIM) to 3.03% in FY2026 (3.22% in FY2025 and 3.43% in FY2024) amid the declining interest rate cycle, with the Q4 FY2026 domestic NIM at 2.93%; the trajectory of margins as deposit costs reprice remains a monitorable. Further, credit growth of 16.9% in FY2026 outpaced deposit growth of 11.0%, pushing up the credit-to-deposit ratio to 82.5% as on March 31, 2026 from 76.6% as on March 31, 2024, while the CASA ratio moderated to 39.46% (41.11% as on March 31, 2024). The bank's ability to fund growth while protecting its deposit franchise and margins, and the impact of the transition to the expected credit loss (ECL) framework, will remain monitorables. Nonetheless, SBI's liquidity remains strong, with the quarterly average liquidity coverage ratio (LCR) ranging between 124% and 143% during FY2026.

The rating on the Basel III compliant Tier 2 bonds is not notched down from SBI's senior/issuer rating. In line with ICRA's methodology for rating Basel III instruments, the notching assessment is driven by the likelihood of invocation of the point of non-viability (PONV) trigger. Given SBI's comfortable capital cushions across tiers, its strong solvency, its systemic importance as India's largest bank and the GoI's demonstrated stance of pre-emptively capitalising public sector banks well above the PONV thresholds, ICRA considers the probability of the PONV trigger invocation to be remote.

The Stable outlook reflects ICRA's expectation that SBI will maintain its dominant market position, comfortable capitalisation, and stable asset quality, while its earnings remain adequate through the interest rate cycle.

# **Key rating drivers and their description**

## **Credit strengths**

**Dominant, systemically most important bank in India with sovereign ownership** – SBI is India's largest bank with total assets of Rs. 76.23 lakh crore as on March 31, 2026, a deposit base of Rs. 59.76 lakh crore and a customer base exceeding 53 crore. It carries the highest D-SIB buffer (0.80%) prescribed by the RBI, reflecting its unmatched systemic importance, and handles ~27% of the UPI ecosystem's transaction volumes. The GoI held 55.03% as on March 31, 2026, and SBI's role in policy transmission and financial inclusion reinforces the expectation of extraordinary sovereign support, if ever required. The bank's franchise spans 29 countries through 245 overseas offices, with digital leadership through the YONO platform (10 crore+ registered users).

**Strengthened capitalisation following the largest-ever domestic QIP** – The Rs. 25,000-crore QIP of July 2025, together with healthy internal accruals, lifted the CET-1 ratio to 12.29% as on March 31, 2026 (10.81% a year earlier), a cushion of ~3.5% over the regulatory minimum of 8.80% applicable to SBI. The Tier 1 ratio and CRAR stood at 13.33% and 15.40% against requirements of 10.30% and 12.30%, respectively. The GoI's stake of 55.03% provides headroom for further capital raising without breaching the 51% floor, and the bank's internal accruals are expected to fund growth capital requirements over the medium term.

**Asset quality at decade-best levels with strong solvency** – The GNPA and NNPA ratios improved to 1.49% and 0.39%, respectively, as on March 31, 2026, from 2.24% and 0.57% as on March 31, 2024, with the fresh slippage ratio contained at 0.54% in FY2026 and credit cost at 0.37%. Net NPAs stood at ~3.6% of CET-1 capital (Rs. 18,830 crore against CET-1 capital of Rs. 5.29 lakh crore), reflecting a strong solvency profile.

**Healthy and diversified earnings** – Net profit rose 12.9% YoY to Rs. 80,032 crore in FY2026 (RoA: 1.12%; RoE: 18.57%), with operating profit growth of 11.2% to Rs. 1,23,015 crore. The loan book is well diversified across retail personal (Rs. 17.36 lakh crore, +15.2% YoY), corporate (Rs. 14.25 lakh crore, +14.8%), SME (+21.0%) and agriculture (+19.7%) segments, with home loans constituting the largest retail product. Strong subsidiaries across life insurance, asset management, cards and general insurance add franchise value and fee income diversity.

## **Credit challenges**

**NIM compression amid the declining rate cycle** – The domestic NIM moderated to 3.03% in FY2026 from 3.22% in FY2025 and 3.43% in FY2024, with Q4 FY2026 at 2.93%, as lending yields repriced faster than deposit costs. Net interest income growth was modest at 4.1% in FY2026. The margin trajectory, as deposit repricing catches up, remains a key monitorable for the earnings profile.

**Credit growth outpacing deposits; moderating CASA** – Credit growth of 16.9% in FY2026 exceeded deposit growth of 11.0%, raising the credit-to-deposit ratio to 82.5% as on March 31, 2026 (76.6% as on March 31, 2024), while the CASA ratio slipped to 39.46% (41.11%). Sustained faster credit growth could increase the reliance on higher-cost term deposits, pressuring margins and consuming on-balance-sheet liquidity. The transition to the ECL provisioning framework also remains an industry-wide monitorable, though SBI's provision buffers and pre-provision profitability provide adequate absorption capacity.

# **Environmental and social risks**

**Environmental considerations** – As a lender, SBI's direct environmental footprint is limited, though its portfolio is exposed to climate transition and physical risks across borrower segments, including agriculture (portfolio above Rs. 4 lakh crore). The bank has financed renewable energy projects of Rs. 1,48,993 crore (portfolio capacity of 59,208 MW), has instituted an in-house ESG rating model for borrower risk review, and reduced its Scope 1 and 2 emissions intensity by 8.84% during FY2026.

**Social considerations** – SBI's social profile is supported by its financial-inclusion mandate — 18.49 crore basic savings bank deposit accounts, 15,174 rural and semi-urban branches and coverage of 20,000+ villages through CSR — while its large workforce (28.5% women; 98.97% retention) and directed-lending obligations expose it to execution and human-capital risks inherent to its scale and quasi-policy role.

# **Liquidity position: Strong**

SBI's liquidity is strong, underpinned by the granularity and stickiness of India's largest deposit franchise, quarterly average LCR of 124-143% during FY2026 (comfortably above the 100% regulatory requirement), and a large statutory investment portfolio (investments of Rs. 18.01 lakh crore as on March 31, 2026, predominantly government securities) that provides substantial on-balance-sheet liquidity. The rising credit-to-deposit ratio (82.5%) consumes a part of this buffer and remains a monitorable, though the bank's access to deposit and wholesale funding markets, domestic and international, is unmatched in the Indian banking system.

# **Rating sensitivities**

**Positive factors** – Not applicable.

**Negative factors** – Negative pressure on the ratings could arise in case of a significant deterioration in the asset quality, leading to pressure on the earnings and solvency profile, or a decline in the capital cushions (CET-1 cushion below 1-2% over the regulatory minimum on a sustained basis). Any material change in the sovereign ownership below majority levels or in the expectation of GoI support would also be a rating sensitivity, including for the Basel III Tier 2 bonds, whose rating is linked to the remote likelihood of PONV invocation.

# **About the company**

State Bank of India, constituted under the State Bank of India Act, 1955, is India's largest commercial bank, with total assets of Rs. 76.23 lakh crore, gross advances of Rs. 49.33 lakh crore and deposits of Rs. 59.76 lakh crore as on March 31, 2026. The GoI held a 55.03% stake as on March 31, 2026. The bank serves over 53 crore customers through the largest branch and ATM network in India (374 new branches added in FY2026; ~30% market share in installed ATMs/ADWMs) and 245 overseas offices across 29 countries. Its digital platform YONO has over 10 crore registered users, and the bank accounts for ~27% of UPI ecosystem transaction volumes. SBI's major subsidiaries include SBI Life Insurance, SBI Funds Management, SBI Cards and Payment Services, SBI General Insurance and SBI Capital Markets.

In FY2026, SBI raised Rs. 25,000 crore of equity through a QIP (July 2025), the largest in the Indian capital markets, with more than 64% participation from foreign investors.

# **Key features of the rated instrument**

The servicing of the Basel III Tier 2 bonds is not subject to the bank's capital ratios or profitability, and the bonds carry no coupon-deferral clause. However, the bonds are expected to absorb losses upon the invocation of the point of non-viability (PONV) trigger by the RBI, whereupon they may be written down (partially or fully) or converted into common equity at the RBI's option. These equity-like loss-absorption features may translate into a higher severity of loss vis-à-vis conventional debt instruments. The claims of the bondholders are subordinated to those of all depositors and general creditors of the bank, and senior to instruments eligible for inclusion in Tier 1 capital.
