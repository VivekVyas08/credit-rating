## Rationale and key rating drivers

The rating assigned to the commercial paper (CP) issue of ITC Limited (ITC) reflects long-term fundamental credit quality at the highest level, mapped to the short-term scale in line with CareEdge Ratings' criteria on Rating of Short Term Instruments, wherein the short-term rating is derived from the entity's long-term credit assessment in conjunction with an evaluation of its liquidity profile and financial flexibility.

ITC's long-term fundamentals derive strength from its leadership position in the strictly regulated domestic cigarettes industry — where large size, dominant market share and strong brands confer the pricing power that, per CareEdge Ratings' methodology for manufacturing companies, distinguishes a price-maker from a price-taker — and from a highly diversified, synergistic business portfolio spanning FMCG-Cigarettes, FMCG-Others, Paperboards, Paper & Packaging and Agri Business, which lends a higher degree of sustainability to cash flows. Earnings capacity is demonstrated by consolidated total operating income (TOI, net of excise duty) of ₹78,868 crore in FY26 (refers to April 01 to March 31; ₹75,323 crore in FY25) with PBILDT margin stable at 34.6% despite input-cost pressure, evidencing gross margins sufficient to absorb operating and fixed costs.

The financial risk profile is exceptionally strong across CareEdge Ratings' ratio categories — profitability (PBILDT margin 34.6%, PAT margin 26.2%), leverage and coverage (overall gearing 0.03x; TD/PBILDT ~0.1x; interest coverage ~321x for FY26) and liquidity. Cash flow from operations comfortably covers investing and financing needs with no reliance on financing cash flows; total debt (including lease liabilities) of about ₹2,399 crore as on March 31, 2026, compares with cash and current liquid investments of about ₹23,759 crore, a net-cash position of over ₹21,000 crore.

The rating takes note of the unprecedented increase in cigarette taxation effective February 01, 2026 — GST raised from 28% of transaction value to 40% of retail sale price alongside higher excise duties on phasing-out of the Compensation Cess, lifting ITC's cigarette tax incidence by an estimated 60-65% after nearly eight years of relative stability. With cigarettes contributing approximately 84% of segment profit before interest and tax in FY26, this is the dominant industry-risk development bearing on ITC's profit pool; however, it is an earnings and growth headwind rather than a solvency or liquidity event, and the entity's demonstrated ability to tide through adverse industry phases with better performance than peers — a factor viewed positively under the methodology's industry-risk analysis — supports the assessment. The trajectory of cigarette volumes, pricing pass-through and illicit-trade migration through FY27 remains a key monitorable.

The rating remains constrained by the concentration of profits in a single, strictly regulated industry, susceptibility of profitability to volatility in agri-commodity input prices, exposure of the paperboards segment to low-priced imports, and intense competition in the FMCG industry.

## Rating sensitivities: Factors likely to lead to rating actions

### Positive factors

- Not applicable — the rating is at the highest level on the short-term rating scale.

### Negative factors

- Durable and severe impairment of profitability and cash generation from the cigarette-taxation transition or further adverse regulatory action — for example, a sustained, large migration of volumes to illicit trade — materially eroding the net-cash position.
- Any large debt-funded capex/acquisition disproportionate to the balance sheet, with overall gearing exceeding 0.50x or TD/PBILDT exceeding 0.50x on a sustained basis.

# Detailed description of key rating drivers:

# Key strengths

## Economy and industry position: leadership of a large, entry-barrier-protected industry with demonstrated resilience through regulatory cycles

The domestic cigarettes industry is strategically significant to government revenue, strictly regulated and licensed, with high entry barriers and a large illicit segment (estimated at about a third of the legal industry; India is estimated to be the fourth-largest illicit cigarette market globally). Within this industry, ITC — established in 1910 and the largest cigarette manufacturer and seller in India — holds sustained market leadership across a wide portfolio of trademarks and price points. Per CareEdge Ratings' manufacturing methodology, a sustained leadership position leads to better revenue visibility and long-term cash generation, and a market leader typically has the financial resources and flexibility to pass on input-price and tax increases; ITC has demonstrated exactly this ability across successive duty increases over decades, consistently posting better operational and financial performance than other legal players through industry downturns — a factor viewed positively in the industry-risk assessment.

## Diversification: segmental, geographic and counterparty spread lending sustainability to cash flows

ITC operates across four principal operating segments — FMCG-Cigarettes, FMCG-Others, Paperboards, Paper & Packaging and Agri Business — with non-cigarette businesses now constituting about two-thirds of net segment revenue. Each major segment's income, profitability and contribution are material: FMCG-Others posted segment revenue of ₹24,210 crore in FY26 (up 10.1% y-o-y, segment results up a faster 14.1%) across staples, biscuits, noodles, dairy, personal wash, homecare and incense sticks; Paperboards improved through H2FY26 aided by the Minimum Import Price on virgin paperboards and moderating wood prices; the Agri Business, though affected by West Asia-related trade disruptions and export restrictions, underpins raw-material sourcing for the other segments. Customer and supplier diversification is wide, with a pan-India omni-channel distribution platform (including strong traction in e-commerce, quick-commerce and modern trade) and no material counterparty concentration. The hotels business was demerged into ITC Hotels Limited effective January 01, 2025 (ITC holds ~40%, an associate), without material impact on the financial risk profile.

## Earnings capacity and cost structure: stable, superior margins with integrated operations

Consolidated TOI grew ~4.7% y-o-y in FY26 with PBILDT margin stable at 34.6% (34.5% in FY25) — among the healthiest in the sector — despite elevated input costs across edible oil, wheat, cocoa and wood and a rising mix of the lower-margin agri business, evidencing gross margins comfortably sufficient to absorb other operating and fixed costs. Operating efficiency is supported by a high level of integration: in-house agri-sourcing (seed-to-smoke integration in tobacco), captive paperboard and packaging capability, cost-competitive manufacturing, and sustained investment in brands and R&D-led premiumisation. This integration provides the flexibility, recognised under the methodology's cost-structure analysis, to protect margins against input-price movements.

## Financial risk profile: robust ratios, cash flow adequacy and financial flexibility

Across CareEdge Ratings' ratio categories for manufacturing companies: growth (TOI up ~4.7% in FY26), profitability (PBILDT margin 34.6%; PAT margin 26.2%), leverage and coverage (overall gearing 0.03x as on March 31, 2026; TD/PBILDT ~0.1x; interest coverage ~321x) and liquidity ratios are all at the strongest levels. Cash flow from operations (consolidated PBILDT of about ₹27,318 crore in FY26) comfortably funds investing needs (routine capex and the announced acquisition of the Century Pulp and Paper undertaking, indicated at up to about ₹3,500 crore, to be funded through internal accruals) and financing outflows (sizeable dividends), with no use of financing cash flows to support operations. Financial flexibility — assessed per the methodology without assuming refinancing or equity raising — is exceptional: unencumbered cash and current liquid investments of about ₹23,759 crore, non-current investments of about ₹12,090 crore (mainly market securities and strategic holdings), a very large market capitalisation, and consistently undrawn banking lines. Accounting quality is strong, with clean audit opinions and prudent disclosure (the company transparently disclosed that post the February 2026 taxation change, FY26 gross revenue and excise are not strictly comparable with the prior year).

## Management evaluation: professional management with conservative financial policies and strong governance

ITC is professionally managed with no identified promoter — a fragmented shareholding with the largest holder (Tobacco Manufacturers (India) Limited, a British American Tobacco group entity) at 20.31%, and no promoter pledge considerations. The management's track record through regulatory cycles is long and demonstrated; its response to the February 2026 taxation reset — staggered, calibrated pricing with portfolio re-architecture at protected price points rather than immediate full pass-through, specifically designed to limit volume migration to illicit trade — illustrates the strategic capability the methodology seeks in management evaluation. Corporate strategy is conservative: growth (including the Century Pulp and Paper acquisition) is funded from internal accruals with no debt appetite. Governance features a board with a substantial independent component, extensive disclosures and no adverse regulatory strictures.

# Liquidity: Strong

Assessed per CareEdge Ratings' criteria on Rating of Short Term Instruments and Liquidity Analysis of Non-Financial Sector Entities: the ₹500 crore CP programme is a small fraction of available liquidity — unencumbered cash and current liquid investments of about ₹23,759 crore as on March 31, 2026, against total debt (including lease liabilities) of about ₹2,399 crore, largely short-term and self-liquidating. Working capital limit utilisation is negligible, so drawing-power variation poses no risk to back-up liquidity for CP repayment. Internal accruals (consolidated PBILDT ~₹27,318 crore in FY26) cover capex, dividends and working capital with a large surplus; there is no reliance on refinancing or rollover. The asset-light cash position means CP repayment capacity is effectively independent of market conditions.

# Key weaknesses

## Industry risk: concentration of profits in a strictly regulated, taxation-driven industry — FY26 reset a key monitorable

The cigarettes industry's defining risk is regulatory: taxation policy directly determines industry economics, and the February 01, 2026 reset (GST from 28% of transaction value to 40% of retail sale price, plus higher excise duties on phasing-out of the Compensation Cess) lifted ITC's tax incidence by an estimated 60-65% — the steepest increase in over a decade, requiring an estimated ~35% MRP increase for EBIT-neutrality. Because cigarettes generate ~84% of segment PBIT, this concentrates the impact on ITC's dominant profit pool; external estimates for FY27 cigarette EBIT range from broadly flat to a decline of ~19%, with the risk of volume migration to illicit trade. Q4 FY26 cigarette performance held up ahead of full price transmission (gross revenue up ~31.7% y-o-y). Mitigants: ITC's pricing power as market leader, portfolio re-architecture levers, seed-to-smoke integration and — decisively — a net-cash balance sheet under which the earnings volatility has no bearing on debt-servicing capacity.

## Susceptibility of profitability to raw material price volatility, import competition and intense FMCG competition

Profitability is exposed to fluctuations in prices of key inputs (edible oil, wheat, cocoa, soap noodles, wood) — agri-commodities whose supply is further exposed to monsoon variability. The paperboards segment faces low-priced imports (mitigated by the Minimum Import Price) and the Agri Business faces export/trade disruptions, including West Asia-related, and domestic agri-commodity restrictions. The Indian FMCG market is intensely competitive across organised and unorganised players, requiring sustained advertising and promotional spend; ITC's brand equity, premiumisation, net-revenue management and cost programmes provide meaningful mitigation.

## Analysis of Environmental, Social and Governance risk factors

**Environmental:** ITC is exposed to physical climate risk, as agricultural commodities are key raw materials across most segments, and to evolving norms on manufacturing waste and packaging plastics. Under Sustainability 2.0, ITC targets 50% renewable energy share, 30% reduction in specific energy consumption and 50% cut in specific GHG emissions by 2030 (FY19 baseline), with net-zero operations (Scope 1 and 2) by 2050. **Social:** Tobacco consumption is considered a social hazard and is strictly regulated; this exposure crystallised in the February 2026 taxation reset and is the clearest illustration of the asymmetric social/regulatory risk attaching to ITC's dominant profit pool. **Governance:** professional management with no identified promoter, a board with a substantial independent component, extensive disclosures and no adverse regulatory strictures — supporting the credit assessment.
