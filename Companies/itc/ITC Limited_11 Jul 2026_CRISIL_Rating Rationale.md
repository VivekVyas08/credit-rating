### <u>Detailed Rationale</u>

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its 'Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+' ratings on the bank facilities of ITC Limited (ITC).

Operating income (net of excise duty) grew to Rs 78,868 crore in fiscal 2026 from Rs 75,323 crore in fiscal 2025 on a consolidated basis, driven by growth in the FMCG-Others, Cigarettes and Paperboards segments, with EBITDA margin healthy and broadly stable at 34.6% (34.5% in fiscal 2025) despite elevated input costs in edible oil, wheat, cocoa and wood. The financial risk profile remains exceptionally strong: total debt (including lease liabilities) of about Rs 2,399 crore as on March 31, 2026, compares with cash and current liquid investments of about Rs 23,759 crore, leaving the company deeply net-cash (around Rs 21,360 crore) with interest coverage of over 300 times.

The dominant development in the credit profile is the unprecedented increase in cigarette taxation effective February 1, 2026 — GST on cigarettes was raised from 28% of transaction value to 40% of retail sale price alongside higher excise duties on the phasing-out of the Compensation Cess, lifting ITC's cigarette tax incidence by an estimated 60-65% after nearly eight years of relative tax stability. As cigarettes contributed approximately 84% of segment profit before interest and tax in fiscal 2026, this directly pressures ITC's dominant profit pool; the company is passing the increase through in a staggered, calibrated manner (with portfolio re-architecture at protected price points) to limit migration of volumes to illicit trade, which is already estimated at about a third of the legal industry. Fourth-quarter cigarette performance held up ahead of full price transmission (gross revenue up ~31.7% year-on-year). Crisil Ratings believes this is an earnings and growth headwind, not a solvency or liquidity event: even on bearish external estimates for fiscal 2027 cigarette EBIT (flat to down ~19%), consolidated cash generation remains very large and the balance sheet stays deeply net-cash. The trajectory of cigarette volumes, pricing pass-through and illicit-trade migration through fiscal 2027 remains a key monitorable.

The ratings continue to reflect ITC's robust business risk profile — assessed per Crisil Ratings' criteria for the manufacturing, trading and corporate services sector across industry risk, market position and operating efficiency — marked by its presence in diverse businesses viz. leading FMCG marketer in Cigarettes, Branded Packaged Foods, Personal Care, Education & Stationery, Safety Matches & Incense Sticks, foremost agri player, and leadership in Paperboards, Paper & Packaging, delivering strong and sustainable profitability. The management risk assessment is supportive: ITC is professionally managed with a conservative risk appetite — leverage is negligible and growth (including the announced Century Pulp and Paper acquisition) is funded from internal accrual rather than debt. These strengths are partially offset by exposure to regulatory risk in the cigarette business and to risks inherent in the various businesses the company operates in.

### <u>Key Rating Drivers - Strengths</u>

### Dominant market position in cigarettes, anchored by brand equity and distribution reach

Under Crisil Ratings' criteria, the key factors for assessing market position in branded consumer goods and FMCG are distribution network and brand equity — and on both, ITC is the benchmark. Strong brands across a wide portfolio of trademarks and price points, healthy brand loyalty, deep consumer insight and one of India's widest omni-channel distribution platforms have enabled ITC to consolidate its leadership of the domestic cigarette market and sustain market share and profitability through successive duty increases. Critically, ITC's market position confers genuine pricing leadership — the ability to control selling prices rather than merely hold volume share — which is what allows a staggered, calibrated pass-through of the February 2026 tax increase with product re-architecture (for example, shorter-length variants at protected price points), levers unavailable to smaller legal players. Revenue diversity reinforces the market position: non-cigarette businesses constitute about two-thirds of net segment revenue, led by FMCG-Others (segment revenue Rs 24,210 crore in fiscal 2026, up 10.1%, with segment results up a faster 14.1% on premiumisation and strong traction in e-commerce, quick-commerce and modern trade), reducing dependence on any single product-market.

### Superior operating efficiency from vertical integration and access to resources

On the operating-efficiency factors in Crisil Ratings' criteria — access to resources, level of integration, capacity utilisation and R&D — ITC scores strongly. Its seed-to-smoke backward integration in tobacco (leaf sourcing through the agri business, packaging through the paperboards business) and integrated agri-sourcing for packaged foods provide captive access to key raw materials and bargaining power with suppliers, imparting flexibility to protect operating margins — reflected in consolidated EBITDA margin holding at 34.6% in fiscal 2026 despite elevated edible oil, wheat, cocoa and wood prices. Sustained R&D and product innovation capability supports premiumisation across cigarettes and FMCG-Others. The Paperboards segment improved through the second half of fiscal 2026, aided by the Minimum Import Price on virgin paperboards and moderating wood prices; the announced Century Pulp and Paper acquisition (up to about Rs 3,500 crore, from internal accruals) deepens this integration.

### Strong financial risk profile with exceptional cash flow adequacy and financial flexibility

Crisil Ratings' financial risk analysis centres on the sustainability and adequacy of cash flows relative to debt obligations — and ITC's cash flows cover its obligations by an exceptional margin. Consolidated EBITDA of about Rs 27,318 crore in fiscal 2026 compares with total debt (including lease liabilities) of about Rs 2,399 crore; adjusted gearing is effectively nil and interest coverage about 321 times. On the balance-sheet side, the capital structure is deeply net-cash — cash and current liquid investments of about Rs 23,759 crore, supplemented by non-current investments of about Rs 12,090 crore — and working capital management is strong, with negligible utilisation of bank limits. Financial flexibility is outstanding: a very large pool of marketable securities, negligible reliance on debt funding, demonstrated capital-market access and full flexibility to defer or phase capital expenditure. Accounting quality is sound, with clean audit opinions and transparent disclosure of the fiscal 2026 excise non-comparability arising from the February 2026 taxation change.

### <u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>

### Exposure to regulatory risk in the cigarette business and input-price volatility across segments

Industry risk in cigarettes is dominated by government policy: the February 2026 reset — the steepest in over a decade — requires an estimated ~35% MRP increase for EBIT-neutrality and raises the risk of volume migration to illicit trade (already about a third of the legal industry). As cigarettes contribute ~84% of segment PBIT, this concentrates the earnings impact. Additionally, per the criteria's analysis of input-output price linkages, parts of ITC's portfolio have weak linkage between input prices and selling prices in the short run: agri-commodity inputs (edible oil, wheat, cocoa, wood) are externally priced while FMCG price points are sticky, and paperboards realisations are governed by the landed cost of imports — both of which can compress margins in adverse phases. The agri business is further exposed to trade disruptions (including West Asia-related) and export restrictions. These risks are partially offset by ITC's backward integration, pricing leadership, premiumisation pipeline and cost programmes — and, decisively for the ratings, by a balance sheet that absorbs earnings volatility without any impact on debt servicing.

### <u>Liquidity:</u> Superior

Liquidity is driven by cash and current liquid investments of about Rs 23,759 crore as on March 31, 2026, and consolidated EBITDA of about Rs 27,318 crore in fiscal 2026. Bank limit utilisation — which Crisil Ratings evaluates against drawing power as the key external liquidity indicator — is negligible, leaving the sanctioned working-capital lines effectively fully available as back-up. With negligible debt (largely short-term and self-liquidating), cash accrual comfortably covers capital expenditure (including the announced Century Pulp and Paper acquisition), dividend payouts and working capital requirements over the medium term, with no reliance on refinancing.

### <u>ESG Profile</u>

Crisil Ratings believes the environment, social and governance (ESG) profile of ITC supports its credit risk profile. The tobacco sector has a high social impact owing to health hazards associated with tobacco use, as well as the risk of government intervention through sales restrictions, marketing regulation and higher taxes — a risk that materialised in the February 2026 taxation reset. The company's strong focus on addressing ESG risks supports its already strong credit risk profile.

**Key ESG highlights:**

- Under its *Sustainability 2.0* agenda, ITC targets 50% renewable share of total energy consumption, a 30% reduction in specific energy consumption and a 50% cut in specific GHG emissions by 2030 (fiscal 2019 baseline), with a commitment to net-zero operations (Scope 1 and 2) by 2050.
- Exposure to physical climate risk is inherent, as agricultural commodities are key inputs across most segments.
- Governance is characterised by a board with a substantial independent component and extensive financial disclosures; ITC is professionally managed with no identified promoter.

### <u>Outlook:</u> Stable

Crisil Ratings believes ITC will maintain its robust financial risk profile and strong market position across the segments in which it operates over the medium term, notwithstanding the cigarette-taxation transition.

### <u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>

**Downward factors**

- Large, debt-funded capital expenditure or acquisition weakening the financial risk profile, with gearing exceeding 0.5 time
- Adverse impact of changes in regulations for the cigarette industry materially beyond current expectations — for example, a sustained, large migration of volumes to illicit trade durably impairing cash generation
- Considerable decline in cash and liquid investments

### <u>About the Company</u>

ITC operates in a variety of business segments: FMCG-Cigarettes; FMCG-Others (branded packaged foods, personal care, education & stationery, safety matches, agarbatti); Paperboards, Paper & Packaging; Agri Business; and Others. The hotels business was demerged effective January 1, 2025, into ITC Hotels Limited, in which ITC holds about 40%. IT services are provided through subsidiary ITC Infotech India Ltd. ITC is professionally managed with no identified promoter; Tobacco Manufacturers (India) Limited (a British American Tobacco group entity) held 20.31% as on the reporting date. Cigarette manufacturing and sales remain the largest contributor to profits (approximately 84% of segment PBIT in fiscal 2026).
