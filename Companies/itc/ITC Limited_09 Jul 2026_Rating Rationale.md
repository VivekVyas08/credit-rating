## Rationale

The rating reaffirmation continues to factor in ITC Limited's (ITC) leadership position in the domestic cigarettes industry, which translates into strong and stable cash flows and a robust financial profile characterised by healthy return metrics, a conservative capital structure, superior debt-protection metrics and a superior liquidity profile. On a consolidated basis, ITC's operating income (net of excise duty) grew to Rs. 78,868 crore in FY2026 from Rs. 75,323 crore in FY2025, while operating profitability (OPBDIT/OI) remained healthy at around 34.6%, broadly stable year-on-year despite elevated input costs across edible oil, wheat, cocoa and wood, and a rising mix of the relatively lower-margin agri-business. The financial profile remains effectively net-cash: total debt (including lease liabilities) of about Rs. 2,399 crore as on March 31, 2026 compares with cash and current liquid investments of about Rs. 23,759 crore, resulting in a net cash position of around Rs. 21,360 crore and interest coverage that is not economically meaningful. (ITC has noted that, following the February 2026 change in the cigarette taxation structure, gross revenue and excise duties for FY2026 are not strictly comparable with the prior year; operating income above is stated net of excise duty.)

ICRA notes the demerger of ITC's hotels and hospitality business into ITC Hotels Limited (ITCHL), effective January 1, 2025, following which ITC holds around 40% in ITCHL, now an associate. Consistent with the hotels business having accounted for less than 5% of consolidated revenue and profits, the demerger did not have a material impact on ITC's financial risk profile; it does, however, make FY2024 (which still included hotels) not strictly comparable with FY2025 and FY2026 continuing operations. ITC continues to hold a leading position across its diversified portfolio — its four principal operating segments being FMCG-Cigarettes, FMCG-Others, Paperboards/Paper & Packaging and Agri Business (with a small residual "Others" segment) — supported by strong inter-business synergies in agri-sourcing, cost-competitive manufacturing and a wide omni-channel distribution platform. Non-cigarette businesses now constitute about two-thirds of net segment revenue.

The dominant near-term development in the credit profile is an **unprecedented increase in cigarette taxation effective February 1, 2026** — the GST rate on cigarettes was raised from 28% of transaction value to 40% of retail sale price, alongside a steep hike in excise duties on the phasing-out of the Compensation Cess (source: ITC Q4 FY2026 press release). By industry estimates this raised ITC's cigarette tax incidence by around 60–65% and would require an MRP increase of roughly 35% for EBIT-neutrality — the sharpest reset after nearly eight years (2018–2025) of relative tax stability. This is material because the cigarettes segment, while a declining share of revenue, contributed approximately 84% of ITC's segment profit before interest and tax in FY2026 (company segmental results). ITC has responded with staggered, calibrated pricing rather than an immediate full pass-through, together with portfolio re-architecture (e.g. shorter-length variants at protected price points), specifically to limit the shift of volumes to illicit trade (already estimated at about a third of the legal industry, making India the fourth-largest illicit cigarette market globally). ICRA notes that Q4 FY2026 cigarette performance held up ahead of full price transmission (cigarette gross revenue up ~31.7% year-on-year; net-of-tax revenue up ~10–11%), but the company itself has cautioned that "the year ahead presents an extremely challenging operating environment," and external analyst estimates for FY2027 cigarette earnings range from broadly resilient to a decline of around 19% in segment EBIT. The trajectory of cigarette volumes, pricing pass-through and illicit-trade migration through FY2027 is a **key monitorable**.

The FMCG-Others segment delivered double-digit growth, with segment revenue of Rs. 24,209.75 crore in FY2026 (up 10.1% year-on-year) and segment results growing a faster 14.1%, reflecting scale benefits, portfolio premiumisation and disciplined cost management, with robust traction in e-commerce, quick-commerce and modern-trade channels. The Paperboards, Paper & Packaging segment operated in a challenging environment of soft demand and low-priced imports, though performance improved through H2 FY2026 aided by the imposition of a Minimum Import Price on virgin paperboards and moderating wood prices. The Agri Business revenue was affected by West Asia-related trade disruptions and agri-commodity export restrictions. ITC has also announced the acquisition of the Century Pulp and Paper undertaking (indicated at up to about Rs. 3,500 crore, to be funded through internal accruals). Given ITC's superior liquidity and internal accrual generation, such investments are not expected to alter its net-cash profile.

Critically for the rating, this earnings headwind does **not** impair ITC's debt-servicing capacity. The company carries negligible debt against a net-cash position of over Rs. 21,000 crore; even on the more bearish external FY2027 cigarette-earnings scenarios, consolidated cash generation remains very large and the balance sheet stays deeply net-cash. The near-term challenge is therefore to profitability and growth, not to solvency or liquidity. As the rated instrument is a short-term Commercial Paper programme, no outlook is assigned. The rating reflects ICRA's opinion that ITC's dominant market position, diversified portfolio, still-healthy profitability and exceptionally strong net-cash balance sheet will sustain timely servicing of its obligations at the highest level of safety through the cigarette-taxation transition.

## Key rating drivers and their description

### Credit strengths

**Market leadership in the domestic cigarettes industry with strong brand equity** – ITC remains the clear leader in the organised domestic cigarettes industry, supported by continuous product innovation, deep consumer insight and strong on-ground execution across a wide portfolio of trademarks and price points. The cigarettes business, while a declining share of revenue over time, remains the dominant contributor to ITC's profits (approximately 84% of segment PBIT in FY2026), underpinning the company's strong cash generation. This leadership and portfolio breadth are also central to ITC's ability to navigate the FY2026 taxation reset (see challenges), giving it more pricing and re-architecture levers than smaller legal players.

**Diversified, synergistic business portfolio with a large scale of operations** – ITC operates across FMCG-Cigarettes, FMCG-Others, Paperboards/Paper & Packaging and Agri Business, with consolidated operating income (net of excise) of Rs. 78,868 crore in FY2026. The FMCG-Others business (segment revenue Rs. 24,209.75 crore, +10.1% in FY2026) has scaled into a broad-based portfolio spanning staples, biscuits, noodles, dairy, premium personal wash, homecare and incense sticks, with strong momentum in premium and new-generation channels. Integrated agri-sourcing, cost-competitive manufacturing and a wide distribution platform lend meaningful inter-business synergies and revenue diversity.

**Robust financial profile — healthy profitability, negligible debt and a superior liquidity position** – Consolidated OPBDIT/OI stood at around 34.6% in FY2026 (34.5% in FY2025) and PAT/OI at around 26.2%, among the healthiest in the sector. The capital structure remains highly conservative, with TOL/TNW of about 0.3 times and total debt/OPBDIT of about 0.1 times as on/for FY2026. The company held cash and current liquid investments of about Rs. 23,759 crore as on March 31, 2026 (in addition to non-current investments of about Rs. 12,090 crore, mainly market securities and strategic holdings), against negligible debt — a substantial source of financial flexibility.

### Credit challenges

**Unprecedented FY2026 cigarette taxation reset — the primary near-term earnings risk, now crystallised** – Effective February 1, 2026, cigarette taxation rose sharply (GST from 28% of transaction value to 40% of retail sale price, plus higher excise duties on phasing-out of the Compensation Cess), lifting ITC's tax incidence by an estimated ~60–65% — the steepest hike in over a decade and a break from eight years of relative stability. Because cigarettes generate ~84% of segment PBIT, this directly pressures ITC's dominant profit pool. ITC is passing the increase through in a staggered manner (with product re-architecture) to protect volumes against illicit trade, which necessarily compresses cigarette unit economics in the transition; external estimates for FY2027 cigarette EBIT range from broadly flat to a ~19% decline, with volumes expected under pressure from Q1 FY2027. Mitigants: ITC's portfolio breadth, pricing levers, seed-to-smoke integration and premium pipeline, and — decisively for the rating — a net-cash balance sheet that absorbs the earnings volatility without any impact on debt servicing. This remains a **key monitorable**.

**Margins exposed to agri-commodity price volatility and, in paperboards, to import competition** – Profitability is exposed to fluctuations in prices of key inputs (edible oil, wheat, cocoa, soap noodles, wood), and the paperboards segment remains exposed to low-priced imports and soft realisations. ITC's pricing flexibility, premiumisation, net-revenue management and cost initiatives, together with policy support such as the Minimum Import Price on virgin paperboards, provide meaningful mitigation. The Agri Business is additionally exposed to export/trade disruptions (e.g. West Asia conflict) and domestic agri-commodity restrictions.

## Environmental and social risks

**Environmental considerations** – ITC remains exposed to physical climate risks, as agricultural commodities are key raw materials across most segments; variability in crop output can affect both raw-material availability and cost. The company is also exposed to evolving norms on manufacturing waste/discharge and on plastics used in packaging, which could affect its cost structure. Overall environmental risk for the FMCG industry is assessed as low, aided by pricing power to pass through cost increases over time.

**Social considerations** – Tobacco consumption is considered a social hazard and is strictly regulated; this social-risk exposure has crystallised in the February 2026 taxation reset, which materially raises cigarette tax incidence and is pressuring near-term segment economics. This is the clearest current illustration of the asymmetric social/regulatory risk attaching to ITC's dominant profit pool. More broadly, the FMCG industry depends on human capital and remains exposed to product-quality and changing consumer-preference risks, which ITC manages through quality systems, brand investment and innovation.

## Liquidity position: Superior

ITC's liquidity is Superior. On a consolidated basis, the company generated strong cash flows from operations (consolidated OPBDIT of about Rs. 27,318 crore in FY2026) and held cash and current liquid investments of about Rs. 23,759 crore as on March 31, 2026, supplemented by non-current investments of about Rs. 12,090 crore (mainly market securities and strategic holdings). Against these sources, claims are minimal: total debt including lease liabilities of about Rs. 2,399 crore (largely short-term, self-liquidating), routine capital expenditure, and sizeable dividend payouts — all comfortably covered by internal accruals, with negligible use of working-capital lines historically and no reliance on refinancing. The Rs. 500 crore Commercial Paper programme is a fraction of available liquidity.

## Rating sensitivities

**Positive factors** – Not applicable (the rating is at the highest short-term rating level).

**Negative factors** – Given the highest short-term rating, downgrade triggers are remote. Pressure could arise only if the cigarette-taxation transition (or further adverse regulatory/taxation action) were to durably and severely impair ITC's profitability and cash generation well beyond current expectations — for example, a sustained, large migration of volumes to illicit trade — or if a large debt-funded organic/inorganic initiative disproportionate to the balance sheet were to weaken coverage and liquidity on a sustained basis. On present evidence, the net-cash balance sheet leaves substantial headroom to absorb the taxation impact.

## About the company

ITC Limited (ITC), established in 1910, is the largest cigarette manufacturer and seller in India. Following the demerger of its hotels business into ITC Hotels Limited (effective January 1, 2025, in which ITC holds about 40% as an associate), ITC reports five operating segments — FMCG-Cigarettes; FMCG-Others (staples, biscuits, noodles, dairy, personal care, homecare, incense sticks, etc.); Paperboards, Paper & Packaging; Agri Business; and Others. IT solutions/services are provided through its subsidiary ITC Infotech (reflected within the consolidated group). ITC is professionally managed with no identified promoter; British American Tobacco group entities are among its largest shareholders (Tobacco Manufacturers (India) Limited: 20.31%), with the balance held by Indian financial institutions/insurers, SUUTI, foreign portfolio investors and the public.
