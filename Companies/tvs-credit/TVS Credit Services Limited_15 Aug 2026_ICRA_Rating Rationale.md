# **Rationale**

The ratings continue to factor in the strong expectation of support from the parent, TVS Motor
Company Limited (TVS Motor), which held **85.63%** of TVS Credit Services Limited (TVSCS) as on
March 31, 2026. TVSCS is the captive and strategic financing arm of the TVS group, originating a
substantial share of its business through the parent's dealer and customer network, and shares the
TVS brand. ICRA expects support to be forthcoming on both an ongoing basis and in the event of
distress, given the strategic importance of the financing business to the parent's vehicle
franchise and the reputational consequences of a shortfall at a same-brand entity.

The ratings also factor in the company's established franchise in two-wheeler, used-vehicle and
tractor financing, its improving leverage and its comfortable profitability. In FY2026, total
income grew 8.89% to Rs. 7,196.00 crore and profit after tax rose 19.02% to Rs. 913.17 crore.
Return on average assets improved to **2.76%** from 2.59% and return on average equity was 16.58%.
Net interest income was Rs. 4,279.06 crore, equivalent to 12.94% of average total assets — a yield
profile that reflects the high-yield retail segments in which the company operates.

Leverage improved: gearing (borrowings to net worth) declined to **4.56 times** as on March 31,
2026 from 4.99 times a year earlier, and the company's own net debt to equity measure fell to 4.01
times from 4.27 times. Net worth rose to Rs. 6,067.63 crore from Rs. 4,947.92 crore. Tier I
capital adequacy strengthened to **16.49%** from 15.84%, though total CRAR moderated to 19.44%
from 20.30% as Tier II capital amortised, falling to 2.95% from 4.46%.

These strengths are partially offset by a credit cost that remains high in absolute terms, an
operating cost ratio that deteriorated materially in FY2026, and a concentrated funding profile.

# **Key rating drivers and their description**

## **Credit strengths**

**Strong expectation of support from the TVS group** – TVS Motor held 85.63% of TVSCS as on
March 31, 2026. The company is the group's captive financing vehicle, and the shared brand, common
customer origination and the strategic role of financing in supporting vehicle sales create both a
strong incentive and a demonstrated track record of support. ICRA's assessment of the standalone
credit profile is uplifted for this linkage; a material dilution of TVS Motor's shareholding, or a
weakening of the parent's own credit profile, would be a rating negative.

**Comfortable profitability** – RoA improved to 2.76% in FY2026 from 2.59% in FY2025, and PAT grew
19.02% to Rs. 913.17 crore following 34.17% growth in FY2025. Net interest income of Rs. 4,279.06
crore represented 12.94% of average total assets, reflecting the company's positioning in
higher-yield two-wheeler, used-vehicle and tractor finance. Profitability has been supported by a
falling credit cost, which declined to 3.50% of average total assets from 4.38%.

**Improving leverage and adequate capitalisation** – Gearing declined to 4.56 times as on
March 31, 2026 from 4.99 times, with net worth rising to Rs. 6,067.63 crore. Tier I CRAR improved
to 16.49% from 15.84%, comfortably above the 15% minimum total CRAR that the RBI requires of the
company. Capital has been built substantially through internal accretion. Perpetual debt
instruments represent only 1.84% of Tier I capital (2.25% previously), so the Tier I base is of
high quality and not reliant on hybrid instruments.

## **Credit challenges**

**Elevated credit cost inherent to the target segments** – Impairment on financial instruments was
Rs. 1,156.59 crore in FY2026, equal to 3.50% of average total assets. While this improved from
4.38% in FY2025, it remains high and reflects the borrower profile in two-wheeler, used-vehicle and
tractor finance, where customers are typically first-time or informal-income borrowers with limited
credit history. The trajectory of the credit cost through a weaker rural or agricultural cycle
remains the single most important monitorable for the standalone profile.

**Material deterioration in the operating cost ratio** – The cost-to-income ratio rose to **49.81%
in FY2026 from 44.98% in FY2025**, an increase of 483 basis points. Operating expenses grew 25.7%
to Rs. 2,385.05 crore against net total income growth of 13.5%, with other expenses rising 47.4% to
Rs. 1,054.70 crore and employee costs 12.2% to Rs. 1,274.23 crore. Earnings growth in FY2026 was
therefore delivered despite the cost line, not through it — carried by the lower credit cost. If
credit costs normalise upward while the operating cost ratio stays at current levels,
profitability would come under pressure from both directions simultaneously. Arresting this trend
is a key monitorable.

**Concentrated funding profile** – The top 10 borrowings represented **70.68%** of total borrowings
as on March 31, 2026. This improved from 77.34% a year earlier, but the profile remains
concentrated, and as a wholesale-funded NBFC without a deposit franchise the company is exposed to
refinancing risk and to shifts in bank and mutual fund risk appetite towards the sector.
Diversification of the borrowing mix, including through the external commercial borrowings and
non-convertible debenture issuances undertaken during the year, is a credit positive that should
continue.

# **Environmental and social risks**

**Environmental** – As a financial-sector entity, TVSCS has limited direct environmental exposure;
its operations are branch- and technology-based rather than industrial. Indirect exposure arises
through the assets it finances. The company's lending is concentrated in two-wheelers, used
vehicles and tractors, which creates exposure to transition risk in the event of a sharper
regulatory shift toward electric mobility, particularly in the resale values that support its
used-vehicle and repossession recoveries. The parent's electric two-wheeler programme partially
offsets this at group level by giving the company a route to finance the replacement technology.

**Social** – The social profile is more material than the environmental one and cuts both ways.
Positively, the company extends formal credit to first-time and informal-income borrowers in
semi-urban and rural India, which is a financial-inclusion contribution. Negatively, that same
customer base makes conduct risk material: collections and repossession practices in vehicle
finance are subject to close RBI supervision under the Scale Based Regulation framework, and fair
practices lapses at any NBFC in this segment carry regulatory and reputational consequences. The
company's data-security and customer-data-protection obligations are also heightened by its
digital origination model.

# **Liquidity position: Adequate**

The company is wholesale funded and has no deposit franchise, so liquidity rests on borrowing
access and on the granularity of collections from a large retail book. Total borrowings stood at
Rs. 27,667.87 crore as on March 31, 2026 against cash and equivalents implied by the gap between
total and net debt of approximately Rs. 3,327 crore. Borrowing relationships span a wide
public- and private-sector bank panel, and the company accessed external commercial borrowings and
non-convertible debentures during FY2026, diversifying beyond bank lines. Against this, the top 10
borrowings remain 70.68% of the total. The assessment is **Adequate** rather than Strong on that
concentration and on the absence of a deposit base.

_A dated asset-liability maturity ladder was not extracted from the documents reviewed; the
liquidity assessment above rests on the funding-profile and balance-sheet disclosures cited. This
is recorded rather than presented as a complete ALM review._

# **Rating sensitivities**

**Positive factors** – An upgrade would require a strengthening of the parent's own credit profile
alongside a sustained improvement in the standalone profile, specifically a reversal of the
operating cost deterioration and a credit cost sustained materially below current levels through a
full cycle.

**Negative factors** – Pressure on the ratings could emerge from a significant decline in TVS
Motor's shareholding leading to a lower likelihood of support, reduced operational linkages with
the group, or a weakening of TVS Motor's own credit profile. A sustained rise in the credit cost
that erodes profitability, gearing rising materially above current levels, or a further
deterioration in the cost-to-income ratio without offsetting gains would also be credit negative.

# **About the company**

TVS Credit Services Limited **is a non-banking financial company (NBFC-ICC)** registered with the
Reserve Bank of India and classified in the **Middle Layer** under the RBI's Scale Based Regulation
framework. Incorporated in 2008, it is the financing arm of the TVS group and is 85.63% held by TVS
Motor Company Limited. The company finances two-wheelers, used vehicles, tractors and allied retail
and small-business products, originating substantially through the group's dealer network as well
as through direct and digital channels.

The company's registered office moved from Tamil Nadu to Karnataka, and it is now registered under
CIN U65920KA2008PLC218369 with its registered office at No. 383, 16th Main, 3rd Block, Koramangala,
Bengaluru. The FY2024 and FY2025 annual reports carry the earlier Tamil Nadu CIN
U65920TN2008PLC069758.

In FY2026, TVSCS reported a profit after tax of Rs. 913.17 crore on total income of Rs. 7,196.00
crore, against Rs. 767.25 crore and Rs. 6,608.68 crore respectively in FY2025. Total assets stood
at Rs. 35,068.09 crore as on March 31, 2026.
