# 04 — CORE ANALYTICAL FRAMEWORK · ICRA · CRISIL · CareEdge (ONE file, all three frameworks)

> **Flow:** `UNIFIED_RATING_GUIDE.md` (root) is the 19-step process with a gate at every step. This file is the reference detail — **all three agencies in one place**.

## The same company, three lenses — how each agency structures the analysis

| | **ICRA** | **CRISIL** | **CareEdge** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Frame** | Industry -> Business -> Financial -> Management -> Other elements -> ESG | **3 risk profiles**: Business -> Financial -> Management (+ project risk, ESG) | **Blocks A–E**: A Economy & Industry -> B Business -> C Financial -> D Management -> E Project (+ ESG) |
| **Industry** | growth, cyclicality, competitive intensity, regulatory risk | **industry risk sets the sector MEDIAN rating**; company factors move it around that | can the industry **command pricing** or is it a price-taker to landed import cost; **outperforming peers through downturns is a positive** |
| **Business** | competitive position, diversification, asset quality | **market position** = ability to sell *profitably* & control price (FMCG: **distribution + brand equity**); **operating efficiency** = integration, input-output price linkage, capacity, R&D | **diversification** (segment/geo/customer/supplier), size & market share, **earnings capacity**, cost structure (**gross margin must absorb fixed costs**) |
| **Financial** | profitability, leverage, coverage, liquidity, cash flows | **accounting quality first**; **cash-flow adequacy** (P&L step + balance-sheet step); liquidity & flexibility | accounting quality; 5 ratio families; **financing cash flow funding operations = unfavourable**; **no refinancing/equity assumed unless already arranged** |
| **Management** | quality, financial policies | **risk appetite explicit** — high leverage appetite or projects larger than existing operations viewed unfavourably | track record, ownership (**promoter pledge viewed negatively**), group concerns, controls, governance |
| **Liquidity test** | sources-vs-claims over ~12m -> descriptor | **bank-limit utilisation vs drawing power (monthly)** | **CP carved out of WC limits** -> back-up liquidity; drawing-power variation |
| **EBITDA called** | **OPBDIT** | **EBITDA** | **PBILDT** |
| **Leverage metrics** | Gearing (TD/TNW), **TOL/TNW**, TD/OPBDIT, NCA/TD | Gearing, TOL/Adj networth, Debt/EBITDA, **NCA/TD** | **Overall gearing**, **TD/PBILDT**, **TD/GCA** |
| **Interplay rule** | low business risk -> tolerate more leverage; cyclical -> need stronger financials | volatile earnings (steel/cement/petrochem) bear **less** leverage; stable (power) bear more | cyclical entity with comfortable capital structure is **not** automatically risky |

**Shared, all three:** ratings are ordinal, forward-looking opinions on **probability of default of timely servicing** (not expected loss, except EL-scale infra); scales are SEBI-standard (`AAA…D`, `A1+…A4`) differing only by prefix; **LT->ST mapping** is identical (AAA/AA -> A1+ always; overlap bands decided by liquidity); outlook attaches to **long-term ratings only**.

## Data sourcing (binding, all three)
Every ratio is computed from the company's OWN documents. ICRA/CRISIL/CareEdge reference rationales **and** published rating actions are craft/context references — **never a source of any figure**. A third-party database (screener.in) is a **cross-check to verify an AR-extracted figure**, never a source; **if they disagree, the AR wins**. Undisclosed limits = "to be confirmed by issuer". Never mix bases in one ratio row.

# PART 1 — ICRA framework detail

*Distilled from 960~Corporate Credit Rating Methodology (Jul 2025), 927~Financial Ratio Analysis, 1014~Liquidity Analysis, 934~Consolidation, 963~Implicit Support, 110~Outlook Policy, 134~Default Recognition, mapping.md. Re-read the source files before any live rating.*

## Fundamental premise

Ratings are ordinal, forward-looking opinions on relative probability of default of timely debt servicing — cash-generation ability, predictability and adequacy vs obligations, plus supplementary support (cash, liquid securities, external financing, third-party support). Ratings do NOT differentiate on secured/unsecured or expected loss (except EL-scale infra ratings); seniority and structural features CAN differentiate.

## Risk categorisation (corporate framework)

| Industry Risk | Business Risk | Financial Risk | Management Risk | Other Factors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Growth prospects; Cyclicality; Competitive intensity; Regulatory risk | Competitive position; Diversification (customer/geographic/product/supplier); Quality of assets (debtors, inventory, fixed assets) | Profitability; Leverage; Coverage; Liquidity; Cash flows | Management quality; Financial policies | Project risks; Tenure mismatches/refinancing; Financial flexibility; Industry diversification; Currency risk; Contingent liabilities; Parentage; ESG; Accounting quality; Event risks |

Interplay rule: low business/industry risk → higher tolerance for financial leverage; cyclical/high-risk industries need stronger financial profiles. Financial metrics validate the business risk assessment ("Validation of business risk through profitability metrics").

## Key ratios (927 + 960 definitions)

- **Profitability**: Operating margin = OP/OI; Net margin = PAT/OI; RoCE = PBIT/Avg Capital Employed. OP includes core other income (job-work, scrap, duty drawback); excludes interest/dividend income. Capital Employed = Total Debt + Net Worth + DTL − CWIP − Capital advances. RoCE read business-risk-adjusted; volatility matters; capex-year dips acceptable if returns expected.
- **Leverage**: Gearing = Total Debt/TNW; Indebtedness = TOL/TNW (strongest ≤0.9x, weakest >3.0x); Debt/OP (strongest ≤0.5x, weakest >5.0x — read with debt tenor); NCA/Total Debt. Total Debt = LT+ST debt + leases + off-BS (discounted receivables) + debt component of hybrids − cash earmarked for debt servicing. TNW = Net worth − revaluation reserves + minority interest + share application money + equity component of hybrids.
- **Coverage**: Interest cover = OP/Interest; DSCR = (PAT+Interest+Depreciation)/(Gross interest+Repayments+Pref dividend). DSCR<1 not automatically distress if refinancing ability/liquidity strong.
- **Working capital/liquidity ratios**: Current ratio; Working capital cycle = debtor + inventory − payable days; WC intensity = NWC/OI.
- **Cash flows**: FFO = operating cash flows − interest; CFO = FFO − WC consumed; RCF = CFO − dividends; FCF = RCF − capex − repayments; FFO/Debt, RCF/Debt.
- Sensitivity analysis on key variables is part of the assessment.

## Liquidity analysis (1014)

Sources: fund flows from operations (+non-operating), unencumbered cash/liquid investments, undrawn committed lines, committed parent/investor funding. Claims: debt repayments, working capital needs, capex, dividends, contingent claims. Keep aside a % of OI as operating cash need. Assess adequacy over next ~12 months → descriptor Superior/Strong/Adequate/Stretched/Poor. Also: adequacy of long-term funding sources, financial flexibility, event risks. Liquidity links LT↔ST rating choice within overlap bands.

## Consolidation (934)

Consolidate (fully or by adjustment) where funding interchange possible or businesses closely interlinked; standalone with support-adjustment otherwise. Adjust reported consolidated financials (e.g., exclude non-recourse SPV debt where ring-fenced, include surplus-sharing arrangements among infra SPVs). Always state approach in report + Annexure II list with ownership % and method.

## Parent/Group support (963)

Support likelihood factors: stated intent + track record + cross-default/surplus-sharing; reputation sensitivity; strategic importance; business linkages/integration. Strong parent → notch-up from standalone; weak parent → possible notch-down (cash-drain risk). Individual promoters and PE/financial investors generally NOT treated as support-providing parents. Final rating grid: standalone assessment × likelihood of support × parent rating (see 960 Annexure illustration).

## Outlook & Watch (110)

Outlook = view over ~6 months–2 years: Stable (low likelihood of change), Positive (upgrade likely), Negative (downgrade likely). Watch (Positive/Negative/Developing implications) for discrete events under short-term resolution. Outlook/Watch not a precursor guarantee; ratings can change from Stable directly.

## Default recognition (134)

Default = missed payment (1-day-1-rupee for listed debt); [ICRA]D. Curing period norms before upgrade from D (typically 90 days satisfactory track record, case-dependent). Technical defaults/rectifications per policy. INC treatment per 132: continue rating with "ISSUER NOT COOPERATING" tag based on best available information.

## LT ↔ ST mapping

Stated in mapping.md text: AAA/AA category → A1+ invariably; B/C category → A4 invariably; overlap bands decided by liquidity (e.g. A+ → A1+ if liquidity superior/strong, else A1). ## Rating Watch notation

Rating on watch carries "&" suffix (e.g. `[ICRA]AA+&`) with the watch stated in words: "Rating Watch with Positive/Negative/Developing Implications" (per SEBI Oct 2022 descriptors; policy 110). No outlook is assigned while on Watch.

## Financial-sector framework (verified against 929 Banks / 1003 NBFCs structure)

- **Banks (929, Oct 2024)**: Industry risk = operating environment + regulatory environment. Business risk = franchise; management, governance & information reporting; risk management. Financial risk = asset quality; diversity in funding & liquidity; profitability, earnings stability & prospects; capital adequacy & management (Basel III regulatory minima vs actuals). Plus promoter support & financial flexibility, and ESG. Each factor has indicative-metrics assessment tables in the source doc — read them at rating time. In reports this shows up as: GNPA/NNPA, slippages, SMA-1/SMA-2 + restructured "vulnerable book", CET-I/CRAR, NIM, PAT/ATA, CASA/retail deposit share, LCR. Bank hybrids (AT-I, Tier-II) per 998 with loss-absorption analysis.
- **NBFCs (1003, Dec 2025)**: Business risk = business profile (product mix & riskiness; track record, responsiveness, sustainability) + operating environment. Financial risk = profitability; liquidity & financial flexibility; capital adequacy; asset quality. Management risk = management + risk-management systems/credit policies/processes. Other = ownership/parentage. ESG separately. Report metrics: AUM, Gross NPA/Stage-3, CRAR, gearing, NIM, credit costs, RoMA.

## ESG (all methodologies)

E&S risks sector- and entity-specific; asymmetric impact (unmitigated material risks pull ratings down; favourable ESG rarely pushes up). Governance largely entity-driven: board composition, disclosures quality, group complexity, RPTs, auditor/ID resignations. Report carries dedicated Environmental/Social considerations paragraphs.

## Rating scale summary

Long-term: [ICRA]AAA (highest safety) … [ICRA]D; modifiers +/− from AA to C. Short-term: [ICRA]A1+ … A4, D. Also: (CE) suffix, Provisional prefix, EL scale for infra concession EL-ratings, [ICRA]AAA(mfs) etc. for mutual funds, IrAAA issuer scale.

Amounts the company does not disclose (e.g. facility limits) are marked "to be confirmed by issuer", never reconstructed or imported from a CRA action. Never mix computation bases in one ratio row; show AR-basis and model-basis separately, each labelled.

# PART 2 — CRISIL framework detail

*Distilled from `basics-of-ratings.md` and `criteria-for-manufacturing-trading-and-corporate-services-sector.md` (Sections I & IV), plus `criteria-for-consolidation.md` and `criteria-for-factoring-parent-group-government-linkages.md`. Re-read the source sections before any live rating; this is a routing/recall aid, not a substitute.*

## Fundamental premise
A CRISIL rating is a forward-looking opinion on the likelihood of timely servicing of debt. The analysis is **cash-flow based** — CRISIL uses a proprietary model to estimate the cash the company will generate and tests its adequacy against debt obligations. The rating combines three risk profiles: **business risk, financial risk and management risk** (plus project risk where relevant, and ESG on top).

## The three risk profiles

### 1. Business risk
- **Industry risk** — size, growth prospects, competitive scenario, demand-supply dynamics, technological change, importance to the economy, government policy, entry barriers, profitability, cyclicality. The industry risk score sets the *median* rating for the sector; company-specific factors move the final rating around it.
- **Market position** — the ability to **profitably** sell goods/services in a stable, sustainable way. Large share is NOT market position if the company cannot control selling prices (e.g. commodity businesses, import-parity pricing). For **FMCG / branded consumer goods, the key factors are distribution network and brand equity**; for services, customer relationships and differentiation; for pharma/IT/consumer durables, track record and ability to innovate and commercialise. Diversification (geographic + product) and low client concentration strengthen it.
- **Operating efficiency** — ability to produce at competitive cost, sustainably. Factors: technology, access to resources (captive raw material/power, supplier bargaining power), **price volatility of key inputs and the linkage (or not) between input and output prices** (weak linkage → volatile margins → viewed less favourably), human resources (esp. for services — attrition, skills), capacity utilisation and flexibility, **level of vertical integration**, R&D.

### 2. Financial risk
- **Accounting quality** — assessed first; statements are recast for over/understatement of profits, auditor qualifications, income-recognition and depreciation methods, inventory valuation, off-balance-sheet/contingent items.
- **Adequacy of cash flows** — two steps: (A) income-statement analysis: profitability level, trend, peer comparison, sensitivity to price/cycle; (B) balance-sheet analysis: capital structure, cost of funding, working capital, planned capex/M&A, debt maturity. Volatile-earnings businesses (steel, cement, petrochem) can bear less leverage; stable ones (power) can bear more.
- **Liquidity & financial flexibility** — internal sources (accrual, saleable assets) + external (banker relationships, capital-market access, marketable securities). **Key operational test: bank limit utilisation vs drawing power, monitored monthly** — low utilisation = flexibility. For IT/services, cash held to cover a policy number of months of employee cost is netted out. Ability to defer capex is a positive.

### 3. Management risk
Goals, philosophy and strategy driving performance; organisational/reporting structure; experience and track record; commitment and track record in **debt servicing**; planning and control systems; success with new products and managing regulatory/technological change. **Risk appetite is explicitly assessed — high leverage appetite or projects larger than existing operations are viewed unfavourably.** Succession risk where single-promoter-dependent; corporate governance and transparency.

### Project risk (if applicable)
Size vs existing operations; implementation risk (time/cost overrun, technology obsolescence); funding risk (capital structure, tie-ups); market risk for the new output; track record with similar projects.

## Key ratios (Section IV — non-financial)
CRISIL's headline credit-protection metrics (definitions per Section IV; adjusted/recast figures):
- **Leverage/capital structure**: Gearing = Debt/Networth; Total Outside Liabilities / Adjusted Networth; Debt/EBITDA.
- **Debt protection / coverage**: Interest coverage = EBIT (or EBITDA)/Interest; Net Cash Accrual to Total Debt (NCA/TD); Debt/EBITDA.
- **Profitability**: Operating margin (EBITDA/Operating income); PAT margin; Return on Capital Employed (RoCE).
- **Working capital / liquidity**: Current ratio; Gross Current Assets (GCA) days; working-capital cycle.
- For **trading companies** the criteria specifically watch RoCE, interest coverage, total-indebtedness ratio and cash-to-indebtedness ratio, because working-capital management (not term-debt servicing) is paramount.

## Consolidation
CRISIL **combines** the business and financial risk profiles of the parent with subsidiaries, step-down subsidiaries, JVs and associates where there are significant business/financial linkages (moral obligation, shared brand, funding fungibility). Homogenous corporate groups may be assessed on a group-average approach. State the approach and give the "List of entities consolidated" annexure with extent and rationale. (Example: CRISIL consolidates ITC Hotels into ITC per its analytical approach even at ~40% holding.)

## Parent / group / government support
`criteria-for-factoring-parent-group-government-linkages.md`: notch the standalone rating up (or down) for support based on strategic importance, shared name/reputation sensitivity, economic incentive, moral obligation and track record. Framework gives explicit notch-up bands; a weak parent can drag a strong subsidiary down.

## Rating scale & outlook
- Long-term: **Crisil AAA … Crisil D** (+/− modifiers AA to C). Short-term: **Crisil A1+ … A4, D**.
- **Outlook** (long-term ratings): Stable / Positive / Negative. **Rating Watch** (Positive/Negative/Developing) for discrete pending events.
- **Default** = any missed payment on rated debt (1 day / 1 rupee) → Crisil D; recognition rules and curing period in `basics-of-ratings.md` Section III.
- **PP-MLD** prefix for principal-protected market-linked debentures.
- LT↔ST mapping in `basics-of-ratings.md` Section VIII (AAA/AA → A1+; overlap bands resolved by liquidity).

## ESG
Assessed within the manufacturing master (not a separate score) — environment (emissions, energy, water, waste), social (human capital, product/customer, community), governance (board, ownership, disclosures) — factored on materiality, most relevant for entities tapping global capital or with large global operations.

Undisclosed facility/programme LIMITS = "to be confirmed by issuer" (bank facilities are the usual case). State drawn borrowings separately from rated limits; never mix bases in a ratio row.

# PART 3 — CareEdge framework detail

*Distilled from `Criteria_Rating_Methodology_Manufacturing_Companies_March_2025.md` (and the parallel Services Sector master), `Rating_of_Short_Term_Instruments_Feb_2025.md`, `Financial Ratios – Non financial Sector`, `Consolidation-and-Combined-Approach`, and the Notching papers. Re-read the source papers before any live rating; this is a routing/recall aid, not a substitute.*

## Fundamental premise
A CARE (CareEdge) rating is an opinion on the relative ability and willingness of the entity to make timely payment on its debt obligations over the instrument's life. The manufacturing/services methodology assesses credit risk through five blocks, **A–E**, plus an explicit ESG section. The relative weight of qualitative vs quantitative factors varies by entity type; rating is a holistic, forward-looking judgment.

## The five analytical blocks (A–E)

### A) Economy and Industry Risk Analysis
Industry prospects and competitive factors that set the operating risk of the business: industry size, major players' investment plans, demand-supply, price trends, technology change, domestic/international competition, entry barriers, capital intensity, business cycles. Economy-wide and policy factors: strategic nature of the industry, regulatory oversight/licensing, raw-material access/protection, anti-dumping duties. Key CareEdge lens: whether the industry can **command pricing** or is a price-taker to landed import cost; and average gross margins (tolerance to absorb cost increases). **An entity that consistently tides through industry downturns with better performance than peers is viewed positively.**

### B) Business Risk Analysis
- **Diversification** — segmental, geographic, customer and supplier. Diversified cash flows are viewed more favourably; each major segment's income/profitability/contribution is analysed.
- **Seasonality and cyclicality** — cyclical entities with a comfortable capital structure are not necessarily risky; working-capital capability assessed against peak-season need.
- **Size, market share and competition** — large size → economies of scale, bargaining power, market access. A sustained **leadership position → better revenue visibility and long-term cash generation**; the market leader typically has the financial resources and flexibility to pass on input/price increases. A fragmented-market player is a price-taker with weaker margins in a downturn.
- **Earnings capacity** — revenue visibility, sales-volume and realisation trends.
- **Cost structure and operating efficiency** — capacity utilisation, production flexibility, input cost per unit, inventory, technology/R&D, level of integration. CareEdge evaluates **gross margin (realisation net of raw-material cost)** and whether it is sufficient to absorb other operating and fixed costs.
- (Force majeure noted but not rated, as ratings follow a probability-of-default approach.)

### C) Financial Risk Analysis
- **Accounting quality** — policies on depreciation, inventory valuation, income recognition, investment valuation, provisioning; impact of auditor qualifications quantified.
- **Financial ratios** — five families (see below); a *means*, not an end, benchmarked to peers.
- **Cash flows** — operating cash flow adjusted for working capital; an entity **continuously using financing (debt) cash flows to fund operating cash flows is viewed unfavourably**. Cash-flow adequacy = ability to fund capex and support group entities in addition to debt servicing.
- **Liquidity** — unencumbered liquid investments, undrawn committed lines, group liquidity support (see the dedicated Liquidity Analysis paper).
- **Financial flexibility** — alternative liquidity sources, capital-market access, monetisable non-core assets, ability to defer capex. **No equity raising or debt refinancing is assumed unless funds are already arranged.** A steep fall in market capitalisation reduces flexibility. High promoter-share pledge is viewed negatively.
- **Working capital** — debtor, inventory and creditor analysis against industry benchmarks; contingent liabilities; forex risk and hedging.

### D) Management Evaluation
Track record and market reputation; corporate strategy and succession; legal constitution and ownership (resourceful promoters with controlling stake viewed positively; **high pledge of promoter shares viewed negatively**; partnerships carry capital-withdrawal risk); performance of group concerns and related-party transactions; organisational structure; control systems and MIS; personnel policies/attrition; corporate governance and transparency.

### E) Project Risk Analysis (if applicable)
Rationale and size vs current scale/net worth; funding pattern; implementation risks (financial closure, approvals, supplier agreements, contractor track record, cost/time overrun); post-implementation risks (raw-material/fuel tie-ups, marketing). Flexibility to defer or phase capex is viewed favourably.

### ESG
Environmental, Social and Governance risk factors analysed as a distinct section — environment (emissions, energy, water, waste, packaging), social (human capital, product safety, community, and for regulated sectors like tobacco the social-hazard/regulatory exposure), governance (board independence, disclosures, regulatory strictures).

## Financial ratios (five families — CARE conventions)
Per `Financial Ratios – Non financial Sector`:
- **Growth**: TOI growth, PBILDT growth.
- **Profitability**: **PBILDT margin** (Profit Before Interest, Lease rentals, Depreciation and Tax / TOI), PAT margin, RoCE, RONW.
- **Leverage & coverage**: **Overall gearing** (Total debt/Tangible net worth), **TD/PBILDT**, **TD/GCA** (gross cash accruals), interest coverage, DSCR.
- **Turnover & liquidity**: operating cycle (debtor + inventory − creditor days), current ratio, working-capital utilisation.
- Note the naming differences vs ICRA/CRISIL: CARE uses **PBILDT** (not OPBDIT/EBITDA), **overall gearing** and **TD/GCA**.

## Short-term instrument rating (CP)
`Rating_of_Short_Term_Instruments`: the ST rating is the **long-term fundamental credit quality mapped to the short-term scale, with a liquidity/financial-flexibility overlay**. Specific CP concern: CP is usually carved out of working-capital limits, creating implicit back-up liquidity — but inability/unwillingness of the lender to restore limits, or a drawing-power shortfall, can leave inadequate back-up at maturity, so **working-capital limit utilisation and drawing-power variation are examined in detail**. Superior liquidity can lift the ST rating within the mapping overlap.

## Consolidation & combined approach
`Consolidation-and-Combined-Approach`: consolidate subsidiaries/JVs/associates with operational, financial and managerial linkages, common treasury, or corporate guarantees / letters of comfort between them; combined approach for entities in the same line under common management. State the approach and give the entity list (extent: Full/Moderate; rationale) as an annexure.

## Support notching
`Notching_by_Factoring_Linkages_with_Parent` / `...with_Govt`: notch the standalone up or down for parent/group/government support based on linkage strength, strategic importance and track record.

## Rating scale, outlook, default
- Long-term: **CARE AAA … CARE D** (+/− modifiers). Short-term: **CARE A1+ … A4, D**.
- **Outlook**: Stable / Positive / Negative (long-term ratings); **Rating Watch** for discrete events (`Assigning Outlook or Rating Watch`).
- **Liquidity descriptors**: Superior / Strong / Adequate / Stretched / Poor (note CARE uses "Strong" where ICRA/CRISIL often say "Superior").
- **Default & curing** per `Policy on Curing Period`; **INC / best-available-information** per `Information_Adequacy_Risk-and-INC`.
- **PP-MLD** prefix for principal-protected market-linked debentures.
