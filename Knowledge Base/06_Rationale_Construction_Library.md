# How ICRA Rationales Are Constructed — Learning Library
*Built by studying reference reports across sectors (Titan, HAL, Union Bank, Ugro, Torrent Pharma, Uno Minda, Wipro, VRL, Welspun, Vodafone Idea, AU SFB securitisation, Deepak Builders INC, and structural sweeps across all 649). Purpose: learn the ANALYTICAL CRAFT — never copy facts from these reports into a new one.*

## The universal anatomy of a rationale

Every rationale answers five questions, in roughly this order:

1. **Why this rating level?** — the 2-3 structural factors that anchor the rating (market position, parentage, financial profile). Always stated in the first sentence with the single most-defining number attached (revenue scale, market share, order book).
2. **What happened since last review?** — operating performance with period-on-period numbers (growth %, margin bps, coverage), major events (acquisitions, orders, settlements, capex) and their credit direction.
3. **What could go wrong?** — the constraining factors, always quantified against buffers (e.g., "LD of 1.6-2.3% of revenue vs margins of 27-32%").
4. **What does ICRA expect?** — the forward view with explicit ranges ("revenue growth of 8-10% over FY2026-FY2028", "margins to remain above X%") and named monitorables.
5. **Why this outlook?** — final paragraph, always beginning "The Stable/Positive/Negative outlook … reflects ICRA's opinion that …".

The report's standard KI table is the SAME seven indicators for all corporates (OI, PAT, OPBDIT/OI, PAT/OI, TOL/TNW, TD/OPBDIT, interest coverage) — sector differentiation lives in the NARRATIVE, where sector-specific metrics are quoted.

## Credit driver construction — the craft rules

- A driver headline = positioning claim + its consequence ("Market leadership position in branded jewellery … with a large scale of operations"). Never a bare adjective.
- Every driver paragraph must contain 3+ verifiable specifics: shares, counts, ratios with dates, peer context.
- Strengths and challenges must be SYMMETRIC with sensitivities: each challenge maps to a negative trigger; strengths at AAA make positive factors "Not applicable".
- Quantify the mitigation, not just the risk ("customer advances fund the inventory build-up" beats "working capital is managed").

## Liquidity paragraph construction

Always an arithmetic argument, never an assertion: sources (cash + liquid investments with as-on date, undrawn lines, expected CFO) vs claims (repayments by year, capex, dividends), closing with financial flexibility. The descriptor must be the conclusion of that arithmetic: Superior (huge net cash, no claims), Strong (comfortable surplus), Adequate (covered but thin), Stretched/Poor (dependence on refinancing/support).

## Sensitivity calibration patterns

- AAA: Positive = Not applicable; Negative = structural (leverage sustained above X, loss of parent linkage, liquidity impairment).
- Mid-grade (A/BBB): both directions, quantified where possible ("upgrade if TD/OPBDIT below 2.0x on a sustained basis").
- "On a sustained basis" qualifier is standard — one bad quarter doesn't trigger.
- Support-driven ratings: sensitivity to change in ownership/strategic importance is mandatory.

## What reference reports are FOR and NOT FOR

| Legitimate use | Illegitimate use |
|---|---|
| Learn sector narrative metrics and driver taxonomy | Copying facts, figures, amounts into a new report |
| Learn calibration (what metrics earn AAA vs A in a sector) | Inheriting claims (rated amounts, utilisation, order values) without user confirmation |
| Format, section order, house phrasing | Substituting for independent analysis of company documents |
| Post-analysis consistency check (does my view diverge from ICRA's last view? why?) | Anchoring the rating to the old rating before forming own view |

## Cross-agency use of this library (binding)

This library was built by studying **ICRA** rationales, so its section order and phrasing are ICRA's. When writing a **CRISIL** or **CareEdge** report:
- **Use it only for AGENCY-NEUTRAL craft** — how a credit driver is argued from evidence; how a risk is quantified against its buffer ("LD of 1.6-2.3% of revenue vs margins of 27-32%"); how a forward view is stated with named monitorables; why a descriptor is a conclusion, not an adjective.
- **NEVER import ICRA's section order, headings or house phrasing** into another agency's report. Learn those from that agency's OWN peers in `CRISIL/Report for Reference/` or `CareEdge/Report for Reference/`, and from `03_Report_Template.md` (which now carries all three formats side by side).
- The universal rule is unchanged and applies to every agency's reference reports: **learn the craft, never copy a fact.**
