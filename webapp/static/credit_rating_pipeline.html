Credit rating pipeline

Twenty-one steps from intake to surveillance, grouped into five phases. Six gates decide whether a case moves forward, loops back to the step that caused the problem, or stops. The rating itself is decided by the committee, not by any step before it.

3. Review agents

Each phase has an agent that reviews the work already done there, finds mistakes and fixes them, then proves the fix by re-running that phase's gate. A head agent runs them in order and signs a case off only when the delivery audit is clean.

4. Rules that hold across the flow

  • Facts come only from the issuer's documents. Published reports teach format and calibration; they never supply a figure, a driver or a phrasing.
  • The view forms before the prior report is opened. Step 13 comes first, Step 14 compares; a rated-amount table may be read earlier only to fill the facility schedule.
  • Every number is computed in code. The draft copies from the workpaper, and two drift checks at Step 17 re-verify both computed and non-computed figures.
  • The committee makes the rating. Step 15 is the analyst's proposal; the decision is taken at Step 17c on the full, re-verified package.
  • A rating is monitored, not closed. A surveillance trigger re-runs the pipeline from intake as a surveillance engagement.
