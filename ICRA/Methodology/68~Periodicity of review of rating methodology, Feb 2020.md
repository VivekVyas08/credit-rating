# **Periodicity of review of Rating Methodology/ Criteria** 

## **Overview** 

CRA’s credit ratings are a symbolic representation of its opinion on the relative credit risk associated with the debt instrument being rated. This opinion is arrived at after evaluating an entity’s industry, business and financial risks, its liquidity and financial flexibility, the management’s financial policy, its corporate governance practices and support from group entities. This analytical approach draws from the various rating methodologies/ criteria laid down by ICRA covering both sector-specific as well as sector-agnostic aspects. 

To ensure that its credit ratings not only take cognizance of the risks that are understood well at a given point in time, but also appropriately factor in risks that are emerging (as also reduce emphasis on risks that may have been blunted), ICRA follows a process of ongoing methodology review. This internal process is outlined below: 

## **FACTORS THAT TRIGGER AN INTERNAL METHODOLOGY/ CRITERIA REVIEW** 

- » Higher than usual deviation observed in ratings performance including Rating Stability Rates and Default Rates 

- » Identification of a new risk factor that was earlier not considered pertinent but now merits inclusion in the analytical approach 

- » Coming to light of a risk factor on which greater emphasis is required to be accorded going forward, as assessed based on empirical evidence or change in expectations 

- » Conversely, a risk factor which was earlier considered to hold a good degree of importance while assessing credits, but is later observed to wield lesser influence on rating performance 

- » Passage of two years since the latest methodology review 

However, an existing methodology may not be reviewed if the related product is to be discontinued in the near future (within six months) and no fresh ratings or gradings relating to such a product are proposed to be assigned during this period.
