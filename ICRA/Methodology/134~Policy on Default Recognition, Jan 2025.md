## **AUTHOR CONTACTS** 

This document describes ICRA’s policy on default recognition, applicable when an entity fails to meet its debt servicing obligations in a timely manner. This document also specifies ICRA’s policy on upgrading the rating once the default is cured. The policy is in line with the various guidelines issued by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) and the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on this subject. 

This policy supersedes the earlier document on this subject, published in December 2024. 

# **ICRA’s Definition of Default** 

### **Default recognition in case of debt instruments that have a pre-defined repayment schedule** 

ICRA defines default as a missed or a delayed payment by an entity in breach of the contractual terms of the debt instruments (securities, bank facilities, commercial papers, fixed deposits etc.) Thus, even a ‘single day single rupee’ delay beyond the contractual terms—in case of debt instruments having a pre-defined repayment schedule—is treated as a default. Upon a missed or a delayed payment, the rating of the debt instrument and/ or the Issuer (as applicable) is downgraded to [ICRA]D. ICRA has been using this definition of default consistently for all its default-related studies, and the practise has remained unchanged over the years. 

### **Default recognition in case of bank facilities that do not have a pre-defined repayment schedule** 

In the case of bank facilities that do not have any pre-defined repayment schedule (such as cash credit and overdraft facility), ICRA recognises default only in case of continuous overdrawals for more than 30 days.

### **Default recognition in case of rescheduling of debt** 

Non-servicing of the debt (principal and/ or interest) as per the existing payment terms in anticipation of a favourable response from the banks/ investors for changing/ rescheduling the payment terms is considered as a default.

# **Default recognition in case of hybrid instruments** 

A missed or a deferred payment on a hybrid instrument vis-a-vis the scheduled payouts is treated as a default, even if the contractual terms and conditions of the hybrid instrument allow for a deferral/ skipping of payments under certain conditions. 

# **Default recognition post the withdrawal of rating** 

<mark>As per ICRA’s Policy on Withdrawal of Credit Ratings</mark><sup>**1**</sup> <mark>, the rating of securities (such as bonds, non-convertible debentures or NCDs etc.) can be withdrawn subject to ICRA having rated the security continuously for five years or 50% of the tenure of the security, whichever is higher. Also, when a security is rated by multiple credit rating agencies without a regulatory requirement for multiple ratings, the rating can be withdrawn subject to ICRA having rated the security continuously for three years or 50% of the tenure of the security, whichever is higher. In such cases, post the rating withdrawal but before the maturity of the security or the completion of the cohort</mark><sup>**2**</sup> <mark>(whichever is earlier), if it comes to the notice of ICRA that there is any delay or default in payment on the said security, ICRA would include such an instance in its default statistics, irrespective of the rating on that security having been withdrawn earlier. For tracking the timeliness (or lack thereof) of payment on such security, ICRA would rely on the information received by it from the Debenture Trustee for the said security and/ or the stock exchange filings (in case of a listed security) or information received by ICRA from any other source.</mark> 

# **Rating approach when the non-payment of dues relating to listed or proposed to be listed debt instruments is for reasons beyond the entity’s control** 

<mark>There may be scenarios where non-payment of dues (principal and/ or interest) relating to listed or proposed to be listed debt instruments arises because of reasons beyond the control of the entity, namely, failure to remit payment due to absence of correct information or due to incorrect or dormant investor account furnished by the investor(s) or due to notice/ instruction received from a government authority to freeze the account of investor(s). In such scenarios, ICRA does not recognise a default, provided the following conditions are met:</mark> 

- <mark>There is evidence that the entity had adequate funds on the due date to make the payment, and</mark>

- <mark>The required amounts are duly paid by the entity into a separate escrow account maintained with a scheduled commercial bank on the due date of payment</mark>

# **Impact of Default on Credit Ratings** 

Upon a default, ICRA downgrades the ratings for all the rated debt instruments to [ICRA]D, regardless of the magnitude or duration of the default. 

However, if there are reasons for differentiating between the rating of the debt instrument that is in default and that of the other debt instruments that aren’t, the rating of the other debt instruments may not be revised to [ICRA]D, but suitably reviewed. The above-described rating action could be taken in the following cases, among others: 

- The other debt instruments on which there is no default are senior to the debt in default and the default probability of the senior debt is distinctly lower than that of the debt in default - such as in the case of default on hybrid instruments. 

- The cash flows meant for servicing the other debt instruments (that are not in default) are ring fenced, and no crossdefault clauses apply. 

- The other debt instruments on which there is no default are supported by a third-party explicit support such as a corporate guarantee. 

- The default on a given debt instrument is because of operational reasons and not owing to any fundamental deterioration in the credit profile of the entity. 

# **Rating Upgrade after the Passage of the Curing Period** 

For an entity or its debt instruments rated in the default category, to qualify for an upgrade to within the non-investment grade, generally at least 90 days should have passed from the date of regularisation of the most recent default<sup>**4**</sup> . Also, for the rating to be further upgraded to the investment grade, generally 365 days should have passed from the date of regularisation of the most recent default. The period of 90/ 365 days mentioned above is henceforth referred to as the “curing period”. 

**Curing period when loan restructuring/ rescheduling happens** : If the debt of an entity in default is restructured/ rescheduled such that a revised payment schedule becomes applicable, the curing period would be considered to have commenced from the date when the loan restructuring/ rescheduling is implemented. 

**Exceptions to the curing period norm** : ICRA may make exceptions to adhering to the general curing period specified above and may consider upgrading the rating of an entity even prior to the passage of the curing period. This may happen in cases where the fundamental credit profile of the defaulting entity undergoes a significant improvement. Such improvement in the credit profile may be because of a change in ownership and/ or management, sizeable inflow of long-term funds, or benefits arising out of a regulatory action, or access to some form of explicit support from a stronger third-party, or redressal of the adverse effects caused by a force majeure event, or any other such discrete event that has a strong positive influence on the entity’s/ debt instrument’s fundamental credit quality. It is clarified that normal business-related developments like receipt of substantial new orders from customers are not construed as discrete events. 

Also, there may be situations wherein an entity misses its payments on a debt instrument solely because of operational reasons and where such an instance is not a reflection of the lack of ability or willingness of the entity to pay its debt obligations, or in other words, not a sign of alteration in the fundamental credit profile of the entity. **Factoring in past defaults** : In certain circumstances, ICRA may become aware of delays in debt servicing by a rated entity with a considerable time lag. Accordingly, the rating of the debt instruments of such an entity would not have been downgraded to the default category immediately at the time of the occurrence of the default. However, as soon as ICRA becomes aware of such delays, the rating would be downgraded to [ICRA]D. If by the time ICRA becomes aware of past delays, the debt servicing might have become regular over the course of the curing period, ICRA will still recognize the past default by downgrading the rating to [ICRA]D. In such cases, the rating may also be simultaneously upgraded to a non-default category, in accordance with ICRA’s policy outlined in this document. 

**Other points** : The curing period principle for default category ratings would apply to both fresh rating assignments as well as surveillance assignments and usually at the entity level. This implies that if ICRA is rating an entity afresh, a non-default rating would not be assigned if the curing period post an earlier default on any debt instrument of similar seniority has not lapsed. However, for ratings on subordinated or hybrid bonds, since a default on such debt instruments may not necessarily imply a default by the entity on senior debt instruments, the curing period principle would apply at the instrument level. Further, if a rated debt instrument is credit enhanced or the cash flows are ring-fenced, the curing period will apply at the instrument level, as default by the entity on other debt instruments may not imply or lead to a default on a credit enhanced or a ring-fenced debt instrument.
