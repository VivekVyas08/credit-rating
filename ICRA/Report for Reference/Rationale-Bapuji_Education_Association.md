## **Rationale** 

The ratings reaffirmation of Bapuji Education Association (BEA) factors in the healthy operating surplus margins, low leverage and strong debt coverage metrics. BEA’s operating income is expected to grow by 2–4% in FY2027 (PY: Rs. 695.5 crore), driven by healthy enrolments. The operating surplus margins remained healthy at 40.5% in FY2026 and are likely to continue to be the same over the medium term, supported by improvement in the operating income (OI). The total debt outstanding of Rs. 125 crore as on March 31, 2026, was fully repaid in April 2026 and BEA is expected to take fresh term debt of Rs. 200-250 crore in FY2027 for funding its capex plans of Rs. 600-650 crore in the next 2-3 years. The debt protection metrics are likely to remain strong, despite the expected debt-funded capex. The ratings continue to favourably factor in its established track record in education of over six decades and strong reputation in the Devangere region. The association’s dental college has been ranked among the top 50 in the National Institutional Ranking Framework (NIRF 2024), released by the Ministry of Education (MoE), Government of India. Further, it has sizeable cash balance and deposits with an unrated cooperative bank, which limits its financial flexibility to an extent. 

The ratings, however, remain constrained by significant concentration of BEA’s revenues from its top three institutes, contributing 75-80% of the fee receipts and 80-85% of the operating income provided by revenue from education activities over the last three years. The association faces geographical concentration risk, with all the 50+ institutes located in and around Davangere (Karnataka), making it susceptible to potential socio-political disturbances and localised adverse events. Further, ICRA notes that BEA has sizeable exposure of Rs. 302.92 crore as on March 31, 2026, in Bapuji Cooperative Bank through deposits and bank balances. Hence, the liquidity position and consequently the credit profile of BEA will remain exposed to the associated risk with such relatively illiquid investments and credit profile of investee entity/bank. With sizeable, planned capital expenditure, BEA is exposed to project execution risk, and any delays or cost overrun could further exert pressure on BEA’s liquidity position. The ratings also factor in the high competition from both private and public educational institutions, and the significant regulatory risks arising from stringent compliance requirements imposed by multiple regulatory authorities. Any adverse changes in the regulatory framework, including the imposition of fee caps (particularly for medical courses), could exert pressure on the association’s financial profile. 

The Stable outlook on the long-term rating reflects ICRA’s expectations that BEA’s debt protection metrics will continue to remain strong over the medium term, supported by low leverage levels.

# **Key rating drivers and their description** 

## **Credit strengths** 

**Established track record in higher education sector** – BEA operates more than 50 institutions located in and around Davangere, Karnataka, offering programmes across multiple disciplines, including medical and dental sciences, engineering, nursing, pharmacy, and school education (primary to pre-university levels). The association’s dental college was ranked among the top 50 institutions in the National Institutional Ranking Framework (NIRF 2024) released by the Ministry of Education, Government of India. BEA’s institutions benefit from a strong regional reputation, particularly in medical education, supported by an established track record and adequate infrastructure, which aids in attracting quality students and retaining qualified faculty. 

– **Healthy enrolment levels support revenues** The enrolment levels across BEA’s institutions remained healthy at 78–80% in AY2025–26, albeit moderating from 90–93% in AY2023–2024 and 85-87% in AY2024-2025, primarily on account of the introduction of new courses. Its flagship institutions, J.J.M. Medical College and S.S. Institute of Medical Science and Research, continue to benefit from a strong reputation, as reflected in consistent robust enrolment levels of over 90% over the years. The healthy enrolment levels support BEA’s operating profile by providing stability and predictability in fee income over the near term. 

**Low leverage levels** – The association has low leverage levels as denoted by total debt/OPBDITA of 0.4 times (PY: 0.3 times) and TOL/TNW of 0.1 times (PY: 0.1 times) as on March 31, 2026. The total debt/OPBDITA is likely to remain strong at less than 1.0 times in the next 1-2 years and ICRA expects BEA to maintain a comfortable financial profile, supported by stable cash accruals and a sizeable net worth base. Further, BEA has repaid Rs. 125 crore debt in April 2027 (outstanding as on March 31, 2026) and the coverage metric as reflected by DSCR is projected to remain strong in the medium term. 

## **Credit challenges** 

**High concentration of revenue and low degree of autonomy** – BEA faces revenue concentration risk as the top three institutes contributed 75-80% of the fee receipts and revenues from education services accounted for 80-85% of its operating income in the last three years. It further faces geographical concentration risk as all the 50+ institutes are in and around Devangere, Karnataka making it susceptible to potential socio-political disturbances and localised adverse events. The dependence on certain institutes and medical education sector makes it vulnerable to changes in the demand-supply scenario and reputational damages. There is low autonomy in terms of fee caps, student intake and addition of new courses due to affiliation of its medical colleges to Rajiv Gandhi University of Health Sciences, Bengaluru and engineering colleges to Visvesvaraya Technological University, Belgaum. 

– **Exposure to intense competition and regulatory risk** The higher education sector in India is characterised by a stringent regulatory framework, with oversight from multiple bodies such as the University Grants Commission (UGC), the National Medical Commission (NMC) and the All India Council for Technical Education (AICTE), necessitating adherence to strict compliance requirements. BEA’s financial risk profile remains exposed to factors like adverse changes in regulations including the imposition of fee caps. It also faces intense competition from both private and public educational institutions. However, this risk is partly mitigated by its established reputation. 

– **Exposed to execution risk** BEA faces execution risk as it has high capex plans of Rs. 600-650 crore over the next 2-3 years, expected to be funded through term debt along with internal accruals and on-balance sheet liquidity. However, a track record of timely execution in the past provides comfort. Further, it maintains a sizeable portion of its deposits and bank balances with Bapuji Cooperative Bank (Rs. 302.92 crore as on March 31, 2026) affecting its liquidity position and exposing it to the related credit risk with such relatively illiquid investments and credit profile of investee entity/bank. Also, any delays or cost overruns could put further pressure on BEA’s liquidity position.

# **Liquidity position: Adequate** 

BEA’s liquidity position remains Adequate, with free cash and liquid investments of ~Rs. 168 crore as on March 31, 2026. BEA is expected to fund its high capex plans of Rs. 600-650 crore in the next 2-3 years through fresh term debt of Rs. 200-250 crore, along with internal accruals and on-balance sheet liquidity. Further, it is expected to meet its repayment obligations comfortably by its cash flow from operations for debt outstanding as on March 31, 2026, as well as projected fresh debt. BEA continues to maintain a sizeable portion of its bank balances with Bapuji Cooperative Bank (Rs. 302.92 crore as on March 31, 2026), which reduces its financial flexibility to an extent. 

# **Rating sensitivities** 

**Positive factors –** ICRA may upgrade the ratings if there is a material improvement in the scale of operations and liquidity position along with diversification of revenues, on a sustained basis. 

**Negative factors –** Pressure on the ratings could arise if there is a material decline in fee collections, adversely impacting the financial profile or liquidity position of BEA. The ratings could also be downgraded in case of a large unanticipated debt-funded capital expenditure or if regulatory developments adversely impact BEA’s business prospects. Specific credit metrics that could result in a rating downgrade include total debt/OPBDITA of more than 1.5 times on a sustained basis.

# **About the association** 

Bapuji Education Association (BEA) was set up in 1958 to provide quality education in Davangere, Karnataka. BEA began its operations with a single institution in 1958, and at present, runs more than 50 institutes (including colleges and schools) offering courses in medical sciences, dental sciences, nursing, pharmacy, engineering, management, law, polytechnic and degree courses in arts, science and commerce. 

The society also runs six hospitals, which includes teaching hospital facilities at Bapuji Hospital and SSIMSRC Hospital for the medical courses offered at J.J.M. Medical College and S.S. Institute of Medical Sciences and Research, respectively. It also operates 15 hostels, located centrally in Davangere, to cater to boarding requirements of students studying in various institutions run by the Trust. It also manages eight schools located in and around Davangere providing primary to pre-university level education. All operations of the Group are concentrated in Davangere, Karnataka.
