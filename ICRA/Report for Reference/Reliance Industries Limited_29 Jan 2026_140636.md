# **Rationale** 

To arrive at the ratings, ICRA has considered the consolidated financials of Reliance Industries Limited (RIL/the company). Additionally, ICRA has combined the business and financial risk profiles of Jamnagar Utilities and Power Private Limited (JUPPL), and Sikka Ports and Terminals Limited (SPTL) as they have a high degree of operational integration with RIL and are thus of significant strategic importance to the company. The liabilities towards Summit Digitel Infrastructure Limited {SDIL, rated, [ICRA]AAA (Stable)} and the committed payments towards Pipeline Infrastructure Limited {PIL, rated [ICRA]A1+} have been capitalised as the services offered by these entities are integral to the business operations of RIL. The pass-through certificates (PTCs) of First Business Receivable Trust and the spectrum payment liabilities of RJIL towards the Department of Telecommunications (DoT) have been added to the debt. 

The reaffirmation of the ratings assigned to RIL’s non-convertible debentures (NCD) and the commercial paper programme (CP) factors in the company’s increasingly diversified earnings mix with the digital services and retail segment contributing ~53% to the consolidated segmental EBITDA in 9M FY2026. The increasing share of earnings from the non-cyclical segments (retail and digital services) is expected to moderate the impact of volatility in the hydrocarbon segment. 

The ratings also factor in the company’s leadership position in the oil-to-chemicals (O2C) business, given the highly integrated operations and a product portfolio spanning across transportation fuels, polymers, elastomers, intermediaries and polyesters. RIL’s Jamnagar refinery is one of the most complex refineries globally, supported by the downstream refinery off-gas cracker (ROGC) and pet coke gasifier. These units enable RIL to add significant value to the refinery’s downstream low-value products. The refinery complex has strong downward integration in the polymer and polyester segment. The company has also established a virtual pipeline from the US to India to import ethane for its crackers, providing significant cost advantage. 

In digital services, the company has carved a leadership position with Reliance Jio Infocomm Limited (RJIL) being the largest telecom operator in the country with a total subscriber base of 515.3 million as on December 31, 2025. The company is also the leader in fixed broadband services with around 25 million subscribers as on December 31, 2025. 

The company has established its retail business under Reliance Retail Ventures Limited (a subsidiary of RIL). It operates 19,979 stores (as on December 31, 2025) across India. RRVL is also scaling up its digital commerce platform through JioMart. The company has been acquiring several brands in order to scale up its retail business, and the hyper-local commerce segment has also witnessed strong traction backed by its wide store network. The retail business is well diversified across various segments i.e. grocery, fashion & lifestyle and consumer electronics. The company has recently demerged the consumer brands business from Reliance Retail Ventures Limited (RRVL) into Reliance Consumer Products limited (RCPL) which is now a direct subsidiary

# **Key rating drivers and their description** 

## **Credit strengths** 

**Diversified revenue and earnings mix across sectors –** Over the past few years, Reliance Industries Limited (RIL) has witnessed a significant shift in its consolidated revenue and profitability mix, driven by a healthy scale-up in its digital services and retail segments. These segments have steadily increased their contribution to the overall earnings, reducing the company’s dependence on the cyclical oil-to-chemicals (O2C) and oil & gas (O&G) businesses. In 9M FY2026, the combined share of EBITDA from Digital Services and Retail stood at ~53%, surpassing the ~42% contribution from the O2C and O&G segments. This change underscores RIL’s transformation from an oil-centric entity into a diversified conglomerate. The growing share of the noncyclical businesses is expected to temper the volatility in consolidated earnings, thereby enhancing the stability and the resilience in the company’s credit profile. 

– **Leadership position in the oil-to-chemicals, retail and telecom segments** RIL maintains a strong leadership position in its oil-to-chemicals (O2C) business, supported by its integrated operations, diversified feedstock flexibility and scale advantages. The high complexity of the refinery enables it to process a wide variety of crude, and downstream processing units like the ROGC and pet coke gasifier enable it to add value to low-value products. The company has also strengthened its supply chain for feedstock sourcing for theO2C business through a largely captive operations at the Sikka Port and establishment of a virtual ethane pipeline for importing ethane from US to India. While the downstream deltas continue to be under pressure, RIL’s integrated operations and advantageous ethane cracking along with high GRMs from the refinery complex are expected to result in a healthy performance of the segment. Through the fuel retailing JV with BP, operating under the brand name Jio-BP, RIL also has domestic fuel retail distribution capabilities with a countrywide network of 2,125 fuel outlets as on December 31, 2025. 

The company has also carved a leadership position in digital services with Reliance Jio Infocomm Limited (RJIL) being the largest telecom operator in the country with a total subscriber base of 515.3 million as on December 31, 2025. The company is also the leader in fixed broadband with ~25 million subscribers as on December 31, 2025. 

The company has expanded its retail business under Reliance Retail Ventures Limited, a subsidiary of RIL. The company has expanded its presence across 19,979 stores (as on December 31, 2025) in India and has also ramped up its digital commerce platform. The company has been acquiring several brands to scale up its retail business, and the hyper-local commerce

## **Credit challenges** 

**Profitability exposed to cyclicality in the O2C and E&P segment** – RIL derives a significant portion of its profits from O2C and E&P operations. These business segments are cyclical in nature as the demand for petroleum products is closely related to the overall global macroeconomic environment. As a result, the margins have witnessed cyclicality. At present, the petrochemical margins are at multi-year lows owing to soft global demand, while sizeable capacity expansions have led to significant capacity surplus. RIL has also made significant investments in the E&P business, which remains exposed to the risks of lower-thananticipated production and volatility in natural gas prices. The cyclicality, however, has been partly mitigated by the rising share of profitability from the retail and the telecom sectors. 

– **Significant competitive intensity in retail segment** RRVL faces stiff competition owing to the presence of numerous players in the unorganised segment along with competition from various organised players in the brick-and-mortar and online segments. The retail sector also remains susceptible to adverse macroeconomic environment and the emergence of quick commerce as a strong participant in the retail industry. Nonetheless, over the years, the company has made significant investments in technology and diverse offline/online retail channels with focus on streamlining operations and a calibrated approach to sustain profitability. 

– **Telecom business faces high competitive intensity and technology risk** JPL has set up a country-wide network infrastructure and acquired spectrum across multiple bands. It may face challenges in achieving optimal returns from its investments in the medium term owing to the high competitive intensity and technological evolution. 

– **Sizeable investments, long gestation period and technology risk in new energy segment** RIL’s new energy foray faces several challenges, given the scale and complexity of the investments involved. The segment requires substantial upfront capital deployment across solar, battery storage, electrolysers and large integrated manufacturing assets, all of which have long gestation periods before generating stable cash flows. The business also carries inherent technology risks, as many of the platforms such as advanced battery chemistries, giga-scale electrolysers are still evolving globally. Any delays in scaling up these technologies, achieving cost competitiveness or securing timely market offtake could weigh on the returns and extend the payback period. However, RIL’s experience of a timely and successful execution of large-scale and complex projects largely mitigates this risk. 

# **Environmental and social risks** 

**Environmental considerations -** There is increasing environmental regulation in the O2C segment, although for RIL, the impact is partly mitigated by the high degree of downstream integration into petrochemicals. Further, the company has announced its target to achieve net carbon zero by 2035. ICRA also takes note of RIL’s operational capabilities with a strong track record of environmental compliance. RIL’s growing diversification into digital services and retail also mitigates the environmental risk for the entity from a longer term credit perspective.

**Social considerations -** The company is also exposed to social risks emanating from changing consumer preferences and the Government’s initiatives to reduce plastic consumption. # **Liquidity position: Superior** 

RIL enjoys superior liquidity on account of its increasing profits and negative working capital cycle which results in healthy retained cash flows. The company has maintained sizeable liquid investments, comprising cash and cash equivalents of Rs. 2,29,794 crore as on December 31, 2025, and has access to unutilised bank lines, which further strengthens its liquidity profile. Besides, the company has a proven track record in raising funds from the international/domestic bond markets with long tenures at fine rates.

# **About the company** 

Reliance Industries Limited (RIL) is India’s largest private sector enterprise. Starting with textiles in the late seventies, the company has pursued a strategy of backward vertical integration – in polyester, fibre intermediates, plastics, other petrochemicals, petroleum refining and oil and gas exploration and production – thereby becoming a highly integrated player with presence across the energy value chain. RIL enjoys global leadership in most of its businesses, being the largest polyester yarn and fibre producer in the world and among the top five to ten producers of major petrochemical products in the world. Through its subsidiary companies, RIL is also involved in diversified businesses spanning retail (grocery, fashion and lifestyle, digital commerce), digital services, FMCG and media and entertainment.
