## **Rationale** 

The rating action for Reliance Power Limited (RPL) reflects the delays in the debt servicing on the loans raised by Samalkot Power Limited (SMPL), a subsidiary of RPL, wherein the latter has provided an unconditional and irrevocable corporate guarantee. SMPL’s lender has raised a demand for the payment of the outstanding principal, invoking the guarantee agreement executed by RPL. Subsequently, SMPL has initiated arbitration proceedings against the lender and facility agent before the London Court of International Arbitration, contending that no debt is due as per the facility agreements. The arbitration proceeding is ongoing, and the matter is currently sub-judice. ICRA also notes the satisfactory operational performance of the two thermal plants—Sasan Power Limited and Rosa Power Supply Company Limited in FY2025 and H1FY2026. 

The ratings continue to be constrained by the uncertainty over the non-operational Samalkot power project under SMPL. The management is currently exploring opportunities to monetise the remaining two modules of SMPL to repay the debt. The ratings also factor in the exposure to the counterparty credit risks associated with state-owned distribution utilities for the Sasan Ultra Mega Power coal-based project in Madhya Pradesh and the Rosa Power coal-based power project in Uttar Pradesh under RPL’s subsidiaries. Further, the Group remains exposed to funding and execution risks for the recently won renewable energy projects through a competitive bidding process by Central nodal agencies.

### **Credit challenges** 

**Continuing delays in debt servicing -** RPL continues to delay in the debt servicing on the loans raised by SMPL, a subsidiary of RPL, wherein the latter has provided an unconditional and irrevocable corporate guarantee. The lender has raised a demand of Rs. 1,641.67 crore towards the outstanding principal, invoking the corporate guarantee agreement executed by RPL. Subsequently, SMPL has initiated arbitration proceedings against the lender and facility agent before the London Court of International Arbitration, contending that no debt is due under the said facility agreements The arbitration proceeding is ongoing and the matter is currently sub-judice. 

**- Uncertainty over non-operational Samalkot power project** The Samalkot project, under its subsidiary SMPL, continues to face uncertainty due to its non-operational status. SMPL had signed an equipment supply contract in March 2020 to sell one module for the development of Phase-1 of the project in Bangladesh. The export of the module has been completed and

The rating also remains vulnerable to fuel supply risks, though this is mitigated by the fuel supply agreements with Coal India Limited for the Rosa power project and through captive mines for the Sasan project. 

**Execution risks for under-development renewable energy projects** - RPL, through its subsidiaries, has won various tenders through the competitive bidding process by Central nodal agencies for the development of renewable energy projects. This exposes the company to significant execution risks. A timely completion of the land acquisition and transmission infrastructure, and procurement of the required equipment within the budgeted costs will be critical to ensure project completion within the scheduled timelines and capital expenditure. Additionally, achieving financial closure for the under-construction capacity in a timely manner remains essential. 

# **Liquidity position: Poor** 

RPL’s liquidity position is poor with the company delaying its debt servicing obligations owing to insufficient cash flow from operations. The company had cash and bank balances of Rs. 62.77 crore as on August 31, 2025, at a standalone level **.**

# **About the company** 

RPL, a part of the Reliance Group promoted by Mr. Anil D Ambani, is the primary vehicle for investments in the power generation sector. As on date, the company’s generation capacity stood at 5,305 MW, including 5,160 MW of thermal capacity and 145 MW of renewable energy-based capacity. RPL’s operational projects include the Rosa coal-based power project (1,200 MW) in Uttar Pradesh, the Sasan Ultra Mega Power coal-based power project (3,960 MW) in Madhya Pradesh, the 40-MW solar photovoltaic (PV) power project at Dhursar, Rajasthan, the 100-MW concentrated solar power project in Jaisalmer, Rajasthan, and the 5-MW wind power project in Tamil Nadu.
