## **Rationale** 

The ratings revision factors in Utkarsh Small Finance Bank Limited’s (Utkarsh) weak asset quality and earnings profile. The bank’s asset quality deteriorated significantly in FY2026, with gross non-performing assets (GNPAs) remaining elevated at 7.7% as on March 31, 2026 (9.4% as on March 31, 2025) despite sizeable write-offs and sale of stressed assets (Rs. 1,737 crore; 9.0% of portfolio as of March 2026). The deterioration was markedly visible in the microfinance segment (GNPAs of 13.3% as on March 31, 2026; 16.4% as on March 31, 2025), though delinquencies have increased in other product segments as well (GNPAs of 3.9% as on March 31, 2026; 2.5% as on March 31, 2025). ICRA notes that the collection efficiency is gradually improving, but slippages remained high. 

Consequent to the asset quality stress, the bank’s earnings profile has weakened amid elevated credit costs and subdued operating efficiency. Muted growth in the portfolio, interest reversal on NPAs and the shift in the portfolio mix towards relatively lower-yielding asset classes have adversely impacted Utkarsh’s operating profitability. The bank reported a net loss of Rs. 1,151 crore in FY2026 vis-à-vis a profit of Rs. 24 crore in FY2025. ICRA expects the earnings profile to remain subdued in the near term due to the elevated cost structure in relation to the scale and lingering asset quality challenges, with a gradual improvement expected over the medium term. 

Given the losses and its weak asset quality, Utkarsh’s capital buffers have deteriorated despite the ~Rs. 950 crore of equity capital raised in November 2025 through a rights issue. Its capital adequacy ratio declined to 17.7% (Tier I of 15.0%) as on March 31, 2026 from 20.9% (Tier I: 17.9%) as on March 31, 2025. Nonetheless, the bank remains adequately capitalised in relation to regulatory requirements (CRAR of 15%; Tier I: 7.5%). ICRA notes that Utkarsh is in the process of implementing a few initiatives to improve its capital adequacy, including the raising of Tier II capital in the near term. This shall help it maintain adequate buffers over the regulatory requirements as it plans to scale up its operations. However, the timely execution of this capital raise is critical from a rating perspective. 

ICRA also notes that Utkarsh’s deposit franchise has a sizeable share of retail deposits {retail term deposits + current account savings account (CASA)} with the same increasing to ~83% as of March 2026 from ~71% as of March 2025. While the share of CASA deposits improved, it remained low at 24% as on March 31, 2026 (22% as on March 31, 2025). Going forward, Utkarsh’s ability to expand its deposit base, increase the share of relatively lower-cost CASA deposits and keep the deposit profile granular shall remain important. 

The Stable outlook on the long-term rating reflects ICRA’s expectation that Utkarsh would maintain an adequate capitalisation profile while gradually improving its earnings profile as it scales up its operations.

# **Key rating drivers and their description** 

## **Credit strengths** 

**Adequate capitalisation; Tier II capital raise planned in the near term** – The bank has an adequate capitalisation profile with a capital adequacy ratio of 17.7% (Tier I of 15.0%) as on March 31, 2026 vis-à-vis the regulatory requirement of 15% (Tier I: 7.5%), though it declined from 20.9% (Tier I: 17.9%) as on March 31, 2025. This was on account of the significant loss of Rs. 1,151 crore incurred in FY2026 despite the primary capital raise of Rs. 950 crore (via a rights issue) during the fiscal. Utkarsh’s gearing (including deposits) has also increased and stood at 8.8 times as on March 31, 2026 vis-à-vis 8.0 times as on March 31, 2025 (6.5 times as of March 2024). ICRA notes that the bank is in the process of implementing a few initiatives to improve its capital adequacy, including the raising of Tier II capital in the near term. This shall help it maintain adequate buffers over the regulatory requirements as it plans to scale up its operations. However, the timely execution of this capital raise remains a key monitorable. 

**Steady portfolio diversification expected over the medium term** – Over the years, Utkarsh has been scaling up its relatively newer products, such as loans to micro, small and medium enterprises (MSMEs), housing loans, construction equipment (CE) and commercial vehicles (CVs), etc. The bank reported assets under management (AUM) of Rs. 19,332 crore as on March 31, 2026 (Rs. 19,666 crore as on March 31, 2025). Given the stress witnessed in the microfinance portfolio, Utkarsh has focussed on scaling up its secured loan portfolio. Consequently, the share of the non-microfinance portfolio increased to 61% of the portfolio as on March 31, 2026 (50% as on March 31, 2025; 38% as on March 31, 2024). ICRA expects the non-microfinance portfolio to constitute more than 50% of the overall AUM in the medium term. Nonetheless, Utkarsh’s ability to maintain the asset quality in the relatively newer product segments remains monitorable. 

## **Credit challenges** 

**Weak asset quality; geographically concentrated portfolio** – Utkarsh’s asset quality deteriorated with GNPAs and net NPAs (NNPAs) remaining elevated at 7.7% and 3.3%, respectively, as on March 31, 2026 (9.4% and 4.8%, respectively, as on March 31, 2025). This was despite the sizeable write-offs and sale of stressed portfolio to asset reconstruction companies (ARCs). The deterioration was primarily due to the stress witnessed in the microfinance portfolio, with GNPAs remaining high at 13.3% as on March 31, 2026 (16.4% as on March 31, 2025). The asset quality in the non-microfinance portfolio also moderated with the GNPAs increasing to 3.9% as on March 31, 2026 from 2.5% as on March 31, 2025. Moreover, the microfinance portfolio remains geographically concentrated with the share of Bihar and Uttar Pradesh remaining high at 46% and 28%, respectively, as on March 31, 2026. The solvency ratio (NNPAs/Net worth) stood at 21.4% as on March 31, 2026 (30.4% as on March 31, 2025). 

ICRA notes that Utkarsh has taken certain corrective measures, including the tightening of customer selection criteria, strengthening of the collections team, etc. ICRA also notes that the collection efficiency is gradually improving, though slippages remain high. In this regard, the bank’s ability to control slippages and achieve recoveries from delinquent accounts shall remain monitorable. 

**Deterioration in earnings profile** – With the rise in the relatively lower-yielding non-microfinance portfolio, muted portfolio growth and interest reversals on NPA slippages, the overall yields have been declining, leading to a reduction in Utkarsh’s lending spread and net interest margins (NIMs). The operating efficiency also declined with operational expenses (as a proportion of average managed assets; AMA) increasing to 6.5% in FY2026 from 6.2% in FY2025 (6.0% in FY2024). Compressed NIMs and elevated operating expenses affected the operating profit with the bank reporting operating-level losses in Q2 and Q3 FY2026 and a subdued operating profit of Rs. 12 crore in Q4 FY2026 (FY2026 operating profit: Rs. 56 crore). Credit costs (as a proportion of AMA) rose to 5.5% in FY2026 from 3.8% in FY2025 (1.6% in FY2024) due to the deterioration in the asset quality. Utkarsh reported a loss of Rs. 1,151 crore in FY2026 compared to a profit after tax (PAT) of Rs. 24 crore in FY2025 (PAT of Rs. 498 crore in FY2024). ICRA expects the bank’s earnings profile to remain subdued in the near term, given the elevated cost structure in relation to the scale and lingering asset quality challenges, with a gradual improvement expected over the medium term.

**Ability to improve CASA share in deposits** – Utkarsh has successfully built a large deposit base, constituting 88% of its total borrowings as on March 31, 2026 (90% as on March 31, 2025). The deposit base remained largely stable at Rs. 21,654 crore as on March 31, 2026 (Rs. 21,566 crore as on March 31, 2025). Despite improving, the share of CASA continued to be low at 24% as on March 31, 2026 (22% as on March 31, 2025). ICRA takes note of the improving granularity of deposits with total CASA and retail term deposits increasing to 83% as of March 2026 from 71% as of March 2025 (66% as on March 31, 2024). The share of the top 20 depositors has also been gradually declining and stood at 14% as on March 31, 2026 (16% as on March 31, 2025). Going forward, Utkarsh’s ability to expand its deposit base, improve the share of relatively lower-cost CASA deposits and keep the deposit profile granular shall remain important from a credit perspective. 

# **Liquidity position: Strong** 

The liquidity coverage ratio was healthy at 163% for the quarter ended March 31, 2026. The net stable funding ratio (NSFR) stood at 117.5% as on March 31, 2026, remaining above the regulatory level of 100%. The bank’s structural liquidity statement, as on March 31, 2026, did not show any negative cumulative mismatches for a period of up to one year. Utkarsh’s liquidity profile is supported by the large portion of relatively shorter-tenor assets. It reported a credit-to-deposit (CD) ratio of 83% as on March 31, 2026 against 87% as on March 31, 2025 (94% in March 2024). 

# **Environmental and social risks** 

**Environmental considerations** – Given Utkarsh’s service-oriented nature of business, its direct exposure to environmental risks as well as those stemming from regulations or policy changes is not material. While the bank is not materially exposed to physical climate risks, it indirectly encounters environmental risks through its portfolio of assets. Further, its lending typically involves short-to-medium-term durations, enabling it to adjust and incrementally invest in less environmentally vulnerable businesses. 

**Social considerations** – Data security and customer privacy are among the key sources of vulnerability for banks, including Utkarsh, as any material lapse could be detrimental to its reputation and could invite regulatory censure. Customer preference is increasingly shifting towards digital banking, which provides an opportunity to banks to reduce their operating costs. However, subpar execution of information technology strategies and the inability to meet customer requirements adequately may result in more costs than benefits. On the positive side, Utkarsh contributes towards enhancing financial inclusion by providing several products and services that are specifically targeted towards the marginalised sections of society and attempts to address and cater to social concerns. Prudent lending to such underserved segments could create growth opportunities. However, these opportunities must be seen in conjuncture with the asset quality risks that could impact the bank’s credit quality. 

# **Rating sensitivities** 

**Positive factors** – Sustained improvement in the asset quality and profitability indicators while maintaining a prudent capitalisation profile could positively impact the ratings. 

**Negative factors** – Utkarsh’s inability to improve its profitability or maintain adequate capital buffers will exert pressure on the ratings.

# **About the company** 

Incorporated in 2009, Utkarsh Micro Finance Limited received a small finance bank (SFB) licence from the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) in November 2016 and completed the conversion to an SFB in January 2017. The bank is headed by Mr. Govind Singh, who was earlier the business head of micro-banking at ICICI Bank Limited. 

Operating in 27 states/Union Territories (UTs), as on March 31, 2026, Utkarsh offers deposits, micro-banking loans, micro, small and medium enterprise (MSME) loans, affordable housing loans and corporate loans, among other products. It had a portfolio of Rs. 19,332 crore as on March 31, 2026, with micro-banking loans (including micro-banking loans sourced by business correspondents) comprising 39% (50% as on March 31, 2025). As for liabilities, the bank had a deposit base of Rs. 21,654 crore as on March 31, 2026 (Rs. 21,566 crore as on March 31, 2025), with term deposits accounting for 76% (78% as on March 31, 2025).
