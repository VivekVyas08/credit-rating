## **Rationale** 

The reaffirmation of the ratings of Hero MotoCorp Limited (HMCL) continues to reflect expectations of a healthy operating performance, aided by its market leadership in the domestic two-wheeler (2W) industry (market share of 28.6% and 42.6% in the overall 2W and motorcycle segments, respectively, in FY2025), its robust product portfolio, established brands, and wellentrenched dealership network. The ratings also continue to favourably factor in its strong financial profile, reflected in robust return and credit metrics, and a superior liquidity position. The company’s strong market position provides comfort that it will continue to generate healthy cash flow going forward, thereby helping it maintain a sound credit profile. Going forward, an improved affordability post the recent revision in Goods and Services Tax (GST) and a recovery in rural demand is expected to support the 2W demand and rural cash flows. Coupled with new product launches, these factors are expected to augur well for HMCL’s revenue growth prospects. 

The ratings continue to factor in the company’s strong financial profile, evidenced by its healthy profitability (OPBDITA margin and core RoCE of 14.6% and 94.3%, respectively, in FY2025 as on March 31, 2025). Supported by multiple timely price hikes and moderation in commodity prices, profitability for the company remained healthy (14.6% in FY2025 and 14.5% in Q1 FY2026 over 12.1% in FY2023). Over the years, HMCL’s management has followed a prudent approach to growth. As a result, despite muted demand over the past few years and undertaking significant capex (for greenfield manufacturing units and portfolio upgrades), its dependence on external borrowings has remained negligible. This trend is expected to continue in the medium term, even as the company steps up its investments in alternative fuel technologies and related products. 

HMCL continues to invest in its investee companies in the financial services business, Hero FinCorp Limited (HFCL), and the electric 2W (e2W) business, Ather Energy Limited (AEL), underscoring the strategic importance of these entities. HMCL has also entered the electric three-wheeler (e3W) commercial vehicle segment by acquiring a 34.1% stake in Euler Motors Private Limited (EMPL) for a consideration of Rs. 510 crore in Q1 FY2026. ICRA will continue to monitor the performance of HMCL’s investee companies, HFCL, AEL, EMPL, and others, and any material support extended by HMCL that could weaken its liquidity position will remain a key monitorable. 

ICRA notes that HMCL’s performance remains exposed to challenges such as increasing regulatory interventions (emission and safety norms) and intense competition in the domestic 2W market. Additionally, its heavy revenue dependence on the domestic market (~94% in FY2025) and limited (albeit growing) presence in the premium motorcycle and scooter segments expose it to adverse demand trends in the domestic and rural markets. A structural shift in preference for e2Ws (as opposed to conventional 2Ws) also remains a medium-to-long-term challenge, with HMCL’s market share in the e2W segment relatively

# **Key rating drivers and their description** 

## **Credit strengths** 

**Market leader in Indian 2W market with a wide dealership network and strong rural connect –** HMCL reported global sales of 5.9 million units in FY2025 and maintained its market leadership position in the Indian 2W market with a ~29% market share (domestic). Among 2W segments, HMCL is a market leader in the domestic motorcycle segment with a ~43% share (in FY2025). It is also among the top four OEMs in the scooter segment, albeit with a low share of ~6% in FY2025. HMCL is likely to maintain its leadership position in the Indian 2W industry with new product launches in motorcycles, especially in the premium and scooter segments. Its strong brand equity, well-entrenched distribution network and wide product offerings will continue to provide comfort. HMCL has over 10,000 customer touch points (globally) with a deep-rooted rural presence in India, making it one of the strongest sales networks among all 2W manufacturers. 

**Strong product portfolio with established brands –** The company had three brands in the top 10 motorcycles and two models in the top 10 scooters sold in India for FY2025, the highest among 2W OEMs. HMCL’s strong brands include Splendor, HF Deluxe and Passion, which account for ~80% of the total volumes of its entry level sub-segment in FY2025. The OEM’s dominance in the largest 2W sub-segment and the expectation of improving presence in other sub-segments as well as product categories (scooters, e2Ws) support healthy revenue visibility for the company. 

**Robust financial risk profile –** HMCL’s strong financial profile is evidenced by a conservative capital structure (TD/TNW of 0.0 times and TOL/TNW of 0.5 times as on March 31, 2025) and a negative net debt position at a consolidated level (zero debt on a standalone basis). The core RoCE of the business, on a consolidated level, improved to ~94.3% for FY2025 (~79.0% in FY2024), driven by higher asset utilisation due to recovering industry sentiments. Additionally, the company has a strong liquidity profile, with over Rs. 10,000 crore of cash and investments as on March 31, 2025. 

## **Credit challenges** 

**Low share in export markets; remains susceptible to vagaries of rural demand sentiment –** The company is largely dependent on the domestic market, which accounted for ~95% of total volumes dispatched in FY2025. While it increased its presence in the overseas markets in FY2025, with a strong 44% growth over FY2024, the contribution of exports to total sales and overall market share of HMCL in exports (~7% in FY2025) remains modest. Nevertheless, the company continues to expand (presence in 50 countries as on date) and deepen its presence in export markets with a target of 12-15% export revenues over the medium term. An improved domestic-export mix could provide a cushion in case of a slowdown in domestic demand. HMCL also has a high dependence on products in the entry and executive sub-segments of motorcycles, which primarily have an income-sensitive rural and semi-urban clientele. This resulted in a steeper decline in HMCL’s volumes after the pandemic and consequently weakened the rural demand. While HMCL has launched several new products to enhance its premium segment

**Increasing competition in domestic 2W market; structural transition to e2Ws over medium to long term –** The Indian 2W industry is highly competitive with regular new product launches and refreshes by several OEMs to gain market share. ICRA notes that the volumes in the e-2W segment have increased considerably for HMCL in FY2025 and H1 FY2026, which led to a healthy increase in the market share (~10% in H1 FY2026) in this segment. The company’s battery as a service (BaaS) model has also gained healthy traction due to low upfront costs and the same has resulted in increased interest in the company’s product. 

## **ESG risks** 

**Environmental considerations –** The rural demand of the automotive industry is exposed to adverse climatic conditions such as droughts and floods which may impact farm incomes and, consequently, the earning prospects of entities. HMCL also remains exposed to climate transition risks emanating from a likelihood of tightening emission control requirements, with the Government focused on reducing the adverse impact of automobile emissions. HMCL (and the 2W industry as a whole) may need to invest materially to develop products to meet the regulatory thresholds or expected transition to alternative fuel vehicles (like e2Ws), which may impact their return and credit metrics if the investments in the E2W segment are material; in this regard, HMCL’s recent gains in market share in the E2W segment provide comfort. The exposure to litigation/ penalties arising from issues related to waste and water management for the company remains low. 

**Social considerations –** HMCL has a healthy dependence on human capital and, hence, retention and maintenance of healthy employee relations as well as supplier ecosystem remain essential for disruption-free operations. Another social risk that automotive OEMs, like HMCL, face pertains to product safety and quality, wherein instances of product recalls and high warranty costs may not only lead to a financial implication but could also harm the brand reputation and create a more longlasting adverse impact on demand. However, as an established manufacture with mature research and development capabilities, strong vendor ecosystem and well-established quality control processes, HMCL has demonstrated capabilities to address the said concerns in past.  The entities also remain exposed to any major shift in consumer preferences/ demographics, which are a key driver for demand and, accordingly, may need to make material investments to realign their product portfolio. 

# **Liquidity position: Superior** 

HMCL’s liquidity is **superior** , as reflected by strong cash and liquid investments of ~Rs. 6,481 crore, non-current investments in debentures/fixed maturity plans of ~Rs. 4,056 crore as on March 31, 2025 and unutilised working capital bank limits. Additionally, healthy accruals from the business, coupled with negligible debt obligations and a favourable working capital cycle, drive the company’s strong financial profile. Over the next 12-15 months, the planned investments and capex are expected to be funded through internal accruals, helping the company maintain a superior liquidity position.

# **About the company** 

Hero MotoCorp Limited is engaged in the manufacturing and marketing of motorcycles and scooters. It is the largest twowheeler manufacturer in the world. In India, it commanded a market share of ~29% in FY2025. The company has eight manufacturing facilities with an annual production capacity of around 9.5 million units. Of the same, six plants are in India, one each at Dharuhera and Gurgaon (both in Haryana), Haridwar (Uttaranchal), Neemrana (Rajasthan), Halol (Gujarat) and Chitoor (Andhra Pradesh), while two are overseas at Columbia and Bangladesh. Additionally, the company has set up R&D centres in Jaipur (India) and Germany. Backed by a large product portfolio across various price segments, HMCL is a market leader in the domestic motorcycle industry with a share of ~43% in FY2025. It is also present in the domestic scooter market, although its market share was modest at ~6% in FY2025. 

HMCL commenced operations as Hero Honda, a joint venture company (both promoters holding 26% equity) between Hero Cycles Limited and Honda Motor Company (HMC), Japan, in January 1984. In January 2011, HMC agreed to transfer its entire shareholding of 26% to the Hero Group, thus bringing an end to the partnership. The company is listed on the BSE and NSE. The BM Munjal family holds ~34% stake in HMCL.
