# **Rationale** 

The assigned ratings draw comfort from Godrej Industries Limited’s (GIL) position as the holding company of the GILAC<sup>1</sup> group (Godrej Industries Limited and Associate Companies), healthy credit profile of its key investee companies and expectation that the same will continue over the medium term. Over the past one year, GIL’s market cover (as measured by market value of listed investments/ total debt) remained in the range of 6.6-7.0 times. The market cover stood at 6.7 times as on June 30, 2025 against 6.6 times as on March 31, 2025. Further, GIL has also received sizeable dividend income from two of its investee companies, supporting its cash flows and liquidity. GIL received Rs. 607 crore from Godrej Consumer Products Limited (GCPL) and Rs. 125 crore from Godrej Agrovet Limited (GAVL) in FY2025. Its cumulative dividend income in H1 FY2026 stood at Rs. 379 crore. Further, ICRA continues to take comfort from the management’s stated guidance of maintaining its borrowings at an optimum level and monetising its investments whenever required. The ratings continue to derive comfort from GIL’s primary listed investee entities – GCPL (rated [ICRA]AAA (Stable)/ [ICRA]A1+), Godrej Properties Limited (GPL, rated [ICRA]AA+ (Stable)/ [ICRA]A1+) and GAVL (rated [ICRA]AA (Stable)/ [ICRA]A1+) – which are present across diverse business segments. 

ICRA notes that GIL’s chemicals business has started improving on account of better demand scenario in the global oleochemicals industry. Improved demand across end-user industries like pharma, FMCG, food, etc. led to an increase in volumes and realisations in FY2025, resulting in an improvement in the operating margin of the chemicals segment. 

While there are no major incremental investment requirements in listed investees, going forward, GIL will continue to invest in its financial services arm, Godrej Capital Limited (GCL), for strategic purposes over the next two-three years. GIL has invested around Rs. 3,169 crore in GCL as of March 31, 2025 and has further investment plans of Rs. 750 crore each year over the next

# **Key rating drivers and their description** 

## **Credit strengths** 

**Position as the holding company of the Godrej (GILAC) Group Companies –** GIL is the holding company of GILAC Group and has a sizeable portfolio of investments in subsidiaries and other Group companies. Over the years, GIL has evolved as a holding company for the Group’s new business initiatives. It has demonstrated its capability to incubate businesses and successfully tied up joint ventures (JVs) and strategic alliances with leading global players for new businesses. 

**Strong credit profiles of large investee companies in portfolio; diverse portfolio mitigates sector-specific risks to a large extent –** GIL’s investment portfolio is spread across diverse business areas through different investee entities viz. GAVL (animal feeds, vegetable oil, agricultural inputs, crop protection, dairy and dairy products), GCPL (personal care, home care and hair care), GPL (property development) and GCL (NBFC and housing finance company), in addition to its standalone business of oleochemicals. This is expected to continue to shield the company from volatility in any single business line or company. Three listed investee companies, GCPL, GPL and GAVL — have strong credit profiles. GIL has also received sizeable dividend income from two of its investee companies (GCPL and GAVL), supporting its cash flow and liquidity. 

**Relatively lower debt levels as compared to the market value of unencumbered investments –** GIL’s market cover (measured as the ratio of market value of listed investments to total debt) remained in the range of 6.0-7.0 times over the last one year, reflecting relatively lower debt level compared to the market value of its unencumbered investments. As on June 30, 2025, the market cover stood at 6.7 times against 6.6 times as on March 31, 2025. The movement in market cover has been broadly in line with the overall performance of Indian capital markets. While some moderation in the market cover is expected due to anticipated increase in debt level to fund future investments, the ratio is likely to remain at a comfortable level, going forward. Additionally, ICRA continues to derive comfort from the management’s stated guidance of maintaining borrowings at an optimum level and monetising investments whenever required. 

**Leadership position in the domestic oleochemicals industry –** The company has a strong market position in the domestic oleochemicals industry, with presence in various sub-segments (like fatty acids, fatty alcohols, glycerine and surfactants). GIL also caters to export markets through this segment. GIL’s oleochemicals division witnessed a YoY growth of 24% in FY2025, led by increase in realisations and volumes on account of improved demand from end-user industries. Improved performance of the oleochemical segment and increased dividend income for GIL led to an increase in the operating profit margin (OPM) to 21.8% in FY2025 from 12.4% in FY2024. Healthy performance of the oleochemicals segment is expected to continue, going forward. 

## **Credit challenges** 

**Moderate coverage indicators and refinancing risks –** GIL’s coverage indicators remain moderate because of its sizeable debt (gross debt of Rs. 10,511 crore, as on June 30, 2025 and Rs. 10,068 crore as on March 31, 2025), which has been primarily deployed towards investments in key subsidiaries and incremental working capital requirements for the oleochemicals business. The company’s interest coverage ratio stood at 1.2 times in FY2025 against 0.6 times in FY2024. The significant

However, dividend income and monetisation of investments in the past have supported its cash flows and are expected to continue, going forward. GIL is susceptible to raw material price movements, however, historically it has kept its inventory level in check to ensure that it does not stock excess inventory and lose when the prices turn unfavourable. For domestic sales, the company is able to pass on the fluctuations in input prices to its customers, however, for exports, the price fluctuations are passed on with a lag of 2-3 months, basis the contract with customers. 

# **Environmental and Social Risks** 

**<mark>Environmental considerations –</mark>** <mark>Given the safety and environmental health-related concerns associated with chemicals, the industry is exposed to the risk of tightening regulatory norms for production, handling, disposal and transportation of chemical products. Some products can face restrictions/substitution over time because of their hazardous nature and availability of more environment-friendly alternatives. Further, in the event of accidents, litigation risks and liabilities for clean-up could be high. GIL’s investee companies also remain exposed to the impact of changes in environmental norms with respect to treatment of manufacturing residual discharge/waste and exposure to physical climate risks.</mark> 

**Social considerations –** GIL and its investee entities are exposed to the risk of shift in consumer preferences over time to more environment-friendly products. Further, instances of non-compliance with the environmental, health and safety norms could have an adverse impact, constraining GIL’s ability to operate or expand capacity. GIL is also vulnerable to data security and data privacy risks and has reasonable reliance on human capital. Overall, there is moderate exposure to social risk. 

# **Liquidity position: Adequate** 

The company’s standalone liquidity position remains adequate with sizeable market value buffer on its listed investments, lending strong financial flexibility. GIL had free cash and bank balances and liquid investments amounting to Rs. 774 crore and undrawn unsecured working capital lines of Rs. 1,025 crore, as on June 30, 2025. Further, being GILAC’s Group's holding company, GIL can access capital markets at short notice and enjoys strong relationships with banks. The company also has a track record of monetising investments to support its cash flows, whenever required. ICRA expects this trend to continue, going forward, as well. These factors are cumulatively expected to support the company's liquidity position over the medium term. The company is expected to have stable accruals from its oleochemicals business and healthy dividend income in FY2026. Against these, it has sizeable capex plans of Rs. 750 crore over next 3-4 years, and debt repayments (Rs. 24.1 crore long-term debt and Rs. 300 crore NCD in H2 FY2025), of which NCD repayments are expected to be refinanced. GIL also has additional investment of Rs. 1,400-1,500 crore planned in GCL over the next two years, which would be funded through debt. 

# **Rating sensitivities**

# **About the company** 

GIL is the holding company of the GILAC Group, which has interests in real estate, chemicals, agriculture and consumer goods (through its subsidiaries, associate companies and JVs). On a standalone basis, apart from being the holding company for the group, the company manufactures oleochemicals, where it is the domestic market leader with presence in various subsegments (like fatty acids, fatty alcohols, glycerine and surfactants). The chemicals business caters to several end-user industries including FMCG, oil & gas, chemicals intermediates, pharmaceuticals, tyres etc. As on June 30, 2025, GIL's unencumbered listed investment portfolio (book value) stood at Rs. 5,141 crore (market value of unencumbered listed investments stood at Rs. 69,972 crore as on June 30, 2025. GIL has key investments in Godrej Consumer Products Limited (a 23.7% stake), Godrej Agrovet Limited (a 64.8% stake) and Godrej Properties Limited (a 44.8% stake) as on June 30, 2025. The company’s manufacturing facilities for its chemicals division are located in Ambernath (Thane, Maharashtra), Dombivli (Thane, Maharashtra), and Valia (Gujarat). GIL also generates rental income from its Vikhroli premises in Mumbai. The promoters hold a 70.97% equity stake in the company (as on June 30, 2025) while the remaining is held by public.
