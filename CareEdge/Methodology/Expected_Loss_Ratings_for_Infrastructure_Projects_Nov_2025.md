# **Overview** 

The infrastructure sector is integral to the country’s development, supporting its economic progress and industrial growth. The various sub-sectors of the infrastructure segment include roads, railways, ports, airports, power, urban infrastructure, and transmission, among others. Infrastructure projects are capital-intensive, having a long gestation period, and are usually undertaken under distinct entities (special purpose vehicles [SPVs]), which have a contractual life and revenue model. 

Infrastructure projects in India are mainly financed by debt from commercial banks and non-banking financial companies (NBFCs), leading to high concentration on these funding channels with limited participation of bond markets in financing infrastructure in India. There is low participation from the bond market, primarily considering the higher perceived risk in the implementation stage – cost and time overruns, single asset concentration, and shorter debt tenure in comparison to the project’s economic life, among others. The high-risk perception stems from the experience of implementation delays, cost overruns, and issues faced in stabilisation in case of infrastructure projects. There are also risks due to the unpredictable ramp-up periods and risks pertaining to counterparties, markets, and operations. These risks result in lower credit ratings until the stabilisation of cash flows on the conventional rating scale, which is based on the probability of default (PD). 

However, infrastructure projects have many unique characteristics, particularly concession-driven cash flows, which enable a regular stream of cash flows post the completion and stabilisation phases. In this backdrop, the expected loss (EL) ratings provide a framework, where the ratings not only factor in the PD, as in the conventional rating scale, but also include the computation of an additional parameter, loss given default (LGD) estimate, by looking at the recovery prospects post occurrence of default. 

The composite rating based on EL is a measure of comprehensive risk and a tool to rank infrastructure project SPVs based on their relative riskiness. Moreover, in its disaggregated form, this scale will be capable of commenting on the PD (for investors seeking timely repayments) and recovery prospects of the principal and interest post-default, and thereby, provides incremental information to investors and lenders regarding the likely loss. 

# **Unique characteristics of infrastructure projectsnd the EL Rating system** 

Infrastructure projects have several unique characteristics, which differentiate them from manufacturing or trading concerns. The presence of these features enables a regular stream of cash flows in projects post the completion and stabilisation phases. Public private partnership (PPP) infrastructure projects have additional features such as the availability of termination payments, contractual protection through some form of non-compete clause, and strong counterparty, among others. Moreover, structural features such as ring-fencing of cash flows, well-defined cash flow waterfall mechanism, and low incremental capex risk also act as risk mitigation tools. 

Some of the characteristics of infrastructure projects that assume importance in EL ratings are: 

- The presence of a long-term arrangement to assure revenues, more so from government or quasigovernment entities, which gives an assurance based on the laws of the land. For example, a power purchase agreement (PPA) with a state utility, which assures off-take at specified tariff levels, and pass-

# **EL ratings – Scope** 

EL ratings cover all infrastructure projects, including under-construction and operational projects, and all debt instruments in the infrastructure sector. The following sectors are included: roads (annuity-based, toll-based, hybrid annuity model), power (thermal, hydro, wind, solar, etc), airports, ports, and other infrastructure project entities. EL ratings can also be extended to Municipal Bonds which are issued for financing infrastructure assets, in line with the extant regulatory guidelines. 

This paper explains CareEdge Ratings’ approach for arriving at the EL ratings of infrastructure projects. 

# **EL methodology** 

The computation of EL is a combination of PD and LGD. While the current system of credit ratings is based on the PD methodology, where default is construed whenever there is a missed payment, the EL rating system is an extension of the current methodology and includes the computation of an additional parameter, LGD estimate, by looking at the recovery prospects post occurrence of default. Therefore, EL indicates the expected credit loss that may arise in a project debt over the residual project life. 

While the PD aspect of the scale focusses on the timeliness of repayment in a conventional manner on the conventional ‘CARE AAA’ to ‘CARE D’ scale, the assessment of recovery prospects considers the project cash flows over its life-cycle or over the concession period, the structure of debt, and other aspects such as the strength of the underlying project agreements. Hence, the framework focuses on additional information regarding the overall project viability and recovery prospects of dues to the investor or lender over the life-cycle of the project. 

# **a. Determination of PD in the EL rating system** 

PD is determined by the credit rating of the debt instrument (‘CARE AAA’ to ‘CARE D’ scale) and the tenor of the project debt. CARE Ratings Limited (CareEdge Ratings) periodically estimates the long run and short run default rates for each rating category based on historical data. The rating on the conventional rating scale, ‘AAA’ to ‘D’ is arrived at for infrastructure projects per CareEdge Ratings’ applicable rating methodologies of various infrastructure segments. Ratings so arrived are mapped to CareEdge Ratings’ long run default rates as computed from time to time. This is considered for the determination of PD in the EL rating system.

# **b. Determination of LGD and recovery prospects in the EL rating system** 

The assessment of recovery prospects is an essential input to the EL rating system and comments on the overall recovery of dues by the investor or lender over the life of the loan or instrument. In arriving at the recovery prospects, due weightage is given to the unique characteristics and inherent strengths of an infrastructure project. LGD gives the estimates of the actual loss that the lender or investor will incur if the issuer defaults and is often expressed as: 1 – Recovery Rate. 

The various parameters considered to compute recovery prospects include an understanding of the cash flows of the infrastructure project for its entire life-cycle and the extent of coverage it can provide to the project debt. This involves an assessment of the infrastructure projects that provide visibility to the project’s revenue stream over its life-cycle. 

# **To compute recovery prospects, CareEdge Ratings computes the present value (PV) of future free cash flows of the project and the coverage it can provide to the outstanding debt being rated. The free cash flows are computed in various stress case scenarios that may lead to default situations.** 

Keeping the in-built strengths of infrastructure projects into consideration, CareEdge Ratings carries out a scenario analysis considering scenarios that can lead to default. Free cash flows are computed in each stress case scenario and the coverage they provide (on a PV basis) to the underlying debt is assessed over the loan repayment period. The scenario analysis comprising default scenarios is carried out and the likely recovery or coverage to outstanding debt is computed to assess the ‘overall recovery prospects’ in a project. Termination payments and insurance claims are also suitably factored in for calculating recovery, especially in the construction phase. 

# **LGD = 1 – Recovery Ratio** 

# **c. Determination of EL** 

CareEdge Ratings computes the EL as a composite assessment factoring in the PD and recovery prospects in the rated exposure at the time of default. 

EL is calculated as: 

# **EL = PD X LGD** 

# **While expected credit loss (ECL) in absolute amount is calculated as:** 

# **ECL = PD X LGD X EAD** 

# **EAD is exposure at default, the outstanding project debt at the time default occurs**

# **Conclusion** 

CareEdge Ratings uses the PD approach while assigning credit ratings to debt instruments. With the focus being on timely debt servicing, even a single day, single rupee delay in payments of principal or interest results in classifying it as ‘default’ (and downgrades it to the ‘CARE D’ category). Once ratings are downgraded to the ‘CARE D’ category, there is a curing period, post which, the ratings can be upgraded. For infrastructure projects, this approach may have the limitation of not providing enough information to differentiate the fundamentally strong projects from the weaker ones. EL ratings provide broader information on associated risks to the prospective investors by considering the unique characteristics of infrastructure projects.
