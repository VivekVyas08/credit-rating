# **Background** 

Road construction has predominantly been under the purview of Government agencies. Involvement of private sector in road construction has been through cash contracts or through build, operate and transfer (BOT) format, annuity, toll, and hybrid annuity models. In case of annuity-based road projects executed under concession agreements, revenues are released through fixed quantum payouts by the concessioning authority on a bi-annual basis, in the operational period of the concession. 

# **Rating methodology** 

CARE Ratings Limited (CareEdge Ratings) has a well laid out methodology for rating companies belonging to the infrastructure sector, which can be accessed on our website www.careratings.com. CareEdge Ratings’ approach is slightly different for under-implementation projects and operational projects. Per the methodology, evaluation begins with sponsor evaluation of the infrastructure project followed by assessment of the counterparty, business risk factors, contractual features and regulatory risks specific to the project. This is followed by an assessment of the financial and project-related risk factors and its viability. However, considering size and diversity within the infrastructure segment, CareEdge Ratings has developed methodologies specific to sectors. Following is a list of such additional factors, and their analytical implications, considered by CareEdge Ratings while arriving at rating of players that operate in the road sector under BOT annuity model. 

Please refer to CareEdge Ratings’ separate criteria published for road projects under hybrid annuity revenue model which is available on our website www.careratings.com. 

# **Rating methodology for project-phase road annuity entities:** 

Risks assessed when the entity is under project phase are as under: 

# **I. Sponsor’s evaluation** 

- Demonstrated track record: The sponsor entities’ established track record in the road sector is viewed positively. 

- Current financial position: The sponsor’s current financial position is assessed to judge its ability to achieve financial closure and provide need-based support for timely completion of the project. 

- Ownership & technical strength: Full ownership of the project against joint ownership between two or more sponsors imparts superior decision making and governance and is viewed positively. Some sponsors may be joining solely basis technical capabilities rather than financial strength. The underlying arrangement is evaluated to understand likely operational and financial implications on the project. 

- Track record of honouring operational and financial commitments: The sponsor’s willingness to honour operational and financial commitments is judged by the demonstrated track record in the same or similar other projects set up by it, wherever applicable. 

- Corporate guarantee of sponsor, wherever meaningful: In such cases, CareEdge Ratings’ criteria for credit enhanced debt will also be applicable, which is available on CareEdge Ratings’ website.

Despite non-recourse nature of the special purpose vehicle (SPV) debt, financial flexibility and execution track record of sponsor are important in pre-commercial operations date (COD) phase. 

# **II. Implementation stage risk / construction risk** 

- Availability of resources: It is important to ensure availability of all resources for smooth implementation. Construction status and progress are driven by mobilisation of adequate financial resources, manpower and machinery. 

- Statutory clearances: Availability of all statutory clearances or advanced stage is viewed positively. Prominent clearances, which impact execution, include land acquisition, forest clearances and railway approvals for construction of Railway over bridge, among others. CareEdge Ratings relies on third-party assessment reports, such as monthly progress reports by Independent Consultant /Lenders’ Engineer to gauge project progress and approval status. 

- Risk of time and cost overrun: Infrastructure projects including annuity-based road projects face the risk of time and cost overrun due to issues at two stages, statutory clearances and availability of resources. This can impact viability and credit profile of the project significantly. CareEdge Ratings evaluates mitigating factors built in for restricting impact of such overrun. 

- Financial closure; extent of financial closure, tie-up of funds, proposed funding mix: Cost of project and break up, means of financing and break up, adequacy of each of these, whether comparable to other similar projects are considered. Projects with full or substantial financial closure are viewed favourably. 

CareEdge Ratings considers following points while analysing project implementation risk: 

- Developer’s track record in execution of large-sized engineering, procurement & construction (EPC) projects. 

- Availability of Right of Way (RoW) on 3H basis in terms of length and area. 

- Gap between lowest bidder (L1), L2 and L3 bidders. 

- Achievement of financial closure and status of funds deployment by sponsor and lender. 

- Status of clearances, including forest clearance. 

- Complexity of the project road in terms of presence of structural work and terrain. 

- Stage of project progress and current project progress against stipulated progress. 

- Presence of fixed-price EPC contract and assessment of the EPC contractor. 

- Sponsor support undertaking to fund cost overrun and cash deficit in the under-construction phase. 

# **II. Contractual risk assessment:** 

CareEdge Ratings undertakes identification of risks and mitigants in place through contractual arrangements. Extent of adequacy of risk allocation framework provides comfort to the project’s overall credit profile of the project. Under the PPP model followed for sectors such as roads, project details are worked out by the relevant concessioning authority awarding the project and terms of concession are put forth through model concession agreements. These terms of concession (Concessions Agreement) broadly determine the risks associated with the project. In its first step of evaluation, CareEdge Ratings examines these terms for clarity of their definitions, inherent risks irrespective of the project developer and rewards thereon. A concession agreement with welldefined terms and conditions, optimum risk allocation and suitable rewards for the risks allocated is considered as adequate/complete. # **III.  Projected financials and sensitivity analysis:** 

CareEdge Ratings’ analysis of annuity road projects includes an in-depth analysis of the financial risk associated with the project. CareEdge Ratings studies adequacy of the expected project cash flows for servicing the debt. Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) – sensitivity is worked out to analyse cash flow resilience across scenarios. Prominent parameters that are stressed comprise operation and maintenance (O&M) expenses and variations in borrowing rates. 

# **Rating methodology for operational phase projects:** 

Risks assessed when the entity is under operational phase are as under: 

- **i. Management risk:** Similar to projects under implementation, it is important to analyse management risk for projects in the operational stage also. 

- **ii. O&M risk:** The O&M cost of the stretch depends on traffic, terrain and quality of road construction. The O&M cost increases for stretches dominated by heavy vehicles and highways falling under high commercial activity area or manufacturing hubs. Typically, annuity road projects are less prone to heavy vehicular movement and hence their O&M expenses are generally lower than toll-based road projects. O&M expenses are found to be generally lower for state highways compared to national highways, which face greater vehicular movement. In case the road stretch is present in zones of high precipitation, possibilities of moisture percolating to bituminous layer are higher, which results in higher O&M and periodic maintenance expenses. Quality of road construction depends on financial and technical capability of the O&M contractor, design capacity of the road against actual traffic on the stretch and roughness index (ranges between 1,800 mm/km and 3,000 mm/km; lower the better). CareEdge Ratings assesses the O&M expenses and periodic maintenance expenses on per lane km basis and compares against the industry aggregates of its annuity-based road projects portfolio. CareEdge Ratings relies on data of the quality of road maintenance, which is generally prepared by the independent consultant appointed by the concessioning authority. Closer or higher-than-industry aggregates O&M assumptions reduces the reliance on the sponsor to extend financial support. O&M cost is expected to be significantly lower in case of rigid pavement compared to flexible pavement. 

- **iii. Cash flow risk:** In case of the operational projects, cash flow adequacy and financial flexibility are important financial parameters which are looked into. In the absence of demand risk for annuity projects, the credit profile of the annuity provider / counterparty, , concessioning authority assumes paramount importance. The track record of timely annuity disbursals is critical for revenue visibility of the project. However, provisions in respect of force majeure, or in respect of financial implications of liquidated damages and their adequacy cannot be ascertained upfront, and are therefore, not factored in credit ratings. 

# **Conclusion** 

The rating outcome is ultimately an assessment of fundamentals and probabilities of change in fundamentals. CareEdge Ratings analyses each of these factors and their linkages to arrive at overall assessment of credit quality, by considering the industry’s cyclicality. While the methodology encompasses comprehensive, financial, commercial, economic and management analysis, credit rating is an overall assessment of all aspects of the issuer.
