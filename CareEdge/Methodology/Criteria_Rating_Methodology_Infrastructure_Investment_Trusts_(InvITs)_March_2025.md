# **Overview:** 

The infrastructure sector is crucial for the economic growth of India as it has multiplier effect on the overall economy. The infrastructure projects are capital intensive with long gestation period, thus requiring long-term capital, which is one of the constraining factors for the growth of infrastructure projects in the country. In India, infrastructure has limited funding options, and traditionally, financing has been met through bank finance with a tenure of 10 to 15 years. In this backdrop, Union Budget in February 2014 introduced Infrastructure Investment Trusts (InvITs) in India, and subsequently in September 2014, The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) had notified InvIT regulations. InvITs provide long-term capital and unlock the developer’s capital, which can be deployed for further growth. 

InvITs utilise the pooled capital of investors to buy and manage income-generating infrastructure assets, such as roads, transmission towers, wind power, solar power, seaports, airports and telecom among others. InvITs are permitted to issue units (equivalent to equity) that are listed and traded on the bourses. Public InvITs invest not less than 80% value of InvIT assets in revenue-generating infrastructure assets. InvITs must distribute not less than 90% net distributable cash flows to the unit holders. The aggregate consolidated borrowings and deferred payments of the InvIT, net of cash and cash equivalents, is restricted to 70% value of the InvIT assets. However, private InvITs (not listed on the bourses) are exempt from restrictions on leverage and mandatory distribution. 

# **Scope:** 

The rating of consolidated debt of InvIT is an opinion on the capacity of InvIT to repay its debt timely. The rating neither signifies returns to unit holders nor does it signify potential yield levels to the unit holders, and should not be construed as a rating of InvIT’s units. It is also not an indicator of the credit quality of individual assets, which are part of the InvIT’s portfolio. Ratings neither predict a specific level or range of performance of an InvIT (measured in terms of the net asset value or otherwise) over given time period nor do they opine on the suitability or otherwise of an InvIT for investment or other purposes. Finally, it is to be noted, InvIT rating is not a measure on the adequacy of market value or address the extent to which trust expenses and costs may reduce distributions to unit holders.

# **Other important features of listed InvITs:** 

- i. The InvITs are to hold controlling interest and not less than 51% equity share capital or interest in the special purpose vehicles (SPVs) except in case of PPP projects, where acquiring or holding is disallowed by the government or under provisions of the concession agreement. 

- ii. InvITs are allowed to invest in two-level structure through holding company (holdco) where the holdco holds controlling interest, and not less than 51% equity share capital or interest in underlying SPVs such that the ultimate holding interest of the InvIT in the underlying SPVs is not less than 26%. 

- iii. InvITs shall have the right to appoint majority board of directors in the underlying SPVs. 

- iv. Mandatory sponsor holding of not less than 15% of the total units of the InvIT after initial offer on a postissue basis for at least three years. 

- v. Mandatory distribution of at least 90% net distributable cash flows by SPVs; for holdco’s 100% net distributable cash flows received from its SPVs has to be distributed to the InvIT and at least 90% net distributable cash flows generated form the own accruals of the holdco (if any) to be distributed to the InvIT. 

- vi. The pass through is provided considering income by way of interest received by the InvITs from SPV, that there is no taxation of such interest income in the hands of the trust and no withholding tax at the SPV’s level. However, the interest income received by non-resident unit holders attracts 5% tax, and for the resident unit holders, it attracts 10% tax.

# **Rating Methodology** 

The credit rating of borrowings of InvIT involves evaluation of broadly three key risk factors: (a) Businesss Risk - cashflows of the underlying assets, (b) Financial risk, (c) Management risk, and (d) Regulatory Risk. 

# **Business risk-** 

The most important driver of the InvIT’s credit rating is cashflows of the underlying assets. CARE Ratings Limited (CareEdge Ratings) recognises the entire cashflow is available to the InvIT to service its debt (that of holdco and the SPVs). The credit quality of the portfolio of assets is assessed using the sector-specific criteria applicable to the entity. Homogeneous assets (assets in same sector) under InvIT will be assessed based on the applicable sectorspecific criteria after pooling of cashflows. However, if there are restrictions due to the fungibility of cash flows of SPV, this will be excluded from pooling of the cash flows. For heterogeneous assets, each SPV will be assessed separately using sector-specific criteria, which are available on CareEdge Ratings’ website www.careratings.com. For example, toll roads housed under InvIT will be assessed using Rating methodology – Toll Road Projects and transmission assets housed under InvIT will be assessed using Rating methodology – Power Transmission Projects. Similarly, the other infrastructure assets (solar power, wind power, seaports and airports), respective sector-specific criteria will be used to assess credit quality of assets. 

Ratings consider the risks that infrastructure projects housed under InvIT are generally expected to face, including stresses to revenues due to the effects of macroeconomic cycles, short-term external shocks, and likely renegotiation of contracts (power purchase agreements), operating and cost stresses. The credit quality of the portfolio of assets would be assessed based on the predictability of revenues from its contracts with counterparties, proportion of revenue generating assets compared to under-construction assets, execution risk associated with under-construction projects, diversification in terms of geographies, asset class and counterparties and operations and maintenance (O&M) risk associated with the projects. Ratings under the InvITs criteria and related-sector criteria do not cover fundamental change of law or change of regulations; fundamental changes in demand due to the disruptive technology or otherwise; or extreme events, such as terrorism, long-term effects of global warming, or collateral consequences of extreme events. The analytical process often includes reviews of third-party reports, including studies prepared to assess design, technology, maintenance costs and demand risks or forecast availability of important resources such as traffic studies for toll roads. Though they may form a basis for reaching reasonable conclusions about the relevant risks, such studies have inherent limitations in methodology and actual outcomes can vary significantly from forecast. 

The rating assigned to the InvIT will normally correspond to the current portfolio of assets and new additions or deletions may be reviewed suitably. However, CareEdge Ratings may include known prospective investments in its analysis. CareEdge Ratings expects the InvIT’s stated investment philosophy will endure over a relatively long period and that there is a high likelihood, the fund maintains a given credit quality over time. New acquisitions would be separately evaluated by CareEdge Ratings after/at the time of acquisition, and if it denotes a deviation from the stated investment policy, the asset acquisition might impact the InvIT’s credit quality. 

# **Financial risk** 

The financial risk of InvITs is assessed based on the debt service protection metrics, stability of cash flows, overall leverage of InvIT, its headroom for further debt raising and liquidity reserves. InvIT regulations allow InvITs to raise debt at InvIT level or the SPV level. If InvIT has entire external debt at the InvIT level, debt protection metrics at InvIT level will be looked at. In case, debt is raised at SPVs, CareEdge Ratings evaluates debt service coverage

ratio (DSCR) at the SPVs and InvIT level and CareEdge Ratings also evaluates the extent of distress/need-based support to weaker SPVs. 

The analysis also includes definition of distributable cash flows to investors and liquidity reserves available as a cushion to manage cash flow mismatches. Since InvIT distributes minimum 90% net distributable cash flows, liquidity buffers such as debt service reserve, major maintenance reserve provide cushion against cash flow mismatches. Lower overall gearing is seen positively for toll roads, whereas for annuity and transmission projects with stable and predictable cash flow with longer repayment period tend to mitigate the high gearing to an extent, provided the counterparty making annuity payments is strong. Accordingly, availability-based projects can sustain on the lower DSCR given the certainty attached to the cash flows of the project. The foreign exchange variation risk is also assessed in case of un-hedged foreign currency exposure. Interest rate assumption, debt structure, tail period, refinancing risk, cross default clauses and financial flexibility of InvIT are also assessed. 

# **Management risk** 

The experience of investment manager and project manager is reviewed based on their track record of performing similar tasks in the past, and in terms of adherence to the standards of corporate governance. It is also noted, the experience of the project manager and investment managers may be considered as a key driver for the individual rating of under implementation projects which are in the portfolio. CareEdge Ratings would also consider the investment strategy detailed in the offer document. CareEdge Ratings will also ensure its assumptions appropriately confirm to the specifics, if any, of the investment strategy, such as minimum cash holding or a voluntary debt ceiling. CareEdge Ratings takes cognisance of the level of support provided by the sponsor to the InvIT. The support could be by way of infusion of equity or sourcing of credit. The strong sponsor will have higher financial flexibility to refinance existing debt facilities and attract equity investments from private equity players and pension funds among others. 

# **Regulatory risk** 

The InvIT and the SPVs are subject to the regulatory risks applicable to the infrastructure sector within which the InvIT/SPV operates. At the SPV level, the regulatory environment, political and legal factors impacting the project’s operations are identified and analysed. Some prominent risks are toll exemption announcements by concessioning authorities, delayed adoption of tariff terms and re-negotiation of contractual terms among others. CareEdge Ratings monitors these industry-related developments, and this is factored in the credit assessment. At the InvIT level, change in regulations considering the debt cap, distribution philosophy and investment policy among others are also assessed. 

# **Conclusion:** 

InvIT is a vehicle to invest in the infrastructure assets. InvITs are governed by SEBI guidelines. Listed InvITs are governed by stringent regulations. Guidelines have been revised over a period to attract interest of the investors and recently not listed private InvITs have been exempted from restriction on leverage and mandatory distribution among others. The rating outcome is ultimately an assessment of credit quality of portfolio of assets housed under InvIT, stability of cash flows and debt protection metrics, refinancing risk, financial flexibility and management risk.
