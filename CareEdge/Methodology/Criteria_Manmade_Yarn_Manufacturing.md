# **Industry Overview** 

Manmade fibres (MMF) can be broadly categorised under two heads- Synthetic and Cellulosic. Under the Synthetic fibre segment, there are three types of major fibres- polyester staple fibre (PSF), acrylic staple fibre (ASF) and nylon staple fibre (NSF); whereas under Cellulosic, viscose staple fibre (VSF) constitutes the major proportion. Unlike other MMFs, VSF is not synthetic and is made from wood pulp, a naturally occurring cellulose-based fibre. 

The share of Synthetic and Cellulosic fibres in global fibre consumption continues to increase over the years owing to substantial growth in production, with China being the major supplier due to vast capacities and competitive pricing. Polyester is the most widely used synthetic fibre worldwide with a market share of ~60% of the global fibre production. Changing fashion trends and modern living standards shall drive the growth of high-end polyester fibres due to its high durability, quick-drying, and easy-to clean properties. MMF is primarily used to produce blended fabrics (blended with cotton or other MMFs) and 100% non-cotton fabrics, which are further used in manufacturing readymade garments, home textiles and other industrial textiles. The manmade yarn industry is relatively fragmented compared to the MMF industry, with small and large spinners operating in the segment. 

The Government of India (GoI) through The Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) implemented Quality Control Order (QCO) on polyester yarn from October 2023, which mainly includes fully drawn yarn (FDY) and partially oriented yarn (POY) to establish quality standards and restrict the import of inferior products. However, there is an exemption for compliance of BIS requirement against the advance licence mechanism. This, and the Production linked Incentive (PLI) scheme, would help the MMF segment grow and become competitive globally. 

# **Rating Methodology** 

CARE Ratings Limited (CARE Ratings) has a standard methodology for rating companies belonging to the manufacturing sector. Per this methodology, CARE Ratings’ rating process begins with evaluation of the economy/industry in which the company operates, followed by assessment of business-risk factors specific to the company. This is followed by an assessment of financial and project-related risk factors and quality of the management. This methodology is followed while analysing all industries that come under the purview of the manufacturing sector. However, considering the size and diversity of the sector, CARE Ratings has developed methodologies specific to industries within the sector. These methodologies attempt to point out factors above those mentioned in the broad methodology, which are assessed while carrying out rating exercises of companies belonging to the particular industry. 

The following are such additional factors, and their analytical implications, considered by CARE Ratings while arriving at the rating of players that operate in the manmade yarn manufacturing industry. 

# **A) Business and Operational Risks** 

# **1. Operational Efficiency** 

Raw material (fibre) cost constitutes the largest portion of total operational cost in a man-made yarn spinning company. Polyester is the largest contributor to the entire man-made fibre/ yarn value chain and a direct substitute of cotton. Price change in cotton fibre and its relative availability has a direct impact on

PSF prices also depend on purified terephthalic acid (PTA) and mono ethylene glycol (MEG) prices, two petrochemical derivatives, costs of which are subject to crude oil price volatility. For a company manufacturing PSF, primary raw material would be PTA and MEG. For companies engaged in polyester yarn manufacturing, raw material can vary from PTA & MEG to Polyester Chips or Partially Oriented Yarn (POY)/PSF depending on the level of backward integration. Being derivatives of crude oil, prices are inherently volatile, making margins for spinners susceptible to adverse fluctuations in fibre prices. ASF and NSF prices are also crude oil dependent, while VSF prices depend on wood pulp prices. Apart from fluctuation in feedstock prices, global supply-demand and exchange rate volatility also have an impact on price of a particular MMF, as fibre is a globally traded commodity. 

Manmade yarn spinning companies are analysed based on their raw material procurement strategies and ability to manage volatility. Spinning companies capable of producing multiple counts and blends of yarns using different varieties of fibres are viewed favourably by CARE Ratings. It allows them to shift from one variety to the other in case of price fluctuation in a particular category. Distance of spinning mill from the raw material supplier is also an important factor. Mills closer to raw material suppliers are at an advantage, as this allows them to save on inward freight costs and are viewed positively. Companies having integrated nature of business (from fibre-to-yarn or yarn-to-fabric) at a single location are viewed favourably, as it leads to saving in packing and transportation cost. Modernised spinning mills, which enhances production yields, reduces waste and optimise raw material consumption, are viewed favourably. 

**Power cost** forms the next major cost component for a company engaged in the manmade yarn spinning segment. Depending on the state where the unit is located and the source of power it uses, ‘power cost per unit produced’ can vary for different players. Different power tariffs and quality of supply in different states can put certain players at an advantage compared to their peers. Players using captive generation have an assured and uninterrupted supply of power. However, this increases their capex requirement. The power cost incurred will also depend on the source of power (, coal, diesel, or renewable) being used in the captive power unit, which again can lead to difference in ‘power cost per unit produced’ for different mills. Managements’ initiatives in reducing power cost are looked at favourably. Power cost is also a function of level of modernisation of the plant, as relatively less power cost is associated with a more modernised plant and vice versa. 

**Labour relation and labour cost** play a significant role in efficient running of inherently labour intensive textile mills. Companies having cordial labour relations are better placed in terms of labour productivity and smooth operations. Mills having higher level of modernisation, have lesser reliance on labour and are viewed favourably. 

# **2. Inventory Management** 

Manmade yarn manufacturers do not face seasonality risk like their cotton yarn counterparts. Inventory management plays a crucial role owing to linkage of raw material with crude oil prices. Track record of the entity in terms of inventory stocking and its prevailing inventory policy is looked at closely. CARE Ratings analyses the inventory carried by a company and compares it with other peer group entities. Mills maintaining optimal raw material against orders and finished goods inventory (compared to peers in the industry) are generally insulated from adverse movement in raw material prices or demand slowdown and are viewed favourably. Owing to the competitive nature of the industry, adverse price fluctuation of inventory can have an impact on a company’s overall financial risk profile. Hence, inventory management

# **3. Scale and Flexibility** 

In the manmade yarn industry, which is characterised by high competition, size and capacity utilisation level of the plant, play an important role in the operational performance of a company. Large scale of operations leads to higher bargaining power for a company and enables it to access different markets. 

Large capacities producing same variety of products lead to optimal utilisation of the mills and offer the benefits of economies of scale, leading to better profitability margins. However, the level of production for two similar capacity units will depend on the specification of products being manufactured. Companies also having flexibility to manufacture diverse varieties of yarns (multiple count/denier, blended or different varieties of value-added yarns) are looked at favourably as it allows mills to account for lower/ higher demand in one particular variety and hence provides stability to the revenue stream. CARE Ratings views companies having flexibility to offer multiple product categories to be better equipped to withstand cyclicality in demand. Players having ‘finer count’ yarn in their product mix, cater to the premium market segment where demand is relatively less price elastic and margins are high. 

# **4. Level of Integration** 

Manmade yarn spinning mills having forward or backward integration are looked at favourably by CARE Ratings. Yarn manufacturers can either have backward integration into fibre manufacturing or forward integration into fabric manufacturing or into certain value addition jobs (yarn dyeing, among others). Backward integration into fibre manufacturing leads to better control over cost and quality of raw material for spinning mills. By having multiple processes in the manufacturing value chain, in-house, value addition across stages lead to cost savings and eventually better profitability margins for such companies. A company having upstream capacities commensurate to downstream capacities ensure that benefits of manufacturing process integration are completely obtained. Such companies are hence viewed positively by CARE Ratings. 

# **5. Geographical and Customer Diversification** 

CARE Ratings closely looks at concentration of sales towards particular end-customer or a dealer or a particular geography while evaluating companies. 

- Geographical diversification: Players catering to domestic and export markets can diversify risk of sluggishness in a particular market. Export-oriented companies catering to different countries have lower country-specific risk. 

- Customer diversification: CARE Ratings analyses revenue contribution of top customers whom the company supplies to. Diversified customer base helps mitigate the risk of business getting affected in case financial health of the counterparty deteriorates. This holds high importance due to cyclical nature of the textile industry. CARE Ratings also looks at the channel-wise break-up of sales derived from top customers. Direct sales to end-customers lead to better profitability margins for yarn manufacturers against sales made through the dealer network or through buying houses, which leads to elongated payment terms. Sales through the dealer network, on the other hand, can result in bulk procurement from the mills and timely payment realisation.

# **6. Fiscal Incentives and Regulatory Risks** 

The Government of India provides fiscal incentives [Scheme for Integrated Textile Parks (SITP), and duty drawbacks, among others] to companies operating in the manmade yarn manufacturing.  To promote investment, certain state governments also provide fiscal incentives in the form of capital or interest subsidy to players setting up new spinning units or undertaking modernisation at their existing units. These incentives constitute a major portion of the profitability margins of the companies and are looked at closely. CARE Ratings analyses incentives available to a company and its impact on profitability, capital structure and debt coverage indicators for the eligible company. 

The industry is governed by central government regulations including GST rates, duties imposed on import of manmade fibres/ yarns, among others. Companies also face the risk of removal or lowering incentives given by state and central governments. Change in policies/ regulations of destination countries in the export market are also looked at closely. Hence, CARE Ratings closely monitors key policy decisions taken by central and state governments and by export destination countries. 

# **B) Financial Risk** 

# **1. Liquidity requirements** 

Owing to volatile raw material prices, cyclicality inherent in the manmade yarn industry and fixed nature of expenses, companies engaged in manmade yarn manufacturing need to have sufficient liquidity cushion to meet their operational expenses and debt obligations. Companies having sufficient liquidity to counter cyclicality and volatility in raw material prices are viewed positively by CARE Ratings. CARE Ratings assesses the cushion available in working capital limits, unencumbered cash and cash equivalents and the management intent to maintain liquidity at point in time. CARE Ratings also closely looks at the current and the quick ratios and the receivable position (apart from inventory position as discussed above) of companies engaged in the manmade yarn manufacturing to analyse the liquidity profile. 

CARE Ratings also evaluates top clients of the entity for their overall credit quality. Business relation with strong counterparties reflects the quality of the company’s offerings and assurance of timely payments. CARE Ratings also analyses the company’s receivable position. Debtor ageing profile and concentration of debtors is looked at closely. Concentration of debtors towards financially weak entities can lead to slow realisation of proceeds or write-offs in the future. 

The receivable position of a company may vary depending on geographies catered to, with entities deriving significant income from exports generally having elongated average collection period. Entities taking Letter of Credits (LCs) from their clients and having cover from Export Credit Guarantee Corporation (ECGC) are viewed positively. 

# **2. Capital structure and debt coverage indicators** 

being a capital intensive business, companies operating in the industry typically have high reliance on external debt to fund their fixed capital expenditure and working capital requirements. Fiscal benefits provided by governments (Technology Upgradation Fund Scheme [TUFS] benefits provided by the central government to spinning players in the past or fiscal incentives provided by state governments) have also encouraged entities to aggressively increase their installed capacities or set-up new capacities. # **3. Forex exchange risk analysis** 

Many companies in the manmade yarn industry are exposed to foreign exchange risk due to export of yarn and/or import of fibre. Though a company might be naturally hedged to an extent, policies of the company to mitigate risk by availing of working capital borrowings in foreign currency, and entering derivative contracts, among others, are closely looked at. In this regard, effectiveness of natural hedging mechansim, instances of forex losses reported in the past and changes in hedging practices undertaken to mitigate reoccurence such losses are also anlaysed. 

# **4. Environment, social and governance (ESG) risk factors** 

CARE Ratings in its overall credit rating framework analyses critical ESG risks and their impact on credit profile of entities. CARE Ratings analyses materiality of ESG risk factors and mitigating factors being implemented by the entity. 

# **5. Environmental risk** 

Manmade yarn manufacturers face environment risks related to compliance with stringent pollution control norms set by regulatory authorities regarding air and water pollution due to crude based raw materials. They are also exposed to proper disposal of hazardous and non-hazardous solid waste, which gets generated in the manufacturing process. Pollution control norms are also being strengthened day-by-day. Violations in compliance with pollution control norms or strengthening of these norms would adversely impact operations. Entities gradually investing/ upgrading their infrastructure to meet the pollution control norms related to air/ water pollution and hazardous waste, would be viewed favourably. There are range of certifications such as BIS Certification/ISI Certification, among others, which validate that products meet specific quality and safety criteria and that production processes adhere to strict standards 

# **6. Social risk** 

MMF entities face social risks related to labour-relation risk and are also exposed to social unrest/strikes within unions/workforce. Compliance with the labour laws and adequate safety for workers provide longterm sustainability. 

# **7. Governance risk** 

CARE Ratings looks at the composition of board of directors and track record of legal and statutory compliance by its management/ directors to understand the corporate governance structure. Quality of disclosures related to financial statements, related party transactions and transparency in sharing information with stakeholders are also helpful to gauge corporate governance practices.
