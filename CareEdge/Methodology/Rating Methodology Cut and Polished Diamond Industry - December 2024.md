# **Background** 

Since its emergence in the 1970s, India’s market share in cut and polished diamonds (CPD) has been growing steadily and the country contributes 60% of the world’s supply in terms of value, 85% in terms of volume, and 92% in terms of pieces. Lower labour costs, operational efficiency, and capability to adapt to new technologies are factors that have made India a global leader in cutting and polishing of small diamonds and larger carat stones. Most players are concentrated in Surat and Navsari, Gujarat. 

The Indian CPD industry contributes about half of the overall gems and jewellery exports from India and contributes a significant portion to the gross domestic product (GDP), net foreign exchange earnings (FEEs), and employment in the country. Majority of diamonds polished in India are exported to key diamond jewellery manufacturing centres, jewellery retailers and trading hubs based in Hong Kong, the Middle East, Belgium, China, and the US. The industry is largely fragmented and unorganised, with intense competition, thin profitability, and high working capital intensity. 

There are a series of processes that rough diamonds pass through before being sold in the retail market. The diamond jewellery value chain begins with mining rough diamonds, moves to processing through cutting and polishing, manufacturing CPDs, and then comes manufacturing jewellery, followed by retail sales across the globe for end consumption. The Indian CPD industry plays a prominent role in processing rough diamonds, which involves sorting, planning, cutting, polishing, and grading, among others. 

# **Methodology** 

CARE Ratings Limited (CARE Ratings) has a detailed methodology for rating companies in the manufacturing sector. The rating process begins with evaluation of the economy and industry that the company operates in, followed by assessing business risk factors specific to the company. This is followed by an assessment of financial and projectrelated risk factors and management quality. This methodology is followed while analysing all industries under the purview of the manufacturing sector. However, considering the size and diversity of the manufacturing sector, CARE Ratings has developed methodologies specific to industries within the sector. These methodologies attempt to bring out factors above those mentioned in the broad methodology, which is considered while analysing companies belonging to an industry. CARE Ratings considers following factors as critical determinants of credit risk associated with CPD industry.

# **1. Management evaluation** 

The CPD sector in India consists mainly of closely held family-owned entities that operate through a close-knit chain of entities encompassing critical business aspects (purchase, processing, and sales), which are largely controlled by family or close relatives. 

# Key points assessed are: 

- i. Experience of promoters or group in the CPD segment and track record of operations, especially in industry downturns and adverse macroeconomic environments. 

- ii. Financial flexibility of the group and track record of fund infusion. 

# **2. Business risk** 

# **i. Access to raw materials (rough diamonds)** 

Despite being a leading CPD manufacturer, India depends on its sole raw material, rough diamonds from miners and secondary markets abroad. Rough diamonds are procured either directly from large mining companies [De Beers (DTC), Alrosa, Rio Tinto, and Dominion Diamond among others] on a longterm contract basis (sight holder status, among others) or from secondary markets (mainly Antwerp, Hong Kong, Dubai) through auction or spot basis. CARE Ratings considers the following factors while assessing business risk associated with raw material procurement: 

- Diversification of rough diamond procurement between primary and secondary sources. 

- Tenure of sourcing arrangements with diamond mining companies. 

- Supplier concentration levels. 

CARE Ratings considers CPD entities having a diversified supplier base and a long-standing association with major diamond mining companies relatively better, as it entails an assured supply of rough diamonds, which helps in better production planning per requirements of its target customers. Companies with long term-term rough diamond procurement contracts with miners (sight holder status) are viewed favourably. Long-term contracts with mining companies signifies compliance with best and transparent business practices and the entity’s superior ability to market polished diamonds. 

# **ii. Scale of operations and operating efficiency** 

CARE Ratings assesses scale of operations and operational efficiency of a CPD entity in terms of its total operating income (TOI), the ability to process a wider variety of stones in terms of carats, and the level of yield obtained from processing a rough diamond. A higher scale of operations and better operating efficiency impart an advantage in terms of economies of scale and the superior ability to withstand cyclicality in demand and downturns in business environment. 

# **Revenue diversification: Geographical, customer, and product** 

**Geographical diversification:** Diamond jewellery demand is largely discretionary and has a strong correlation with macroeconomic environment, which influences consumer spending in major diamond jewellery-consuming nations. Entities exhibiting diversified export sales are expected to be in a better position to mitigate challenges arising from decline in demand in a particular country, adverse changes in duty structure, imposition of trade restrictions/sanctions or economic and political instability in a particular region.

CARE Ratings believes that entities having a well-diversified presence across geographies are likely to exhibit more stable operating performance by reducing the impact of adverse market scenarios witnessed in a particular region. 

**Customer mix:** The CPD segment is primarily an export-oriented business, with the customer mix comprising traders (mainly in Antwerp, Hong Kong, and Dubai) and jewellery manufacturers and retailers (mainly in Hong Kong, the US, Europe, and Japan). Repeat orders from large and reputed jewellery manufacturers and retailers reflect consistency in the quality of an entity’s polished diamonds (4Cs – cut, clarity, carat, and colour), timely delivery, and adherence to international best practices required in the rough diamond processing industry. Cumulatively, these factors impart a greater degree of sustainability to its business. As credentials of many export customers are not readily available in the public domain, sudden deterioration in creditworthiness of overseas customers can adversely impact credit risk profile of CPD manufacturers. 

A diversified clientele is looked at more favourably by CARE Ratings, considering the industry practice of offering clean credit to customers, where the risk of bad debt gets more exacerbated, when sales and receivables are concentrated towards a few customers. 

**Product and sales channel diversification:** CARE Ratings believes that a diversified product portfolio and a higher degree of integration across the value chain imparts greater flexibility in shifting the product offerings per changing demand preferences in key markets. CPD entities with a wider product portfolio (presence across types of polished diamonds and engagement in manufacturing diamond-studded jewellery) are in a better position to cater to the demand of a diversified customer base, which helps in reducing earning volatility. CARE Ratings also believes that adoption of multiple sales channels (direct sales, online, and associates, among others) reflects CPD player’s wider market reach, acceptance, and business stability. 

# **iii. Adoption of technology to maximise yield and grading of diamonds** 

Technological advancements (across processing value chain) enable the manufacturer reduce wastage, increase yield, and plan the most profitable cutting and polishing options for a given rough diamond. The level of adoption of technology also enhances worker productivity, which is critical considering that the industry is labour intensive. In the last few years, the certification of polished diamonds has gained importance to increase transparency, eliminate fake and synthetic diamonds, and protect consumers’ interest in terms of quality assurance. CARE Ratings believes that a larger share of certified diamonds in the overall sales mix of a CPD manufacturer aids in a competitive market, where customers are becoming increasingly quality-conscious and are willing to pay a premium for third-party certification. 

# **iv. Transactions with related parties** 

It is an industry wide practice in CPD that most transaction (Sale/purchase) are routed through related/group entities unlike other industries. CARE Ratings endeavours to assess the nature and value of transactions of a CPD entity with related parties and associates and amounts due to or receivables from them and overall financial position of such group companies. Lack of adequate information on key group and associate concerns is viewed negatively in the credit risk assessment of the CPD entity.

Investments and loans and advances to associate concerns engaged in unrelated businesses are also appropriately factored in while assessing the CPD entity’s credit risk. 

CARE Ratings considers business risk profile of those CPD manufacturers to be favourable in case they have demonstrated a long track record of growth in their scale of operations across industry cycles. It also considers how entities generate free operating cash flows through efficient working capital management and earn superior profit before interest, lease rentals, depreciation and taxation (PBILDT) margin than the industry. 

# **3. Financial risk** 

# **i. Large working capital requirements** 

Long operating cycles and high working capital intensity are often inherent characteristics of entities in the CPD industry. CARE Ratings compares an entity’s operating cycle in comparison to its peers and industry median to assess the level of its working capital intensity. 

Upfront payment to miners for procuring rough diamonds, longer turnaround time in processing (including grading and certification of CPD), high inventory levels, and extension of long credit periods to its customers are inherent operating characteristics of entities in the CPD industry. All these factors cumulatively result in high working capital requirements. 

CARE Ratings believes that entities that have relatively faster inventory turnover, shorter receivable days, and enjoy credit from rough diamond traders are in a better position to generate healthy cash flow from operations. 

For assessing effectiveness of an entity’s working capital management, CARE Ratings analyses the net working capital as a percentage of current assets, manufacturer’s major debtors including a party-wise breakup, its ageing, and movement over the years. Entities demonstrating timeliness in realisation of their export bills and having a cushion in terms of unused working capital lines of credit, and unencumbered liquid investments, among others, are viewed favourably. 

# **ii. Foreign exchange fluctuation risk** 

Although entities in the CPD industry enjoy a natural hedge, which mitigates foreign exchange fluctuation risk to a certain extent, most entities lack an active hedging mechanism for their unhedged foreign currency exposure. 

CARE Ratings believes that in the absence of an active hedging mechanism, steep fluctuation in exchange rates can adversely affect earnings and cash flows of an entity. CARE Ratings evaluates hedging mechanism adopted by entities to access exchange rate risks. 

# **4. Industry risk** 

Industry risk analysis by CARE Ratings focuses on industry prospects and competitive factors affecting the industry. The economic and industry environment is assessed to determine the degree of operating risk faced by an entity in the CPD segment. Evaluation of industry risk encompasses an assessment of key parameters such as entry barriers in the industry, demand-supply dynamics, price trends, and business cycles, among others.

A high degree of fragmentation, limited value addition, and intense competition result in thin profitability. Diamond jewellery demand is largely discretionary and has a strong correlation with growth in economic activity and real income levels. While prices of rough diamonds are determined by miners due to the oligopolistic nature of the segment, CPD prices are largely market-driven, based on demandsupply dynamics. As a result, CPD manufacturers have low bargaining power and are often price takers at both ends of the spectrum and carry the risk of inventory holding and elongated receivables period in times of subdued demand scenario. 

Apart from these factors, instances of over-grading diamonds, increasing penetration of synthetic diamonds (considering drastic reduction in its production cost due to technological innovations), and competition from other fashion items and discretionary spending also exert -pressure on demand and profitability of the CPD segment. 

Increasing acceptance of Lab-Grown Diamond (LGD), with essentially the same chemical, optical and physical properties impacting demand for natural diamonds, continue to pose challenges to CPD especially in the 1-3 carat segment category. 

Inventory valuation is another area of concern in the industry due to high diversity of diamonds, their technical nature and lack of homogeneity. 

# **Environmental Social and Governance (ESG):** 

In its overall credit rating framework, CARE Ratings directly or indirectly analyses critical ESG risks and their impact on the credit profile of entities in the CPD segment. CARE Ratings analyses materiality of ESG risk factors and mitigating factors being implemented by the entity. 

# **Environmental Risk:** 

Environmental risk is factored in credit risk assessment of polluting sectors, where the expected cost to be incurred towards mitigants in the form of pollution control certifications and effluent treatment measures, among others, and impact of those on future cash flows is evaluated. Processing rough diamonds by CPD players is not energy intensive and has no impact on the environment as it does not emit pollutants, hence, the environmental aspect is not relevant in case of processing rough CPD. However, the impact is prevalent in upstream segment, relating to the risk from mining activities. 

# **Social Risk:** 

CPD entities faces social risks related to labour related risk. Compliance with labour laws and adequate safety for workers provide long-term sustainability. 

# **Governance Risk:** 

CARE Ratings looks at the composition of the board of directors and track record of legal and statutory compliance by its management/ directors to understand the corporate governance structure. Quality of disclosure related to financial statements, related party transactions and transparency in sharing information with stakeholders are also helpful to gauge corporate governance practices.
