# **Overview:** 

Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) are vehicles established to invest primarily in income-generating real estate assets by pooling capital from multiple investors. The REIT structure provides investors an exposure to real estate asset classes and steady dividend income, while it provides an opportunity for sponsors to monetise assets and raise capital through a minimum equity stake. The concept of REITs was initially introduced in India in 2008 by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI). Subsequently, regulations governing REITs were finalised in 2014, which laid the framework for REITs in India. These regulations were later amended several times to further encourage the launch of REITs in India. Per regulations, REITs must be established as a Trust and registered with SEBI, with a three-tier structure consisting of Sponsor (or promoter), Manager, and a Trustee. The Sponsor is responsible for setting up the REIT, appointing the manager to handle operations, and selecting the trustee to oversee its activities. 

In India, REITs typically invest in revenue-generating commercial real estate properties such as office buildings, shopping malls, data centres, hotels/resorts and convention centres, among others. Underlying assets are held by REITs either directly, through Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs), or through an Intermediate Holding Company. 

REITs’ debt structure in India is designed to balance leverage and financial stability. Debt is raised through debt instruments such as bank loans, certificates of deposit, commercial paper, bonds, debentures and structured debt instruments. REITs provide additional transparency and liquidity in the Indian real estate market. SEBI has further introduced a new category of REITs called small-and-medium REITs (SM REITs).

# **Scope:** 

The rating of consolidated debt at REIT level is an opinion on its debt repayment capability in a timely manner. The rating neither signifies returns to unitholders nor does it signify potential yield levels to unitholders and should not be construed as a rating of the REIT’s units. It is also not an indicator of individual credit quality of assets, which are part of the REIT’s portfolio. Ratings neither predict a specific level or range of performance of a REIT (measured in terms of the net asset value or otherwise) over a given period nor do they opine on suitability or otherwise of a REIT, for investment or other purposes. Finally, it is to be noted that REIT rating is not a measure on the adequacy of market value or address the extent to which trust expenses and costs may reduce distributions to unitholders. 

# **Important features of REIT (listed):** 

- a. The REIT may invest in properties through a holding company (Holdco), in which it holds at least 50% equity share capital, subject to the ultimate holding interest of the REIT in the underlying SPV(s) is not less than 26%. 

- b. REITs/Holdco to hold controlling stake of not less than 50% equity share capital or interest in underlying SPVs. 

- c. The REIT shall have the right to appoint majority board of directors in underlying SPVs.

- d. Mandatory distribution of at least 90% of the net distributable cash flows by SPVs and 100% of the net distributable cash flows by Holdco to REIT. 

- e. Aggregate consolidated borrowings and deferred payments of the REIT, net of cash and cash equivalents, must not exceed 49% of REIT asset value. 

- f. The sponsor must hold at least 15% of the total units of the REIT, on a post-issue basis, for three years from the date of listing. 

- g. At least 80% of the REIT’s asset value shall be invested in completed and rent generating real estate properties. REITs can invest up to 20% in under-construction assets. 

- h. External credit rating is required, if consolidated debt and deferred payments net of cash and cash equivalents exceeds 25% of the asset value. 

- i. All related party transactions to be conducted on an arm’s length basis. 

- j. A full valuation is required to be conducted by the valuer not less than once every financial year and within three months from the end of financial year and no valuer shall undertake valuation of the same project for over four years consecutively. A half-yearly valuation of REIT’s assets shall be conducted by the valuer for the half-year ending September 30 for a publicly offered REIT. This valuation report must be prepared within 45 days from the end of half-year and should mention significant changes in the previous six months. 

# **Responsibilities of Investment Manager as per SEBI guidelines** 

a. The investment manager must make the investment decisions with respect to underlying assets or projects of the REIT including further investment or divestment of assets. 

- b. The manager shall ensure that investments made by the REIT are in accordance with investment conditions specified and in accordance with the REIT’s investment strategy. 

- c. The manager shall undertake the REIT assets management including lease management, maintenance of the assets, regular structural audits, and regular safety audits, among others, either directly or through the appointment and supervision of appropriate agents. 

- d. The investment manager must ensure that the REIT’s real estate assets or SPV have proper legal titles, if applicable, and that all material contracts including rental or lease agreements entered on behalf of REITs or SPV are legal, valid, binding and enforceable. 

- e. The investment manager must appoint valuers, auditor, registrar and transfer agent, merchant banker, custodian and other intermediary or service provider or agent as may be applicable with respect to activities pertaining to the REIT in a timely manner. 

- f. The manager shall appoint an auditor for not over five consecutive years. 

- g. The manager shall arrange for adequate insurance coverage for the real estate assets of the REIT. 

- h. If the REIT invests in under-construction properties, the manager may undertake development of properties, either directly or through the SPV, or appoint other person for development of such properties and shall oversee the progress of development, approval status and other aspects of the properties up to its completion. 

- i. The manager shall ensure that it has adequate infrastructure and sufficient key personnel with adequate experience and qualification to undertake management of the REIT at all times. 

- j. The manager shall be responsible for dealing with all matters relating to issue and listing units of the REIT. k. The manager shall ensure that computation of net asset value (NAV) of the REIT is based on the valuation done by the valuer and is declared no later than fifteen days from the date of valuation (shall be disclosed

# **Management/Sponsor Evaluation:** 

Per SEBI guidelines, REIT managers should have at least five years of experience in fund management/ advisory services/property management in the real estate industry or in development of real estate. The project manager’s experience is vital for smooth functioning of the REIT and maintenance of adequate internal controls. Accordingly, it is important to examine whether the management team is experienced and well-qualified to handle the REIT’s day-to-day operations. The management team’s ability in decision making, ability to execute their vision and strategic plans and ability to handle risk (including asset acquisition/diversification & fund raising) inherent in operating a REIT, are considered in the rating process. 

CARE Ratings also endeavour to conduct the due diligence on investment manager and its key managerial personnel (KMP) to identify potential ramifications on REITs operations arising from litigations, observations, and historical issues among others. 

# **Operational Risk Assessment** 

The REIT’s operational risk assessment broadly involves evaluating factors such as property location, asset type, construction standards, tenant quality, and occupancy levels. These factors directly impact rental income stability and asset performance, which are critical for assessing the REIT's ability to generate consistent cash flow. The type of property (office space, mall or hotel), in which, the REIT has invested is assessed based on its specific risk

profile. The framework for evaluating the business risk profile of REIT assets is primarily based on the approach outlined in the methodology titled ‘Rating Methodology - Debt Backed by Lease Rentals Discounting’. Key parameters considered in the analysis are covered below in detail: 

- **a. Location, type of asset and quality of properties –** Marketability of REIT’s properties, and hence, strength of its cash flows is likely to be driven by its geographic location, property quality and proximity to key infrastructure. A well-diversified asset portfolio, spanning multiple geographies, with no single asset dominating cash flows and minimal receivable concentration from one tenant or counterparty, is viewed positively. Diversifying through retail assets, such as malls and hotels, strengthens the REIT's credit profile, helping it weather cash flow disruptions in case of reduced demand from a specific sector. The number of properties added to the portfolio over time is also assessed to evaluate the REIT’s capacity for future portfolio expansion. 

Quality of a building including age, size and state of maintenance is also critical as it helps in evaluating the property’s ability to retain existing tenants and attract new tenants. 

- **b. Tenants profile, occupancy levels & evaluation of lease agreements –** REIT’s ability to lease out properties to established corporations across diversified business sectors is analysed. Accordingly, number of tenants added over the years and overall occupancy levels in the last three years is assessed. CARE Ratings evaluates terms of lease agreements such as lease tenure, lock-in period, renewal of lease, security deposits, notice period, and rental escalations, among others. Long-term leases provide stability to the portfolio income and timely escalation of rent provides scope for increase in the income. For assessing lease renewal risk, CARE Ratings looks at weighted average lease expiry (WALE) against average maturity of outstanding debt. 

CARE Ratings also evaluates the tenants’ profile by factoring tenant concentration, industry concentration, lease terms, and track record of timely rental payments. Credit quality of top tenants is evaluated to determine certainty of committed rental inflows from such tenants. Favourable credit quality of tenants indicates stability of rental inflows and a higher likelihood that lease agreements will be honoured throughout the lease term. 

- **c. Demand-supply outlook** - The demand-supply outlook for a property’s micro-market is assessed by considering several factors, including prevailing vacancy levels, anticipated supply additions in the near-tomedium term, the location’s appeal to potential tenants, and growth in leasing rates. These factors are essential to consider, as different micro-markets within a city can have varying demand-supply outlook. 

Development and acquisition pipeline of a REIT is considered. As REITs are allowed to invest 20% of their asset portfolio in developing properties, they are exposed to project execution risk. Analysis of such projects involves reviewing stage of construction, funding risk and regulatory risk in terms of obtaining required approvals. Delays in getting approvals often hamper progress of projects per envisaged schedule. Other factors such as pre-leasing, locational advantage, funding tie-ups are also evaluated while understanding the credit impact of under-construction asset.

**Financial Risk Analysis –** Financial risk analysis includes following factors: 

- **a. Liquidity -** REIT’s consolidated cash flow position and working capital lines are assessed by CARE Ratings to ensure that there is adequate liquid cushion in terms of cash and credit facilities to meet short-term obligations including interest and principal payments of debt, expenditure for maintenance of properties and dividend payments. Timing and quantum of debt repayments is important, and hence, a well-staggered debt amortisation schedule is considered as positive from liquidity perspective. Inclusion of an escrow and waterfall mechanism in the debt structure provides comfort regarding the prioritisation of external debt servicing. CARE Ratings notes the presence of a debt service reserve account (DSRA) ensuring timely debt repayment. 

- **b. Leverage, capital structure, debt financing and terms of sanction-** REIT’s ability to retain sizeable liquidity/surplus is restricted by the regulation of compulsory distribution of dividends to the tune of 90% of net distributable cash flows. Consequently, majority acquisition and development growth are financed through additional equity or debt. Aggregate consolidated borrowings and deferred payments of the REIT, net of cash and cash equivalents shall not exceed 49% of the value of REIT assets per regulation. Such debt can be raised at either at the trust level or at the SPV level. At SPV level, LRD loans or CF loans are taken for undertaking capex, among others. Unsecured loans may be infused by REITs. REIT at trust level raises debt and infuses funds in SPV, generally long term in nature. CARE Ratings takes cognisance of loans at REIT and SPV level. 

REITs’ ability to repay its debt depend on its ability to raise funds in a timely manner and refinancing capability of underlying asset, particularly for debt with large bullet repayment.  A REIT may adopt one of the many options for reducing/refinancing its debt including disposal of assets, raise funds in debt or equity markets, and avail mortgage financing among others. Its ability to exercise these options will depend on prevailing market conditions, debt to market capitalisation, asset quality and the status (encumbered or unencumbered) of the assets. CARE Ratings takes into cognisance the important financial and non-financial covenants, with assessment of potential implication of critical financial covenant breach. 

- **c. Cash flow and earnings–** Similar to the case of a real estate entity, cash flow analysis is crucial while evaluating REITs. Cash inflows are affected by factors such as quality of assets, lease structures, tenant quality, occupancy rates, prospects for rental growth, borrowings, equity issuance and asset sales. Cash outflows include operating costs, asset maintenance, taxes, asset acquisitions, dividend payments and debt repayments. An REIT’s ability to generate high and consistent returns with revenue growth is analysed. These are reflected in the operating margins, and earnings growth rate. The REIT's ability to cover its debt obligations through cash flow generation is a key factor assessed in the rating process. In this respect, CARE Ratings analyse coverage ratios such as debt/rental, debt/Profit before Interest, Lease Rentals, Depreciation and Taxation (PBILDT), and cash coverage ratio. CARE Ratings considers a consolidated approach while assessing cash flow adequacy, considering pooling benefit available to REITs through mandatory surplus distribution by SPVs, strong control exerted by common management in operations of underlying entities, and presence of cross default clauses.  CARE Ratings also analyses the risk of underconstruction property (arising from cost overrun, among others) on REIT’s cash flows. 

CARE Ratings takes into consideration addition/ disposal of assets in the portfolio, and if it is in adherence with stated investment & leverage policy/guidance. In this context, related party transactions are also examined to understand if the asset addition involves acquisition of assets from related parties particularly

# **Environmental, Social and Governance risks** 

In the last few years, environmental, social and governance (ESG) risks have been gaining importance across the globe and are increasingly influencing investment decisions. Companies may have to incur operational or capital costs towards mitigating these risks. CARE Ratings analyses the impact of ESG risks on an entity’s credit profile by assessing the expected impact of these costs on future earnings/revenue/cash flows of entities. 

<u>Environmental risk: Environmental risks tend to be high for real estate as construction activities stem out</u> environmental issues. Efforts taken by the REIT towards a healthy environment such as fostering eco-friendly buildings, increasing the amount of green space or using environmentally compatible energy sources, water conservation, and waste management among others is factored in the analysis. The assessment would include an evaluation of cash-flow implications to develop strategies/initiatives to address these risks, spend on the product design to mitigate environmental risks and cost of processes to recycle, reuse and safely dispose waste. The assessment would also include risk of levy of fines/penalties for not complying with regulatory norms with respect to the environment. 

<u>Social risk: REITs undertaking construction of assets could face social risks associated with labour disputes, working</u> conditions. Strikes, labor shortages, or regulatory changes impacting labor rights can disrupt operations and affect cash flows. For completed assets, accountability towards customers, social responsibilities, including creating a holistic workplace, and providing healthier and safer work environment, among others need to be measured in terms of their short-to-medium term impact on reputation, consequently translating into impact on future earnings and cash flows. 

<u>Governance risk: Governance related risks arise from framework, regulations and procedures that guide how an</u> organisation is managed. SEBI has laid down responsibilities of different parties of the REIT, including sponsor, trustee and investment manager to look after interests of unitholders and making clear distinction from the management’s operations. The analysis further includes assessing board composition, the level of extent of management oversight in day-to-day operations of assets, transparency in disclosure of governance issues, quality of financial reporting, complexity of organisation/group structure including related party transactions, noncompliance-related issues or fines/ penalties in the past and changes in systems to mitigate such risk in future. 

# **Conclusion:** 

REIT is a vehicle to invest in income generating real estate assets. REITs are governed by SEBI guidelines. These guidelines have been revised over time to attract interest of investors and recently unlisted private REITs have been exempted from restriction on leverage and mandatory distribution. The rating outcome is ultimately an assessment of credit quality of portfolio of assets housed under REIT, stability of cash flows and debt protection metrics, refinancing risk, financial flexibility and management risk.
