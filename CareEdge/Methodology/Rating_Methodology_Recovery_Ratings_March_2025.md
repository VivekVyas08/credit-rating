# **Background** 

Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest (SARFAESI) Act, 2002 was enacted to facilitate the lenders to recover their non-performing assets (NPAs) without the intervention of the court. The SARFAESI Act provides three alternative methods for recovery of the NPAs, namely, securitisation, asset reconstruction and enforcement of security without the intervention of the court. This facilitates resolution of longdrawn legal matters in recovery cases and involves professional expertise by way of involving the securitisation companies (SCs) / asset reconstruction companies (ARCs) in the recovery process. However, the recovery process has evolved over the past few years, especially after the enactment of The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC). The IBC consolidated the earlier laws related to insolvency with an aim to resolve insolvencies in a time-bound manner. 

Security Receipts (SRs) are receipts or securities issued under the SARFAESI Act by the SCs / ARCs (against the acquired financial assets) to the qualified buyers, after acquisition of the distressed assets from the lenders. The RBI has issued guidelines for declaration of the net asset value (NAV) on these SRs issued by the trusts formed by the SCs / ARCs to enable the qualified buyers (financial institutions, insurance companies, banks, state finance corporations, state industrial development corporations, trustees or ARCs, etc.) to value their investments in the SRs in accordance with the applicable guidelines. According to the guidelines issued by the RBI, the SRs are required to be rated on a defined recovery rating scale (given below), and this recovery rating is to be used for arriving at the NAV.

Recovery rating is based on the ‘recovery risk (probability of recovery)’ and not the ‘default risk (probability of default)’. The value of the underlying asset and its recoverability is of prime importance in case of recovery rating, whereas cash flow adequacy vis-à-vis the debt obligations holds significance in case of credit rating, amongst various other risk parameters. Recovery rating assigned to the SRs is based on the net present value (NPV) of the estimated cash flows from the resolution strategy during the resolution period, stated as a percentage of the outstanding face value of the SRs. Recovery rating assigned to the SRs is essentially based on the probability of recovery from the assigned loans within the time frame of five years (which may be further extended by three years after the approval from the Board of Directors of the SC / ARC) as per the RBI guidelines. Recovery ratings are reviewed bi-annually as per the RBI guidelines. Upon expiry of the resolution period, the asset is to be declared as a ‘loss asset’ by the SC /

# **Rating methodology** 

CareEdge Ratings considers various qualitative as well as quantitative parameters while assigning recovery ratings. The expected recovery towards the SRs would depend upon various parameters related to the underlying NPAs, including the resolution strategy adopted by the SC / ARC, valuation of the underlying assets, track record of recovery, order of payments, etc. These parameters are discussed below: 

- **Reason for delinquency** 

CareEdge Ratings attempts to delve into the reasons due to which the account turned into an NPA and accordingly, assesses the suitability of the resolution strategy adopted by the SC / ARC. 

- **Resolution strategy** 

The resolution strategy adopted by the SC / ARC, i.e., sale of assets, one-time settlement, settlement through scouting buyers for the assets, restructuring of the business / management, etc., and any change in the resolution strategy is considered while arriving at recovery ratings. 

# **<u>Sale of assets</u>** 

- **Nature and composition of the underlying assets / collateral** 

The composition of the underlying assets in terms of movability, location, age, etc., are considered while assessing the recovery prospects. Immovable assets like land, building, etc., have better recovery prospects while the assets like stock, plant and machinery, etc. depreciate over time and hence, have relatively weaker recovery prospects. 

- **Valuation of the underlying assets** 

CareEdge Ratings relies upon the independent valuation reports for arriving at valuation of the underlying assets. The valuation reports mention the distressed market value (DMV) and the fair market value (FMV) of the assets. CareEdge Ratings generally considers the DMV for the purpose of its valuation as the assets in consideration may involve various legal issues and thus are generally sold on an ‘as is where is’ basis which may deter the investors to buy the assets at the FMV. The value and other aspects mentioned in the valuation reports like location of the asset, marketability, possession status, occupancy status, etc., are also considered while arriving at recovery ratings. 

# `o` **Status of possession of the underlying assets** 

CareEdge Ratings considers physical possession or symbolic possession of the underlying assets positively whereas the assets which are not in possession of the SC / ARC may lead to delay in recovery and hence, are considered negatively.

# **<u>One-time settlement</u>** 

- **Terms of settlement and track record of payments as per the agreed terms** Terms of settlement agreed upon are considered and track record of payments made is seen to assess whether they are in accordance with the agreed terms. The estimated cash flows are accordingly drawn up to arrive at the expected recovery during the resolution period. 

- **Probability of meeting the payments as per the agreed terms** 

The strategy of ‘one-time settlement’ is generally adopted when the business of the underlying entity is generating adequate cash flows as a going concern. As such, the past operational track record of the entity is factored in to gauge the probability of the entity being able to meet the agreed-upon terms. 

# **<u>Sale of business or management / business restructuring</u>** 

- **Probability of sale / restructuring materialising** 

This resolution strategy is adopted when the business can potentially be sold in its entirety to a strategic investor willing to revive the business or when the business can be restructured to make it financially viable. CareEdge Ratings considers a higher probability of recovery if the sale deal / restructuring is in its final stages. Also, under this resolution strategy the estimated cash flows are drawn considering the relevant business and financial risk parameters. 

   - **Financial position of the strategic investor / experience of the new management** If the prospective buyer is financially a strong entity or the new management is experienced in the given line of business, the probability of turnaround of the business and thus of higher recovery is greater.

# **Seniority of payments to the SR holders / seniority of debt** 

The order of payments is factored in while arriving at the expected recovery proceeds available for redemption of the SRs.

# **Extent of debt acquired and composition of the lenders** 

CareEdge Ratings considers the extent of debt aggregated / acquired by the concerned SC / ARC. Higher the debt aggregated by the SC / ARC, better are the recovery prospects as the procedural delays with respect to acquiring approval of the other secured creditors (both individual as well as institutional) in various matters are reduced, if the concerned SC / ARC holds over 60% of the total debt of the distressed asset under the SARFAESI Act. 

- **Time elapsed from the assignment of the loan** 

Track record of recovery during the recovery process gives an idea about the recovery prospects. Cases where there is uncertainty in realising the estimated cash flows in the initial period or where significant time has elapsed from the start of the recovery process and where the recovery pace has been slow are viewed negatively, as it hints at weak recovery prospects. 

- **Legal hurdles** 

CareEdge Ratings relies upon the discussion with the SC / ARC or details mentioned in the valuation reports to assess the possibility of legal hurdles in the recovery process and appropriately considers the same in the assessment.

# **Arriving at ‘Recovery Ratings’** 

On the basis of the above factors, CareEdge Ratings assumes the recovery prospects and works out the estimated cash flows during the resolution period based on the adopted resolution strategy. The estimated cash flows are worked out for the resolution period after drawing the assumptions on the recovery expenses, statutory dues, legal fees, management fees, etc. CareEdge Ratings assigns a probability to the assumed recovery, based on the nature of the underlying asset, probable legal hurdles, status of possession of the underlying assets, etc. CareEdge Ratings then arrives at the NPV of these estimated cash flows during the resolution period. CareEdge Ratings uses an indicative yield as the discounting rate for valuation of the SRs. The discounting rate is based on the prevailing interest rate scenario and an appropriate risk premium. Recovery rating assigned to an SR is based on the NPV of the estimated cash flows, stated as a percentage of the outstanding face value of the SR. 

# **Methodology for rating pooled assets** 

At times, the SC / ARC bundles more than one loan from different accounts into a portfolio and issues SRs common to the whole portfolio. For arriving at recovery ratings for the SRs issued under such portfolios, an assessment of all individual assets or at least top assets in the portfolio forming 75-80% of the expected recovery by value is carried out in the same manner as the assessment for individual assets elaborated in the above section.
