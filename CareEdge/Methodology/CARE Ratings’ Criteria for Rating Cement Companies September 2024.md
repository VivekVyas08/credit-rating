**CARE Ratings’ Criteria for Rating Cement Companies** 

# **Background** 

India is the world’s second-largest cement producer after China and the second-largest cement consumer globally. The cement industry, part of the manufacturing sector, plays a pivotal role in the infrastructure development of the country. The industry growth rate is positively correlated with the country’s gross domestic product (GDP) growth rate. Though the industry is highly cyclical, it is largely insulated against global demand-supply cycles and volatility in international prices, unlike other commodities. 

# **Methodology** 

CARE Ratings Limited (CARE Ratings) follows a detailed methodology for rating of companies belonging to the manufacturing sector. The rating process of CARE Ratings begins with an evaluation of the economy/industry in which the company operates, followed by an assessment of business risk factors specific to the company. This is followed by an assessment of financial and project-related risk factors and the quality of the management. The manufacturing sector methodology is applicable while analysing all industries coming under the purview of the manufacturing sector. However, considering the size and diversity of the manufacturing sector, CARE Ratings has developed methodologies specific to industries within the sector. These methodologies attempt to point out factors, over and above those mentioned in the broad ‘manufacturing sector methodology’. Additional factors and analytical implications considered by CARE Ratings while arriving at ratings in the cement industry are mentioned below. 

# **1. Industry Risk** 

# **Regional demand-supply dynamics and cyclical nature of industry:** 

Cement, being a low-value, bulky commodity, is unviable to transport across the country due to high logistic costs. Therefore, the domestic cement industry is divided into six geographical regions, North (Jammu & Kashmir, Punjab, Delhi, and Rajasthan, among others), South (Telangana, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, and Tamil Nadu, among others), East (Odisha, Jharkhand, Bihar, and West Bengal, among others), West (Gujarat, and Maharashtra, and others), Central (Uttar Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh), and North East, each region characterised by its own demand-supply dynamics. The industry is influenced by regional rather than national demand-supply dynamics. Likewise, the industry is relatively insulated from global demandsupply and pricing dynamics with negligible imports and modest exports. Therefore, while rating an entity, CARE Ratings focuses on assessing the likely demand-supply scenario in the region in which the entity operates. Moreover, due consideration is also given to the degree of consolidation existing in the region(s), as that determines the extent of supply rationalisation and the cement price volatility in that region. 

Indian cement industry is exposed to cyclicality in end-user industries. This is accentuated by the bunching of capacity additions leading to uneven capacity additions which drives regional demand-supply imbalances. The consequent periods of unutilised capacities and depressed realisations may weaken the financial profile of players in that region. Hence, CARE Ratings factors into its analysis the expected capacity additions, which could lead to demand-supply imbalances in the regions of operations of the rated entity.  The industry is also affected by seasonality, demand is generally low in the monsoon season and the festival period and peaks towards the second half of financial year as governments’ expenditures rise.

# **Government policies and actions:** 

There is no direct role of the government in regulating the cement industry. However, government plays an important role in creating demand through economic policies and infrastructure projects. 

The cement industry is exposed to the risks related to regulations governing the land acquisition, mining rights, environmental clearances, and auction of limestone and coal mines. The industry is also susceptible to government/statutory actions such as temporary ban on region-specific construction activities in environmental extremities such as poor air quality, water scarcity, and floods among others, restriction on usage of pet coke, price freeze pact, and others. 

# **2. Business and operations risk:** 

# **A. Market/Competitive position** 

# **Market position and size:** 

Size is an important determinant of a company’s market position, its revenue-generating capability, and its resilience to shocks, such as sudden shifts in demand or rapid cost increases. The size of operations for cement companies is measured in terms of their capacity and sales volumes. Large companies generally have more flexibility to allocate capacity and absorb expenses under different demand and cost scenarios and are viewed favourably by CARE Ratings. CARE Ratings also understands the regional presence of the rated company, largely classified as single region player, presence in more than one region or pan-India presence. Each region has different demand and supply dynamics impacting sales volume and realisation. 

A company’s market position is a meaningful indicator of its resilience to economic downturns and intensifying competition. Entities which have a dominant market position and geographically diversified capacities are usually better placed to cope with the regional demand-supply volatility and hence are considered favourably by CARE Ratings. 

CARE Ratings analyses infrastructure projects and real estate development in each region of presence. This allows forecast of demand growth in the area of presence against the available capacity in the company to cater to the market. Any delays in projects or slowdown in real estate development adversely impacts the volumes sold by the company. 

# **Brand strength and trade mix** 

Cement industry has significantly consolidated in the last 10-15 years. The product differentiation is negligible across brands as it is of commoditised nature. Hence, the consumers’ choices are largely driven by prices. However, amidst this, the established brands keep enhancing their brand awareness among the consumers to increase their brand recall, which also allows premium pricing and higher brand equity. This is largely targeted towards the individual customers and hence stronger brand strength may lead to higher trade mix (B2C) and resultantly higher volume of blended cement sales. 

# **B. Operating efficiency/ Cost competitiveness** 

The operating margin provides indications of a company’s competitive strength and cost efficiency. One of the key metrics to measure a cement entity’s operational efficiency is operating profits (profit before interest, lease rentals, depreciation, and taxation [PBILDT]) per tonne of cement sold. The value of this parameter depends on the net cement realisation and the cost structure of an entity. Realisations largely depend on the market demand-supply dynamics. In a consolidated market with cement being a commoditised product, cost competitiveness has been the key differentiator among the cement

Greater emphasis has been given to different cost components ranging from supply chain and internal operating efficiency to last-mile delivery. Due to vagaries of input costs, the PBILDT per tonne is observed for 3-5 years, which captures significant cyclicality present in the cement industry. 

CARE Ratings assesses the following factors to determine an entity’s cost structure: 

- **Locational advantage (proximity to raw material sources, distance from end-markets):** In most cases, cement production plants are near limestone mines (raw material source) which are generally far away from the consumption centres. Most of the players have increasingly established split grinding facilities closer to the end market to optimise their freight costs. CARE Ratings favourably views companies having split location plants whereby clinkerisation plants are near limestone reserves and grinding units are in proximity to end-user market. By adopting this strategy, companies can increase their market reach and also optimise their freight costs as clinker can be transported in open wagons/trucks. 

CARE Ratings also examines the long-term mining rights for limestone reserves that the company possesses with respect to the adequacy of reserves for the company’s current and future operations. Apart from royalty and lease rent, the limestone cost also depends on its availability at surface level, quality and transport charges to the plant location. 

- **Freight cost:** 

Transportation of cement over long distances and freight cost accounting for about 24%-27% of the operating cost are among the largest expenses for a cement company. The average market radius (lead distance) to which the company caters is analysed. Cement can be transported through rail, road, or sea routes. Rail transport is cheaper than road transport if cement is transported over long distances and in bulk. Also, companies benefit if they have their own wagons and railway sidings. Transporting cement over short distances by road is advantageous, as it does not involve secondary freight and handling costs. Road transport accounts for a major share of the total cement movement in the country. CARE Ratings analyses the transport mode used by the companies for dispatching cement and favourably views companies using a judicious mix of the same to reduce the freight cost. 

- **Power and fuel cost:** 

In addition to freight costs, power and fuel cost is one of the key cost components accounting for about 25%-35% of the operating cost. The cost of captive power can be much lower than grid power if such a plant is working on low cost and easily available fuels. However, with elevated coal prices, focus has been more on green power mix of the company, waste heat recovery system (WHRS), solar power, and wind power. Companies with higher green energy mix benefit from lower power cost. Coal and pet coke are the most common fuels used to operate kilns. Usage of Alternative Fuels and Raw Materials (AFR) for kiln is also observed in terms of its Thermal Substitution Rate. Companies with established coal linkages are viewed favourably. CARE Ratings assesses the means of coal and petcoke procurement (imports, domestic, e-auction) to analyse the adequacy of the fuel supply arrangements for a rated entity. Cement companies with firm domestic coal linkages are in a better position compared to companies whose reliance on imports and e-auctions is more. Apart from cost, an uninterrupted supply of quality power is also essential for better-operating efficiency. To optimise the power availability and its cost, companies have resorted to fulfilling their captive

- **Plant vintage and capacity utilisation:** 

On an overall operating cost basis, new cement plants have an edge over the older plants. The capacity utilisation of an entity in comparison with the peers in its region of operations is another important factor which is analysed by CARE Ratings given the high fixed cost nature of the industry. CARE Ratings favourably considers higher capacity utilisation, as this results in a better cost structure. 

- **Product mix:** 

CARE Ratings analyses the product mix of rated entities which would comprise Ordinary Portland Cement (OPC) and blended cement, including Portland Pozzolana Cement (PPC), Portland Slag Cement (PSC) and Portland Blast Furnace Slag Cement (PBFSC). Blended cement such as PPC, PSC, and PBFSC enjoy majority of the market share currently as they have more comprehensive strength, are more environment friendly, have wider applications and have lower production costs as the limestone requirement is low for blended cement and this also results in cost savings concerning freight and power. PPC allows a manufacturer to produce more cement using the same amount of limestone and clinker capacities. In this context, it is also important to check the proximity of the entity’s plants to the supply of fly ash and slag as these are integral constituents. Therefore, CARE Ratings analyses the product mix of rated entities and its overall impact on the company’s profitability besides factoring in its efforts to shift the product mix in favour of blended cement. 

# **3. Project risk:** 

Cement players have to periodically increase their capacities to maintain or increase their market shares and geographical diversification. This may be achieved through debottlenecking, brownfield, or greenfield expansions or by doing acquisitions. CARE Ratings analyses these plans of the rated companies based on the size of expansion in relation to the companies’ current capacity or net worth, region in which capacity expansion is being undertaken and funding pattern of the expansion and its impact on the company’s debt profile in the projected period. Apart from the capacity expansion, the companies in current situations may undertake capex for improvement in operational efficiencies, increasing green power mix, and modifying kilns to use AFR, among others. The impact of project risk on credit rating depends on a study of the below factors which may vary considerably from case to case. 

- **Timing expansion against demand-supply scenario:** 

The demand-supply situation, although important for cyclical commodity-based industry, assumes more importance for Cement due to the capital-intensive nature and long gestation period for setting up a new plant. In case of a downturn, companies having project-related debt repayment obligations could find it difficult to service debt as both estimated cash accruals and fund-raising ability deteriorate. The companies facing a downturn immediately post-expansion or in an expansion are likely to be more at risk. CARE Ratings believes that the timing and size of the expanded capacity coming on stream is critical to the success of a cement manufacturer. CARE Ratings also assesses the risks involved in implementation, which

Since most of the greenfield projects are being set up under the assumption of the availability of limestone reserves and coal as captive sources, entities which proceed with projects without tying up the necessary captive raw materials are considered riskier. 

# **4. Financial risk:** 

CARE Ratings follows its standard ratio analysis methodology for manufacturing companies per CARE Ratings’ criteria on Financial Ratios-Nonfinancial Sector (Financial Ratios – Nonfinancial Sector for this section on our website www.careratings.com). An entity with a weak financial profile as reflected in its weak liquidity, high leverage and low-interest coverage may likely outweigh strong business and sector characteristics in assessing ratings. Apart from the general ratios, some sector-specific points considered to assess the financial risk of companies in the cement sector are as follows: 

- **Leverage and coverage:** 

Leverage ratios provide an important indication of a company’s financial flexibility and long-term viability in the analysis of a cement company due to the sector’s high capital intensity. These metrics can also provide insight into management’s philosophy regarding the company’s capital structure and how much financial risk an entity is willing to undertake. The key ratios analysed by CARE Ratings to evaluate an entity’s financial health include overall gearing (gross and even net of cash and cash equivalents), debt to PBILDT (gross and net) and interest and debt service coverage indicators  PBILDT/interest and DSCR. Low leverage and stronger coverage indicators impart greater financial flexibility to tide over economic downturns, and hence are considered more favourably by CARE Ratings. 

- **Liquidity:** 

CARE Ratings, while evaluating the liquidity of a cement entity, analyses available cash balances, ability of an entity to generate consistent levels of internal accruals, debt repayment obligations, unutilised working capital limits (by drawing a comparison between working capital utilised against lower of the sanctioned limits or drawing power), and support from the parent/group. Liquidity measures an entity’s ability to meet its obligations in the near-term (typically the next one year) from its cash accruals and external sources available to it. An entity’s obligations may be in the form of debt servicing, working capital requirements, capital expenditure plans, investment plans, dividend payments, and share buybacks, among others. Being a cyclical industry, companies in this sector usually maintain cash balances (including liquid short-term investments and cash) that are far more than their operating needs to tide over downcycles/competitive pressures. Hence, the underlying policy of the issuers of maintaining high cash balances and assessing their consistent track record in maintaining such liquidity is an important credit consideration.

# **5. Environmental, Social, and Governance Risk Factors:** 

Over the last few years, Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) risks have been gaining importance across the globe and are increasingly influencing investment decisions. Companies may have to incur operational or capital costs towards mitigating these risks. CARE Ratings analyses the impact of ESG risks on the credit profile of an entity by assessing the expected impact of these costs on the future earnings/cash flows of entities. 

The cement sector faces high risks emanating from the environmental and social factors considering producing cement is an energy intensive process and requires substantial use of fuel, which currently is fossil based. CARE Ratings has been broadly assessing the impact of several ESG factors on the overall sustainability of business operations and its financial impact on several entities across the value chains. In addition to the impact of ESG risks, CARE Ratings has also been monitoring the steps taken towards mitigating these risks in future. CARE Ratings continues to monitor these factors to understand the possible future impact of these factors on the entity’s credit profile. 

# **Environmental risk** 

Significant operating expenditure and capital expenditure may be required in terms of complying to different environmental guidelines, which also vary per location. Regarding energy consumption, several entities have already planned to increase the share of renewable energy in overall energy consumption. Cement companies  have increasingly been focusing on increasing the usage of AFR and improving the share of blended cement in their total pie and consequently reduce the clinker consumption. 

# **Social risk** 

Social risk also emanates from the mining and production activity when it comes to the health and safety of the miners and the employees involved in production activities. The sector is also exposed to labour-related risks and issues that may arise with local communities, potentially impacting the expansion plans of the entities. 

# **Governance risk** 

This involves the structure and functioning of the board, including the presence of independent directors, an investor grievance redressal system, and extensive disclosure practices. Effective and strong governance practices are essential. 

CARE Ratings assesses the impact of the steps required to mitigate the ESG risks enumerated above on the entity’s revenue/cash flows. 

# **Conclusion:** 

The rating determination is a matter of experienced and holistic judgment, based on the relevant quantitative and qualitative factors affecting the credit quality of the issuer. 

CARE Ratings analyses each of the above factors and their linkages to arrive at the overall assessment of credit quality. Credit rating is a futuristic assessment, and the rating outcome is ultimately an assessment of the fundamentals and probabilities of change in the fundamentals in future. To arrive at the rating outcome, CARE Ratings also considers future estimation of the company’s financials based on past trends and future strategies, competition, industry trends, economic conditions, and other considerations.
