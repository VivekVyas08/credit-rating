# **Industry Overview** 

The Commercial Vehicle (CV) Industry is the barometer of the economy, facilitating the movement of goods and passengers across the country. About two-third of goods and 85%-90% passenger traffic in the country moves through road. The demand for commercial vehicles (CVs) in India is closely linked to the country's economic performance, particularly the gross domestic product (GDP) growth rate and the index of industrial production (IIP). This correlation makes CV demand a valuable lead indicator for predicting economic trends. 

Commercial vehicles (CVs) can be categorised based on their usage in goods carriers and passenger carriers. Goods carriers make up ~85%-90% of the total CV sales volume and display notable cyclicality in demand. CVs can be classified based on their gross vehicular weight (GVW) as light commercial vehicles (LCV) and medium & heavy commercial vehicles (M&HCV) with M&HCVs accounting for between 35%-40% of the total domestic CV sales volume. Currently, the industry is dominated by the trucks (goods carrier) segment, whereas buses (passenger carriers) account for less than 15% of the industry volumes. 

The CV industry has evolved over the years: from the days of traditional all-purpose 9-tonne trucks, the industry has moved towards more utility-specific vehicles with higher tonnage and multi-axle vehicles. The fast improving road infrastructure is giving a push to a modern hub and spoke model of distribution of goods, which is changing the kind of vehicles being deployed for goods transportation. The increased presence of international OEMs in CV industry has intensified the emphasis on future development, alternative powertrains, technological partnerships, and R&D initiatives. 

The industry is now witnessing a clear segmentation in demand, with vehicles over 16.2 tonnes (HCVs and multi axle vehicles) being used for transportation on the highways and less than = 3.5 tonnes being used for intra-city transport. Similarly, in case of passenger vehicles (buses), there is an increasing demand for luxury buses from the private players unlike earlier when the demand used to be largely driven by the state road transport undertakings (SRTUs). The demand for electric buses is rapidly increasing, driven by government initiatives and the push for cleaner transportation options. 

# **Characteristics** 

The CV industry draws its demand from the economy and hence is prone to cyclicality. However, due to greater versatility of usage, the LCV demand is relatively less cyclical than the M&HCV demand. 

The industry is capital intensive but with CV manufacturers moving towards increased ancillarisation, the initial capital outlay for setting up a greenfield plant has decreased substantially. Instead, manufacturers are now directing significant effort towards development and rationalisation of vendors. The industry is also highly susceptible to technological changes. 

With environmental norms becoming stricter and buyers demanding better technology vehicles, it has become imperative for the manufacturers to either have technology partners or a strong in-house R&D. The need of having a wide product range in each of the segments and the short lifecycle of these vehicles makes it mandatory for the

# **Demand drivers** 

The demand for commercial vehicles (CVs) has been driven by several key factors in recent times. A vibrant economy and extensive road development programs have significantly boosted CV demand. Changes in distribution models and supportive government regulations have further fueled the demand. While the expansion of the rail network and establishment of rail freight corridors impact road freight demand, particularly for long-haul routes due to shorter delivery times, the high cost of Indian rail freight per ton-km and overall door-to-door delivery costs make road freight the preferred mode of transport for most users. Significant investments in infrastructure projects, such as road construction and urban development, drive the need for heavy commercial vehicles (HCVs) and construction equipment. The rapid growth of e-commerce has led to a substantial increase in demand for light commercial vehicles (LCVs) for last-mile delivery. The mandatory scrappage policy for older vehicles is driving replacement demand, particularly for SRTUs and private fleet operators. Easy availability of vehicle financing, a wide product range, extensive distribution and service center networks, and the availability of spares are crucial factors for the buyers. Factors such as vehicle price, application, and level of technology also influence customer preferences. These elements collectively shape the demand for commercial vehicles in India.

# **Business/Operational risk** 

# **Market position** 

The market position of a commercial vehicle (CV) OEM is primarily determined by its sales volumes and scale in the industry. A higher market share indicates a strong competitive position, allowing the OEM to influence pricing strategies and higher bargaining power with suppliers. Annual changes in selling prices reflect the company's ability to pass on input cost increases to customers. Therefore, evaluating the OEM's scale and positioning within the industry is crucial. Market share can fluctuate due to shifts in customer preferences, competitive intensity, new product introductions, and pricing strategies. 

The commercial vehicle industry is highly competitive, in economic downturns, a strong market share can help mitigate risks and maintain stability, as the company has a larger customer base and better market presence. CARE

# **Presence across segments and product range** 

As the CV industry draws its demand heavily from the level of economic activity in the country, the demand for products in segments and sub-segments varies significantly from time to time. Generally, LCVs experience less volatility in demand than MHCVs. Companies which have a wide array of products, not just in the segments, but also in the sub-segments are able to better withstand demand-side pressures, making them less vulnerable and would be viewed positively. In addition, presence in passenger transportation segment can act as a cushion in times of economic downturn, as this segment is not highly correlated to economic growth and hence experiences lesser volatility in demand. 

CARE Ratings believes a wide and diversified product offering across tonnage and usage segments renders the manufacturer’s ability to serve a large variety of customers and hence reduces the volatility in sales volume, which is an inherent attribute of the sector. 

# **Operating efficiency** 

Given the pronounced cyclicality in the CV industry, it is imperative that OEMs maintain a low fixed cost base to ensure high operating leverage. A typical strategy for achieving this is deploying flexible manufacturing systems that enable the production of multiple models on the same platform and in the same facility. This would allow an OEM to adapt to shift in demand patterns across different models in the same category, while keeping fixed costs to a minimum. It is crucial for companies to adapt their products—whether through pricing, design, or specifications—to align with the evolving needs of customers. An OEM also has to strive to achieve efficiencies in variable costs as well to be able to survive in an intensely competitive market. Effective inventory management, use of best-in-class machining technologies, intelligent production management with adequate integration and efficient ancillarisation and ensuring economies of scale benefits are some of the other means to reduce manufacturing costs. Overall higher operating efficiency through the curtailment of fixed and variable costs has a direct bearing on its profit before interest, lease, depreciation, and taxation (PBILDT) margin, which is closely assessed by CARE Ratings. 

# **Servicing and distribution network** 

CV purchases are largely influenced by the nature and availability of the product and value preposition.  A widespread sales and service distribution network across the country is imperative for the players to tap geographical demand. Geographically diverse presence will help generate higher sales volume, a necessity in a highly capital-intensive industry. 

Once the customer has been acquired, it is important to service the customer to minimise downtime, develop brand consciousness and build a brand loyalty. Availability of spare parts and service centres is crucial for servicing the customer and developing brand loyalty. 

CARE Ratings views a widespread distribution and service network as a key positive for players operating in the CV industry. Easy availability of genuine and affordable spare parts would serve as an added advantage in the brandbuilding exercise.

# **Technological / Research and development/ Product development capabilities** 

Changing customer preferences, rising fuel costs, growing environment and safety concerns and consequent government regulations call for employing of improved technology. With the opening up of global markets, there is a need for the domestic players to meet international technology standards. Thus, technology is the key to survival and growth in the CV industry. Domestic players will need to have strong research and development capabilities to be able to keep pace with evolving environmental and safety regulations, and strong product development capabilities to be able to introduce new models and product upgrades on regular basis. 

Global CV manufacturers operating in India have access to a large product portfolio and a product development pipeline already in place in their home countries, backed by R&D capabilities and budgets. 

CARE Ratings views strong technical capabilities together with the ability to develop vehicle models that effectively address market demands positively while analysing player in the CV industry. CARE Ratings analyses the players’ track record in terms of R&D spend, product innovation and the management’s ability to gauge the future needs of the market. 

# **Regulatory risk** 

The CV industry in India has seen significant regulatory changes over the years. The transition from BS-IV to BSVI emission norms in April 2020 was particularly impactful, reducing vehicle emissions and requiring technological upgrades in engines. This shift also led to a temporary halt in production to clear BS-IV inventory. Revised axle load norms caused a decline in MHCV demand, while other changes like the mandatory scrappage policy, enhanced safety standards (example: anti-lock braking systems), and the move towards electric vehicles (EVs) have driven advancements in technology, safety, and sustainability. Consequently, CV manufacturers must be flexible and technologically apt to adapt to these regulatory changes and shifting demand patterns. 

Large established CV companies with strong R&D capabilities and flexible manufacturing systems are inherently better placed to deal with regulatory risks, especially if their financial profiles are resilient to withstand volume disruptions due to regulatory changes. CARE Ratings would therefore view these entities favourably. 

# **Geographical diversification** 

Geographical diversification within and outside the country is preferred as it insulates the company over sales disruptions/ issues with a single location/geography. Such diversification not only helps in de-risking a single location-specific issues but also provides access to newer markets and improves reach for the products. 

Exports have traditionally accounted for a significant proportion of revenues for domestic CV manufacturers, ranging from 8% to 13% of the total CV sales volumes for FY16-FY22. An OEM which has successfully diversified its revenues through exports will be looked at more favourably given than exports (if significant) can help provide stability to the revenue stream which inherently exposed to high degree of cyclicality in the domestic market. Greater the geographic diversification and higher the proportion of export revenues, lower would be the volatility in cash flows for CV manufacturers. 

# **Level of ancillarisation**

# **Vehicle financing** 

The availability of vehicle finance at the point of purchase can foster sales. Traditionally, financing was left to the institutions for whom the business was their core competency. However, to provide complete buying assistance to the purchasers at the point of purchase, many CV manufacturers set up their own financing companies or tied-up with banks. 

CARE Ratings views the availability of vehicle financing options at the point of purchase, a key positive to improve customer servicing and fostering sales. 

# **Financial risk** 

CARE Ratings’ assessment of the overall financial risk profile of commercial vehicle manufacturers is based on financial indicators, aligned with its methodology for the credit rating of non-financial sector companies. Refer to the CARE Ratings criteria “Financial Ratios – Non Financial Sector” on our website www.careratings.com for additional details. 

# **Foreign exchange risk** 

Considering the high level of indigenisation in the domestic CV industry, imports by CV manufacturers is limited. The exposure to foreign exchange risk arises primarily from exports of CVs, a large proportion of which has traditionally been to neighbouring countries. Forex risk would arise on external commercial borrowings availed by the CV manufacturer. Such risk is analysed on a case-to-case basis after considering the company’s hedging policy. 

# **Environmental, social and governance (ESG) risk factors** 

CARE Ratings also considers the environmental, social and governance (ESG) risks that CV manufacturers are exposed to considering their operations and the wider environmental and social impact that their business activities may potentially have. 

**Environment:** CVs being inherently more polluting than other automobile categories, OEMs are under pressure to continuously upgrade their technology to minimise emissions. Stricter emission standards necessitate cleaner production processes. Companies have been investing in sustainable practices and reducing their environmental footprint. 

**Social:** Positive social impact of CV manufacturing can be assessed in terms of job creation and adherence to evolving safety standards which entails investments in additional safety features which helps to reduce the risk of accidents. OEMs have to ensure smooth manufacturing with healthy employee relationships and continuous trainings imparted to non/semi-skilled workers for disruption free operations. Companies are enhancing workplace safety, promoting diversity and inclusion, and engaging in community development initiatives.
