# **Background** 

India’s renewable energy (RE) sector has gained significant traction in the last few years with the growing concern of energy security. The country has set a target to achieve 50% cumulative installed capacity from non-fossil-fuelbased energy resources by 2030. The anticipated growth in the wind installations would be driven by central and state auctions, increased demand due to sector-based decarbonisation goals (especially in the commercial and industrial segments) and overall peak power demand, onshore wind renewable purchase obligations (RPOs), and grid stability needs. Additional drivers include the mandated minimum share of RE procurement for discoms and the ability of consumers to purchase green electricity, in addition to the waiver and the introduction of interstate transmission system charges and wind energy-specific RPOs from 2023 to 2030. However, the sector continues to face challenges, including state level issues for right of way, transmission infrastructure, integrity of power purchase agreement (PPA) and delayed payments, and land allocation. 

This criteria document covers the risks faced by players operating in the wind industry and CARE Ratings’ approach to evaluate their credit profiles. 

# **Rating methodology** 

Infrastructure assets are usually developed through special purpose vehicles (SPVs), wherein, the cash flows are ring-fenced, and the debt is non-recourse in nature. These assets have a long useful life (usually 25 years) and CARE Ratings factors in the same for its analysis. While rating an under-construction or an operational SPV, CARE Ratings assesses four broad parameters, which include examining the following: 

- Business risk 

- Industry risk 

- Financial risk 

- Management and parentage 

To get a detailed perspective on assessment of infrastructure sector, kindly refer to CARE Rating’s criteria for infrastructure sector ratings at www.careratings.com. 

# **Business risk** 

Some of the major factors determining the business risk of an RE entity are listed below. This list is not exhaustive, as there can be additional considerations impacting the entity’s credit profile. However, in CARE Ratings’ opinion, the following are the major determinants of the credit profile. The first few risks are primarily applicable when a project is in the development stage. 

- **Permitting risk** 

   - Permitting risk for a wind plant can be further subdivided into three segments: approvals for land, transmission connectivity, and statutory clearances. Land acquisition is among the first steps for setting up a wind asset and a PPA generally permits a timeline of six months for a developer to secure the land parcel. Moreover, the quantum of land required for a wind project is between 3 and 5 acre per megawatt (MW), depending upon the size of the turbines, making the overall land requirement significant. - **Construction risk** 

Construction risk refers to challenges faced by a developer to operationalise a plant. A delay in project implementation can lead to time and cost overruns and impact the project returns. Moreover, terms of a PPA generally include the liquidated damages payable by the developer in case of a delay in commissioning. It can be either through the imposition of penalties, encashment of performance bank guarantees (BGs), reduction in applicable tariffs, or a combination of these. While evaluating a project, CARE Ratings assesses the progress in terms of construction, evaluates the underlying engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC) or equipment contracts, and the original equipment manufacturer (OEM) for critical components such as turbines, the extent of penalties and liquidated damages covered in contracts, and the track record of the sponsor and the associated contractors in carrying similar projects. The techno-economic viability is also factored in while assessing a project. 

- **Funding risk** 

Infrastructure projects are capital-intensive in nature and require the mobilisation of a large amount of capital, which is primarily sourced as either debt or through the sponsors’ contribution (plain vanilla equity, quasi-equity, and other hybrid instruments) usually in the debt to equity ratio of 70:30. Therefore, CARE Ratings factors in the prevailing or envisaged funding tie-ups, the timelines of achieving financial closure, the terms of instruments, and the composition of the capital structure. PPAs with government agencies generally stipulate timelines for a greenfield project by which the company has to achieve financial closure. In CARE Ratings’ opinion, the ability of the sponsor to access the capital markets and the resourcefulness of sponsor in case of meeting cost overrun or contingencies, are viewed favourably. CARE Ratings favourably views structures wherein the developer and the off-taker belong to the same promoter group considering enhanced strategic and economic benefits. 

- **Nature and terms of PPA** 

PPA is a legal contract that provides revenue visibility and alleviates the off-take risk for a project. While analysing a project, CARE Ratings examines the PPA tie-up status and its underlying provisions, such as: 

- Tenure 

- Tariff 

- Take or pay obligation 

- Deemed generation 

- PLF floor and cap 

- Billing and payment timelines 

- Payment security mechanism 

- Compensation clauses for grid unavailability and grid curtailment 

- Events of default

The presence of a long-term PPA at a fixed tariff rate is viewed favourably, as it mitigates the risks associated with both, demand and pricing. In the Indian context, within the various revenue models, the PPAs with central counterparties are viewed more favourably compared to the PPAs with state distribution utilities and commercial and industrial (C&I) customers, due to factors such as adequate payment security mechanism, strong credit profiles of the off-takers, the availability of a tripartite agreement (between Reserve Bank of India [RBI], states, and benefitting central public-sector undertakings [PSUs]), among others. The PPAs have enabling clauses for compensation in case of grid curtailments and termination payments, and in the case of buyer event of default, are viewed favourably. CARE Ratings views entities having higher termination payments for a longer tenure more favourably than assets having lower termination payments for a limited tenure. 

- **Tariff competitiveness** 

CARE Ratings assesses the tariff competitiveness of the wind energy tariff in PPA by comparing it with the average power purchase cost and the marginal variable cost of power purchased by the off-taker utility. For projects having PPAs with third-party consumers or for captive consumption, CARE Ratings assesses the tariff competitiveness against the applicable grid tariffs. For open access customers, the regulatory environment, adequacy of the banking and wheeling arrangements, and open access charges are factored in. Moreover, presence of enabling clauses such as lock-in period and termination payments are viewed favourably. 

- **Counterparty credit risk** 

Counterparties in the Indian context primarily comprise central off-takers, state discoms (state distribution utilities), and private C&I players. In assessing the counterparty credit risk, CARE Ratings evaluates the financial position of the counterparty, its credit profile, the track record of payments, and the strength of the payment security mechanism (PSM), which generally comprises one-month letters of credit (LCs). 

- **Operating and climatic risks** 

As tariff in most of the RE projects is one-part tariff solely linked to generation levels, the SPV may report lesser revenues in case of non-generation of power due to a variation in weather conditions and/or equipment quality. This, in turn, will affect its cash flows and debt servicing ability. For estimation of the expected power generation of a site, CARE Ratings considers the report of an external agency appointed by the project company. The agency conducting the resource assessment study typically provides power generation estimates for the given site at three probability of confidence levels, P-50, P-75, and P-90, whereby the P-90 level is considered to be the most conservative estimate. Plant availability and grid availability are assessed about their respective normative parameters. In case there are weak operation and management (O&M) practices, or the credit profile of the O&M vendor is weak, the impact is generally reflected in the plant availability and the generation performance. Presence of adequate penalties in case, machine availability is below the minimum level stipulated in the O&M contract is viewed favourably. CARE Ratings observes that the availability of spare parts for weak O&M contractors has been a common challenge for wind plants, which has at times, constrained their operations. Moreover, the quality of the equipment used also determines the generation profile of the plant. Asset diversification is also factored in case projects in an SPV are spread across multiple locations. This acts as a mitigant against resource variability risk to some extent.

# **Industry and regulatory risk** 

Projects in the RE sector are exposed to systemic risks prevalent in the industry as a whole. Prominent risks are instances of delayed adoption of tariff policies, determination of tariff, and imposing additional charges in a retrospective manner, among others. Moreover, despite PPAs being contractual arrangements, there have been multiple attempts to re-negotiate PPA terms, resulting in prolonged uncertainty, which has also impacted the collections of developers. CARE Ratings monitors these industry-related developments regularly and the same is factored in the credit assessment. Regulatory risks also include changes in the tax structure or other charges, which does become a change in law event for the developers. However, obtaining a revised cost approval involves legal and regulatory proceedings, which often delay the receipt of requisite compensation. 

# **Financial risk** 

- **Stability and adequacy of cash flows** 

Since the creditworthiness of a project is primarily determined by the adequacy of cash flows from the project against the debt service obligations, CARE Ratings draws projections for the life of the asset. Additionally, the past financials of the company are factored in, as they indicate the historical composition of cost structure, revenue profile, and balance sheet composition, among others. The cash flows are drawn considering the applicable tariff, the expected plant load factor (PLF), and the envisaged cost metrics including the interest cost to determine the company’s future debt servicing capability. The cash flows are sensitised to the key variables of the project such as PLF, tariff rates, finance cost, and payment delay from the off-taker, among others, to gauge the degree of cushion available in a particular project. 

For a newly commissioned asset, CARE Ratings draws its projections at the designed energy estimate. In case the asset performance is inferior to the designed energy estimate over a sustained period, CARE Ratings draws the projections factoring in the actual generation. CARE Ratings only considers a higher PLF for its projections in cases where actual generation has consistently exceeded the designed energy for a sustained period. For PPAs wherein the tenor is lower than the debt tenor, the tariff, post the PPA tenor, is assumed close to the prevailing tariff trend. CARE Ratings gives the benefit of interest rate reduction or a favourable change in the repayment structure only once it has been formally approved by the lender. 

For an exchange-driven project, CARE Ratings estimates a near-term tariff aligned with current exchange market rates, while the long-term tariff is projected based on historical exchange market rates. 

- **Leverage and coverage indicators** 

As debt comprises a major part of the capital structure, the leverage indicators for a recently commissioned power plant are higher compared to those for a plant with an established track record. While evaluating an SPV, CARE Ratings compares the project with its industry peers on parameters such as debt-equity mix, cost of debt, repayment schedule, and operating profits generated against the external debt (debt/PBILDT). The debt coverage indicators to be examined include the cumulative debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) and minimum DSCR. For projects exposed to refinancing risk, CARE Ratings relies on the project life coverage ratio (PLCR) to understand the available cover and that, in turn, determines the refinancing ability of the entity. CARE Ratings also factors in the financial flexibility of a project, which is determined by the availability of surplus cash flows over the tenor of the PPA, which determines the bankability of the asset. Between two assets having similar DSCRs, the asset having a materially longer tail period will have a relatively superior financial flexibility, and hence, will be able to tap into the financial markets in a better manner. CARE Ratings benchmarks the desired coverage indicators of a project based on its off-taker.

# • **Working capital and liquidity** 

As power distribution utilities in India are the weakest link in the power sector value chain, projects generally suffer a delay in receipt of dues. For a weaker off-taker, the requirement of a higher liquidity buffer is incumbent to uplift its credit profile. The major sources of liquidity primarily pertain to available cash balances, expected cash flows, availability of a debt service reserve account (DSRA) and other liquidity reserves, undrawn working capital limits, and earmarked equity. 

# • **Refinancing and foreign exchange risks** 

CARE Ratings views a longer debt maturity profile and ballooning repayment structure positively for a RE project rather than a shorter maturity profile and a front-ended repayment schedule. While the short tenor of a facility does expose it to refinancing risks, CARE Ratings analyses the associated risks about the ability of the asset to generate cash flows for the remaining contractual tenor and its financial flexibility in terms of access to the capital market. 

Generally, the cash inflows of a project are denominated in the domestic currency, whereas if the repayment obligations are in a foreign currency and are unhedged either fully or partly, it exposes the project to forex-related risks. CARE Ratings views the entities more favourably wherein the hedging is adequate. 

# **Management and parentage** 

CARE Ratings first assesses the standalone credit profile of an asset and the same is notched up based on its parentage. This depends on the promoter group’s ability, willingness, and likelihood of the parent providing needbased support to the underlying SPV. 

(Refer to CARE Ratings’ ‘Rating Methodology – Factoring Linkages Parent Sub JV Group’ on our website <u>www.careratings.com).</u> 

Furthermore, emphasis is given to factors such as the track record of the group in setting RE projects, the underlying capacity at the group level, the quality of management, the overall group level financial policies, and the financial flexibility and resourcefulness at the parent level. A group having stronger financial flexibility will generally be able to timely access the capital markets, attract investors and enjoy the confidence of the lending community. 

# **Environment, social, and governance (ESG) risks** 

Environment risk: Regarding ESG risks, as wind projects fall under the umbrella of renewable power, they score well on the ‘E’ part, which stands for compliance with environmental norms. Wind plants aid in reducing the carbon footprint, thus helping in dealing with climate change. Moreover, the consumption of wind power also results in the preservation of resources such as coal and gas and these plants do not emit toxic gases into the environment. 

Social risk: Like other infrastructure project, wind power requires the acquisition of a large portion of land and the right of way (RoW) approvals to lay the necessary evacuation infrastructure. This often leads to issues around rehabilitation and compensation between the developers and the landowners or the affected people from the community.

# **Conclusion** 

The rating determination is a matter of experienced and holistic judgment, based on the relevant quantitative and qualitative factors affecting the credit quality of the issuer. CARE Ratings analyses each of the above factors and their linkages to arrive at the overall assessment of the credit quality of the issuer. Credit rating is a futuristic assessment, and the rating outcome is ultimately an assessment of the fundamentals and the probabilities of change in the fundamentals in future. 

For wind power projects, project risks assume higher importance in the implementation phase, whereas postcommissioning, the adequacy and stability of cash flows gain higher importance. Various factors impact the cash flows thereafter, which include tariffs, generation level, the interest cost, debt amortisation schedule, collection days, and liquidity buffers, among others. All these factors are collectively analysed to determine the credit risk of the wind project.
