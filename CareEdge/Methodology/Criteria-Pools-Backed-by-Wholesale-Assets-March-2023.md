# **Background** 

This document explains CARE Ratings Limited’s (CareEdge Ratings’) criteria for rating transaction structures where receivables arising out of a pool of wholesale assets are used to pay the rated obligation. Such structures include alternate investment funds (AIFs), and collateralised debt obligations (CDOs), among others. CareEdge Ratings uses a simulation technique to evaluate the portfolio behaviour and assign rating accordingly based on this criteria. 

The transaction structure should clearly define rights of the investor over cash flows from underlying pool (including credit enhancement, if any). The transaction structure may create differential rights over cash flow in favour of different categories of investors (senior, mezzanine or junior). This criteria paper shall not be applied to obligor and co-obligor structures, where pools of underlying wholesale credits belong to same groups. Ratings assigned to the transaction structures covered under the scope of this criteria shall be accompanied by the (SO) suffix. 

# **Methodology:** 

The broad rating approach for the rating of pools backed by wholesale assets involves credit analysis of underlying pool, analysis of transaction structure and simulation for distribution of cash flow shortfall as explained below: 

# **1. Originator / Sponsor / Promoter analysis** 

The profile of originator / sponsor / promoter is evaluated in terms of their performance track record and operational stability, which is especially relevant in the case of transaction structures where the underlying assets in the pool are actively managed. While rating such transactions, additional factors such as the investment strategy, risk appetite, underwriting capabilities, and management quality among others of the investment manager will be assessed. The transactions belonging to large groups with a long track record are expected to have better internal controls and processes and thus provides greater comfort. 

# **2. Assessment of credit quality of underlying credits forming part of the pool** 

The credit rating of each underlying credit (typically wholesale) is ascertained, while looking at the pool composition. Underlying credits usually have readily available credit opinions (typically from credit rating agencies) and are evaluated on an individual basis to assess likelihood of default. 

# **3. Analysis of the transaction structure** 

The transaction structure is an integral part of the cashflow model used in the rating process. The cash flow model factors number of tranches, agreed waterfall, credit enhancement, soundness of the legal structure, and other details of the transaction. Key aspects of transaction structure are briefly covered below: 

# **Tranches** 

There is a possibility of having multiples tranches (senior, junior or mezzanine) in a structure. This can create differential claims or different ratings for each tranche. The structure can also define priority of payment for tranches rated at the same level. 

The tranches are evaluated with the waterfall mechanism to assess credit quality. Transactions may feature single or multiple tranches with sequential or pari-passu payout mechanisms.

# **Waterfall mechanism** 

The waterfall mechanism decides priority of payment from pool collection and credit enhancement, if any. The waterfall will typically provide for payment of taxes, cost, and expenses as senior payouts. This is followed by sequential payouts to senior, mezzanine and junior claims of investors. Waterfall can be customised for each transaction and can be altered in a pre-defined way for event-based triggers. 

# **Credit enhancement mechanism** 

Transactions may have credit enhancement in the form of over collateralisation or subordination of payouts (implicit credit enhancements) or cash collateral or a third-party guarantee (explicit credit enhancement). In case of stress in collections from the underlying pool, these may provide cushion to the investors. 

# **Interest rate risk** 

The interest rates for underlying exposures and the interest payable to investors may be fixed or floating. In case the interest rates are floating linked to different benchmarks, this may create basis risk / interest rate mismatches. The interest rate risk may also arise due to mismatch in the timing of investment / reinvestment. 

# **Legal structure** 

CareEdge Ratings also looks at the legal structure adopted, regulatory and legal compliances in the transaction. The assignability and related aspects of underlying assets and bankruptcy remoteness from the sellers is also examined wherever applicable. CareEdge Ratings may require the client to submit an opinion from an independent legal counsel and relies on the same for these purposes. 

**4. Simulation to create distribution table of shortfall in pay-outs against rated structure** CareEdge Ratings uses Monte Carlo Simulation to create multiple scenarios based on random numbers. This is carried out to analyse portfolio behaviour in the rating process. The analysis uses a default probability of each underlying credit, asset cash flows, asset correlation and recovery assumptions to simulate a portfolio default and generate shortfall distribution statistics. 

While assessing shortfall against rated amount, due consideration is given to the transaction structure including availability of internal /external credit enhancements. In simple terms: 

- a) Number of independent scenarios are simulated. Each scenario randomly generates a set of numbers, each number having a one-to-one correspondence with an identified underlying credit. 

- b) In each independent scenario, based on the relevant random number generated, each underlying credit is determined to have either paid on time or defaulted in a manner consistent with the probability of default associated with that underlying asset’s credit rating. 

- c) The model also incorporates asset correlation assumptions while simulating portfolio behaviour. 

- d) Thus, total defaults in the underlying portfolio are generated for each given scenario. The cash flow in each scenario (considering simulated defaults, recovery assumption, waterfall mechanism and availability of credit enhancement) is evaluated for sufficiency against rated claims. 

- e) This generates a shortfall distribution table in which shortfall rates across different scenarios are plotted with the corresponding probabilities of occurrence.

Asset correlation assumptions are based on the experience in the Indian corporate debt market, across industries. Underlying credits that are from the same sector are assumed to have higher correlation than credits from different sectors. If a few of the borrowers belong to the same group, higher correlation assumption is taken to factor interdependence and linkage. The assumption around Loss Given Default (LGD) or Recovery Assumption and time to recovery are based on the experience of the banking sector non-performing assets (NPAs), Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), and other similar forums. The LGD and timing of recovery are impacted by (a) nature, seniority, value and liquidity of security (if any) and (b) the servicer’s recovery track record. Any expected recoveries after maturity of claims of investors are ignored in cash flow modelling. 

# **Conclusion:** 

CareEdge Ratings’ criteria for rating of pools backed by wholesale assets evaluates credit quality of the underlying pool constituents, the detailed transaction structure including credit enhancement, if any, and the regulatory and legal aspects of the structure being rated. For abundant clarity, such ratings assigned by CareEdge Ratings do not consider market risks and shall not be viewed as an indication of the future pool performance. Such ratings do not validate the quality of the fund management and reputational risks. CareEdge Ratings’ credit ratings for the structures covered in scope of this criteria are essentially not a confirmation that transaction structures adhere to regulatory requirements.
