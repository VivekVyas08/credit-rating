# **Background:** 

The hotel industry is driven by factors such as the prospects of the Indian tourism industry (which depends on the overall economy and disposable income), business and leisure travel, foreign tourist arrivals (FTAs), and the trend of meetings, incentives, conferences, and exhibitions (MICE). 

The risk associated with the hotel industry is high due to its highly seasonal and cyclical nature, which leads to high volatility in its revenue and profitability depending on the economic cycles. During an economic boom, the industry is positively impacted and witnesses higher spending and better margins backed by healthy operational parameters. However, in case of economic downtrends, the industry is adversely impacted and struggles in terms of revenue and profitability. The fact that the industry is characterised by high capital costs and long gestation periods also put pressure on its cash flows in times of economic downtrends. 

# **Rating Methodology** 

CARE Ratings Limited (CARE Ratings) has a standard methodology for the credit rating of entities belonging to the service sector. It encompasses an assessment of the various risk factors which could potentially affect the credit risk of an entity such as economy and industry risk analysis, business risk, financial risk, and management quality. However, considering the size and diversity of the service sector, CARE Ratings has devised methodologies specific to various industries within the sector. These methodologies attempt to point out factors, over and above those mentioned in the broader methodology devised for the service sector, which is considered while analysing entities belonging to the particular industry. The following is a list of such additional factors, and their analytical implications, considered by CARE Ratings while arriving at the rating of an entity operating in the hotel sector. 

# **A. Promoters/Management Analysis** 

Experience and track record of the promoters in the line of business is given utmost importance while evaluating management competency and quality. CARE Ratings assesses management’s ability to run the business efficiently while addressing the inherent business risk associated with hotel operations and undertaking judicious expansion. Considering the capital-intensive nature of the hotel business, a company with financially strong promoters has an advantage over others, as its ability to infuse and/or raise funds in a timely manner is better. Moreover, given the long gestation period in the hotel industry, active involvement of promoters in the operations of the company and ability to support by way of infusion of funds in the initial stages operations becomes important. 

In cases where hotel companies are a part of conglomerates, the extent of linkages, and support of the parent company or group, in terms of financial and/or operations, is also analysed (refer to CARE Ratings’ Methodology- Notching by factoring linkages in Ratings available on www.careratings.com.

# **B. Industry Risk Analysis** 

The demand-supply dynamics for hotel rooms are heavily influenced by cyclical economic patterns, marketspecific factors, and the timing of new supply additions. While strong demand drivers such as tourism growth and business activity can drive room rates and occupancy, oversupply resulting from lumpy supply additions in response to short-term demand projections can lead to market imbalances, price competition, and lower profitability. 

# **i. Regional Demand - Supply dynamics and cyclical nature of industry - a) Demand –** 

The performance of hotels is deeply influenced by a variety of location-specific demand drivers and external event risks that can either spur or severely hinder the demand. Each region, state, and location has specific demand drivers being business hubs, leisure location, more airports, ease of connectivity, currency valuation, and economic trends, among others, which may have a direct bearing on the hotel’s performance and are the indicators of potential growth, investments and others. These apart, there are event risks such as natural calamities, disease spread, terrorist attacks, and political disturbance among others, which may also hamper the demand. 

# **b) Supply –** 

The supply of hotel rooms is shaped by a complex set of factors, including the existing inventory, new developments, economic and leisure potential, land availability, and the regulatory environment. These factors together determine the supply-demand balance in the market. When supply growth outpaces demand, it can lead to pricing pressures and lower profitability for hotel operators, particularly in markets with lumpy supply additions or in sectors with high competition. By monitoring supply dynamics at both the macro city and micro market levels, CARE Ratings assesses the risk of supply surplus, which in turn influences pricing power and financial performance. 

# **c) Cyclicality -** 

The hospitality industry is cyclical in nature. During positive cycles, the industry witnesses periods of sustained growth and registers healthy average room rates (ARRs) and occupancy rates (ORs). The trend continues until the economy goes through a downturn or if there is excess supply. When recession sets in, the ORs begin to decline followed by the ARRs. In the recovery phase, ORs begin to rise, followed by an eventual increase in the ARRs. 

# **ii. Competition –** 

While high capital investment and operational costs create significant barriers to entry, the Indian hotel industry remains highly competitive. Larger cities with constrained land availability may offer some relief in terms of competition. However, overall, pricing power and profitability remain heavily impacted by the constant addition of new inventory and the fierce competition from both domestic and international players.

# **C. Business Risk Analysis** 

# **i. Evaluation of business model** 

Hotels can be owned/leased, franchised, or managed. A healthy mix of revenue from different business models would be favourable from a credit perspective. The selection of business model depends on how experienced and financially equipped a promoter is to independently own and operate a hotel under its own brand. Owned and franchised models require initial investments which leads to significant borrowings. However, due to agreements with established hotel chains under the franchised model, they attract relatively higher occupancies from the initial phase. Entities that are not financially sound or tend to avoid huge capex generally opt for leased models, resulting in lower borrowings but higher lease rent obligations. For a hotel manager with a strong brand, both franchise agreements and management contracts present an opportunity for capital-light, rapid expansion. These models allow for scaling without incurring high capital investments and operational expenses, while still leveraging the brand's power and operational expertise. However, it is crucial to carefully monitor quality control, manage relationships with franchisees and property owners, and optimise the overall operational performance of each location. The business model directly influences a hotel company’s capital structure, scalability, and financial performance, all of which have a significant bearing on its credit profile. CARE Ratings carefully analyses the entity’s business model being rated by and determines the entity’s ability to balance its growth, risk, and investment, which has an impact on its overall financial health and credit profile. 

# **ii. Location analysis** 

The location of the hotel property portfolio is important in determining the volatility and relative level of a hotel’s earnings. The strategic location of a property near a place of interest/commercial area helps attract more and repeat customers and is viewed positively. At the same time, competing properties in the vicinity would tend to exert pressure on pricing. 

The hotel properties can be categorised as business and leisure destinations. At times, the two are not mutually exclusive as some locations have a fair mix of business and leisure travellers. The demand dynamics are different for both segments. Properties located in leisure destinations are likely to have significant seasonality in occupancies and ARR and high gestation period. Similarly, the chains with a higher dependence on foreign travellers are likely to be affected severely in extraordinary events such as economic slowdown, terrorist attacks, and other events. Location at gateway cities, key business centres, and popular leisure destinations may provide some buffer in periods of economic slowdowns, as occupancies at these locations may not decline as sharply. 

# **iii. Brand strength and competitive position** 

CARE Ratings favourably views hotels operated under renowned and established brands as it helps in higher visitation and pricing power leading to better revenue visibility and maintaining market share even in an increasingly competitive scenario. Operational risk is lower in hotels operated by a renowned hotel chain against hotels operated by a hotel operator/owner with little or no prior experience in operating hotels. 

By assessing competitive advantages, such as operational track record, location, brand strength, global tie-ups, and other factors, CARE Ratings determines how well a company is positioned to compete in the market, manage risks, and generate sustainable revenue streams. These competitive

# **iv. Scale** 

While assigning the rating, CARE Ratings considers the scale of business operations in terms of room inventory and other infrastructure. The scale of operations is an important rating consideration as it provides the entity with competitive advantages such as the ability to influence pricing, economies of scale with better operational efficiency and financial flexibility. 

# **v. Revenue diversification- Geographic and Segmental** 

# **a) Mixed Use Properties –** 

Mixed-use properties (hotels and office or commercial/residential leased out spaces), by offering multiple revenue streams, are less vulnerable to downturns in a single sector (such as the hotel industry) and are viewed favourably by CARE Ratings. These properties can provide a stable, diversified income base due to their commercial, residential, and hospitality components. However, overall success and stability of the revenue streams depend on key factors such as location, the ability to lease out commercial space, and effective property management. 

For the purpose of credit assessments, mixed-use properties are often seen as less risky compared to hotels that rely solely on fluctuating tourist/business demand. 

# **b) Geographical -** 

While scale provides a hotel with competitive advantages, diversification ensures the stability of cash flows. CARE Ratings views companies with geographical diversification with a large number of properties more favourably compared to companies with a single property/location, as it insulates the company from any region-specific demand/supply risks. 

An entity with a geographically diversified presence across both business and leisure destinations is better equipped to manage fluctuations in demand, seasonal variations, and economic cycles. This diversified approach enhances revenue stability, operational flexibility, and financial resilience, all of which contribute to a stronger credit profile. 

# **c) Segmental -** 

CARE Ratings also analyses the revenue concentration of an organisation in terms of customer type, region, or service type. The industry comprises three broad sub-segments, viz, luxury/upscale segment, mid-scale segment and economy/budget segment. Revenue per available room (RevPAR) of the luxury/upscale segment is more vulnerable to cyclicality against RevPAR of mid-scale and economy hotels. However, in high demand, the luxury/upscale segment has higher pricing flexibility compared to mid-scale and economy hotels. CARE Ratings examines the presence of hotel entities in sub-segments. Moreover, as they cater to different customer segments across price points, they enjoy a larger customer base. 

While analysing hotel companies, companies operating in one type of destination (either business or leisure) may be more vulnerable to market downturns in that specific segment, exposing them to higher financial risk and potentially impacting their creditworthiness. Thus, diversification—

# **vi. Operational performance** 

CARE Ratings analyses performance indicators of hotels such as ARR, OR and RevPAR. RevPAR is a derivation of ARR and occupancy levels, which, in turn, depend on many factors such as location, demand-supply dynamics, brand, sub-segment, economic scenario and others. For an operational hotel, CARE Ratings evaluates past movement in RevPAR and the ability of a hotel to charge ARR at a premium over industry ARR without compromising on occupancy levels. CARE Ratings also evaluates the mix of revenue from room rent against food and beverage (F&B) /banqueting and their interdependence. In the case of a hotel company, the degree of stability to revenues and profits is of high importance. Healthy sales and profitability are a direct derivation of higher RevPAR. Higher RevPAR indicates strong market positioning of the hotel, whereas healthy profitability reflects the ability to generate cash accruals to support business operations and fund ongoing maintenance, expansion if any. Revenue and profitability in the sector are seasonal and cyclical in nature and CARE Ratings takes the same into cognisance. While revenue is skewed towards the second half of a year, expenses are spread more evenly impacting the profitability. During an economic boom, the industry is favourably impacted by higher disposable income, whereas it struggles during economic downturns. 

# **D. Project Risk** 

CARE Ratings analyses the rated companies for their greenfield/brown-field projects or under-construction entities based on the factors such as size of expansion in relation to the companies’ current net worth, region/place in which capacity expansion/project is being undertaken and funding pattern of the expansion, and its impact on the company’s debt profile in the projected period. The impact of project risk on credit rating depends on a study of the following factors, which may vary considerably from case to case. 

- i. **Timing expansion against industry cycle and project viability:** The demand-supply situation, although important for cyclical industry, assumes more importance for hotel players due to the capital-intensive nature and long gestation period for the industry. CARE Ratings also assesses the risks involved in implementation, which include aspects such as the achievement of financial closure, the status of regulatory approvals, agreements entered with hotel operators/brands, the company’s track record in executing similar projects, project progress against scheduled implementation, cost or time over-runs, and project cost against industry benchmarks, among others. While examining expansion plans, CARE Ratings assesses the demandsupply dynamics of the region in which the new property/expansion is being planned. CARE Ratings believes that the timing and size of the expanded capacity or new project coming on stream is critical to the success of a hotel player. 

- ii. **Funding Risk –** The mode of project financing—particularly the level of leverage—is a key determinant of credit risk in hotel projects. Highly leveraged hotels face significant financial pressure in the early years due to the gradual ramp-up of operations and fixed debt servicing obligations. In case of a downturn, companies having project-related debt repayment obligations could find it difficult to service debt as both estimated cash accruals and fund-raising ability deteriorate. # **E. Financial Risk Analysis** 

# **i. Leverage and Debt Servicing** 

The hotel industry is highly capital intensive and is characterised by a long gestation period. Hotel entities also regularly invest in upgradation and expansion to remain competitive in the industry. Therefore, CARE Ratings evaluates the capital structure of a hotel entity to determine its ability to service the debt to evaluate its financial flexibility. The extent of leveraging determines the possible level of stress on operational cash flows, particularly in cyclical downturns. While computing the leverage, CARE Ratings also factors in the lease liabilities as part of total debt. High leverage reduces the financial flexibility of a hotel company, thereby, its fund-raising ability in case of future capex plans or any shortfall in cash flows in adverse market conditions. 

CARE Ratings evaluates total debt position against the level of gross cash accruals to ascertain the adequacy of its cash flows to service debt repayment obligations. While evaluating future cash flows, capex requirements (usually for new properties and renovation of existing properties) and its funding pattern are also factored in. Given the fact that hotel entities have high initial investment and long gestation periods, repayment schedules of longer tenors are preferred so that they are mapped adequately with the expected cash flow of the hotel to avoid liquidity stress. Any significant interest rate change may have an impact on the debt servicing capabilities of hotel entities and the same is considered in the analysis by CARE Ratings. 

During economic downturns, companies with lower financial leverage may be able to withstand some moderate weakening in operational performance due to relatively lower debt servicing requirements and relatively better financial flexibility.

# **Environmental risk:** 

While the hotel industry is exposed to natural disasters and extreme weather conditions, the overall environmental risk is considered relatively low in terms of credit impact, primarily due to the availability of insurance and the diversification of hotel portfolios. For hotels with fewer properties or a high concentration in risk-prone areas, the credit risk may be more material due to higher occupancy volatility and potential operational disruption. 

# **Social risk:** 

While the hotel industry faces moderate exposure to social risks, the impact of these risks on credit ratings largely depends on how well individual hotel entities adapt to changing social trends, consumer preferences, and potential disruptions such as health crises (example Covid-19) or data breaches. Hotels that are agile, proactive in adopting new technologies, and focused on employee welfare, health and safety, and data security will likely be more resilient. 

# **Governance risk:** 

This involves the structure and functioning of the board, including the presence of independent directors, an investor grievance redressal system, and extensive disclosure practices. Effective and strong governance practices are essential. 

CARE Ratings assesses the impact of the steps required to mitigate the ESG risks enumerated above on the entity’s revenue/cash flows. 

# **Conclusion** 

The rating outcome is ultimately an assessment of the fundamentals and the probabilities of change in the fundamentals. CARE Ratings analyses each of the above factors and their linkages to arrive at the overall assessment of credit quality by considering the industry’s cyclicality. While the methodology encompasses comprehensive financial, commercial, economic, and management analysis, the credit rating analysis is ultimately an overall assessment of all aspects of the issuer focussed on assessing the timely debt servicing ability of the issuer.
