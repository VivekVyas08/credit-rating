# **Background** 

The Infrastructure Sector Ratings (ISRs) of CARE Ratings Limited (CareEdge Ratings) encompass ratings assigned to debt programmes of issuers in power (generation, transmission and distribution), roads (toll, annuity and hybrid annuity), telecommunications, airports, ports, and other such infrastructure-related sectors. CareEdge Ratings adopts a separate methodology for evaluation and assignment of ISR, distinct from corporate sector debt ratings, as rating drivers for ISR are quite different from that of the corporate sector. For example, revenues are driven by tariffs determined by the regulator or the government and changes in government policies will impact ratings of entities in the infrastructure segment significantly. Demand-related risks are minimal because of traditionally high demand-supply gap in infrastructure segments, whereas demand growth is one of the key drivers for the corporate sector. 

# **Scope** 

Infrastructure projects are capital intensive and have a long gestation period. Projects are usually undertaken under distinct entities (special purpose vehicles – SPVs), which have contractual life/asset life and revenue model. Risks are assessed separately for project phase (funding risk, completion risk) and operational phase (revenue risk, regulatory policy framework) of the entities. While CareEdge Ratings has separate rating methodologies for each sub-sector of infrastructure (refer to our website www.careedge.in), this criteria paper explains broader aspects considered while rating infrastructure sector entities. The ISR methodology also covers rating of debt, which has access to pooled cashflows of SPVs; including rating of SPV debt of an infrastructure investment trusts (InvITs) in conjunction with CareEdge Ratings’ rating methodology for InvITs, which is available on www.careratings.com. 

Rating methodologies for the project implementation phase and operational infrastructure entities are explained below. 

# **Rating methodology for project implementation phase:** 

Risks assessed for an entity, which is under project implementation phase are: 

# **I. Sponsor’s evaluation** 

- Ownership structure: SPVs are owned by a single owner/multiple owners or joint ventures. Hence, the ability of majority owner and other minority owners to complete the project, the potential for change in ownership, and explicit undertakings or guarantees provided by each of these owners are assessed. 

- Track record: Track record of sponsor entities in completing infrastructure sector projects, particularly in the same segment, besides the track record of supporting distressed projects in the past is evaluated. 

- Rationale for setting up the project by the sponsor: Rationale for setting up specific project is evaluated. It is generally observed that the sponsor would extend support to the project if it is strategically important to the sponsor. It can also be assessed based on management articulation and the sponsor’s stated position on the project through shared brand, management, majority shareholding and strategic importance of the project in operations of other businesses, among others.

- Current financial position: The sponsor’s current financial position is assessed to judge the ability to provide need-based support for timely completion of the project. Credit rating of the sponsor is considered as a reference tool/parameter. Stated position of the sponsor on support and its track record of support is considered. 

- Technical strength: The sponsor’s experience with the technology and success of developing similar projects in the past are evaluated. In case of multiple sponsors, the role of each sponsor is examined. Some sponsors may be collaborating solely based on technical capabilities rather than their financial strength. Underlying agreement/clauses on transfer of technology, exclusivity about technology, among others, are examined to understand likely operational and financial implications on the project. 

# **II. Approval and completion risk** 

- Geographical location of project: Difficult geographical terrain may impose challenges in physical progress of the project, which may also impact project cost. CareEdge Ratings evaluates project location and its access to resources and mobilisation of resources. 

- Availability of land, water, construction equipment, skilled manpower: For a greenfield project, it is important to ensure availability of all resources for smooth implementation, latest status of land acquisition and likely obstacles in acquiring balance right of way (RoW) for completing the project. For example, deforestation and environmental permits or rehabilitation issues could take a longer time. While greenfield project comes with relatively easy access to land, it may require additional infrastructure for setting up the project. CareEdge Ratings evaluates whether resources such as water and power are already available nearby or need to be obtained by setting up another linked project. 

- Permits and statutory clearances: Availability of all permits, licenses, approvals and statutory clearances, including their status is evaluated. Main clearances impacting execution include, land acquisition, water allocation, mining rights, pollution, and environmental, among others. 

- Risk of time and cost overrun: Infrastructure projects, especially green field projects, face risk of time and cost overrun mainly due to issues between two stages, statutory clearances and availability of resources, which can impact the project’s viability and credit profile significantly. CareEdge Ratings evaluates mitigating factors built in to minimise the impact of such overrun. For example, inflationlinked cost escalations in a contract or sub-contracting to reputed entity at fixed price mitigates cost overrun risk to a larger extent. 

- Third-party risk assessment (lender’s engineer, and design consultants, among others): CareEdge Ratings relies on reports from third-party experts such as lenders’ independent engineers (LE/ LIE), design consultants, and traffic consultants among others, to judge the project’s technical viability and status of project implementation in construction. 

- Extent of financial closure, tie-up of funds, proposed funding mix: Cost of project and break-up, means of financing and break-up, adequacy of each of these, whether comparable to similar kinds of projects are evaluated. 

- Track record and reputation of contractor: For construction of infrastructure projects, contractors are engaged. CareEdge Ratings evaluates experience of contractor in executing similar kind of projects. Credit quality of contractors is also generally assessed. 

- Physical status of project implementation and  pending issues: Timely progress on construction based on envisaged milestones provides comfort. Pending issues that may hamper the construction are analysed, and impact thereof is considered in arriving at the revenue visibility.

# **III. Contractual risk assessment:** 

CareEdge Ratings undertakes identification of risks and mitigants in place through contractual arrangements. Extent of adequacy of risk allocation framework provides comfort to the project’s overall credit profile. 

For certain sectors, road, transmission, and irrigation, among others, project details are worked out by the Ministry sponsoring the project and the terms of concession are put forth by model concession agreements. These terms of concession broadly determine allocation of key risks associated with the project. In its first step of evaluation, CareEdge Ratings examines these terms for clarity of their definitions, their inherent risks, which are irrespective of the project developer and the rewards thereon. A concession agreement with well-defined terms and conditions, optimum risk allocation and suitable rewards for the risks allocated is considered as adequate/complete. For instance, in power projects, it is pertinent that the risks related to environmental clearances, forest clearances, resettlement and rehabilitation and off-take arrangements are well documented. In this context, regulatory and legal environment and track record of entities are considered. 

For other aspects related to the infrastructure projects, contracts are put in place, including engineering procurement and construction (EPC) contract, operation and maintenance contract, among others, which are also examined from a clarity and completeness point of view. Presence of contracts in clear, comprehensive and enforceable terms provides comfort to the overall credit profile. 

# **IV. Revenue risk:** 

CareEdge Ratings’ analysis of revenue risk involves assessment of demand and price risk. Availability-based projects, such as annuity roads, transmission projects derive their revenue streams through contractual agreements with counterparties and are therefore less exposed to demand and price risks, whereas projects with no contractual agreements (for example a power project, which sells power on merchant basis) are exposed to the demand and price risks. In case of projects with high demand and price risks, CareEdge Ratings evaluates third-party viability assessment reports furnished by issuer - such as traffic studies for transportation projects, resource assessment study for renewable energy projects, among others, to determine adequacy of revenues/cashflows for servicing debt. 

# **V. Debt maturity and structural features:** 

Shorter tenor loans may get exposed to refinancing risks, while longer tenor debt for similar kinds of leverage ratio may result in better debt service coverage ratios (DSCRs). For projects where ramp-up in operations takes time, a ballooning structure is preferable. Structural features, such as major maintenance reserve, invertor reserve and other liquidity support mechanisms, such as debt service reserve account (DSRA), among others, provide liquidity support. Presence of an escrow mechanism and/or Trust retention account (TRA), ring-fencing of cashflows and presence of trustee/signatory, who effectively control payments in a timely manner and monitor the structure related to payments provide support to the rating. 

# **VI. Financial risk:** 

Cash flow analysis is given significant emphasis, while rating entities in the infrastructure segment. CareEdge Ratings evaluates stability of cashflows, profitability of project, leverage, including top-up loan, liquidity, debt service protection metrics and financial flexibility, while rating projects in the infrastructure segment. # **Rating methodology for operational phase projects:** 

Risks assessed when the entity is under operational phase are as under: 

- **I. Contractual arrangement, risk allocation:** The regulatory environment and legal factors impacting project operations are identified and analysed. Identification of risks involved over project life and mitigation thereof through contractual arrangements are analysed. Risk allocations through penalty payments, retentions and liquidated damages if the project is not completed within the budgeted cost, time and performance guarantees to adhere to performance standards are evaluated. The comprehensiveness and enforceability of relevant project-related contracts are examined; for example concession agreement, O&M, raw material and utilities supply, off-take arrangement, among others. Provisions in respect of force majeure, provisions in respect of financial implications of liquidated damages, whether they are balanced/ proportionate for parties to the project are evaluated. 

- **II. Business risk:** While analysing business risk, following aspects are covered. 

   - **Pricing risk:** Due to contractual or regulator-driven pricing, infrastructure projects may have very low pricing flexibility. The mechanism for arriving at pricing is evaluated. 

   - **Demand risk:** Usually the demand risk is also low for availability-based infrastructure projects. For projects, which are exposed to demand risk, CareEdge Ratings examines data pertaining to consumption to understand major deviations from the original assumptions considered in construction stage, which are based on third-party studies; for instance number of units supplied to off-taker of power, and actual traffic on the toll road, among others. 

   - **Counterparty risk:** Long-term contracts ensure off-take. However, the ability to make timely payments assumes paramount importance. 

   - **Raw material/fuel supply:** The arrangement of procurement of raw material, its pricing and its impact on realisations are examined. Cost structures, the ability to pass on increase in input costs are evaluated. 

   - **O&M arrangements:** CareEdge Ratings views fixed-price contracts for O&M favourably. Financial profile and operating experience of O&M contractor are considered. 

- **III. Financial risk:** For operational projects, cash flow adequacy and financial flexibility are important financial parameters, which are being evaluated. Refinance risk is analysed in relation to the overall debt profile and future earning capacity of the asset at the time of refinancing. Some key financial aspects considered include cash flow, DSCR/ cash coverage ratio, and waterfall arrangements, among others. Financial and operational covenants stipulated in financing agreements are evaluated. Analysis of financial risk also covers forex and interest rate risk. Generally, cash inflows of a project are denominated in the domestic currency, whereas if repayment obligations are in a foreign currency and are unhedged either fully or partly, it exposes the project to forex-related risks. **IV. Management risk:** CareEdge Ratings evaluates management’s risk appetite, competence, their active involvement in managing operations of the project and integrity of the promoters. Especially for such sectors, where performance can be affected due to disruptions in factors beyond management control such as fuel supply, wind pattern, and non-availability of transmission network, among others, cash flows may require support from promoters. Aspects considered are financial position of sponsors, continued strategic importance of project for the group, and management experience in handling similar kinds of projects, among others. 

# **Force majeure risk in infrastructure projects:** 

The force majeure events such as natural disasters (floods, cyclones, earthquakes, fire etc); political or social upheavals (wars, civil disturbances, strikes etc) are unforeseen and cannot be anticipated. Occurrence of such unpredictable events can severely disrupt an entity’s operations and consequently its cash flow generating capabilities. Typically, the issuers/borrowers try to mitigate the impact of force majeure events by availing insurance cover, in-built contractual remedies with counterparty etc. which become available only post-default. Therefore, due to unpredictability around occurrence of such events, the consequent impact of occurrence of such events cannot be factored in the credit risk assessment, as credit ratings principally follow probability of default approach. 

# **Analysis of environmental, social and governance risk factors:** 

In the last few years, environmental, social and governance (ESG) risks have started gaining importance across the globe and are increasingly influencing investment decisions. The companies may have to incur operational or capital costs towards mitigating these risks. CareEdge Ratings analyses the impact of ESG risks on the credit profile of an entity by assessing the expected impact of these costs on the future earnings/revenue/cash flows of entities. 

Considerations with respect to ESG aspects are an integral part of assessing credit risk and get addressed under parameters, wherever relevant. 

# Environmental Risk: 

The environmental risk is factored in credit risk assessment of polluting sectors, where the expected cost to be incurred towards mitigants in the form of pollution control certifications, effluent treatment measures, among others, and impact of those on future cash flows is evaluated. This apart other environment issues specific to infrastructure project, including tree felling, and dredging, among others are also considered. CareEdge Ratings also analyses aspects related to environmental and forest clearance including issues faced in getting such clearances or delays in receipt of such clearances. 

# Social Risk: 

In terms of social risk, infrastructure companies require large tract of land and RoW and right of use (RoU) approvals to lay necessary infrastructure, exposing them to social risk. Issues would be around resettlement and rehabilitation (R&R) or compensation on land acquisition between infrastructure companies and affected parties/land owners. Effective management of social risk through the smooth settlement of issues with locals in land acquisition, and RoW / RoU approvals are factored in the analysis. Some subsegments of infrastructure sector are labour intensive, and hence, evaluation of the social risk also involves the analysis of measures adopted by the entity to manage labour relationships.

## Governance Risk: 

Governance risk in a corporate is determined by a host of factors, which includes and is not limited to the following, , financial reporting, corporate practices, adherence to code of conduct, board composition, whistle-blower policies, investor relations, among others. CareEdge Ratings tries to assess governance standards of the company based on information and discussions. From the standpoint of corporate governance, CareEdge Ratings also generally analyses board composition and degree of compliance with regulations. Quality of financial reporting and adequacy of disclosures among others are also evaluated. 

CareEdge Ratings’ adoption of a distinctive rating methodology for ISR enables investors/ issuers/ regulators to gather a better perspective on attributes of the rated entity. The rating determination is a matter of experience and holistic judgement of the Rating Committee, based on relevant quantitative and qualitative factors affecting credit quality of the issuer.
