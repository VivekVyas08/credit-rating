# **CARE Ratings’ Criteria on Rating of Investment Holding Companies’ Debt Rating Methodology - Services Sector (Including Backed by Pledge of Shares)** 



## **Background** 

An investment holding company is a company whose majority of the assets are in the form of investments in equity, debt and loans & advances to the group entities operating in diverse / unrelated business activities or generally have non-controlling shareholding in such group entities. Such holding companies typically do not have any operations of their own and their income is primarily in the form of dividend, interest and capital gains on their investment portfolio. These companies typically raise debt on the strength of their investments in the investee / operating entities to mobilise funds for further investments or extend loans & advances to other investee / operating entities and usually refinance the debt on maturity. This paper highlights the approach used by CARE Ratings Limited (CARE Ratings) to assess the debt raised by such type of holding companies with or without pledging of their investments. 

## **1. Debt raised by the investment holding companies (without pledge of investments)** 

The investments made by the investment holding companies in the investee / operating entities are generally long-term in nature. Also, the debt raised by such holding companies is primarily backed by the value of investments held by the company. Therefore, in the analysis of the investment holding companies, the expected sources of funding these investments and the possible impact on the financial risk profile of the investment holding company are assessed. As the main source of income of the investment holding company is dividend, rent and interest from the investee / operating entities, CARE Ratings also looks at the quality of the investment portfolio held by the investment holding company with respect to the credit risk profiles of the investee / operating entities. The investment holding companies’ market value of investments would get affected if the investee / operating entities are under stress. CARE Ratings also checks for impairment in value of these investments. 

The investment holding companies generally refinance their debt on maturity. As a result, the credit risk assessment of such companies is generally based on their ability to refinance their existing debt / raise additional debt which in turn is dependent upon the market value of the investments and volatility is the valuation thereof. Thus, CARE Ratings looks at the debt-to-market value of the investments, financial flexibility, resource raising capacity / refinancing ability and cash flow adequacy while assessing the credit risk of the investment holding companies. For this purpose, CARE Ratings also considers the off-balance sheet exposures such as corporate guarantees extended by the investment holding company while assessing its leverage and other debt-related aspects. Further, the cushion of the market value of the unencumbered investments vis-à-vis aggregate debt level (including off-balance sheet exposures) is also looked into. While assessing the cushion available to raise debt against the unencumbered investments, CARE Ratings duly considers that an investment holding company may not be inclined to encumber all the shares it holds in the investee / operating entities, even if those shares might be eligible to be encumbered technically. In this regard, the investment holding company’s management articulation about its future borrowing plans and its investment and financial policy are assessed by CARE Ratings. 

To assess the resource raising capacity / refinancing ability, CARE Ratings inter-alia considers the shareholding pattern of the investment holding company. Resourceful promoters with a majority stake will be in a better position to support the investment holding company in a timely manner. However, if the shareholding of the investment holding company is fragmented without a clear majority, it would entail further analysis on the commitment of the individual shareholders to support the investment holding company. # **<u>Core Investment Companies (CICs)</u>** 

CICs are Non-Banking Finance Companies (NBFCs) registered with the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). However, such entities are primarily engaged into activities of making investments in the promoter group companies or extending loans and advances to the promoter group companies. The basic approach for rating the debt raised by the CICs is similar to that of investment holding companies as explained above. Additionally, for CICs, CARE Ratings examines credit quality / asset quality of the underlying investments to ascertain the sustainability of the income stream of the investment holding company for servicing its debt and other expenses. 

For assessing the liquidity position, asset liability management (ALM) statement of the CIC is analysed across various time buckets. For assessing the leverage of a CIC, CARE Ratings also examines the outside liabilities to adjusted net worth of the CIC which is an RBI specified metric. Lastly, diversification of the investments / loans & advances to the investee / operating entities in terms of number of entities in which investment has been made, sectoral, product, customer and geographical diversification, extent, quality, consistency and periodicity of disclosures, transparency of information sharing and management quality of the CIC are evaluated. 

# **2. Debt raised by the investment holding companies backed by pledge of shares** 

Investment holding companies monetise their holdings in the investee / operating companies by availing debt against these investments. Loans against shares (LAS) are structure-based transactions wherein the shares held by the investment holding company are pledged in favour of the lender / trustee with a structure that places reliance on the security of the underlying shares to ensure timely debt servicing as per the pre-defined terms. 

# **<u>Peculiarities of LAS transactions</u>** 

The loan is granted / proposed on the primary basis of pledge of the shares of listed entities to the lender / trustee. This criteria does not apply if the shares of unlisted companies are pledged. The entities that avail LAS are mainly investment holding companies whose revenue streams are restricted to the investment income in the form of dividend or profit on sale of shares. As such, these entities may not be in a position to repay their debt from their operational cash flows and that their repayment capacity and financial flexibility is assessed on the basis of the strength of the shares held by the investment holding companies. The value and effectiveness of the security can be judged from the performance of the pledged shares of the underlying company through their average daily volume traded, volatility and liquidity, security cover and the robustness of the legal structure, together with the effectiveness of the structure of the LAS transaction as enumerated below: 

- <u>Lender / trustee-administered mechanism</u> 

A LAS transaction is generally administered by a trustee or a lender who ensures adherence to the defined structure and monitors the share price to ensure top-up of the shares when required within the stipulated timelines or sale of pledged shares as defined in the structure.

The structure stipulates a particular number of days before the due date when the issuer / borrower has to arrange for cash to the extent of the total repayment obligation (including interest) and maintain the same in an account operated by the lender / trustee. If the issuer / borrower fails to arrange the required amount, the lender / trustee has the right to sell the pledged shares to meet the shortfall. 

- <u>Share top-up or cash collateral</u> 

During the tenure of the loan, if the share price of the underlying company falls such that the stipulated security cover is breached, the trustee / lender instructs the issuer / borrower to provide a cash collateral or pledge additional shares to maintain the stipulated security cover within a pre-defined time period, failing which the trustee / lender has the right to sell the pledged shares to arrive at the stipulated security cover. As such, CARE Ratings factors the sufficiency of the unencumbered shares required for top-up, over and above the pledged shares, in its analysis. 

Further, the debt to be rated should be secured by way of first and exclusive pledge on the underlying shares. CARE Ratings assigns the rating based on the term sheet, information and clarification related to the transaction, as provided by the issuer / borrower. 

**CARE Ratings’ criteria on rating loans backed by shares of listed companies factors in the assessment of the credit / liquidity enhancement provided, to ensure credit / liquidity protection offered to the investors / lenders.** 

# **Some of the important features that help assess the share performance include the following:** 

- **Collateral risk analysis** 

The collateral in these transactions are shares of one or more listed companies. The factors which influence the rating are security cover, tenure of the instrument, promoter holding, beta of the stock, liquidation avenues and credit rating of the underlying company. 

- **Security cover** 

Security cover is the market value of the pledged shares to the amount of rated / to be rated debt. A high security cover is considered positively as it entails better cushion for debt repayment. Besides this, pledge of a diversified set of stocks is seen positively as it reduces unsystematic risks unique to a specific company or industry. 

- **Liquidity risk** 

Liquidation horizon is the number of days that would be required to liquidate the pledged shares to arrive at the total repayment obligation (including interest) assuming average traded volume of the pledged shares. Though it is known that the share price of the investee / operating company may witness a fall if the promoter shareholding reduces, this parameter gives a fair indication of the time that would be needed to sell the shares, if required. CARE Ratings generally considers only Group 1 shares (as defined by the National Stock Exchange) or Group A shares (as defined by the Bombay Stock Exchange) as security. Also, the underlying security in the form of shares should have been traded on all the trading days (unless it is not traded on any of the trading days for technical reasons) for the past 12 months. If the shares of the underlying company are heavily traded, the entity will score well on the liquidity parameter. CARE Ratings evaluates the value of the unencumbered shares held by the investment holding company apart from the pledged shares to be able to top-up in case of a fall in the share price of the underling company. 

- **Valuation and market risk**

Share price movement over a long period may be very steep, and hence, longer the tenure of the rated / to be rated debt, the riskier it is considered to be. The movement in the share price of the underlying stock vis-à-vis the movement in the respective benchmark index over a period of time is evaluated to assess the volatility in the value of the underlying security. A high beta to SENSEX / index will be considered to be riskier. The price-to-earnings (PE) multiple of the underlying company based on the consolidated financial results for the four trailing quarters is compared with the PE multiple of the industry to which the company belongs to for assessing how the underlying company fares against the other players in the same industry with respect to the price of shares and earnings potential. A lower variation as compared to the industry PE is viewed positively. 

- **Credit rating of the underlying company** 

The analysis for rating a LAS transaction inter-alia factors in the standalone rating of the debt of the company whose shares are pledged to incorporate the fundamental credit quality of the underlying company in the analysis. 

The standalone rating of underlying company is critical as: 

- The standalone rating of a company takes into account a host of factors like the company’s track record, resourcefulness, group strength, operational performance, financial risk profile and future outlook. 

- If the underlying company is a fundamentally strong investee / operating company as determined by its credit rating, it is more likely to generate stable operating cash flows. A company with a sound business risk profile and good growth potential is expected to have a more resilient stock price performance. 

- Financial flexibility will be higher if the investee / operating company is strong from a credit perspective and has resourceful promoters. 

Further, CARE Ratings believes that the performance of the underlying company will have a bearing on the price of equity shares affecting the value of the shares under asset cover. Also, the rating of loans of investment holding companies by pledging shares of investee / operating companies held by them is generally capped at the rating of the investee / operating company whose shares are pledged or the lowest rating in the investee / operating companies in case the shares of multiple companies are pledged. 

- **Legal risk analysis** 

CARE Ratings reviews the transaction documentation to determine whether the structural features are adequately captured in the document(s) and takes a legal opinion on the enforceability of the pledge, right to sell the shares or any other matter as deemed necessary.
