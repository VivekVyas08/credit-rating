# **Background** 

The banking sector highly depends on the overall economic environment in the country and has high correlation with the economic growth. In India, banks continue to dominate the financial services space, constituting a critical part of the economy. As a result of their importance to the overall economy and financial markets, Indian banks continue to be highly regulated by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). The banking sector in India is classified broadly into public sector banks, private banks, foreign banks, and small finance banks (SFBs), with major share of public sector banks in banking assets. 

CARE Ratings Limited’s (CareEdge Ratings’) credit ratings on banks are an opinion on the relative ability and willingness of these banks to make timely payments on the specific debt obligations over the life of the instrument. This criteria paper discusses the key quantitative and qualitative factors considered by CareEdge Ratings while rating the debt instruments of banks including SFBs and regional rural banks (RRBs). 

# **Quantitative factors** 

The starting point for reaching a rating decision is a detailed review of the following key measures of financial performance and stability: 

# **A) Capital adequacy** 

Capital adequacy is a measure of the degree to which a bank’s capital is available to absorb possible losses. It also indicates the bank’s ability to undertake additional business. RBI, in accordance with the Basel norms, has prescribed certain minimum amount and the type of capital to be maintained by the banks on an on-going basis.

The minimum CAR prescribed for SFBs by RBI is 15% with Tier-I CAR of 7.5% (under Basel II), while the minimum CAR prescribed for RRBs by RBI is 9% (under Basel I) and CCB and counter-cyclical buffer is not applicable for them. 

Maintaining the CAR above the prescribed regulatory threshold is critical for a bank, as breaching of such benchmarks might lead to regulatory action, including placing the bank under ‘Prompt Corrective Action’ (PCA) framework, where restrictions are put on a bank’s lending and other operations. Higher capital cushion enhances a bank’s ability to absorb unexpected losses in the future and also provides a bank with the ability to grow its advances. 

CareEdge Ratings examines the conformity of the bank to the regulatory guidelines including CAR and Common Equity Tier-I (CET I) ratio and the available capital cushion over and above the minimum regulatory requirement that a bank maintains. A higher proportion of CET I capital (core capital) in the overall capital is viewed favourably as it enhances the bank’s loss absorption capacity. Apart from the core equity capital, banks have the option to enhance the capital by way of subordinated instruments, including Tier-II Bonds and Additional Tier-I (AT I) Bonds. Although the core capital is important, a bank’s ability to raise non-core capital is analysed as it reduces the dependence on equity. CareEdge Ratings analyses capital raising track record of the bank which indicates its financial flexibility. The capital adequacy of the bank under stressed scenario is also assessed factoring in the growth and riskiness of the assets. 

RBI’s Basel III capital regulations also prescribe that banks should maintain minimum leverage ratio, which is a ratio of minimum capital to be held in relation to the exposures taken by the bank. Leverage ratio is defined as Tier-I capital / total exposure (including on-balance sheet and off-balance sheet exposures adjusted for credit conversion factors). The guidelines prescribe minimum leverage ratio of 4.0% for domestic systemically important banks (D-SIBs), 3.5% for other banks, and 4.5% for SFBs. 

# **B) Asset quality** 

Asset quality of a bank relates to the recovery and the risk priced return that a bank can generate from its loans and investments. The assessment of asset quality primarily factors in the non-performing assets (NPA). While analysing the asset quality of a bank, the trend in NPA, slippages, loan losses and writeoff/provisions and percentage of assets classified into standard, substandard, doubtful or loss, and the track record of recoveries of the bank are examined closely. 

Increase in the quantum of non-performing assets entails higher credit costs and reversal of interest earned on NPA, which impacts the profitability of the bank and in turn impacts the capitalisation level of the bank reducing its loss absorption capability. 

The overall asset quality is assessed by the proportion of NPA that a bank has against its advances and the internal accruals. The net worth cover that it has to absorb losses from the NPA which are unprovided for, is also examined. The portfolio diversification, level of concentration to certain groups / individual borrowers, and exposure to troubled industries/areas are evaluated to arrive at the level of weak assets. Sector-wise exposures of the bank are evaluated to assess the existing and potential level of stress. 

CareEdge Ratings analyses the growth in advances and the credit / deposit ratio for a bank. The break-up of the advances book into wholesale / corporate advances and non-corporate advances, which in turn are

divided into  retail loans, agriculture loans and MSME loans, is also analysed. the growth, level of NPA and stressed assets in each of the segments are assessed. 

With the corporate loans, the risk of large ticket size and associated concentration is high where slippages in a few accounts could increase the NPA for a bank, whereas the retail loans are more granular and have relatively lower risk of slippages impacting the NPA ratios in a short period. CareEdge Ratings also examines the proportion of special mention accounts (SMA 0 - accounts overdue up to 30 days, SMA 1 – accounts overdue more than 30 days and up to 60 days, and SMA 2 – accounts overdue more than 60 and up to 90 days) which are indicators of stress on the bank’s asset quality. 

The analysis of the wholesale book entails assessment of top exposures, their rating and sectoral distribution and track record of recovery from and provision coverage on the bad loans. While analysing the non-wholesale exposures, assessment of movement in NPA, secured or unsecured nature of loans, track record of recovery, among others, are analysed. 

CareEdge Ratings also assesses the potential stress on the book by evaluating the restructured assets and quantum of identified weak / stressed assets in the bank’s total exposure and exposure to security receipts (SRs) issued by asset reconstruction companies (ARCs) to whom NPAs are sold, to arrive at the potential NPA of the bank in conjunction with the provisions made by the bank and the possible impact of the unprovided exposure on the profitability of the bank. 

While assessing the asset quality of a bank, CareEdge Ratings also examines the bank’s credit risk management framework. 

# **C) Resources** 

Deposits are a major resource for banks, and raising deposits at competitive cost is the core of banking business. A bank’s resource base is analysed in terms of cost and composition while rating a bank. Apart from deposits, banks have access to raise funds from multilateral agencies (domestic and international), which provide refinance to banks. However, deposit raising / funding remains core to the functioning of a bank. 

CareEdge Ratings studies the franchise built by the bank over the years, which helps a bank garner deposits and attain granularity of the depositor base. The proportion of low-cost deposits in total deposits and retail / wholesale deposit mix is examined. Deposit growth rates and their rollover rates which indicate stability of the funding profile of a bank are also analysed. 

The banks look to garner current accounts and savings accounts (CASA) and retail term deposits (which are generally low-cost deposits and tend to be sticky) to have a stable and low-cost funding source. The banks also raise bulk deposits and certificate of deposit (CDs) from corporate and institutional depositors, which are of short tenure, and are relatively more interest rate-sensitive and volatile in nature compared to CASA deposits and retail term deposits. As a result, higher proportion of CASA and retail term deposits is viewed more favourably, while analysing the resource profile of a bank. The proportion and growth of CASA deposits to liabilities is viewed in conjunction with the industry and the cost benefit a bank derives in generating such deposits.

In addition to deposits, the banks also raise long-term refinance from All India Financial Institutions (AIFI) for eligible loan portfolios. While deposits remain the core source of funding, availing refinance facilities diversify the resource profile of banks and enhance the financial flexibility. The resource raising capability of the bank also entails the bank’s history and the ability to raise resources by way of Tier-II Bonds, AT I Bonds to enhance the capitalisation levels and raising of infrastructure bonds to fund exposures towards infrastructure and housing sectors subject to extant regulations. 

The average and incremental cost of funds is examined in the context of prevailing interest rate regime. The bank’s ability to raise additional resources at competitive rates and its dependence on or concentration in depositors is examined critically. 

In its Basel III regulations, RBI has prescribed net stable funding ratio (NSFR) to be maintained by the banks. The objective of NSFR is to ensure that the banks maintain a stable funding profile in relation to the composition of their assets and off-balance sheet activities. The NSFR limits overreliance on short-term wholesale funding, encourages better assessment of funding risk across all on-balance sheet and offbalance sheet items, and reflects funding stability. 

NSFR is defined as a ratio of Available Stable Funding (ASF) to Required Stable Funding (RSF), where ASF is portion of capital and liabilities expected to be reliable over the time horizon, which extends to one year, and RSF of an institution is a function of the liquidity characteristics and residual maturities of the assets held by that institution and those of its off-balance sheet exposures. The NSFR should be equal to or at least 100% on an ongoing basis. 

# **D) Liquidity** 

Lack of liquidity can lead to a bank’s failure, while strong liquidity can help even an otherwise weak institution to remain adequately funded in difficult times. CareEdge Ratings evaluates the internal and external sources of funds to meet the bank’s requirements. 

The liquidity risk is evaluated by examining the assets liabilities maturity (ALM) profile, deposit renewal rates and the liquidity coverage ratio (LCR). The short-term external funding sources in the form of refinance facilities from RBI and the inter-bank borrowing limits available and CRR and SLR investments are important sources of reserve liquidity. 

The LCR requires banks to maintain high quality liquid assets (HQLAs) to meet 30 days net outgo under stressed conditions. The banks need to maintain minimum LCR of 100% from April 01, 2021. In addition, the banks have access to market liquidity support by way of borrowings under liquidity adjustment facility (LAF) and marginal standing facility (MSF) from the RBI. 

# **E) Earnings quality** 

CareEdge Ratings analyses the composition of a bank’s income by segregating it into those generated from fund-based activities and fee-based activities. The trend in the bank’s interest income, composition, and trend of non-interest income are analysed to ascertain the earnings quality. Core earnings are also identified by excluding non-recurring income from the total income. Each business area that contributes to the core earnings is assessed for risks and for its earnings prospects and growth rate.

Profitable operations are essential for banks to operate as a going concern. The yield on business assets (including advances) and investments are viewed in conjunction with cost of funds to arrive at the spreads earned by the bank. The net interest margin (NIM) is a key indicator of the margins earned by a bank and is analysed accordingly in relation to the peer groups and overall interest rate scenario. 

The quality of the bank’s earnings is also influenced by the level of interest rate and foreign exchange rate risks that the bank is exposed to. 

The operating efficiency is also examined in terms of expense ratios and is analysed in conjunction with the industry average. The trend in the pre-provisioning operating profit (PPOP) is assessed to evaluate the profitability of the bank and the ability of the bank to absorb credit costs. 

A bank may be profitable operationally, however, deterioration in asset quality requires higher credit cost in terms of provisioning requirement and write-off of bad debts, which could severely impact a bank’s overall profitability. A trend in the impact of credit costs on the profitability in terms of return on assets and return to shareholders’ funds is analysed and also compared to the peers. 

Evaluation of quantitative factors is carried out not only of the absolute numbers and ratios, but also of their trends and volatility. The attempt is to determine core and recurring measures of performance. CareEdge Ratings also compares the bank’s performance on each of the above-discussed parameters with its peers. A detailed inter-bank analysis is carried out to determine the relative strengths and weaknesses of the bank. 

# **Qualitative factors** 

Some of the qualitative factors that are deemed critical in the rating process are: 

# **a. Industry profile and economic and operating environment** 

Banks are a key constituent in the economy and have high corelation with the economic growth and their performance is linked to operating environment including factors such as credit demand, inflation, market liquidity, exchange rates among others. An analysis of the trends in the economy and operating environment and its impact on the banking sector as a whole and the rated bank in particular is done. While these operating conditions and business cycles are implicit for banking business, material impact of the operating environment on the bank’s performance would be factored in the rating and may be highlighted. Any material impact of regulatory action or change in regulations would be factored in ratings of the bank. 

Considering their systemic importance and high level of interconnectedness with other constituents of the economy, banks receive regulatory support in terms of liquidity facilities, certain forbearance or any other form of support as needed. 

# **b. Ownership** 

An assessment of ownership pattern and shareholder support in periods of stress is critical. In case of public sector banks, the stated intent and willingness of the government to provide capital support remains crucial and is an important consideration and a demonstrated track record of such support.

# **c. Management quality** 

The quality of management is an intangible yet a very important factor while evaluating a bank. While analysing management quality and governance, the composition of the board, frequency of change in the top management team and the organisational structure of the bank are considered. The bank’s strategic objectives and initiatives in the context of resources available, its ability to identify opportunities and track record in managing stress situations are taken as indicators of managerial competence. 

The management’s approach towards risk is gauged by understanding their stance in terms of product segments /sectors the bank intends to target and the rate at which it intends to grow such segments. 

The adequacy of the information systems used by the management is evaluated in terms of quality and timeliness of the information made available to bank managers. The extent of frauds committed in the bank is taken as an indication of imperfections of the control systems. 

CareEdge Ratings focuses on the modern banking practices and systems, degree of digitalisation, capabilities of the senior management, personnel policies and extent of delegation of powers. The track record of labour relations is also examined. 

# **d. Risk management** 

The management stance on the risk and the risk management framework is examined. Credit risk management is evaluated by examining the appraisal, monitoring and recovery systems and the prudential lending norms of the bank. 

The bank’s balance sheet is examined from the perspective of interest rate sensitivity and foreign exchange rate risk. CareEdge Ratings also assesses the extent to which the bank has assets denominated in one currency with liabilities denominated in another currency. The derivatives and other risk management products used by the bank are also examined. 

With increasing customer preference and banks’ focus towards offering banking products and services through the digital mode, it is critical for banks to have the strong technology focus and robust cyber security measures to avoid possible instances of data theft or data breach. Any data breach may result in financial loss or/and may result in adverse regulatory action. 

The banks are subject to supervision by the RBI and are required to disclose divergence in asset classification and provisioning if the incremental provision required as assessed by RBI exceeds the prescribed proportion of published net profit for the year and/or additional gross NPA assessed by the RBI exceed prescribed proportion of the published computation for the year. High amount of divergences and its impact on the bank’s financials is considered. 

# **Market risk** 

Interest rate risk arises due to differing maturity of assets and liabilities and mismatch between the floating and fixed rate assets and liabilities. CareEdge Ratings assesses the market risk inherent in the investment portfolio of the bank with respect to classification of the portfolio under Held-to-Maturity (HTM), Held-forTrading (HFT), Fair Value through Profit and Loss (FVTPL) and Available for Sale (AFS), and the impact of the interest rate movement on the AFS and HFT book.

# **Operational risk and digital capability** 

Banks are susceptible to operational risk and instances of frauds. Frauds involving high amounts may require high provisioning which impact the bank’s profitability apart from reputational risk. Number of instances of frauds in the review period, the amount involved and provisioning made on the same and measures taken by the bank to avoid such instances in future are analysed while rating a bank. 

With increasing customer preference and banks’ focus towards offering banking products and services through the digital mode, it is critical for banks to have the strong technology focus and robust cyber security measures to avoid possible instances of data theft or data breach. Any weakening in the data security makes the bank susceptible to cyber-attack or data breach which may result in financial loss or/and may result in adverse regulatory action. 

# **e. Compliance with statutory requirements** 

CareEdge Ratings examines the track record of the bank in complying with various regulatory requirements and adherence to regulatory requirements. Any regulatory actions affecting the bank, divergences in reporting, and penalties levied by regulators, among others, and its impact on the operations or financial position of the bank is examined. The quality and timeliness of disclosures, mandatory or otherwise, indicate level of transparency of the management. 

# **f. Accounting quality** 

The rating relies on the audited data. Policies for income recognition, provisioning and valuation of investments followed by the bank are examined. Suitable adjustments to reported figures are made for consistency of evaluation and meaningful interpretation. 

# **g. Size and market presence** 

The established franchise of a bank provides it with a deposit base and reach through its branch network and can have a bearing on the bank’s competitive position. Ratings also factor in market share of a bank in advances, deposits, and other relevant parameters. Banks have higher systemic importance and may get more support from of the regulators and government in times of stress. Large banks with established franchise tend to gain competitive advantage in terms of access to a stable base of low-cost funds and greater geographical reach, whereas small banks typically would either be geographically restricted or would have challenges in ramping-up their deposit base necessitating a different approach to its business model.

# **Analysis of Environmental, Social and Governance Risk Factors** 

Over the recent years, the awareness of developing sustainable businesses factoring the Environment, Social and Governance (ESG) factors has been gaining importance in the corporate sector and are increasingly influencing investment decisions. Banks are creators of credit in an economy and although the direct impact of banks on environment is limited, banks as lenders to industries play an important role in providing credit to environmentconscious borrowers. Companies might have to incur operational or capital costs towards mitigating these risks. 

CareEdge Ratings analyses the impact of ESG risks on the credit profile of an entity by assessing expected impact of these costs on future earnings/revenue/cash flows of entities. As appreciation of ESG risks increase, the company’s ability to raise capital might be affected as higher scrutiny from investors in terms of ESG compliance will increase, especially for players raising funds from overseas markets. 

# Environmental risk: 

As a part of the environmental policies, many banks are developing green products and have put in place an ESG rating model, which rates large borrowers on objective ESG criteria. Banks are raising green funding in terms of bonds and other capital, which are designed to support specific climate-related or environmental projects. Many banks have been progressing by adopting Board-approved ESG policies and Board oversight on ESG and making it part of their risk management process. 

As a part of the rating methodology, CareEdge Ratings looks at various measures on the ESG agenda while analysing the banks. As a part of factoring ESG in rating analysis of banks, CareEdge Ratings assesses bank lending to the sectors, which have material impact on the environment. Factors including emissions, water usage, and waste management are assessed subject to availability of information. 

# Social risk: 

Social risk would play out prominently in a labour/manpower-intensive services industry such as banks and financial services, where social issues such as employee policies, workplace conditions, and customer relationships are critical components of credit risk assessment. Considerations include how a company manages its workforce, its corporate social responsibility practices, and its interactions with the community. Issues related to social risks can have a significant impact on a company’s reputation and financial stability, influencing its credit risk profile. 

# Governance risk: 

Governance risk refers to internal factors related to how a company is managed and controlled, which directly impacts safeguarding stakeholders’ interest. Key governance parameters include transparency, adherence to applicable regulations, public disclosures, and costs associated with maintaining strong governance structures. Companies with strong governance practices are generally considered to have lower credit risks, as they are better
