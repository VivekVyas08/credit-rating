# **Background** 

Based on energy source, power generation companies may be classified as thermal (coal, lignite, gas, or diesel), nuclear or renewable (solar, wind, hydro, biomass and waste-to-energy). Thermal power producers continue to be an important constituent in the power generation scenario in India, in terms of capacity and generation, despite rapid growth in renewable capacity in the recent past. Currently, thermal power plants comprise more than half of the installed capacity and more than 3/4<sup>th</sup> of the energy supplied. Share of thermal in installed capacity and energy supplied has declined in the last decade, which is expected to continue going forward considering limited thermal capacity addition. However, thermal power projects remain critical in meeting the base load energy demand especially considering the significant increase in share of renewable projects in the future, which have intermittent nature of generation. 

# **Methodology** 

Thermal power projects are capital intensive and leveraged in nature. CARE Ratings has developed a rating methodology for debt issues of thermal power projects. The rating procedure is designed to facilitate appropriate credit risk assessment, considering the characteristics of the Indian thermal power sector. CARE Ratings looks at a time horizon over the life of the debt instrument being rated and covers the following factors while rating thermal power projects.

# **1. Promoter group and management team** 

Evaluation of management quality is an essential part of all rating assessments. CARE Ratings evaluates the management from different perspectives including financial capabilities, experience in the industry, track record in implementing and operating large projects, and availability of technical manpower. The commitment of promoters/management to the business, strengths/weaknesses of other group entities, track record of supporting group entities and the group’s plans on new projects, and acquisitions, among others, funding support from the operational power project being analysed is also critically examined.

# **2. Project implementation risk** 

Project implementation risks assume significance in a power project due to the long gestation period and large investments involved. CARE Ratings analyses following factors in this regard: 

# **Availability of land and permitting risk** 

Land acquisition and project-related approvals are critical for timely implementation of thermal power projects, as this activity usually takes considerable time in the entire implementation schedule of the power project. Thermal power projects require approvals from several government bodies including the Ministry of Environment and Forests & Climate Change (MoEF&CC), Ministry of Civil Aviation and State Pollution Control Board among others. Projects developed under Case-I competitive bidding mechanism, where land acquisition and all related approvals are to be obtained by the developer are relatively riskier compared to projects under Case-II mechanism, where the responsibility of arranging land and project clearances/ approvals rests with government nodal agency. 

Within the thermal power sector, land requirement for gas-based projects is relatively lower and gas being environment friendly, related approvals are also obtained easily compared to coal/lignite-based projects. Considering challenges in land acquisition and related approvals, CARE Ratings evaluates issues related to land acquisition and statutory clearances as delays beyond expected timelines could result in escalation of time and cost for the project. 

# **Construction risk** 

The construction risk mainly involves analysis of project location, credentials of engineering, procurement & construction (EPC) contractor and promoter’s experience in executing similar projects. Financial strength of EPC contractors and a track record of executing similar projects in the past is also a key factor for evaluating construction risk. CARE Ratings critically analyses terms of the EPC contracts to understand their obligations for timely completion of the project and liquidated damages (LD) clauses for delays in project completion. Lump-sum turnkey EPC contracts with clearly defined LD clauses are viewed positively. The EPC contractor’s ability to arrange equipment and manpower on time and within budgeted estimates is critical for timely execution of projects. CARE Ratings also evaluates availability of associated infrastructure required for implementing thermal power plants, which includes power evacuation line, water availability and railway siding for coal transportation among others. 

# **Evacuation infrastructure** 

CARE Ratings analyses availability of evacuation infrastructure and in the case of an under-development transmission line, timelines for completion against the project’s scheduled commercial operations date (SCOD) are examined. Delays in the construction of transmission infrastructure can cause the plant to remain idle despite becoming ready to generate power. 

# **Financial closure** 

For timely completion of the project, CARE Ratings positively considers financial closure. Thermal power projects are capital intensive and generally funded in a debt-equity ratio of 70:30, having high leverage. CARE Ratings critically evaluates the status of infusion of promoter funds, debt tie-up, pre-disbursement conditions and critical covenants of tied-up debt (interest rate, moratorium period, repayment period, structuring of repayments, cash flow waterfall mechanism, TRA, and subordination of promoter’s

The foreign exchange component of the debt and its hedging mechanism is also relevant for analysis, especially in case of competitively bid tariffs. The promoter’s strong track record in executing similar-sized projects, achieving financial closure in the past and their financial strength are important risk mitigants, in terms of timely execution and arranging finances in cases of increase in project cost due to contingencies.

# **3. Project operations/ Business risk** 

# **Demand-supply risk** 

CARE Ratings evaluates the demand risk for the project by analysing the mix of long/medium/short-term Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) tied up by the project. CARE Ratings also evaluates the projected demand-supply scenario at state and national level, considering assumptions including likely capacity additions and drivers of demand growth. 

# **Revenue Risk** 

A Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) is a crucial document outlining rights and responsibilities of the power producer and its evaluation forms a critical part of the rating exercise. CARE Ratings looks at the contract tenor to assess the horizon of revenue visibility. PPAs can be long-term, medium-term and short-term in tenure. Long-term PPAs may be executed on a cost-plus basis or competitively bid basis and carry low offtake risk with pre-agreed pricing, leading to lesser volatility in revenues. Long-term PPAs, on a cost-plus basis, covering full loan tenor are viewed positively by CARE Ratings, followed by competitively bid PPAs on a long-term basis, while medium/short PPAs are seen least favourably. The extent of capacity tied up under long-term arrangement against overall installed capacity determines revenue visibility. Nonavailability of PPA tends to have an impact on the availability of linkage fuel as well. Medium-term PPAs may be executed with Discoms or Nodal agency/trading company having back-to-back selling arrangements with Discoms. Medium-term PPAs can be either competitively bid or based on mutually agreed tariffs for bulk industrial customers. These PPAs carry relatively higher risk, as these generally do not cover the full loan tenor and the company’s ability to timely renew it at remunerative tariff is critical. Power projects may also sell power on short-term basis/power exchanges at spot tariff rates and remain exposed to vagaries of price fluctuation and quantum of power off-take and bears the highest sales risk. PPAs having escalation or indexation clauses may provide further comfort against those not having such clauses. 

Tariff rates are critically analysed to determine their adequacy to ensure profitability and debt servicing capability of power projects at the minimum operating performance level. In case of plants with sizable untied capacity, their track record of sales (in terms of volume and rate) in the merchant market and their sustainability is also factored in the projection.

The project’s revenue and profitability largely depend on the nature of the PPA and cost of the fuel. In the case of a cost-plus-based PPA, profitability depends on the project’s ability to declare the plant availability and maintain actual costs within normative benchmarks. Tightening normative benchmarks by regulator may have a negative impact on the project. Timely issuance of tariff orders and true up of accounts under cost plus mechanism is a key monitorable. In case of a competitively bid-based PPA, actual returns for a company would depend on its ability to keep actual operating and cost parameters within the tariff bid levels. For a company with a cost-plus and competitively bid PPAs, CARE Ratings evaluates the extent of under-recovery in capacity charges and energy charges. In case of short-term PPAs, the company’s profitability remains exposed volume and tariff volatility in the short-term market and fuel price level volatility. 

# **Fuel Supply Risk** 

Coal-based plants face challenges of mining output and logistic issues, while gas-based stations also face gas supply challenges, which make fuel supply a significant risk for thermal power plants necessitating rigorous assessment. CARE Ratings evaluates adequacy of fuel supply to declare capacity at normative levels. CARE Ratings also analyses the extent of tie-up of fuel requirement through long-term arrangements, actual despatch of fuel in the past against committed volumes in fuel supply agreements, proximity of the plant to mines, mode of transportation and its associated costs. The fuel cost pass-through risk is assessed by analysing escalation in energy charge quoted in the PPAs. A plant having full fuel cost pass-through is perceived to be less risky than those with partial/no cost pass through. In plants with fuel cost pass through, it is critical to meet operating norms such as station heat rate and auxiliary consumption to ensure full recovery of fuel cost. The plants having supply from captive mines tend to enjoy improved coal availability at a competitive rate, leading to higher scheduling. In case the power project is using imported fuel without having forex variation as pass-through in PPA, foreign currency risks are also assessed. 

The thermal power plants not having fuel supply agreements rely largely on auctions/imported fuel to meet their fuel needs. In such cases, fuel cost, availability of fuel and distance from mines/ports (affecting fuel transportation costs), plays an important role in continuing to bid in auctions feasibly and securing PPAs. Apart from availability, quality of fuel is also assessed as lack of uniformity in the fuel mix may also impact the power plant’s operational performance. 

# **Plant efficiency and cost competitiveness** 

The power distribution companies (Discoms) are required to procure thermal power based on a merit order despatch, where power plants with highest variable costs are given least priority. In such a scenario, lower fuel costs ensure higher priority in merit order, contributing to higher actual generation. Though fixed costs might be recoverable based on plant availability, higher actual generation leads to higher contributions above availability-based compensation. 

In its analysis, CARE Ratings considers cost per unit generated for past years including fixed and variable costs. CARE Ratings also considers other operational metrics driving these costs such as auxiliary consumption, and station heat rate among others. 

Superlative operational performance of a typical thermal power plant is characterised by maintenance of availability beyond normative plant availability, healthy plant load factor (PLF) due to higher scheduling,

Cost competitiveness is particularly important for plants with untied capacity. Pit head plants having high availability of coal at competitive rates, lower auxiliary power consumption and station heat rates tend to demonstrate better volume sales and realisation in the short-term power market, thus ensuring better profitability. 

# **Off-taker risk** 

Quality of off-taker and diversification: Counterparty risk could significantly impact the project’s credit quality, as there is a long-term tie-up of the project with the off-taker and minimal chances to move out. The off-taker plays a critical role in arriving at the rating for the project. Off-takers are broadly classified into three categories, State distribution companies (Discoms), Nodal Agency/trading companies having back-to-back selling arrangements with Discoms and commercial/industrial consumers. For assessing the first category of off-taker, CARE Ratings relies on parameters, such as past financial performance, credit rating, past payment track record, health of respective state government and track record of subsidy payment to Discoms, movement in the level of AT&C losses over time, trend of cost coverage & tariff revision, and past stance of honouring PPA commitments, among others. Credit support mechanisms offered by an off-taker such as revolving letters of credit, escrow accounts, guarantees, and tri-partite agreement with the RBI aids the quality of issue and its rating. In case of assessing the quality of nodal agency/trading company and industrial consumers, CARE Ratings relies on business fundamentals of the entity, their credit rating, analysis of past performance, market standing, and expected performance of the industry in which the entity operates, among others. CARE Ratings considers a contractual sales agreement with multiple off-takers as a better proposition in general, when compared to a single off-taker, as it provides benefits of diversification. In case of PPAs with commercial/industrial consumers, CARE Ratings factors in financial health of the off-taker, cost savings from these PPAs compared to grid supply, past track record of payment and open access norms in states where the plant and consumers are. 

Payment track record: Apart from analysing the fundamental credit quality of the off-taker, CARE Ratings also analyses payment track record of the off-taker and attaches due weightage to the timely payment track record of the off-taker on a sustained basis. For an operational project, CARE Ratings analyses monthly billing & payment track record for a reasonable period (6-12 months on a case-to-case basis) and trend in debtors in the recent past. Barring a few state Discoms, the majority state Discoms in India have a weak-to-moderate financial profile, and they demonstrate delayed payment track record for varying periods of delays, which typically constrain the rating. 

# **Force Majeure Risk** 

Impact of a force majeure event is usually higher for infrastructure projects including power projects given the single asset nature of operations. This may be mitigated to a large extent through insurance or adequate provisions in contractual agreements. In case of an insurance claim, extent of coverage, quantum of admissible claim and timely receipt of claim proceeds are evaluated.

# **4. Regulatory risk** 

The power sector in India is governed by regulators at central and state levels including Central Electricity Regulatory Commission (CERC) and State Electricity Regulatory Commissions (SERCs). Power projects involved in the sale of power in more than one state is required to adhere to CERC guidelines, while other projects are required to follow SERC guidelines. 

Broadly, SERCs work as guided by CERC and are responsible for framing guidelines for state generation, transmission and distribution utilities and determining tariffs as payable by state Discoms. 

For assessment of regulatory risks, CARE Ratings analyses timeliness and adequacy of tariff determination/revision applicable for regulator-determined tariff PPAs. Delays in such determination/ revision could be a credit concern and may have occurred due to delay in petition filing by the power producer or delay in issuance of an order by the regulator. Regulatory orders by CERC/SERC are critically analysed even for competitively bid PPAs with respect to regulations such as changes in law, domestic fuel shortages, and tariff compensation, among others. CARE Ratings assesses approval for tariff change and timeliness in realisation of regulatory receivables. CARE Ratings accesses accounting treatment of regulatory assets by the company and timeline stated by regulatory for liquidation of such assets. Ministry of Power has issued rules for financial disclosure by Discoms, where only revenue approved within tariff orders must be recognised. Changes in accounting norms for revenue recognition for thermal power projects is monitored by CARE Ratings. 

# **5. Financial risk** 

# **Future cash flows** 

CARE Ratings carries out an analysis of projected operations of a project to get insights in the power project’s ability to service debt. The analysis would involve a critical examination of underlying assumptions, location of possible stress points and extent of flexibility available to tide over difficulties. CARE Ratings evaluates strength of key assumptions and trends in the projected free cash flow to arrive at a base case cover against the scheduled debt payment. CARE Ratings also sensitises the projected free cash flow through stress scenarios to assess the extent of buffer available for debt coverage. Refinance risks are analysed in relation to the overall debt profile and future earning capacity of the asset at the time of refinancing. 

# **Leverage** 

Generally, thermal power projects are financed at a debt-equity ratio of 70:30. For an operational project, the company’s capital structure would depend more on the depreciation policy adopted, apart from its revenue generation potential. Accordingly, the company’s capital structure is seen in consonance with its depreciation policy. CARE Ratings also looks at the Total debt to profit before interest, lease, depreciation, and taxation (TD/PBILDT) to nullify the effect of depreciation on leverage indicators. 

CARE Ratings also evaluates the quantum of capital creditors pending for settlement for a long time, large contingent liabilities and disputes, and financial impact in case of unfavourable events triggering payments.

# **Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)** 

CARE Ratings considers DSCR as one of the important ratios to assess relative debt servicing capability of the project, as it largely captures all critical aspects of the project. CARE Ratings analyses average DSCR and minimum DSCR for the tenure of the debt and DSCR in the next 3-5 years, while analysing debt repaying capability of the developer. CARE Ratings also sensitises base case DSCR for key variables of projects including PAF, PLF, tariff rates, fuel cost, finance cost, and payment delay from off-taker among others. 

# **Liquidity back-ups** 

As power from thermal projects is supplied to state Discoms, there exists a risk of delayed payment from counterparty. CARE Ratings considers adequate liquidity backup as an important rating consideration as debt repayments are normally evenly spread out (monthly/quarterly). Higher the delay by the counterparty, greater the liquidity buffer the developer needs to maintain to curtail off-taker payment risk. For a thermal power project, liquidity back-ups are created primarily in the form of debt service reserve account (DSRA), which covers 1-2 quarters of debt repayment obligations in the form of FD / bank guarantee and working capital limits. Stipulation and compliance in maintenance of designated accounts (escrow accounts, and TRA for maintaining priority in payment, among others) is seen positively in credit analysis. CARE Ratings assesses the company’s overall liquidity profile based on its methodology on “Liquidity Analysis of NonFinancial Sector Entities” on our website <u>www.careratings.com.</u> 

For evaluating detailed credit metrics, CARE Ratings follows its standard ratio analysis methodology to assess the financial risk of companies (Please refer to CARE Ratings’ Financial ratios – Non-Financial Sector on our website www.careratings.com). 

CARE Ratings analyses each of these factors and their linkages to arrive at the overall assessment of credit quality. Peer comparisons are carried out as an integral part of the financial analysis. Mitigation of credit risk due to credit enhancement provided is carefully evaluated before assigning the final rating. 

# **Environmental, Social and Governance Risk Factors** 

Environmental and Social (E&S) risks tend to be high for thermal plants, especially in construction, due to potential hazard of pollution and damage caused to the environment. 

Environmental Risk: Thermal plants, especially coal-fired ones, have large consumption of fossil fuel, high emission of pollutants and consume large quantity of water. Flexibility in financing thermal power plants tends to get limited due to increasing Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) awareness and compliances. Based on the location and proximity to cities, thermal plants have been notified timeline by MoEF&CC to install Flue Gas De-sulphurisation (FGD). Given the significant environmental risk posed by an operating thermal plant, initiatives with respect to FGD implementation by the company can be an indicator of its risk mitigation. CARE Ratings assesses implementation risk and risk associated with FGD capex recovery through future tariffs. The cost of the project, its rationality, means of finance and status of financial closure are factored in the analysis. In-principal approval of cost by the regulator provides some visibility (even though partial) with respect to recovery of cost and adds comfort to an extent. CARE Ratings assesses physical and financial progress of capex and implementation schedule. Presence of an experienced project management consultant and EPC contractor provides comfort to ratings.

Social Risk: The ability to manage social risk through smooth settlement of discord with locals in land acquisition is factored in the analysis. # **Conclusion** 

The rating outcome is ultimately an assessment of fundamentals and probabilities of change in fundamentals. CARE Ratings’ Ratings analyses each of these factors and their linkages to arrive at an overall assessment of credit quality, by considering the industry scenario. While the methodology encompasses comprehensive financial, commercial, economic and management analysis, credit rating is an overall assessment of all aspects of the issuer.
