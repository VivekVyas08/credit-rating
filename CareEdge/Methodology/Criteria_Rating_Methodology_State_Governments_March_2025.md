# **Background** 

CARE Ratings Limited (CareEdge Ratings) evaluates credit quality of state governments as part of the process of assigning ratings to debt instruments of state public sector enterprises (SPSEs) that are backed by explicit state support such as government guarantees, letter of comfort, among others or implicit support to strategically important SPSEs. Over the years, CareEdge Ratings has carried out the credit rating of state governmentguaranteed borrowings by state power distribution companies, irrigation corporations, road development corporations, state finance corporations, and infrastructure development corporations, to name a few. With more state government enterprises accessing the debt market for requirements with state government backing, credit ratings of state governments will become an important consideration in lending and investment decisions. 

CARE Ratings’ assessment of the credit quality of a state government is based on the following three broad factors, economic risk, financial risk, and economic management. 

# **1. Economic risk** 

Overall objective of the economic risk assessment is to provide a means of evaluating a state’s economic strengths and weaknesses. In general, greater strength of economic parameters will imply lower economic risks and vice versa. Relatively, a well-developed economic and social infrastructure serves as a critical input and reflects a strong economic structure. CareEdge Ratings believes that good social and economic infrastructure can result in improvement in the state government’s finances in the medium-to-long term, in terms of widening the tax base and lowering budgetary requirements of providing for development expenditure. Social infrastructure such as educational facilities and healthcare institutions by enabling human resource development will ultimately enable economic development. It is known that higher percapita incomes are an outcome of better economic development and contribute significantly to enhancing a state’s revenue potential. Indicators that CareEdge Ratings factor in its assessment of economic risk are described below: 

- **Growth and sectoral composition of gross state domestic product (GSDP)** 

CareEdge Ratings believes that strong secondary and tertiary sectors considerably enhance a state’s tax base, while providing a measure of stability to revenue flows. Over-dependence on the primary sector, in particular, agriculture, which CareEdge Ratings recognises as an important activity, can act as a constraint to a state’s tax potential under currently prevalent taxation structures. This usually also puts pressure on expenditure in the form of subsidies or loan waivers that are given. It has been seen by CareEdge Ratings in practice, that states with stronger secondary and tertiary sectors are more often the ones in better financial position. In our opinion, multiplicity and diversity of economic activities insulate the state from negative effect of downturns in output from one activity. 

- **Demographics** 

CareEdge Ratings analyses traditional demographic indicators since it believes that availability of healthy and trained manpower significantly improves a state’s growth potential, while removing hard budgetary constraints towards expenditure allocations on social development.

# **2. Financial risk** 

# **State government finances** 

CARE Ratings’ methodology makes an objective assessment of state government finances by looking at broad aspects, such as revenue performance, expenditure management, dependence on the state’s own revenues, dependence on external resources, deficit position, and debt profile including guarantees issued and other contingent liabilities. Since ratings are relative, an inter-state comparison is made of parameters to evaluate the risks and their impact on the credit profile for the state. CareEdge Ratings has identified a series of economic and financial indicators essential in understanding the performance, prospects, and hence, creditworthiness of a state government. While many parameters are quantifiable, subjective opinion is also applied to assess factors such as state policies, which although have a bearing on the economic and financial risks, do not readily lend themselves to quantification. CareEdge Ratings also assesses finances of a state government against fiscal responsibility and budget management (FRBM) targets, recommendations of the Finance Commission, and the extent of differences in the budgeted and revised estimates. 

- **Revenue performance** 

Typically, revenue sources may be classified under four heads: 

   - State’s Own tax revenue (SOTR) 

   - State’s own non-tax revenue (SONTR) 

   - State’s share in central taxes (SCT) 

   - Grants from the Centre. 

- Proportion of own tax revenues in total revenues, in CARE Ratings’ opinion, indicates the government’s ability to generate revenue and the degree of control it has over its revenues, which is vital to ascertain revenue stability. Typically, state governments with greater tax potential show a higher proportion of their own tax revenue in total revenue. Tax potential depends on per-capita income and composition of the state economy. Higher per capita income levels, signifying higher purchasing power, have the potential of generating higher revenues from consumption taxes. A larger share of the secondary or tertiary sector in a state’s economy translates into greater tax potential. On the other hand, a larger

The ‘gap filling’ approach adopted by the Finance Commission favours financially less-efficient states. However, there are attempts to rework and standardise the formula. CareEdge Ratings considers excessive dependence on this revenue source as a weakness as it indicates low self-reliance. Higher dependence on Finance Commission’s grants, which are given to fill the non-plan revenue deficit, indicates resource insufficiency and poor expenditure management and thus a credit weakness.

- **Expenditure management** 

In assessing expenditure management, CARE Ratings’ methodology tries to assess the efficacy of expenditure control mechanisms and efficiency in the use of state resources. CareEdge Ratings favourably views those state governments, whose expenditure is in tune with available resources. Particular attention is paid to composition of the expenditure, which is broadly classified as developmental and non-developmental. A growing proportion of non-development expenditure is viewed less favourable, as these are relatively less-productive expenditures. Efficiency of usage of resources and degree of control exercised on expenditure are other factors that are evaluated. For example, while subsidy is a large element in many government programmes, their ineffective targeting leads to wastage of resources. CareEdge Ratings also assesses the extent of committed expenditure, salaries, pensions, and interest payments in the total revenue expenditure, where the scope of curbing the expenditure is low. Hence, a low share of committed expenditure is considered favourable. Conversely, a higher share of capital expenditure against revenue expenditure is viewed favourably as it is an indicator of quality expenditure, which is pertinent from a long-term growth perspective and

# • **Deficit management** 

Well-managed state governments are those that not only can meet their revenue expenditure from their revenue receipts but also generate a revenue surplus to meet capex. Capex creates assets such as infrastructure that enhance future revenue generation potential of the state government by encouraging economic activity. Therefore, CareEdge Ratings is concerned about the trend and level of revenue deficit, as it reflects quality of management of state government finances and has the potential to translate into higher borrowing requirements, raising future debt service expenditure of the state government. In CARE Ratings’ opinion, the size and trend in the revenue deficit is a better indicator of financial stress than the gross fiscal deficit. The gross fiscal deficit is predicated on negotiated borrowing limits of the state government, to which expenditure then adjusts. However, composition of gross fiscal deficit gives meaningful insights into the purpose of borrowing. A lower share of revenue deficit in the gross fiscal deficit indicates that borrowings are predominantly to fund capex, which is sustainable if output growth exceeds the real interest rate. Of equal concern is the primary balance, which is the revenue balance after non-interest revenue expenditure of the state government. A negative primary balance indicates that the borrowing is taking recourse to finance current interest expenditure, while a positive balance indicates that all or part of the interest payment is being financed out of current revenues. A persistent primary deficit will lead to steady growth in debt, and thus, will need higher resources through tax and non-tax revenue for servicing the debt.

# **3. Economic management** 

CareEdge Ratings analyses economic policies of the state government to assess their impact on finances and economic development of the state. Key factors considered by CareEdge Ratings for assessing economic management of a state are: 

- **Industrialisation and investment policy framework** 

The level of industrial activity and its nature has a significant impact on the economic development of a state. For instance, primary industrial activities such as mining and metals are particularly susceptible to economic downturns compared to high technology industries, which offer more value-added products. CARE Ratings’ analysis also factors in specific projects that are being undertaken by the states to evaluate its future prospects. CareEdge Ratings also considers industrial policies and investment climate of the state that determines pace of economic growth. 

# **Economic policies** 

• Economic policies are of paramount importance for encouraging economic activity and growth. Unfavourable economic policies, including populist schemes such as waiver of loans or free electricity, make private sector investments unattractive and affect growth prospects of states, and thus, are detrimental to the fiscal health of the state. On the other hand, effective implementation and strict adherence to prudent economic policies are viewed more favourably. 

- **Political risk** 

The objective of political risk assessment is to provide a means of evaluating political stability of state governments. CareEdge Ratings believes that political stability is vital for continuity in economic decision-making and growth, as political consensus enables economic reforms. Political risk is a judgmental factor and is arrived at after considering stability of the state government, attitude of major political parties to important issues, socio-economic conditions, law and order, and quality of the administration. Political relationship between Central and state governments is also considered, as this may have an impact on discretionary grants from the Central Government and direction of investments in new projects by central public sector undertakings (PSUs), railways, National Highways, and so on, which may compliment/accelerate the state’s economic development.

# **Liquidity support** 

State governments have access to liquidity support through special drawing facilities (SDF), ways and means advances (WMA) and overdraft facilities from the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) to bridge their short-term resource gaps. CareEdge Ratings considers sustained usage of the liquidity support facility of the RBI as a poor liquidity management indicator and susceptibility to liquidity stress. 

In some instances, when the state government may not have availed WMA or overdraft from the RBI but failed to honour guarantee commitment to state entities, liquidity position of the state government is viewed negatively, as this reflects underlying state government’s inability to meet its commitments.

- **Management of guarantees** 

   - Guarantee management systems of state governments are assessed basis the government’s policy for extending guarantees, monitoring mechanisms, and threshold stance towards outstanding contingent liabilities. CareEdge Ratings also assesses maintenance of reserves such as consolidated sinking fund (CSF) and guarantee redemption fund (GRF) for meeting its debt and guarantee obligations in case of eventuality. 

- **Debt servicing track record** 

   - Assessment of debt servicing track record of entities that carry or carried the state government guarantee is critical in evaluating the risks associated with the state government’s guaranteed borrowings. Default in debt servicing of a state government guaranteed instruments will be an area of concern and have a bearing on the credit quality of all instruments that carry this state government’s guarantee. Timely information pertaining to guarantee invocation or delay in payment is generally not readily accessible for state guaranteed entities, which restricts response time. 

- **Performance of state PSUs** 

   - Performance of PSEs, boards, SPVs, and other state-promoted entities, especially state power utilities, has a significant bearing on state government finances. They have significant public investments locked-in, form an important component of state government capex and are claimants of planned resources. Poorly performing entities are also beneficiaries of explicit and implicit subsidies and loan bailouts. CareEdge Ratings also analyses non-guaranteed financial liabilities of financially dependent and strategically important state level entities, if deemed relevant and wherever such information is publicly available. 

# **Conclusion** 

In concluding its assessment of credit quality of a state government, CareEdge Ratings makes a careful study of overall risks arising from linkages between economic risks, financial risks, and economic management by constructing a risk profile and after inter-state comparisons. A credit rating is then assigned using CareEdge Ratings’ standard long-term rating scale.
