# **Industry Overview** 

Cotton is one of the principal crops in India, with it being one of the largest producers of cotton in the world along with China. The cotton textile industry value chain comprises cultivation, spinning, weaving/ knitting, processing, garmenting, distribution and retailing. India has an advantage in terms of availability of cotton and low labour cost. Industry players can be categorised as companies having presence in only one segment and those having presence in multiple segments across the value chain. The entire cotton textile value chain is highly fragmented, having small and large players operating, making the industry highly competitive. 

# **Methodology** 

CARE Ratings Limited (CARE Ratings) has a standard methodology for rating companies in the manufacturing sector, which is available on www.careratings.com. As per this methodology, CARE Ratings’ process begins with evaluation of the economy/industry, in which the company operates, followed by assessing business-risk factors specific to the company. This is followed by an assessment of financial and project-related risk factors and management quality. This methodology is followed while analysing all industries that come under the purview of the manufacturing sector. However, considering the size and diversity of these sectors, CARE Ratings has developed methodologies specific to industries within the sector. These methodologies attempt to point out factors above those mentioned in the broad methodology, which will be assessed while carrying out rating exercises of companies belonging to that particular industry. 

Following are such additional factors, and their analytical implications, considered by CARE Ratings, while arriving at the rating of players that operate in the cotton textile manufacturing industry. 

# **A. Business and Operational Risks 1. Operational Efficiency** 

**Raw material cost** constitutes the largest portion of the total operational cost in the entire cotton textile value chain. 

- **Cotton Spinners:** Cotton fibre prices are highly volatile, making profitability margins of cotton spinners susceptible to adverse fibre price fluctuations. Being an agricultural commodity, cotton availability and price depend on vagaries of nature, international and domestic demand and supply, expectation of crop in the on-going season and the next season. It also depdends on Minimum Support Price (MSP) fixed by the Government, time of procurement, polyester fibre/yarn prices and by the distance of cotton spinning unit from major cotton fibre sourcing centre. Cotton spinning companies are analysed based on their cotton procurement strategies and ability to manage price volitality. Companies capable of procuring large quantities of similar quality cotton fibre backed by orders are looked at favourably, as it enables them to maintain uniform quality of yarn manufactured. Cotton spinners capable of producing multiple counts/ varieties of yarns using different varieties of cotton fibres are viewed favourably, as it allows them to shift from one variety to the other in case of price fluctuations in one variety. Modernised spinning mills that enhance production yields, reduce waste and optimise raw material consumption, are viewed favourbaly.

- **Cotton Fabric Manufacturing:** For companies engaged in cotton fabric manufacturing, raw material procurement cost will depend on factors governing prices of cotton fibre and demand and availability of yarn. However, for a fabric manufacturer, profitability is less susceptible to adverse movement in the raw material (yarn) prices, as majority fabric manufacturers generally procure cotton yarn on need basis considering its ready availability throughout the year. 

- **Cotton garment manufacturer:** For a cotton garment manufacturer, raw material procurement cost depends on the demand-supply scenario for fibre, yarn and fabric. Susceptibility to adverse fluctuations in raw material (fabric) cost remains low, as most apparel manufacturers undertake manufacturing against confirmed orders, where prices are fixed as per prevailing fabric market prices. 

Fabric and garment manufacturers managing raw material price volatility by accumulating need-based inventory, are looked at favourably by CARE Ratings. Companies having integrated businesses (from yarnto-fabric/ from fabric-to-garment/ or, from yarn-to-garment) generally benefit from synergies associated with lower raw material procurement cost/lower logistics cost and are viewed positively. 

**Power cost** forms the next major cost component for a company engaged in the cotton textile value chain (especially spinning). Depending on the state where the unit is located and the source of power it uses, ‘power cost per unit produced’ can vary for different players. Different power tariffs and quality of supply in different states can put certain players at an advantageous position compared to their peers. Players using captive generation have an assured and uninterrupted supply of power. However, this increases capex requirement. Power cost incurred will also depend on the source of power (coal, diesel, or renewable) being used in the captive power unit, which can lead to difference in ‘power cost per unit produced’ for different mills. Managements’ initiatives in reducing power cost are looked at favourably. Players using different sources of power enable them to shift to the cheaper option, when available. Power cost will also be a function of level of plant modernisation, as relatively less power cost will be associated with a more modernised plant and vice versa. The compnay’s ability to regularly replace obsolete machines with modern state-of-the-art machines helps it reduce its power cost and is viewed favourably. 

**Labour relation and labour cost** play a significant role in efficient running of a cotton textile mill, especially for garment players. Companies having cordial labour relations are better placed in terms of labour productivity and smooth operations. Hence, adequacy of quality labour at competitive cost in the near vicinity of a textile mill is viewed positively. Mills having higher level of modernisation, have lesser reliance on labour and are viewed favourably. 

# **2. Inventory Management** 

- **Cotton Spinners:** Companies engaged in cotton yarn spinning are exposed to higher inventory risk compared to companies engaged in manufacturing manmade yarn. This is considering seasonal nature of cotton. Procurement of cotton by spinning mills start from October every year (with start of cotton harvesting) and continues till February-March. Mills have to ensure that they have sufficient raw material inventory in the off-season so that their operations are not affected. Spinning mills usually procure cotton fibre stock in the harvesting season to ensure optimisation of operations in the off-season. Its quantum is decided considering expected order flow, anticipated price and

availability of fibre in domestic and international markets. However, this exposes players to adverse fluctuations in raw material prices. Significant decline in fibre prices, especially for entities having excess inventory on their books, can lead to inventory losses. Therefore, the entity’s track record in terms of inventory stocking and its prevailing inventory policy is looked at closely. Proximity of mills to cotton growing regions reduces transportation costs and transit time helps in better inventory management and enhances profitability. Inventory management plays an important role as companies procuring optimum quantity of crop and stocking raw material can ensure availability of good quality raw material at a reasonable price. Owing to inherent volatility in cotton prices, excess stocking can expose the companies to inventory losses. Companies using efficient inventory management policies are viewed positively by CARE Ratings. 

- **Cotton Fabric and Garment manufacturer:** For fabric and garment manufacturers, the susceptibility of margins to raw material price fluctuations remains low as manufacturing is generally order backed, where prices are fixed per prevailing raw material market prices. Companies manufacturing against confirmed orders, fixing prices considering prevailing raw material prices, and maintaining raw material inventory position commensurate to their order book positions, are insulated from raw material price fluctuations. Such companies are viewed favourably by CARE Ratings. Players maintaining raw material inventory position higher or lower in proportion to their order book position bear the brunt in case of adverse fluctuations in raw material prices. Higher stocking of raw material compared to the order book position can affect margins in case of downward movement in raw material prices. Lower stocking can also have an impact on profitability margins if there are  upward movements in raw material prices. 

# **3. Scale and Flexibility** 

Large size generally implies benefits such as economies of scale, higher bargaining power and the ability to access different markets against small-size entities, which are generally operate in select market segments, exhibiting low resilience in margin protection, especially in adverse market conditions. 

As the industry is characterised by intense competition, profitability depends on volume of sales and product profile. Large capacities producing same variety of products lead to optimal utilisation of mills and offer benefits of economies of scale. However, level of production for two similar capacity units depends on specification of products being manufactured. A spinning unit manufacturing ‘finer count’ of yarn or a fabric unit manufacturing ‘higher thread count’ fabric will have relatively lower production levels. 

Companies having flexibility to manufacture diverse varieties of yarns (multiple count or different varieties of value-added yarns) or fabrics (gram per square metre range, and thread count range, among others) are looked at favourably, as it allows mills to account for lower/ higher demand in one particular variety, and hence, provides stability to the revenue stream. 

Apart from cost advantage, large capacities allow mills to deal directly with large domestic or international end-customers rather than selling products through dealers/ distributors. 

CARE Ratings views companies having flexibility to offer multiple product categories and having their own design capabilities to be better equipped to withstand cyclicality in demand. Players having ‘finer count’ yarn or ‘higher thread count’ fabric in their product mix, cater to the elite market segment, where demand is relatively less elastic and margins are high.

# **4. Level of Integration** 

Spinning mills having their own ginning facilities or engaged in contract farming for cotton get the advantage of uniform raw material quality. Such companies ensure regular supply of quality cotton. Fabric manufacturers having backward integration with spinning units save on to their freight expenses, selling cost and packaging expenses, apart from having better control on the quality. 

A garment manufacturing unit can also have backward integration with a fabric or a yarn manufacturing unit (though restricted to large players only because of the capital intensive nature). this generally leads to relatively higher profitability margins owing to more value-added jobs in-house. For a garmenting unit, backward integration with a fabric manufacturing unit also allows it to have better control on quality at weaving/knitting and processing stages. 

In an integrated unit, cost saving will also be a function of the level of modernisation at the mill as a more modernised plant will have better profitability margins owing to better efficiency. 

# **5. Geographical and Customer Diversification** 

CARE Ratings closely looks at sales concentration towards a particular end-customer or dealer or a particular geography, while evaluating companies. 

- **Geographical diversification:** Players catering to domestic and export markets can diversify risk of sluggishness in a particular market. Export-oriented companies catering to different countries have lower country-specific risk. 

- **Customer diversification:** Direct sales to end-customers can lead to better profitability margins for textile companies against sales made through the dealer network or through buying houses. However, this can lead to elongated payment terms and exposes companies to credit risk of endcustomers, especially if major sales are being derived from a particular set of customers. Sales through the dealer network, can result in bulk production for mills and timely payment realisation. A diversified customer profile helps reduce counterparty risk linked to reliance on few large customers. Domestic apparel manufacturers, who sell products through their own brand with presence across all end-user categories, target markets and seasonal offerings can minimise seasonality risk, sales volatility and adopt to evolving fashion trends. 

# **6.    Fiscal Incentives and Regulatory Risks** 

The Government of India provides fiscal incentives (Amended Technology Upgradation Fund Scheme [ATUFS], Scheme for Integrated Textile Parks [SITP], Rebate of State and Central Taxes and Levies [RoSCTL], among others) to companies operating in the textile value chain. These incentives constitute a major portion of the companies’ profitability margins and are looked at closely. To promote investment, certain State governments also provide fiscal incentives in the form of capital or interest subsidy to players setting up new textile units or undertaking modernisation at their existing units. CARE Ratings analyses incentives available to a company and its impact on profitability, capital structure and debt coverage indicators. 

The industry is governed by central and state government regulations. The Government of India decides the MSP of cotton every year. Fragmented and competitive nature of the industry and competition with manmade yarn fibre/ yarn (where prices are governed by crude oil prices) limits cotton spinning

# **B. Financial Risk** 

# **Liquidity management** 

Companies engaged in cotton yarn spinning have large working capital requirements in the peak season (in inventory procurement for the off-season), compared to their average requirements. Apparel manufacturers generally have seasonality in their operations with major sales happening in the winter season (festive season in India and abroad). However, owing to fixed nature of expenses, companies need to have sufficient liquidity cushion to meet operational expenses and debt obligations. CARE Ratings assesses the cushion available in the sanctioned working capital limits and other forms of liquidity available to companies to analyse liquidity position. High repayment obligations, especially when companies are holding excess inventory, can be a cause of concern. 

CARE Ratings takes cognisance of the entity’s top clients for their overall credit quality. Business relation with strong counter-parties ensures timely payments. Debtor ageing profile and concentration of debtors is closely looked at. Receivable position may vary depending on geographies catered to, with entities deriving significant income from exports generally having elongated average collection period. Entities taking letter of credits (LCs) from their clients and having cover from export credit guarantee corporation (ECGC) are viewed positively. 

# **Capital structure and debt coverage indicators** 

Being a capital intensive business, especially in spinning, companies having operations in the industry typically have high reliance on external debt to fund their fixed capital expenditure and working capital requirements. Fiscal benefits provided by governments [Technology Upgradation Fund Scheme (TUFS) and benefits provided by the central government to spinning players in the past or fiscal incentives provided by state governments] have also encouraged entities to aggressively increase their installed capacities or set-up new capacities. Hence, companies’ leverage ratios and debt coverage indicators in this industry are closely looked at in conjunction with their overall liquidity profile. Analysis of these ratios also hold importance due to cyclical nature of the industry, as comfortable capital structure and debt coverage indicators will make the company less vulnerable to cyclical downturns. 

Some of the ratios analysed by CARE Ratings include: long-term debt/ tangible net worth; total-debt/ tangible networth; total debt/ profit before interest, lease, depreciation, and taxation (PBILDT); total outside liabilities/

# **Foreign exchange risk analysis** 

Many companies in the industry are exposed to foreign exchange fluctuation risk due to export of products manufactured and/or import of raw materials. Though a company might be naturally hedged to an extent, policies to mitigate risk by availing working capital borrowings in foreign currency, and entering derivative contracts, among others, are closely looked at. In this regard, instances of heavy forex losses booked in the past and changes in hedging practices undertaken to mitigate reoccurence such losses are also anlaysed. 

# **Environmental, social and governance (ESG) risk factors** 

In its overall credit rating framework, CARE Ratings analyses critical ESG risks and their impact on the entity’s credit profile. CARE Ratings analyses materiality of ESG risk factors and mitigating factors being implemented by the entity. 

# **Environmental risk** 

Being a natural fibre, cotton is exposed to vagaries in agro climatic conditions. Cotton textile industry face environment risks related to compliance with stringent pollution control norms set by regulatory authorities. Processing units are exposed to proper disposal of hazardous and non-hazardous solid waste, which gets generated in the manufacturing process. Pollution control norms are also being strengthened every day. Violations in compliance with pollution control norms or strengthening of these norms would adversely impact operations. Entities that are gradually investing/ upgrading their infrastructure to meet pollution control norms related to water pollution, hazardous waste, and sourcing power from sustainable renewable energy, would be viewed favourably. Higher utilisation of organic cotton, which is also margin accretive, will be credit positive for players. There are range of certifications in textile industry such as Better Cotton Initiative (BCI), OEKOTEX, and Global Organic Textile Standard (GOTS) among others, which validate products that meet specific quality and safety criteria and production processes that adhere to strict standards. 

# **Social risk** 

Cotton textile entities face social risks related to labour and are exposed to social unrest/strikes within union/workforce. Compliance with labour laws and adequate safety for workers provide long-term sustainability. 

# **Governance risk** 

CARE Ratings looks at the composition of the board of directors and track record of legal and statutory compliance by its management/ directors to understand the corporate governance structure. Quality of disclosure related to financial statements, related party transactions and transparency in sharing information with stakeholders are also helpful to gauge corporate governance practices. 

# **Conclusion** 

The rating outcome is ultimately an assessment of fundamentals and probabilities of change in fundamentals. CARE Ratings analyses each factor and their linkages to arrive at the overall credit quality assessment, by considering the industry’s cyclicality.
