# **Introduction** 

In August 2006, the Securities & Exchange Board of India (SEBI) released the guidelines for capital protectionoriented schemes (CPOS). The said SEBI guidelines define a CPOS as a mutual fund scheme which is designated as such and which endeavours to protect the capital invested therein through ‘orientation of its portfolio structure’ and ‘not with guaranteed returns’. This means that the orientation towards protection of the capital originates from the portfolio structure of the scheme and not from bank guarantee, insurance cover or others. A CPOS aims at protecting investors from losing their capital in volatile markets, while at the same time providing them an opportunity to participate in capital market upturns. 

CARE Ratings Limited’s (CareEdge Ratings’) CPOS ratings assess the degree of certainty with which the portfolio structure can achieve the objective of capital protection on maturity of the scheme. Capital protection, in this context, means that the net asset value (NAV) should at least be equal to or greater than the face value of the scheme on maturity. A CPOS rating is a structured obligation and is conditional on the fulfilment and maintenance of certain minimum criteria by the asset management company (AMC). Since investments by these schemes are guided by a structure aimed at achieving capital protection, suffix (SO) will be attached to the rating symbols of CPOS. These schemes are close ended in nature. 

# **Methodology** 

CareEdge Ratings’ methodology for assessing the strength of the portfolio structure involves a comprehensive analysis of the investment strategy adopted by the AMC and the prevailing market conditions such that the maturity value of the debt component adjusted for expenses should be equal to the present value of the protected principal. Given these parameters, CareEdge Ratings assesses the structure of the portfolio under market scenarios and stressed conditions. CareEdge Ratings then estimates the likelihood of shortfall in the NAV with respect to the face value of the units of the scheme on maturity. Higher rating is assigned to the structures having a lower likelihood of a shortfall in NAV. 

CareEdge Ratings will obtain ‘Representations and Warranties’ from the AMC defining certain minimum conditions under which the structure will operate to ensure that the rated structure retains its basic characteristics. 

# **Investment Strategy** 

Though there are strategies and structures for ensuring capital protection, CareEdge Ratings’ methodology for rating CPOS is largely based on the application of static portfolio protection strategy. Under this strategy, capital protection is provided only through the debt component such that the maturity value of the debt component should be equal to the present value of the protected principal and the residual amount is invested in the equity component. At maturity of the scheme, the debt component compounds to the protected principal and the equity component provides the returns. In case of changes in strategy by AMC, the same shall be evaluated accordingly.

# **Determination of the initial debt component** 

CareEdge Ratings analyses the indicative portfolio for the minimum debt proportion of the scheme required to ensure capital protection on maturity of the scheme. The debt levels depend on the expected yield and duration of the debt securities. CareEdge Ratings assesses the following risk factors in its analysis of the scheme for its CPOS ratings. 

# **Credit Risk** 

Credit risk of the debt component comprises the risk of default and the risk of downgrade of debt securities. The extant SEBI guidelines do not allow CPOS to invest in debt instruments having credit rating of below ‘AAA’ which indicate highest degree of safety; as a result, the schemes invest in securities having the highest credit rating (including the securities issued by Government of India). 

To ensure lowest credit risk of underlying debt portfolio, CareEdge Ratings obtains 'Representations and Warranties’ from the AMC which stipulates the following: 

- The investment in debt security shall be in the form of government securities or securities rated as ‘CARE AAA’ or equivalent. 

- Action on downgrade of debt security. 

In case of any debt security being downgraded, it shall be sold and replaced with government security or security rated as ‘CARE AAA’ or equivalent. In case this is not possible, CareEdge Ratings shall re-evaluate the entire portfolio (equity and debt investments of the scheme) to review the rating assigned to the capital protected fund. 

# **Reinvestment Risk** 

Reinvestment risk refers to the risk that interim cash flows (coupons) received from the debt component may get reinvested at lower than the original yield. CareEdge Ratings analyses the reinvestment risk assuming that the inflows are reinvested at a low interest rate. The expected impact of reinvestment risk needs to be adequately covered by allocation to debt component. 

# **Market Risk** 

Market risk is the risk of a fall in the market value of portfolio due to change in the underlying market factors, including equity prices, interest rates, exchange rates, and inflation, among others. Market risk for the debt component can affect the portfolio if maturity of the debt instruments cannot be aligned with maturity of the scheme due to nonavailability of instruments. However, for static strategies, the debt component is generally managed passively, where the debt instruments are held till maturity and portfolio churn only happens in case of deterioration in credit quality of the paper. 

# **Liquidity / Marketability Risk** 

Liquidity/Marketability risk refers to the difficulty of selling or buying an investment. Some debt instruments are not actively traded in the secondary market and may be difficult to offload and are characterised by a wider bidask spread. In the equity market, some stocks are thinly traded and may entail high impact cost at the time of selling. For dynamic strategies, this risk becomes critical and is factored in the rating. For all the strategies, redemption management at the time of maturity of the scheme is also analysed under marketability risk.

# **Gap Risk / Crash Risk:** 

Gap risk refers to the occurrence of a situation called a gap event, where portfolio value falls below the floor and capital protection is violated. A gap event may occur due to credit risk, reinvestment risk, market risk, or liquidity risk (explained below), which need to be evaluated in the context of the gap risk. Value of the gearing factor (multiplier) in Constant Proportion Portfolio Insurance<sup>1</sup> (CPPI) strategy needs to be determined only after thoroughly projecting the gap risk factors: 

1. Volatility of the equity component - a sudden fall in its value can bring the structure close to the gap event. 

2. Simultaneous liquidity in debt and equity markets - Availability of debt instruments and marketability of the equity at time of crash in prices determines the structure's ability to transfer securities just before the gap event. These risks can be mitigated to some extent by limiting the size of the multiplier and carefully monitoring the volatility of the equity component and credit risk of the debt component. 

# **Float Risk** 

Float risk considers the opportunity costs, in case the funds are not deployed in a timely manner. Delayed deployment of funds may lead to investments being done at lower interest rates than originally planned. 

# **Structured Obligation** 

CareEdge Ratings’ CPOS ratings are based on the fulfilment of certain minimum conditions by the AMC under which the scheme will operate. The AMC needs to ensure that these minimum conditions are met at all times in the life of the scheme, as a violation of any of these conditions may adversely affect the structure of the scheme and the rating. 

Ratings are based on warranties from the AMC, which are required to be adhered to, till the maturity of the scheme. These conditions relate to: 

- Asset allocation - Ensuring a certain proportion of investment in the debt component depending on the investment strategy. 

- Screening rules - A predefined universe of instruments in which the fund can invest, based on their credit quality, volatility, and liquidity, among others. 

- Structure covenants- Adherence to the basic investment strategy and structure. 

- Maturity profile of debt instruments. 

- Any other condition adding to the strength of the portfolio structure.

Once the scheme has received regulatory clearances for launch and proceeds are invested in accordance with warranties executed, CareEdge Ratings will provide a final rating to the scheme. # **Monitoring of Rating** 

Based on information from AMCs, CareEdge Ratings conducts review of CPOS quarterly to analyse and test the strength of the portfolio structure such that the maturity value of the debt component adjusted for expenses should be equal to the present value of the protected principal. 

# **What CPOS Ratings are not?** 

- CareEdge Ratings’ CPOS ratings are not recommendations to purchase, sell, or hold a security. These ratings do not comment on the volatility of NAV of the scheme or the level of NAV compared to the face value in the tenure of the scheme any time before maturity. Ratings are valid only for the specific rated CPOS and are valid only during the tenure of the scheme till maturity of the scheme. However, CareEdge Ratings reserves the right to revise/reaffirm/withdraw the rating at any time based on new event or information or on unavailability of information or any other circumstances, which CareEdge Ratings believes may have impact on the rating. 

- These ratings are not an opinion on the fund's management practices (including fund structure, expense ratios, and marketing activities), financial performance and management quality of an AMC and do not factor in the business practices, reputation risks, and compliance to regulatory requirements. 

# **SEBI Regulations** 

SEBI issued regulations on CPOS on August 3, 2006, and issued a notification with further clarifications on August 14, 2006. The key provisions of the regulations are presented below: The regulations require that in all the documents and advertisements, the mutual fund should disclose that the scheme is "oriented towards protection of capital" and "not with guaranteed returns". 

- The orientation towards protection of capital should originate from the portfolio structure and not from bank guarantee, or insurance cover, or others. 

- Close-ended and No Re-purchase - All CPOS should be close-ended and the AMC is not allowed to repurchase units of the scheme before maturity. This implies that there is no exit route available to the investors unless the scheme is listed on an exchange 

- Debt component of the portfolio structure should be of the highest investment grade rating. 

- Rating -SEBI regulations state that "the proposed portfolio structure indicated in the offer document and key information memorandum must be rated by a SEBI-registered credit rating agency from the view point of assessing the degree of certainty for achieving the objective of capital protection." 

- Rating should be reviewed quarterly.
