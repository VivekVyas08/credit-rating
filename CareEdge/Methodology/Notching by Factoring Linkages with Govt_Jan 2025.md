# **CARE Ratings’ Criteria on Notching by Factoring Linkages with Government** [Issued in August 2022] [Issued in January 2025] 

## **Background** 

The credit risk assessment of the entities supported by the central or state government would capture the linkages with the respective government. Public Sector Entities (PSEs) and/or entities majorly held by the PSEs may receive support from the central / state governments depending upon their status, role, policy function served, strategic importance to the government and implications of default on the sector. The analysis of the linkages of the entity with the government would be applied as a building block on the top of the standalone credit risk assessment and will result in a rating which is notched-up vis-à-vis the entity’s standalone rating and would be based upon the track record, probability and extent of support that the government provides to the entity. This paper aims to provide an insight into the effect of these linkages and the extent of notch-up based on the strength of the linkages, on the rating of an entity. 

CARE Ratings Limited (CARE Ratings) takes a view on the strength of the linkages by assessing the following factors: 

- **Economic and strategic importance of the entity to the centre / state** 

   - Extent of ownership and control of the government 

   - `o` Policy function served through the entity 

- **Demonstrated track record of support by the government** `o` Instances of timely support in the past 

- **Legal or moral obligation of the government to support the entity** 

   - Guarantees, letters of comfort, undertakings, etc. given by the government. Please refer to <u>www.careratings.com</u> for the methodology on ‘Rating Credit Enhanced Debt’ for more details on this aspect. 

   - `o` Probable impact of default of the entity on the sector 

The above factors are elaborated below: 

## **a) Economic and strategic importance of the entity to the centre / state** 

- **Extent of ownership and control of the government:** The economic and strategic importance of an entity to the centre / state is assessed by the extent of ownership / control of the government in the operations of the entity. 

- **Policy function served through the entity:** CARE Ratings takes into account the strategic importance of the entities to the government and the objectives / policy function served by them while assessing the extent of the linkages of such entities with the government. Entities operating in sectors of significant economic and strategic importance would be exhibiting strong linkages with the government. Entities which are established with a mandate to take forward certain socio-economic objectives of the government like infrastructure development or providing finance to strategically important sectors, etc. operate as extended arms of the government, and thus the government is expected to provide the required support to these entities to take forward these socioeconomic objectives. It is expected that the government would support such entities in times of distress in view of their strategic importance of the socio-economic objectives being fulfilled by them.

# **b) Demonstrated track record of support by the government** 

The demonstrated track record of the government in extending support to the PSEs in the past underlines the government’s willingness to extend support. Explicit financial support by way of budgetary allocations, subsidy payments, facilitation of bank lines or other means of finance, capitalisation, extension of loans, etc., also highlights the government’s stance towards supporting the PSEs. 

# **c) Legal or moral obligation of the government to support the entity** 

# **a. Guarantees / letters of comfort etc. from the government:** 

   - The government’s commitment to an entity is further strengthened if there is an explicit support extended by the government to the entity in the form of legally enforceable arrangements like unconditional and irrevocable guarantees, undertakings, , etc. An unconditional and irrevocable guarantee with a structured payment mechanism given by the government to the lenders of the PSE works on the principle of credit substitution and the rating of the PSE is equated to the rating of the government. The ratings in these cases are suffixed with the symbol ‘CE’ (Credit Enhancement) which denotes comfort derived from an explicit external credit enhancement. Further details about the approach on external credit enhancements can be accessed from CARE Ratings’ methodology on ‘Rating Credit Enhanced Debt’ on www.careratings.com. The support can also be demonstrated through arrangements like letters of comfort provided by the government which entail a moral obligation on the government. Presence of such arrangements further emphasizes the strength of the linkages, and the rating of the PSE is notched-up appropriately depending upon the strength of the stated support. 

- **b. Probable impact of default of the entity on the sector:** 

CARE Ratings assesses the impact of the probable default by an entity on other entities in the sector. If default by an entity is likely to have large scale implications in terms of cascading effect on other entities in the sector, it is more likely to timely receive distressed support from the government. PSEs belonging to certain sectors like banks / non-banking financial institutions are systemically important and thus, a default by any of these entities may have a spiral effect on the financial markets. It is thus expected that the government will support these entities in times of distress. 

# **Extent of notch-up** 

CARE Ratings notches-up the rating of the PSE depending upon the extent and strength of the linkages as explained above. If the linkages are assessed to be very strong due to strategic importance of the entity to the government, the government’s demonstrated track record to support the PSE or legal or moral obligation of the government to support the PSE, the standalone rating of the PSE can be notched-up by multiple notches and may also be equated with the rating of the government. For e.g. the rating of the central government entities which are of significant strategic importance to the centre may be equated with the sovereign (‘CARE AAA’) rating. In case, the extent of the linkages are assessed to be moderate, the rating of the PSE after notching-up will be somewhere between the standalone rating of the PSE and the rating of the government. A commercial entity not serving any policy functions dedicatedly or not having significant socio-economic objectives, even if majorly owned by the government, would tend to operate largely independently, and may face competition from the private players in the segment. Such entities can be found mainly in the manufacturing sector and would exhibit lower integration with the government’s policy framework. These entities are generally assessed on the basis of their standalone credit profile without any significant notch-up.
