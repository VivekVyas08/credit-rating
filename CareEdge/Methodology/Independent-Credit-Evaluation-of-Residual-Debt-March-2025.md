## **Background:** 

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) had introduced various schemes from time to time which aimed at resolution of the stressed assets in the banking system (e.g., Corporate Debt Restructuring, Revitalising Distressed Assets – Joint Lenders’ Forum (JLF) and Corrective Action Plan (CAP), Flexible Restructuring of Long-term Project Loans, Strategic Debt Restructuring, etc.). 

On February 12, 2018, RBI issued a circular titled ‘Resolution of Stressed Assets – Revised Framework’ for resolution of stressed assets through which all the previous circulars / schemes were substituted with a simplified framework for the resolution of stressed assets, consistent with the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC). Later, on June 07, 2019, RBI revised the above framework and issued a circular titled ‘Prudential Framework for Resolution of Stressed Assets’. The framework, inter-alia, extended the scope to a large universe of lenders, including non-banking finance companies (NBFCs) and small finance banks. As per the said circular, the decision to take defaulters to the bankruptcy court is voluntary for the lenders and all the lenders must put in place Board-approved policies for resolution of stressed assets, including the timelines for such resolution. In case a borrower is reported to be in default by any of the lenders, the lenders shall undertake a prima facie review of the borrower’s account within 30 days from such default. Also, disincentives such as additional provisioning for delay in implementation of resolution plan and penal provisions were introduced for the lenders. 

In its June 2019 circular, RBI stipulated that for stressed accounts having exposure in excess of Rs.100 crore where the resolution plan involves restructuring or change in the ownership, the lenders would have to get an independent credit evaluation (ICE) of the residual debt<sup>1</sup> by an authorized Credit Rating Agency (CRA), and cases where the exposure is over Rs.500 crore, the ICE has to be undertaken by at least two CRAs. CARE Ratings Limited (CARE Ratings) has been authorized by RBI to undertake ICE under the said framework. 

The eligible borrowers must apply for restructuring to the lending institutions by submitting a resolution plan. The lending institutions are required to frame Board-approved policies within the contours of RBI circular to approve or reject such plans. A resolution plan is said to be approved, or the resolution is said to be invoked, when 75% of the lenders by value and 60% of the lenders by number agree to the RP. Post approval, there is a process of signing the inter-creditor agreement within 30 days. The date of implementation of the plan must be within 180 days of the invocation date. 

As per the same RBI circular, ‘specified period’ refers to the “period from the date of implementation of RP up to the date by which at least 20 per cent of the sum of outstanding principal debt<sup>2</sup> as per the RP and interest capitalization sanctioned as part of the restructuring, if any, is repaid.” Hence, after a resolution plan is implemented, “any default by the borrower in any of the credit facilities with any of the lenders (including any lender where the borrower is not in ‘specified period’) subsequent to upgrade in asset classification as above but before the end of the specified period, will require a fresh RP to be implemented”. 

> 1The residual debt of the borrower entity means the aggregate debt (fund-based as well as non-fund-based) envisaged to be held by all the lenders as per the proposed resolution plan. 

> 2Outstanding principal debt shall include all credit facilities, including debt / debt like instruments (viz., non-convertible debentures, optionally convertible debentures, optionally convertible preference shares, non-convertible preference shares etc.) that exist post implementation of the resolution plan. Only equity and instruments compulsorily convertible into equity (without any embedded optionality) shall be exempt from determining outstanding principal debt.

# **Methodology:** 

Credit rating of an entity begins with a review of the economy / industry in which the entity operates along with an assessment of the business risk factors specific to the entity. This is followed by an assessment of the financial risk factors and quality of the management of the entity. For project-stage entities / entities undertaking large projects, project risk is also analyzed for arriving at the entity’s credit rating. For the purpose of ICE, broadly the same methodology is used for assessment along with industry-specific factors pertaining to the sector in which the entity is engaged in. Refer to www.careratings.com for various methodologies / criteria. 

However, the prominent difference between credit rating and ICE is that ICE is undertaken: 

1. for the residual debt of an entity and 

2. relies on a ‘plan’; to analyse sufficiency of cash flows vis-à-vis timely repayment obligations of the residual debt, with more emphasis on cash flow adequacy during specified period. 

   - Typically, a resolution plan is drafted by the lenders based on techno-economic viability study, quantum of residual debt, etc., basis which operational cash flows from the business as well as extraordinary cash flows out of sale of non-core assets, recovery of debtors / claims, etc.) can be used for timely servicing of the residual debt. It is important to note here that, like credit rating, a single day missed payment is treated as default.

# **Approach:** 

Keeping in view the nuances of ICE explained above, CARE Ratings evaluates the following aspects: 

**1. Analyzing why the stress occurred and how those factors are addressed in the resolution plan:** It is important to probe into the reasons why the entity got into stress in the first place. It could be due to various reasons such as industry (downturn in the industry cycle), business (availability of raw material, utilities, competition etc.), or financial factors (capital requirements, skewed capital structure etc.). CARE Ratings analyses factors that caused stress and how those factors are addressed in the resolution plan for its successful implementation so that the entity does not encounter the same stress again. 

**2. Capability of the existing management / promoters to turn around:** 

   - If the failure of the business was NOT attributable to the existing management / promoters, and the lenders are working out turnaround / restructuring plan with the same management / promoters, it is important to examine their resourcefulness and risk appetite to turn around the operations. In such a case, the resolution plan may include conditions pertaining to commitment from the existing management / promoters, e.g., equity contribution, maintenance of Debt Service Reserve Account (DSRA), etc. 

**3. Competence, relevance and resourcefulness of the new management / promoters:** 

   - At times, the turnaround strategy may encompass change in the management / promoters. In such a case, competence and relevant experience of the new management / promoters in the industry / sector is examined. Also, synergies, strategic importance of the business to the overall business philosophy of the new management / promoters, their commitment, credit risk profile, risk appetite and resourcefulness to bring in required funds, etc., are also factored in. 

# **4. Stress testing of the cash flows as per the resolution plan:** 

CARE Ratings analyses the future cash flow generation capability of the entity. For the same, various assumptions underlying the financial projections and cash flows as per the resolution plan are sensitized to critically examine the impact on the debt servicing ability. 

# **5. Liquidity backup:** 

A resolution plan which stipulates maintenance of DSRA (either in a funded manner or as non-funded bank guarantee or promoter support) and provision to top it up in case of utilization, provides liquidity cushion to withstand unforeseen cash flow volatilities. Thus, CARE Ratings examines presence of any liquidity back-ups in the resolution plan.

# **Periodic monitoring of ICE:** 

ICE of a resolution plan for the stressed assets is a one-time exercise to be used by the lenders. As such, these ratings are not reviewed subsequently. 

# **Conclusion:** 

ICE outcome is ultimately an assessment of various assumptions underlying the resolution plan and likelihood of successful implementation of the same. CARE Ratings analyses each of the factors covering industry, business, management and financial factors and their linkages to arrive at the overall assessment of credit quality for the residual debt as per the resolution plan of the stressed asset.
