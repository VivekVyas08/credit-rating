# **Background** 

Lease rental discounting (LRD) is essentially a loan facility availed by an entity against future expected rentals from types of properties, including  commercial, retail, and warehouses, among others, earning a specified amount of rentals at scheduled intervals. Future rentals are generally governed by contractual lease agreements with lock-in periods and periodic rent escalation clauses. The quantum of debt facility to be extended by lenders is typically assessed based on valuation of rental properties and discounted value of expected rentals from such properties. Rentals and other incidental revenue such as common area maintenance (CAM), parking, and revenue share derived from such properties are deposited into a designated escrow account, on which the lender has first charge and application of such funds generally concurs with well-defined waterfall mechanism with statutory payments and debt commitments being the priority. Usually, in construction phase, developers avail term loan and post construction and tie-up with tenants for rentals; such term loan gets converted into LRDs. 

# **Methodology and its scope** 

CARE Ratings Limited (CARE Ratings) analyses risk factors associated with leased out properties, such as type of asset, location, maintenance of properties, occupancy level, lock-in tenure, loan-to-value, lease terms, debt to rentals, tenants’ profile and track record of developer in leasing out facilities. Emphasis is also placed on cash flow analysis and evaluation of financial flexibility available to developers by determining how projected rental cash flows (net of operational expenditure such as CAM charges, electricity charges, property taxes, insurance, among others) will be applied towards debt commitments. 

Developers often execute residential and commercial projects for sale and leasing projects, in such cases, this methodology is applicable on factors over and above those covered in the methodology on Real Estate projects (wherever applicable). This methodology should not be construed as comprehensive list of all factors that are reflected in ratings, however, it covers parameters that are most critical to understand. # **A. Management evaluation** 

A developer with an understanding of local area nuances, established brand image in area of operations, demonstrated track record of quality construction and timely delivery has a competitive advantage. Companies that have been through business cycles are generally better placed compared to peers with limited experience. Management quality of the property developer/manager influences the ability to negotiate favourable terms with tenants and lenders, and attract and retain tenants. 

CARE Ratings takes cognisance of resourcefulness of the management/promoters, financial strength of the group, and involvement of the group in other business segments. While the rating exercise is focused on cash flow analysis, CARE Ratings also reviews other qualitative elements, such as auditor qualifications, pledge of promoter shareholding, and RERA details among others, to analyse if there are critical observations against the developer and related financial implications. 

# **Experience of promoters/top management in real estate development** 

Experience and track record of the promoters/management in the line of business is given significant importance, while evaluating management competency and quality. Considering the capital-intensive nature of the business, a company with financially strong promoters enjoys an advantage over others as its ability to infuse and/or raise funds in a timely manner is better. 

# **Real estate space developed & leased out by the group in the past** 

Completed real estate projects in the past and their leasing indicates the promoters’ operational level expertise. CARE Ratings places emphasis on details of past projects such as scale, location and types of projects executed (commercial malls, commercial offices, warehouses, etc.) to better understand the developer’s experience. 

# **Constitution of the entity** 

The legal constitution of an entity indicates the level of oversight, and hence, has a bearing on the governance and transparency requirements, which an entity is subjected to. Partnerships/proprietorships are subject to comparatively milder disclosures and regulatory requirements than private/public limited companies. Such entities also carry an inherent risk of withdrawal of capital which is factored appropriately in ratings of partnerships/proprietorship concerns. 

# **Organisational structure and group support** 

Typically, forming special purpose vehicles (SPVs) for undertaking different functions and projects is a general industry norm. 

In a group structure, where leasing projects are executed through SPVs, such SPVs are usually analysed with project-wise cash flows, on a standalone level. However, with high level of financial and operational linkages between the group’s real estate entities, CARE Ratings generally attempts to understand risks at the group level and inter-company transactions are carefully evaluated. Financials, including cash flow details and operational details of other group entities are generally assessed on best-effort basis. CARE Ratings also attempts to assess the level of support extended by the parent and is generally factored in the analysis. Please refer to CARE Ratings’ website <u>www.careratings.com</u> for CARE Ratings’ criteria on notching by factoring linkages in the rating.

# **B. Business risk evaluation** 

Understanding business risk of an entity is a combination of assessment of asset profile and tenants’ profile. CARE Ratings performs the detailed analysis of the assets being leased out and aspects associated with the assets. The evaluation of asset profile provides broad indication ~the market position of the developer after analysing factors, such as property’s location and asset quality, average lease rentals ~the vicinity, and average occupancy ratio. These factors are considered in conjunction with other factors, such as promoters’ track record and their exposure to business cycles, competitive position and the leasing track record of the assets. CARE Ratings evaluates the tenants’ profile by taking factors into consideration such as tenant concentration, types of tenants, credit quality of tenants and lock-in tenure of leases. The key factors relevant to assess the business risk are covered below: 

# **Evaluation of execution risk in project stage entities** 

While assessing execution risk, there are factors that requires consideration, such as experience of the developer in the industry and region of operation, construction progress of the portfolio and ability of the developer to obtain timely approvals of the project. Real estate projects require multiple approvals from several authorities (central, state and local) across stages of project execution. Delays in getting such approvals often hamper progress of projects per the envisaged schedule. Once facilities are constructed and relevant spaces are leased out, term loan is normally converted into LRDs to get advantage of lower finance costs and efficiently manage debt servicing. Factors considered while rating the project-stage entities include identification of land and construction related approvals, stage of construction and related construction costs, past track record of developer in executing such projects, significance of the project to the group, track record of the group in supporting the SPV executing such project, cost and time overrun, percentage of Letter of Intent, prevailing rental rates in the vicinity, funding pattern, and financial closure and terms of sanction, among others. 

# **Location and construction quality** 

Type, location, upkeep of assets, surrounding infrastructure, and amenities provided are evaluated to determine pricing power to lease out assets or roll over already leased assets at envisaged rates. Grade A assets positioned at prime location attract strong tenants with high credit quality, providing financial discipline to the company. Further emphasis is placed on type of assets being leased out (commercial offices, malls, warehouses, among others) as the prime location differs for different types of assets. In case a location is remote and it remains in proximity to the key industrial area, then it could be considered as prime for warehouses with good approachability and vicinity. Similarly, the location covering prime areas of city and target population is considered crucial for shopping malls, prime location and good connectivity for offices spaces and proximity to factory location of the client and connectivity to state/national highways for warehouses. 

With different asset classes being impacted differently and prone to different risks, while performing analysis, trends in vacancy rates, lease rentals, and lease collection efficiency are considered to assess the company’s credit profile. 

# **Market standing and demand-supply outlook** 

CARE Ratings views the developer’s market position to determine if the firm has an established position in a particular micro-market and if such leadership translates into strong pricing position. While analysing demand in the micro-market where the property is located, CARE Ratings considers proximity to key areas,

CARE Ratings analyses demand-supply gap and accordingly determines if such scenario would mitigate or enhance vacancy risk. Strong demand with weak supply in a micro-market benefits the entity in filling vacant spaces and would have a positive bearing on rental rates. Conversely, weak demand with oversupply could divert existing tenants to other properties, and thus, weaken customer stickiness. # **Occupancy levels** 

CARE Ratings analyses past vacancy trend of properties forming part of the developer’s portfolio, which is compared to the current vacancy level. Change in occupancy rate is considered to determine the reason for variation and future occupancies are delved upon. Lower occupancy level in the past could be due to properties under construction phase, however, reputed developers often let out the space by tying up with tenants through letter of intent, and thus, vacancy risk in the future gets mitigated. CARE Ratings analyses demand-supply trends in the micro-market and prevailing rental rates and type of lessees. 

CARE Ratings also assesses diversity in terms of geography, tenants, and industry of tenants. Properties in multiple geographies are viewed positively as it protects the developer against the downturn in single micro-market. Similarly, diversified tenant profile entails stability in the occupancy levels and cash flows. CARE Ratings also considers earnings before interest, taxation, depreciation, and amortisation (EBITDA) margin at an entity and asset level to understand operational efficiency. 

# **Tenant profile assessment** 

CARE Ratings evaluates the tenants’ profile by taking several factors into consideration, such as tenant concentration, industry concentration, lease terms, and track record of timely rental payments. 

Typically, high tenant concentration could have a bearing on occupancy of the rented property as worsening credit quality of a single tenant could delay collection of lease rentals, which may impair the lessee’s financial capability. While the property occupied by multiple and diversified set of tenants with high occupancy levels entails lower stress on cash flows. Major emphasis is placed on credit profile of tenants as in exceptional cases, where properties leased out to single tenant with strong fundamentals at favourable leasing/lock in terms, generally garner more stable cash flows compared to multiple tenants with moderate credit profile occupying the property. 

Similarly, high number of tenants from single industry, such as ITes, financial institutions, or automobile, among others, could result into higher vacancy if that industry witnesses slowdown. 

Credit quality of the tenant is evaluated to determine certainty of committed rental inflows from such tenants. Favourable credit quality of tenants indicates stability of rental inflows and likelihood that terms of lease agreements will be honoured over life of leases, even during downturn. 

CARE Ratings also attempts to understand other critical factors, such as significant investments by tenants in fit-outs, and other challenges in shifting from existing property, among others, to gauge tenant stickiness towards the property. # **Evaluation of lease agreements and lease renewal risk** 

Features of lease agreements such as lease tenor, lock-in period tenor, renewal of lease, security deposits, notice period, and rental escalations, among others, are evaluated. For understanding lease renewal risk, CARE Ratings considers weighted average lease expiry (WALE) against average maturity of residual debt. If the expiry of most lease agreements falls near or beyond loan tenure, it provides stability in cash flows and reduces credit risk of the entity to a major extent. Typically, retail malls would have shorter leasing period compared to other two types of assets. This can enable malls to attain positive pre-leasing spread post vacancy. While lease agreements expiring within loan tenure expose the entity to roll-over risk, tie up of leases with sizeable lock-in period and regular escalations over time ensures stability in rental income and overall cash flows, reducing the vacancy risk. CARE Ratings also considers timeframe of notice periods covered in underlying lease agreements. Shorter notice period could adversely impact occupancy level of entities especially during downturns. Thus, longer lease tenor with reasonable lock-in periods and availability of renewal clauses with fixed escalations and longer tenure notice periods provide stability to cash flows and are seen as credit positive. 

# **C. Financial risk evaluation** 

While assessing the financial risk profile, CARE Ratings takes note of critical ratios of debt to equity, loan to value, debt to rental/debt to profit before interest lease depreciation and taxes (PBILDT) and results are compared to peer firms with similar asset portfolios. Lower ratios imply better financial discipline of the developer and strong ability to withstand economic cycles. Financial ratios are correlated for analysis and are not seen in isolation. CARE Ratings also considers robustness of sanction terms of loan facility with rent receipts flowing to designated escrow account, debt service reserve account (DSRA) and well-defined waterfall mechanism. The entity’s funding pattern and utilisation of loans is also checked thoroughly. Key parameters considered, while analysing financial risk are covered below in more detail: 

# **Debt financing and sanction terms** 

Sanction terms of LRD loan are viewed to understand if such terms stipulate the entity to maintain additional security measures such as ringfencing of cash flows, maintenance of DSRA, time gap between rental receipt date and scheduled repayment dates, among others. Presence of defined mechanisms such as routing of rent through escrow account and discipline of such cash flows entails timely servicing of debt commitments. In such mechanisms, escrow account typically remains in control of the lender and accordingly rental receipts are utilised for meeting debt commitments on priority after meeting statutory obligations and remaining amount could be utilised for other expenses based on approvals from lender. Maintenance of DSRA is another aspect that provides liquid cushion in repayment of debt, particularly in case of delay in payment of rent by tenants or in case of tenant vacating the space. Usually, a DSRA equivalent to one to three months of instalments is maintained by the companies in accordance with sanction terms. The time gap available between rent receipt date and loan repayment dates also provide additional comfort as sufficient time gap between the two dates mitigates risk arising from delay in rental payment by the tenant.

# **Refinancing risk** 

New debt structures where debt of short tenure with bullet repayment are availed by companies. In such cases, CARE Ratings analyses strength of sponsor and asset, loan to value (LTV) and presence of liquidity to assess refinancing ability at maturity. 

# **Leverage and financial flexibility** 

Gearing is an important metric to analyse financial profile of an entity, since the capital base required for acquisition/construction of assets in the real estate industry is normally high and assets are largely funded through external borrowings. Therefore, strong promoters / institution backed entities have greater financial flexibility / refinancing options. While projects are normally executed under SPV having lower net worth base, gearing levels of such entities normally tend to be higher. Hence, CARE Ratings also considers the loan to value ratio, based on latest available asset valuation report to determine the company’s financial flexibility. However, sanction terms are also given cognisance, while analysing the company’s financial flexibility as refinancing could be limited if sanction terms stipulate strong covenants directly or indirectly restricting the entity to raise additional debt. While assessing financial profile of an entity, CARE Ratings also evaluates another important metric of ‘debt to rental’. The ratio indicates the number of years an entity will take to repay the entire debt at average annuity income. Lower the ratio, the better the financial capability of entity. An entity with lower gearing, loan-to-value and debt to rental is viewed positively due to its better financial flexibility and ability to avail additional debt in unprecedented economic scenarios. 

# **Cash flow analysis** 

CARE Ratings analyses visibility and sustainability of cash flows against the company’s debt servicing obligation. Stability of cash flows can also vary depending on the industry segment (retail, office or warehouse), which was observed in COVID-19 where the retail segment recovered at a slower pace compared to other segments. Presence of under-construction loans will have a bearing on credit risk of LRD loans. 

Inflows are usually in the form of rental cash flows, CAM income (net of operational expenditure such as CAM charges, electricity charges, property taxes, insurance, etc), which will be applied towards debt commitments. 

In this regard, cash coverage ratio (CCR) is computed to evaluate times of cash cover available to the company for servicing debt commitments. While computing CCR, entire cash inflows consisting of debt drawdown, lease rentals, parking charges, or other income generated from properties are plotted against operational expenses, payment of refundable security deposit in case of vacancies and projected expenses/ capital expenditure to arrive at available cash balances for debt repayments. CARE Ratings also evaluates the ratio with inclusion of opening cash balance/accumulated surplus to inflows generated in particular projected period. Average CCR in the projected period is considered as a critical metric to evaluate the financial risk profile of the entity. An adequate CCR throughout the tenure with presence of DSRA entails comfortable financial risk profile. CCR is sensitised for lack of rental escalation, change in vacancy rates, and change in the interest rate, among others. Large potential security deposit outflow in vacancy, which may have an impact on cash flow will also have to be considered.

# **Availability of liquid balances** 

Availability of adequate liquid funds can protect the company against unprecedented downturn in the economy, impacting cash flows. CARE Ratings analyses percentage of repayments due in the following year being covered by available unencumbered liquid investments and accordingly evaluates if significant buffer/cushion is available to meet repayments due in the subsequent year. 

# **Real Estate Investment Trust** 

A real estate investment trust (REIT) is a corporation or a trust, which utilises pooled capital of multiple investors to purchase, and in most cases, operate income generating real estate such as offices, shopping complexes, hotels and warehouses. While assessing REITs, CARE Ratings analyses overall REIT structure and assets profile in accordance with its methodology on analysis of REITs (which is available on our website <u>www.careratings.com) in conjunction with this methodology.</u> 

# **Environmental, Social and Governance risks** 

In the last few years, Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) risks have been gaining importance across the globe and are increasingly influencing investment decisions. Companies may have to incur operational or capital costs towards mitigating these risks. CARE Ratings analyses the impact of ESG risks on an entity’s credit profile by assessing expected impact of these costs on future earnings/revenue/cash flows of entities. 

# <u>Environmental risk:</u> 

Environmental, Social and Governance risks tend to be high for real estate as construction activities stem from environmental issues. Efforts taken by the company towards a healthy environment such as fostering eco-friendly buildings, increasing the amount of green space or using environmentally compatible energy sources, water conservation, waste management among others is factored in the analysis. The assessment would include an evaluation of cash flow implications to develop strategies/initiatives to address these risks, spend on the product design to mitigate environmental risks and cost of processes to recycle, reuse and safely dispose waste. The assessment would also include risk of levy of fines/penalties for not complying with regulatory norms with respect to the environment. 

# <u>Social risk:</u> 

Real estate entities undertaking construction of assets could face social risks associated with labour disputes, working conditions, or reliance on informal labor. Strikes, labor shortages, or regulatory changes impacting labor rights can disrupt operations and affect cash flows. For completed assets, accountability towards customers, social responsibilities, including creating a holistic workplace, and providing healthier and safer work environment, among others need to be measured in terms of their short-to-medium term impact on reputation, consequently translating into impact on future earnings and cash flows. Analysis of social aspects in real estate also includes, for instance, participation in rehabilitation of public spaces, affordable housing and ensuring security in buildings among others. 

# <u>Governance risk:</u> 

Governance-related risks arise from framework, regulations and procedures that guide how an organisation is managed. SEBI has laid down responsibilities of different parties of the REIT, including sponsor, trustee and investment manager to look after interests of unitholders and making clear distinction from the management’s operations. Analysis further includes assessing composition of the board, extent of management oversight in day-to-day operations with governance rules and guidelines, transparency in

The rating outcome is ultimately an assessment of fundamentals and probabilities of change in fundamentals. Rating determination is a matter of experienced and holistic judgment of the Rating Committee, based on relevant quantitative and qualitative factors affecting credit quality of the issuer.
