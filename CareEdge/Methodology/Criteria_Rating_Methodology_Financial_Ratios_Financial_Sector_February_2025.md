# **Background** 

Ratios are used to make holistic assessment of financial performance of an entity, and help evaluating its performance against its peers within the industry. Ratios are not an ‘end’ by themselves but a ‘means’ to understand fundamentals of an entity. CARE Ratings Limited (CARE Ratings) follows a standard set of ratios for evaluating entities in the financial sector. While critical ratios assessed by CARE Ratings are mentioned, some ratios may be applicable to specific institutions such as banks, non-banking financial institutions / housing finance companies (NBFCs / HFCs) or non-lending financial institutions. These can be divided in five categories: 

- Growth ratios 

- Capital adequacy and leverage 

- Asset quality ratios 

- Profitability ratios 

- Liquidity ratios 

These are given in detail below: 

# **A.   Growth Ratios** 

Trend in growth rates of an entity against the industry reflects its ability to sustain market share, profitability and operating efficiency. In this regard, focus is on growth in interest income, profit after tax (PAT), and loan portfolio/assets under management (AUM). # **B.   Capital Adequacy and Leverage**

Debt equity ratio is also measure of leverage of the institution. # **Total Debt** 

In total debt, CARE Ratings considers all forms of short-term and long-term debt, including redeemable preference share capital, optionally convertible debentures, foreign currency loans, fixed deposits, unsecured loans, commercial paper, inter-corporate borrowings, borrowings from promoters, associates and other group companies. Guarantees extended by the entity (including corporate guarantee given for securitisations) are also added in its total debt. 

- Treatment of Hybrid Instruments: Hybrid instruments have characteristics of debt and equity. Examples include preference shares, compulsorily convertible instruments, optionally convertible instruments, including perpetual debt, subordinated debt, Additional Tier I (ATI) bonds, Tier  II bonds (including Upper Tier II and Lower Tier II bonds) - these instruments are eligible to be treated as part of Tier I and Tier II capital per RBI/NHB guidelines. These instruments normally carry a fixed rate of coupon/dividend. At times, the coupon/dividend may be deferrable, giving the issuer flexibility to conserve cash in times of stress. However, CARE Ratings treats all these hybrid instruments, except for compulsorily convertible instruments, as debt. 

|Redeemable Preference<br>Shares|These have a fixed tenure, at the end of which, they are to be<br>redeemed by the issuer. These also carry a fixed rate of dividend.<br>Hence, preference share capital has characteristics of debt and is<br>treated as such by CARE Ratings in its analysis.|
|---|---|
|Compulsorily Convertible<br>Instruments|Such instruments are compulsorily convertible into equity at the end<br>of a longer time frame, typically 5-7 years. Hence, the company does<br>not have to redeem the instrument at the end of its tenure and as<br>such there is no credit risk. In all such cases, where terms of<br>preference shares/debentures give it equity-like characteristics, CARE<br>Ratings treats such instruments as quasi equity and considers it as<br>part of the company’s net worth. However, if the terms of compulsorily<br>convertible instruments are such that investors have an exit option on<br>the company or conversion to equity shares is conditional, CARE<br>Ratings treats these instruments as debt.|
|Optionally Convertible<br>Instruments|In case of optionally convertible instruments (typically optionally<br>convertible<br>preference<br>shares<br>[OCPS]/<br>optionally<br>convertible<br>debentures [OCDs]), the investor has the option to convert the<br>instrument into equity shares at the end of a certain time frame at a<br>pre-determined price. In this case, alternative of redemption of the<br>instrument cannot be ruled out till it is actually converted into equity.<br>The instrument thus has debt-like characteristics till the time it is<br>actually converted into equity. Thus, CARE Ratings treats such<br>instruments as debt in its analysis.|
|Tier II Bonds|Banks/HFC/NBFCs issue Tier II Bonds, which are considered as part<br>of Tier II capital. CARE Ratings considers them as part of debt for<br>calculating overall gearing. In case of banks, per Basel III guidelines,<br>Tier II bonds also have a clause where, on declaration of point of non-<br>viability (PONV) by RBI, it can be written down. However, this is a<br>remote possibility in a going concern, and hence, they are treated at<br>par with Lower Tier II bonds under Basel II, which did not have such<br>clauses.|

# **Tangible Net worth** 

Tangible Net worth of the entity includes equity share capital, all reserves and surplus (excluding revaluation reserve and reserve for bad and doubtful debt), equity share warrants, share application money, employee stock ownership plans (ESOPs) outstanding, minority interest (in case of consolidated financials) and compulsorily convertible instruments (preference shares and debentures). 

- **Miscellaneous expenditure not written off and Accumulated Losses:** Both items are deducted to arrive at tangible net worth. 

- **Revaluation Reserves:** Revaluation reserves arise from revaluation of fixed assets and are excluded while arriving at an entity’s tangible net worth. 

- **Treatment of intangible assets:** An intangible asset is one which is not physical. Examples include computer software, patents, copyrights, licenses, intellectual property, trademark, and goodwill among others.  Treatment of intangibles is similar to what CARE Ratings follows for the non-financial sector. 

- **Treatment of Deferred Tax Liability (DTL) / Deferred Tax Assets (DTA) (Net):** DTA arises due to timing difference between accounting profit and profit per income tax act. CARE Ratings subtracts DTA (net of DTL) from the company’s net worth, while calculating tangible net worth. 

# **C.   Asset Quality Ratios** 

Asset quality of a bank/NBFC is the cornerstone of its operational efficiency and a direct reflection of its risk management practices and credit appraisal mechanism. A bank/NBFC’s asset quality is also impacted by the state of the economy as a whole. Evaluating asset quality is a significant aspect of analysing banks/NBFCs, as deterioration in credit quality of asset book has dual impact on the entity’s profitability considering (i) weakening income profile and (ii) increase in credit costs. Considerably weakening asset quality would also result in higher capital requirements for the entity to support growth and provide stability. CARE Ratings examines following key ratios to determine asset quality.

# **D.   Earnings Ratios** 

A bank/financial institution’s income profile can broadly be divided in two categories: interest income and noninterest income. Interest income is generated by lending funds, while fee-based income (guarantee commission, loan processing fees, and dividend income) and gains from trading/sale of assets form part of non-interest income. The biggest expense for a bank/financial institution is interest expended on deposits and borrowings. Operating expenses of a bank/ financial institution primarily comprise employee cost and administration expenses. Other major charges to the profit and loss account include provision for nonperforming assets and provision for diminution in fair value of investments. # **E.    Liquidity ratios** 

Liquidity risk has become one of the most important elements of risk management framework in financial institutions. A financial institution’s liquidity framework should maintain sufficient liquidity to withstand all kinds of stress events that will be faced. CARE Ratings evaluates liquidity risk by examining stated liquidity policy, assets liabilities maturity (ALM) profile, and collection efficiency and deposit renewal rates (based on empirical evidence), among others. More specifically CARE Ratings measures the following <u>parameters:</u>
