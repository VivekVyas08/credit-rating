# **Industry Overview:** 

Real estate sector plays an important role in the Indian economy. It is one of the largest employers after agriculture and textile and has numerous allied industries, including steel, cement, paint, glass, sanitary fittings, plywood, concrete blocks, and construction to name a few, linked to it. Broadly, the sector can be classified into two segments - residential and commercial. In case of residential space, the demand is determined by a combination of factors such as property prices, urbanisation, interest rates, economic growth, and income levels, among others, whereas the demand for commercial space is directly linked to the prevailing macroeconomic environment and foreign investments in India. 

Historically, India’s real estate sector was unorganised and unregulated. However, with the introduction of the Real Estate Regulation and Development Act, 2016 (RERA), and other regulatory initiatives, the sector has become more organised and transparent. Implementation of RERA has led to improved transparency and accountability in the real estate sector. The establishment of clear guidelines and stringent regulations has enhanced consumer confidence and stabilised the market. RERA is designed with an aim to provide detailed information to stakeholders’ timely project completions, and a reliable dispute resolution system. Developers also benefit from a more streamlined process and gain credibility. 

# **Methodology and its scope:** 

The methodology covers risks associated with the real estate entities, where the primary business model is to construct and sell residential or commercial space. For commercial projects that are being undertaken for leasing, CARE Ratings Limited (CARE Ratings) has laid out separate methodology and hence developers with primary business model of leasing are assessed differently as outlined in criteria paper titled rating methodology for ‘Debt backed by lease rentals available on www.careratings.com. 

Evaluating the credit profile of a real estate entity calls for an entirely different approach when compared to a typical manufacturing concern. The sector is unique and direct comparison of financial performance of different players may not be meaningful due to (a) different revenue-recognition policies followed by entities (b) profits for a given period may vary significantly from the cash flows, and (c) the revenue booking may fluctuate widely from one period to other, depending upon the stage of execution of various ongoing projects. 

Given the nuances of real estate sector, greater emphasis is placed on evaluating operational parameters covering execution and marketing risk alongside cash flow analysis, and financial flexibility of developer. With highly fragmented nature of industry, evaluation of management quality is also of utmost importance. In view of above, real estate rating framework focuses on factors covered under four risk heads, as listed below:

# **A) Industry risk evaluation** 

The real estate sector is cyclical having direct linkage to macroeconomic scenario, interest rates, and income levels. Apart from being cyclical, the sector is also highly fragmented, capital intensive, and impacted by disclosure issues. All such factors call for in-depth industry analysis. CARE Ratings considers the below-listed aspects to assess the industry risk: 

# Demand-Supply Outlook 

- Demand at industry level is driven by demographic trends such as disposable income, 

- property prices, urbanisation, and movement in interest rates, among others. 

- At the entity level, demand is primarily linked to factors such as brand awareness, 

- location, construction quality, parity between product and customer taste. 

- Inventory supply is largely linked with prevailing price trend and unsold inventory in the 

- micro-market. Lower demand-supply mismatch indicates stability in prices. 

# Cyclicality 

- The sector has close linkages with the economy and therefore is highly cyclical in nature. 

- A typical real estate project has a gestation period of three to four years and any adverse change in the macroeconomic factors in the interim period can affect the cash flows of the developer. 

# Corporate Governance 

- The sector is impacted by transparency issues. Closely-held nature of the entities with 

- limited disclosures, lack of clear land title due to absence of land records are generally the norms. With the implementation of RERA and increasing presence of corporate houses, the transparency level is gradually elevating, however, the perceived risk remains high.

# **B) Management evaluation** 

A developer with an understanding of local area nuances, established brand image in the area of operations, demonstrated track record of quality construction and timely delivery, has a competitive advantage. Besides, the companies that have withstood business cycles are generally better placed when compared to peers with limited experience. 

Thus, CARE Ratings evaluates the vintage of developer in operations, execution and marketing track record with respect to the type of project being undertaken, the resourcefulness of promoters, its growth plans, risk appetite, and leverage policies. This apart, CARE Ratings also assesses, the other qualitative parameters such as auditor qualifications, pledge of promoter shareholding, and RERA details (such as litigation, extension) among others to get an understanding on the related financial implications. 

# **Experience of promoters/top management in real estate development** 

The experience and track record of the promoters/management in the line of business is given significant importance while evaluating management competency and quality. Considering the capital-intensive nature of the business, a company with financially strong promoters enjoys an advantage over others as its ability to infuse and/or raise funds in a timely manner is better. 

# **Real estate space developed by the group in the past** 

Completed real estate projects in the past indicate the operational level expertise of the promoters. A considerable scale of development undertaken in the past demonstrates better execution track record of the developer. To understand developer’s experience, CARE Ratings also places emphasis on details of the past projects, such as scale, location, and types of projects undertaken (villa, gated communities, townships, commercial spaces, and others). 

# **Constitution of the entity** 

The legal constitution of an entity indicates the level of oversight and hence has a bearing on the governance and disclosure requirements to which an entity is subjected to. Partnerships/proprietorships are subject to comparatively milder disclosures and regulatory requirements than private/public limited companies. Such entities also carry an inherent risk of withdrawal of capital which is factored appropriately in ratings of partnerships/proprietorship concerns. 

# **Organisational structure and group support** 

Typically, forming special purpose vehicles (SPVs) for undertaking different functions and projects is a general norm in the industry. 

Accordingly, in a group structure where real estate projects are executed through SPVs, these SPVs are usually analysed based on project-wise operational and financial metrics on a standalone level. However, depending on the level of financial and operational linkages between real estate entities of the group and cash flow fungibility, CARE Ratings generally considers the risks and cash flows at the group level and the impact of the same on the rated entity. In this context, debt covenants including ring fencing structures, commonality among the lenders, and inter-company transactions are also carefully evaluated. The financials and operational details of other real estate entities of the group are generally assessed. CARE Ratings assesses the level of support extended by the parent and accordingly factors it in the analysis. Hence, in line with CARE Ratings’ criteria on ‘Notching by Factoring Linkages in Ratings’, ratings of an entity

# **C) Operational risk assessment** 

# **Execution risk** 

While assessing execution risk, there are various factors that require consideration, such as experience of the developer in the industry and region of operation, construction progress of the portfolio, and ability of developer to obtain timely approvals of the project. In this regard, CARE Ratings considers the following parameters to assess execution risk: 

- **Execution track record:** The scale of the ongoing projects is compared to the aggregate scale implemented in the past to assess if the projects currently being undertaken are large compared to the past projects executed by the developer. If the proportion of area under development is comparable with aggregate area developed till date, it reflects better execution capability of the developer. The type of projects, such as affordable, mid segment, luxury, is also considered to assess if developers have adequate experience in dealing with varied project types. 

- **Stage of construction:** To assess the construction progress, ratio of percentage of cost incurred to total estimated project cost is considered. This is also generally evaluated in conjunction with the physical progress of the projects and construction schedule, if any. Advanced stage of construction indicates low execution risk. CARE Ratings also monitors the update on construction progress per the RERA website (wherever available). 

- **Regulatory risk:** Real Estate industry is marked by high level of regulations and thus execution critically depends on timely receipt of multiple approvals and regulatory clearances from various authorities (central, state and local) at various stages of construction. Delays in obtaining such approvals often hamper the progress of the projects per the envisaged schedule. This can impact the saleability and consequent collections which may elevate the funding risk for players, highly reliant on customer advances.

# **Marketing Risk** 

Marketing risk is largely determined by parameters such as location, adjacent infrastructure and construction quality of the projects, track record of the developer in selling the similar type projects or projects in a given micro-market, comparison of quoted price against current market rate, and competition from projects. While, such parameters provide an understanding about the market standing of the developer, achievability of future sales is also drawn by considering critical parameters listed below: 

- **Booking status:** Higher booking ratio implies favourable market standing of the project. For computing booking ratio, percentage of area/ units booked out of launched area/ units is considered. 

- **Registrations:** Higher percentage of registrations (wherever registrations are done at construction stage) out of booked units is positively correlated to lesser number of cancellations. However, legislatures in different geographies stipulate different rules and regulation, thus registration of units is not a mandatory norm in the construction phase, in few states. CARE Ratings emphasises on the track record of cancellations in such cases. 

- **Sales velocity:** Good market standing of the developer ensures quick sales velocity and regular cash inflows. CARE Ratings looks at the ratio of ‘months to sell the unsold inventory’ which provides an understanding about the estimated period within which the unsold inventory gets converted into sales based on the current sales momentum. Lower ratio indicates greater sales velocity or lower quantum of unsold inventory lying with the developer. 

# **Diversification** 

CARE Ratings considers the project portfolio of the developer to evaluate if such portfolio is well diversified in terms of revenue streams, type of projects being undertaken, geographical positioning and construction stage of the projects. The developer having a mix of projects for sale and leasing is viewed favourably as leasing projects provide consistent cash inflows even in the times of slowdown, while the projects for sale secure funding from customers in the construction phase of the project. A developer’s portfolio with types of projects such as plots, villas, mixed housing, and other developments, is evaluated by CARE Ratings based on profitability, collection velocity, and completion time. Similarly, a developer having presence in few geographies is protected from downturns in a single micro market. The mix of projects at initial stage and advanced stage ensures consistent cash inflows and exposure to different business cycles. 

# **Availability of land bank** 

The size of fully paid unencumbered land bank (either owned or acquired under JDA) available with the developer is crucial for the entity as developers often acquire land for future development purposes thus indicating their development potential. 

# **D) Financial risk assessment** 

Financial risk assessment broadly involves assessment of cash flow position, liquidity position, leverage, and financial flexibility of the entity. For evaluating financial risk profile of the entity, CARE Ratings also considers the past financial performance of the entity and reviews accounting policies, notes to accounts, and contingent liabilities/off-balance sheet items. # **Cash flow analysis** 

CARE Ratings considers the cash flow forecasts of the underlying projects to understand the adequacy of inflows and outflows of real estate entity. In this regard, only those projects that are reasonably certain to be launched in medium term are considered for the analysis. 

Inflows are usually in the form of project receipts (collections from sales and leasing income, if any), debt, promoter’s contribution, and support from group companies while outflows include project expenses (typically include construction expenses, rent expenses for redevelopment projects, land cost including commitments under Joint Development Agreement (JDA), finance cost, administration & marketing expenses), corporate expenses, if any, and repayment of debt obligations. While analysing inflows, CARE Ratings also assesses the collection efficiency (collections realised out of invoice raised) of the developer. 

Cash coverage ratio (CCR) indicates the level of cushion available to the company for meeting the debt obligations. While assessing the cash flow position, various debt payment structures and applicable interest rates are considered. The ratio being critical indicator of cash flow position of the entity is sensitised to accommodate possibilities such as delays in project completion leading to lower cash flows from unsold units, lower collections, increase in finance cost or construction costs, among others. 

# **Adequacy of committed receivables** 

To understand the cash flow visibility, CARE Ratings focuses on the ratio of committed customer advances (receivables) from confirmed sales to fund the balance cost of the projects under implementation and repayment of outstanding debt. Since the ratio will vary depending upon the stage of execution of projects in the portfolio, the movement of this ratio is also viewed. Lower ratio implies higher dependence on fresh sales or promoter contribution to fund the future project costs and debt payments. 

CARE Ratings also assesses the ratio of total receivables which includes committed receivables from sold units and value of unsold units with emphasis on recently completed unsold units, to fund the balance cost of the projects under implementation and repayment of outstanding debt. 

# **Liquidity Analysis** 

The availability of adequate liquid funds can protect the company against unprecedented downturn in the economy, impacting the cash flows. CARE Ratings also factors in the structural elements of the debt such as presence of escrow mechanism and ring-fencing of cash flows, availability of adequate funds in debt service reserve account (DSRA) so as to cover few months’ debt servicing obligations as they provide some cushion to the liquidity. CARE Ratings also considers the ratio of liquidity coverage, which determines liquidity buffer available to cover the debt obligations due in the following year. CARE Ratings’ methodology on ‘Liquidity Analysis of Non-financial sector entities’ is also followed for assessment of liquidity position. 

# **Project funding pattern** 

The real estate business is capital intensive in nature and the entities require huge capital in various phases of project construction. The acquisition of land for a project is more often funded through the promoter’s contribution, while the construction of projects is funded through external sources. Many developers also enter joint development agreements (JDAs), which eliminate the need for upfront land acquisition costs and result in lower initial outflows. CARE Ratings considers the JDA liabilities recorded in the balance sheet for evaluating the developer's financial position. The funding pattern (Promoter Funds: Debt: Customer Advances) is a function of the sales momentum of the project and the reputation of the developer. The

The developer with slow sales momentum may have to resort to avail debt or unsecured loans from promoters/ group companies, which could result in high debt to project cost ratio. 

# **Leverage** 

To assess leverage position, CARE Ratings considers the proportion of project cost which is funded through debt and an entity with low leverage is viewed positively due to its better financial flexibility and ability to avail additional debt in unprecedented economic scenario. CARE Ratings also focuses on the ratio of the overall gearing and the results are compared to the peer firms with the similar asset portfolios. Lower ratio implies better financial discipline of the developer and strong ability to withstand economic cycles. Other ratios used to evaluate the project's leverage position include gross debt to collections, and debt per square foot. Debt per square foot is evaluated alongside factors such as project location and type, which directly affect project costs and may influence the funding mix. While assessing these ratios, subordinated debt (as stipulated in the debt financing documents) infused by promoter is adjusted appropriately. While assessing debt position, lease rental discounting (LRD) loans are discerned with construction finance loans/other debt instruments as, generally, an entity with higher proportion of leasing portfolio benefits with low average cost of debt, and lower volatility in cash flows. 

# **Environmental, social, and governance risk** 

Environmental, social, and governance (ESG) risks tend to be high for real estate as building construction stems out environmental issues and social & governance issues also need consideration. CARE Ratings broadly evaluates these risks with the objective to assess their direct and indirect impact on the entity and how it mitigates or plans to mitigate the impact of such risks on its credit profile. 

Environment Risk: Efforts towards a healthy environment include fostering eco-friendly buildings, increasing green spaces, and using sustainable energy sources. The assessment considers cash-flow implications, product design to mitigate environmental risks, and costs related to recycling and waste management. It also evaluates the risk of fines for non-compliance with environmental regulations. 

Social Risk: In real estate, social risk such as employee welfare, labour relations, and customer accountability, impact reputation and future earnings. The analysis includes the developer’s role in public space rehabilitation, labour relations, and health and safety policies. 

Governance risks: It involves assessing board composition, compliance with governance rules, transparency in disclosures, financial reporting quality, and organisational complexity. The evaluation also considers past non-compliance issues and measures to mitigate future risks. 

# **Conclusion** 

The rating outcome is ultimately an assessment of the fundamentals and the probabilities of change in the fundamentals. Rating determination is a matter of experienced and holistic judgment by the Rating Committee, based on the relevant quantitative and qualitative factors affecting the credit quality of the issuer. CARE Ratings analyses each of the above factors and their linkages to arrive at the overall assessment of the credit quality of a real estate entity. CARE Ratings also considers future estimation of the company’s financials based on past trends and strategies, competition, industry trends, economic conditions, and other considerations.
