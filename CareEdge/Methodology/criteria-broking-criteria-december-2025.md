# **Background** 

The Indian stock broking industry has shown significant growth post pandemic which was considering rising client base following increasing penetration of the industry in Tier-II and semi-urban parts of the country. The sector comprises standalone brokers, bank-led brokers, and the dominant category of discount brokers. Per their business model, these companies earn fee-based income (brokerage and advisory), and income from portfolio management services and distribution operations. They also earn income from underwriting and proprietary trading. To diversify topline, industry players have also grown their margin trading facility (MTF) book, which earns interest income. This apart, players have also diversified into wealth management related businesses such as distribution of financial products, which generates steady income. 

CARE Ratings Limited (CareEdge Ratings) assigns ratings to debt instruments and bank facilities of broking companies by assessing all these industry aspects and risks associated with the company. 

# **Methodology:** 

Risks assessed per CareEdge Ratings’ rating methodology for broking firms are divided into four parts: industry risk, business risk, financial risk, and management risk. The final rating also factors in notching that is applicable for parent/ promoter group linkages, which is carried out per CareEdge Rating’s criteria on ‘notching by factoring linkages in ratings’ available on www.careratings.com. Key parameters considered for a standalone assessment of broking firms are discussed below. 

The rating criteria for broking firms is an indicative list, which covers important factors considered for credit risk assessment of broking firms as elaborated below: 

# **1. Industry risk:** 

- i. **Stock market scenario:** A broking firm is exposed to high level of market risk, given its dependence on the capital market, which is inherently volatile and unpredictable. In times when the industry is growing rapidly as a result of increased volumes and trades, a broking firm’s financial performance will be favourable. On the contrary, if market activities are not favourable, the broking firm’s financial performance is adversely impacted. While assessing the broking firm, CareEdge Ratings considers on-going market environment and its impact on players in the industry. 

- ii. **Regulatory risk:** The broking industry remains significantly exposed to regulatory risks. In the last few years, given the increase in capital market activities, the market regulator, Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) has been monitoring the sector extensively and has been releasing new regulations at regular intervals. Significant change in regulations can have a material impact on the credit profile of a stockbroker and hence remains an important monitorable. Since the broking industry is highly regulated, a robust IT system helps to comply with timely requirements of the regulator in terms of margin reporting, Know-your-client (KYC), and trade order placements, among others. A broking firm’s inability to adapt to these changing requirements with respect to technology, internal controls and quick implementation of

- iii. **Competition risk:** Competition in the broking industry in terms of service quality and brokerages being offered, intensified with the entry of discount brokers, which led to significant changes in the revenue model across the industry right from discounted rates offered to miniscule brokerage for delivery transactions. However, this has changed post FY25 regulations, where even discount brokerages have started charging for trades. Rapid digitisation, regulatory reforms, and rising retail participation are also driving margin compression for stockbrokers. Entry barriers in this industry remain low; however, sustainability of a broking firm comes into question, given the paramount need of technological expertise and strong balance sheet needed to survive with the growing volumes and increased regulatory oversight among inherent volatility of capital markets. 

- iv. The increased competition risk can impact a broking firm’s profitability as brokerage rates have taken a hit for the industry players to attract more clients and volumes. This has a bearing on the credit profile of a broking firm. Hence, the role of competition risk in credit assessment plays a pivotal role. CareEdge Ratings takes into consideration a broking firm’s trend in market share of volumes and new client additions and its impact on the credit profile. With the growth of MTF in the industry, a broker’s share of the overall MTF book and yields earned are assessed to gauge growth in its revenue. 

# **2. Business risk** 

- i. **Market position:** The business of a broking firm is driven by factors such as overall size in terms of trading volumes, active client base, its ranking in the industry, the company’s vintage in the broking business, technology prowess, and balance sheet strength, among others. Technological capabilities are a critical factor in attracting and retaining new customers and acts as a key differentiator. 

These factors help in assessing the ability of a broking firm in managing the market volatility during market downturns and the segments that it operates in. 

- ii. **Diversification in revenue profile:** A broking firm’s business revenue is based on the brokerage earned, which is a function of its scale of operations, franchise, active client base and volumes traded/number of trades by such clients, and hence, leads to volatile earnings profile. Interest income from MTF book has become another major source of revenue for broking firms, which again depends on market activities. To reduce exposure to market-linked business, many broking firms are also focusing on expanding the distribution business. A well-diversified revenue profile between brokerage income, interest income, distribution income and income on other financial services is considered positively by CareEdge Ratings, as it helps to reduce dependence on one source of revenue. Proportion of revenue through segments such as cash, futures and options (F&O), and commodity, among others, also plays an important role, as concentration towards one segment may impact overall revenue in the downfall in specific segment of the market. The proportion of proprietary book is also considered with its contribution to overall revenue. Higher proportion of proprietary book increases the vulnerability to business risk in times of unfavourable market conditions. However, while evaluating brokers with a significant share of proprietary trading income, the company’s track record in terms of profitability and ability to avoid losses is also considered.

MTF interest serves as a key driver for topline diversification for most stockbrokers, as it generates interest income through funding provided against equity share collaterals. While evaluating entities with MTF exposure, CareEdge Ratings considers the company’s risk management practices related to the product, including process of scrip selection, defining client wise exposures, collection of additional margins above exchange-prescribed limits, policy for squaring off positions, concentration levels among clients and scrips, adherence to SEBI guidelines pertaining to leverage, permissible funding sources, and maximum exposure limits per borrower. With growth of MTF and linked fund-based borrowings, CareEdge Ratings factors in the impact of increased levels of gearing on the brokers’ credit profile and also monitor adherence to the SEBI’s regulation on leverage levels. 

- iii. **Diversification in client base:** A well-diversified client base of a broking firm with different segments of customers, including retail, high net worth individuals and institutional clients will help in safeguarding its business from volatility. Higher concentration, especially towards institutional clients, may impact the core revenue of a firm in unfavourable market scenarios. On the other hand, in CareEdge Ratings’ view, a good mix of clientele spread out across retail customers is favourable, as it reduces concentration risk towards limited customers. 

- iv. **Risk management systems:** Broking industry is exposed to risks such as liquidity risk, market risk, operational risk and credit risk. Thus, the risk management framework adopted by the company plays a significant role in its day-to-day operations. To protect the business from market and credit risk, a broking firm’s risk management framework should clearly state the margin policy (scrip categorisation, margin calculation, and maximum limits, among others), types and quality of collaterals taken, procedures to timely square-off the positions to safeguard itself from abnormal losses, and maximum exposure per client, among others. Risk management systems for proprietary book and client book should be separate and assessed separately. Vintage of the proprietary book and performance of the proprietary book for the vintage period is considered. It is important for the brokerage firms to have a dedicated and experienced risk management team to ensure timely implementation of risk-mitigation measures. 

- v. **Operation and IT systems:** Risk management systems also depend on efficiency of IT systems that have been put in place by a broking firm. They should have tech-enabled systems in place for real-time monitoring of client positions and exposures to avoid client concentration risk and real-time monitoring of underlying scripts to avoid script concentration risk. 

Given the shift in the industry from traditional method of broking to online model, tech-driven trading platforms are needed for ensuring smooth customer experience. A strong IT infrastructure with continuous monitoring and development ensures less operational risk with timely execution of trades and the settlements, however, technical failure or disruption such as a system glitch, cyberattack, or inadequate infrastructure can result in operational and reputational risks and regulatory penalties. Broking firms are obligated to undertake Vulnerability Assessment and Penetration Tests (VAPT) at least once in a financial year, which helps identify vulnerabilities in the IT environment. 

- vi. **Credit risk:** The asset quality of a broking firm is gauged by analysing debtors’ ageing profile, bad debts being booked and margin policy laid by the firm. The broking firm is exposed to credit risk by fulfilling margin requirement of clients and lending through MTF book, which is regulated by SEBI. During high market volatility, there are chances of losses being incurred by the broking firm on the margins provided

# **3. Financial risk:** 

The balance sheet of a broking entity tends to be dominated by liquid assets, given the inherently high cash and bank balances. However, these balances are majorly lien marked against borrowing facilities and to exchanges for margin requirements. Hence, a thorough analysis of financial statements need to be done to understand fund sources and its application.

Financial risk is primarily based on four parameters; capitalisation and resource profile, earnings, and liquidity. 

**1. Capital levels and resource profile:** A broking firm needs adequate capital to maintain sufficient margins with the exchange and also to provide funding services to its clients through the MTF product. Given the volatile nature of capital markets, adequate capitalisation will ensure the ability of a broking firm to fulfil the sudden margin requirements in volatile market situation and avoid abnormal losses. Apart from the available capital, other factors including the firm’s ability to access funding sources, unutilised committed limits, liquid assets, and excess margins maintained with the exchanges, among others, also need to be considered while assessing adequacy of capitalisation. 

Firms with proprietary trading book may need sufficient quantum of owned capital for cushion in market volatility considering deterioration in value of investments that may take place. Funded resource profile of broking firms largely comprises short-term facilities like overdraft facility (OD) and commercial papers. Nonfund-based facilities in the form of bank guarantees (BGs), are mainly utilised for fulfilling margin requirements with the exchange. These BGs are fully/partially secured by fixed deposits. Calculation of leverage considers fund-based and non-fund-based borrowings of the firm. 

Given the business model of broking companies, they mainly borrow to keep margins with exchanges per regulatory requirements and to carry on lending business through MTF product. Since funding requirement by broking firms are short term in nature, a higher proportion of long-term borrowings is viewed unfavourably. CareEdge Ratings analyses the following factors to gauge capitalisation and resource profile of a broking firm:

   - Gearing ratio (including non-fund-based debt): A gearing ratio is calculated by considering fund-based and non-fund-based borrowings against tangible net worth available. Gearing is adjusted considering the bank balance marked as lien against borrowings, since they are kept in the form of collateral with the lenders. Surplus (unutilised) margins available with stock exchanges have to be factored in this assessment. 

   - Tangible net worth: Tangible net worth is considered for rating assessment, which is per the CareEdge Ratings’ criteria on financial ratios for financial sector. 

   - Sources of funds: Lines of funding being used by the company are analysed and tenure of such funding since funding requirement is for a shorter period of time. The source of fund for facilitating MTF should adhere to SEBI guidelines. A broking firm’s ability to raise external funds is also an important factor, which needs to be gauged. 

   - Cost of borrowings: This ratio helps understand competitive standing and riskiness of a broking firm against other players in the industry. 

**2. Earnings profile:** Earnings of a broking firm depend on market activities and are exposed to uncertainty and volatility, based on market scenarios. This uncertainty can be partially offset by diversifying into other stable income-generating segments, and hence, CareEdge Ratings considers the extent of diversification in the firm’s overall revenue. Other factors determining quality of earnings include quality of clientele such as retail or wholesale, dependence on proprietary trading, and arbitrage incomes, among others. The proportion of fixed and variable cost in the overall cost structure helps understand a broking firm’ operating leverage. Given the volatile revenue profile, a broking firm with more variable costs will be able to manage costs better during market downturn. In CareEdge Ratings’ view, a low and stable total cost to income ratio of a broking firm is viewed favourably. 

The broking firm with high proprietary book also makes its income profile riskier during market downturn. CareEdge Ratings primarily analyses the following factors to gauge earnings level: 

   - Diversification in revenue profile: To understand the impact of market volatility on revenue of a broking firm and its dependence on the capital market activity, extent of diversification in revenue profile needs to be gauged thoroughly. Diversification by brokers can be achieved by venturing into wealth management and financial products’ distribution businesses, which also provides stability to the revenue. Quality and stability over the period of earnings are analysed. 

   - Ratio analysis: Some important profitability ratios include cost to income (CTI) ratio; profit before interest and taxes (PBIT) margin and profit after tax (PAT) margin. 

**3. Liquidity risk:** CareEdge Ratings considers liquidity policy followed by a broking firm to service debt obligations and maintain sufficient cushion to absorb market volatility. It evaluates sources of liquidity available with a firm: unencumbered liquid assets held, excess margin maintained with the exchanges and committed bank lines available to meet urgent funding requirements. Liquidity coverage available to fulfil short-term debt obligations is also evaluated.

CareEdge Ratings analyses following factors to gauge the liquidity position: 

- Liquidity coverage ratio: Liquidity coverage ratio is calculated by considering unencumbered cash/bank balance and liquid investments available with a broking firm against debt obligations scheduled for maturity in the next one year. 

- Excess margins maintained: A broking firm is required to maintain margins with the exchanges to successfully execute a trade. Most firms maintain margins above required levels to bridge the margin gap that may arise due to market volatility. Hence, margin utilisation levels and proportion of excess margins maintained is also factored in while assessing the entity. 

# **4. Management risk:** 

CareEdge Ratings assigns high importance to the track record and experience of promoters and the management in running the company’s operations. A management having relevant experience to manage the broking business will be more efficient in taking correct decisions during volatile market scenarios. 

CareEdge Ratings considers stability in the management, governance structure and past records in terms of corporate governance, the management’s risk orientation and focus on technology. 

# **Analysis of environmental, social and governance risk factors:** 

Although broking firms have a service-oriented model with limited direct environmental exposure, they may face indirect risks through links with environmentally sensitive sectors. Hence, Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) considerations for investment and advisory practices of broking entities become important to evaluate to avoid exposure to non-compliant companies, and to enhance credibility among responsible investors. 

Social risk has become a key consideration for broking firms, as client relationships increasingly reflect ESG preferences. Data breach or misuse can harm reputation and increase compliance costs, and hence, remains a key monitorable. 

Governance risk reflects how effectively a broking firm is managed to safeguard stakeholder interests. It involves monitoring aspects, including but not limited, to adherence to SEBI regulations, adequacy of internal controls, Board composition, and quality of disclosures. Strong governance enables firms to manage regulatory challenges and maintain financial stability. CareEdge Ratings places significant emphasis on governance quality in its credit assessment.
