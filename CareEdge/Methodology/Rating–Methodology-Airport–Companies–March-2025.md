India has 148 operational airports managed by Airports Authority of India (AAI), while 11 are managed through Public Private Partnership (PPP) model including the busiest ones at Delhi, Mumbai, Hyderabad and Bengaluru. Share of private airports in the overall air traffic in India is ~55%. 

# **Rating methodology** 

This rating criteria provides CARE Ratings Limited’s (CareEdge Ratings) detailed approach on rating airport companies. This criteria paper is a reference tool for issuers, investors and other market participants to help them understand factors considered by CareEdge Ratings, while rating companies involved in operations, management and development of greenfield and brownfield airports. CareEdge Ratings looks at a time horizon over the life of the debt instrument being rated and covers the following areas while rating airports. 

# **1. Business risk assessment** 

# **Contractual structures** 

The development, operations and management of airports under PPP entails involvement of many stakeholders such as Airports Authority of India (Authority), Ministry of Civil Aviation (MoCA), Airports Economic Regulatory Authority (Regulator, AERA), Government of India (GOI)-designated agencies, State Government and the Airport Developer/ Concessionaire. 

The concession agreement between the Authority and airport developer provides the framework within which the airports operate, gets developed and managed under PPP, and such framework is implemented through special purpose vehicles (SPVs). 

CareEdge Ratings examines whether the risks are identified and allocated appropriately among the stakeholders. The contracts entered between SPV, and stakeholders are assessed, which defines allocation of risks. Contracts are assessed for their comprehensiveness, clarity and enforceability. 

# **Revenue risk** 

An airport is primarily exposed to the risks associated with revenue generation. An airport generates revenue from providing aeronautical services and revenues from services other than aeronautical services. 

# **Aeronautical revenue:** 

Aeronautical charges are levied on aircraft and passengers for providing services and infrastructure at airports. Aeronautical charges are regulated by Airports Economic Regulatory Authority of India (AERA, the regulator). A high degree of certainty in tariff determination makes cash flows of airport operators more predictable and are viewed positively. 

# **Non-aeronautical revenue:** 

Non-aeronautical revenue stream includes revenue from lease of commercial space at airports, advertisements, baggage handling, fuel-farms, duty free, parking charges, food and beverage outlets and revenue from development of real estate around airports. Non-aeronautical revenues are not regulated, and

Against established airport, greenfield airport faces uncertainty over generation of non-aeronautical revenues as marketability of streams of revenue are not tested. 

# **Revenue from real estate:** 

Traditionally, AAI airports witnessed limited commercial use of land parcels. However, private players have been given sizeable land parcels as part of the concession. In case the real estate is available for monetisation, the airport’s entering into lease/sale agreements with real estate developers is assessed. Reasonability of the land valuation assumed by the airport in its funding plan and quantum of annual rentals and lease deposits targeted by it are assessed. 

# **Regulatory Environment** 

Typically, aeronautical revenues are regulated, largely to counter the monopolistic nature of airports. It is therefore important to assess concession framework of economic regulation, within which airports operate. 

# **Inherent regulatory risk with respect to timely issuance of tariff order** 

AERA is the designated Regulator by GoI for determination of aeronautical charges (including user development fee [UDF]) for the airport per AERA (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff for Airport Operators) Guidelines, 2011. The aeronautical charges are reviewed by the Regulator once in five years (control period). The first control period was from 2011 to 2014 and thereafter every five years. 

Through the National Civil Aviation Policy dated June 15, 2016, the GOI has approved, (Shared-Till or Hybrid Till) the 30% of the non-aeronautical revenues to be used in shared-till framework for determination and regulation of aeronautical charges for all airports in India, which shall be considered by Regulator for the purpose of the determination of the fees/ aeronautical charges. The regulated return on aero assets with a hybrid-till-tariff structure involving true-up/true-down of the revenue takes care of traffic fluctuations at the time of subsequent tariff determination process. In case of greenfield airports, timely issuance of the first tariff order is examined. 

GOI has proposed a new concession structure for Greenfield projects called NextGen Airports for Bharat Nirman 2018. per the guidelines in the NABH Nirman scheme, the 'Maximum Blended Aeronautical Yield' (MBAY) in terms of INR per passenger shall be pre-determined by the Concession Authority at the beginning of the Concession Period. The total annual allowable aeronautical revenue for the Concessionaire shall be the MBAY multiplied by the annual number of passengers at that airport. Once in five years, the Concessionaire shall submit its proposed tariff card and projected tariff for five-year period to the Regulator. The tariff card shall be developed in such a way that the projected aeronautical yield in the five-year period is less than or equal to the MBAY on a present value basis. 

CareEdge Ratings assesses the transparent framework in deriving aeronautical charges for firmer future estimations.

# **Flexibility in rate revision** 

Given that aeronautical charges determined by the regulator are based on certain assumptions such as projected passenger growth forecasts and air traffic movements, adverse variation in the actual traffic against estimates could lead to cash flow disruptions till truing up is undertaken and approved for recovery by the Regulator. Thus, frequency of tariff revision, and the timeliness of its implementation are important to lend predictability to future aeronautical revenue generation at an airport. 

# **Revenue sharing structure with Authority:** 

So far, share of gross revenue of the airport operator has been the bid parameter in greenfield airports. There are many challenges in monitoring and finalising 'gross revenue' and hence the share thereof payable to the Concession Authority. The new model under NABH Nirman 2018 Scheme addresses this with simple per passenger fee metric as the sole criteria for appointment of \selected bidders and concession fee payment to the Authority. Annual revision in Per Passenger Fee shall be revised considering consumer price index for industrial workers (CPI [IW]). 

CareEdge Ratings views predictable nature of cash flows positively while assessing risk. 

# **Operating risk** 

An airport operator has to adhere to the performance indicators and service levels set forth in the concession agreement for services offered including operating aeronautical assets such as runways, taxiways, aprons and approach area, among others, permitting safe, smooth and uninterrupted movement of users and flow of traffic, terminal building, vehicle parking, cargo facilities, ground handling services, aircraft-fuelling services, check-in services, VIP lounges, immigrations, customs and safety requirements, among others The airport operator shall conform to standards set out by International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) for operating aeronautical assets. The airport operator shall engage with agencies such as Bureau of Civil Aviation Security of India (BCAS), Air Traffic Control (ATC), Ministry of External Affairs of India, Central Industrial Security Force (CISF) and other designated agencies of GOI for smooth operations of an airport. 

Considering increased maintenance standards and service quality levels in managing the next-generation airports, airport operator will incur higher O&M expenses. If an airport operator fails to execute O&M obligations, per provisions set out in the concession agreement; penalties levied by the Authority may lead to higher O&M expenses. 

Hence, assessment of airport operator having established track record in managing airports are viewed credit positively compared to the airport operators with limited track record. 

# **2. Market position and passenger base** 

Passenger traffic is the key revenue driver for airport and the number of passengers using airport would determine its revenues generation capacity. Hence, CareEdge Ratings evaluates factors that have a direct relation with the passenger traffic such as location of the airport, population of the region, service area, region’s economic base and competition from other airports. Credit profile of major airlines operating at the airport is also evaluated to gauge counterparty risk. These factors help in determining earning potential of an airport in a longer period of time. An airport that can maintain healthy traffic levels through economic downturn as well consistent growth throughout is viewed more favourably than an airport with fluctuating traffic.

# **Passenger mix:** 

Passenger mix is an important indicator of envisaged revenue generation and its stability. Higher proportion of international traffic leads to higher yield per passenger compared to domestic passenger. The size of the population in an airport’s catchment area is the most appropriate measure given that passenger volumes are the key revenue drivers for airport. CareEdge Ratings observes that there is a strong correlation between population of the service area, passenger mix and likelihood of airport usage. 

# **Location:** 

Airport’s location is important, as it boosts potential of airport to become a hub for major airlines and a geographically well-located airport would attract higher transfer traffic compared to origin and destination airport. Location that provides better connectivity also attracts a greater number of airlines and consequent higher passenger traffic and better earning profile. 

# **Region’s economic base:** 

The region’s economic strength has significant implications on the passenger traffic. Therefore, CareEdge Ratings analyses region’s per capita gross domestic product (GDP) and income levels, economic diversity (presence of multiple industries), employment rate, among others. Region with important business districts and tourist destinations would be better positioned compared to a region, which highly depends on specific economic activity thus rendering it more susceptible to vagaries of economic cycles. An airport in a location attracting a lot of tourist traffic from cities across country / world will depend less on local economy. 

# **Service area:** 

Determining the area to be served is somewhat complex and subjective. A major international airport of the country, which is considered one of the gateways to the country would have a larger service area compared to a small regional airport which would be able to serve a limited region. CareEdge Ratings views an airport with larger area to be served with no competition from other major airport in vicinity favourably compared to a regional airport with limited-service area. 

# **Traffic pattern and volatility:** 

An established track record of passenger/cargo growth and its volatility plays an important role in assessment of future cash flows of the airport. Hence, an airport with stable growth rate historically and less volatility is viewed credit positive. 

# **Competition:** 

Airports are generally less susceptible to competition due to capital-intensive nature and regulatory hurdles of public utility type entity. This creates strong entry barriers. However, airports remain exposed to the competition from new airports that may be allowed and developed by MoCA/AAI in the nearby vicinity. CareEdge Ratings evaluates competitive position of the airport by reviewing concession documents to understand, if concession agreement safeguards its earning potential from new airport for a sufficiently long period of time, especially covering loan tenor. 

From credit perspective, dual airports in same city are typically exposed to traffic diversion risk. However, wider catchment area, large volume of passenger traffic, higher per capita income, adoption of hub and spoke model and route development strategies are likely to offset the impact of traffic diversion. Therefore, impact of alternate airport is suitably examined. Airports traffic for short-to-medium distance domestic traffic is

# **3. Promoters/sponsors and management track record** 

Airports can be characterised by wide range of ownership from being a PPP to a fully government-owned and operated. Promoters’/sponsors’ experience and capabilities play a key role in running airport operations. Hence, sponsors experience and resourcefulness are given due importance, while evaluating management competency, commitment and quality. 

# **Experience:** 

For seamless airport operations, an airport operator is required to handle multiple functions such as aeronautical operations, cargo handling, terminal operations, airport retail and commercial operations effectively. Hence, assessment of airport operator’s experience is very crucial. Inability to manage operations may lead to higher-than-projected operating and maintenance costs, which may deplete cash flows or may also result in non-conformity to laid out service quality standards by the regulator. Hence, CareEdge Ratings believes that an experienced airport operator tends to benefit, when it comes to managing the airport operations. This also helps in mitigating operational risks. To evaluate management’s experience or the appointed operator’s track record involvement in airports of similar capacity have been operated successfully. Sponsor or an operator with significant experience of similar business with successful track record is viewed favourably. 

# **Financial strength and commitment:** 

Considering capital-intensive business, a company with financially strong promoters enjoys an advantage over others as their ability to infuse and/or raise funds in a timely manner is the key rating consideration. The strong group presence / parentage would result in enhanced financial flexibility. Since infrastructure projects are generally financed on a project finance basis, CareEdge Ratings examines articulation of promoters’ intent to extend need-based support beyond the committed risk capital.

# **4. Financial risk analysis** 

As described in the beginning of this criteria paper, first three factors and their sub-factors aim to capture fundamental strengths and weaknesses of an airport operator, while key credit metrics covers the company’s financial risk profile. 

For evaluating detailed credit metrics, CareEdge Ratings follows its standard ratio analysis methodology to assess financial risk of companies. CareEdge Ratings emphasis on debt to profit before interest, lease rentals, depreciation and taxation (PBILDT), interest coverage, debt service coverage and project life coverage ratio (PLCR) as important parameters for analysis of financial risk. Airports concessions and loan agreement have well-defined waterfall with provision for liquidity reserves or for future capex among others. PLCR is calculated as net present value (NPV) of cash flow available for debt servicing over the remaining life of project divided by the current debt levels. CareEdge Ratings analyses airport companies based on cash flow approach. For more details, please refer to CareEdge Ratings’ criteria Financial Ratios- Non-Financial Sector on our website <u>www.careratings.com.</u>

# **Other aspects:** 

Foreign currency fluctuation risk: Though airports have moderate share of income in foreign currency denominations (revenue from duty-free sales or tariff levied on international passengers), which is susceptible to fluctuations in foreign currency. Airport entities importing equipment, spare parts and having external commercial borrowings are exposed to fluctuation in foreign currency. Thus, the entity’s hedging policy needs to be assessed for cash flow impact. 

Refinancing Risk: Debt from banks/FIs for funding expansion plan for airports are available for a tenor of 3- 10 years; it is exposed to refinancing risk. So, while refinancing risk is in general considered as negative from a ratings perspective, long-term income generation ability of an airport lends substantial financial flexibility, which is duly considered while assessing refinancing risk. As such, impact of this risk on the credit profile of an airport will differ on a case-to-case basis. 

The airport operator not only collects landing and parking charges from an airline but also passenger and user development fees, which is usually built into the ticket price. While analysing cash flow, CareEdge Ratings also takes into consideration the debtor’s analysis- outstanding debtors, ageing debtors and right off  in the past. 

# **5. Project risk analysis** 

If the company is implementing the greenfield/expansion airport project, then apart from parameters discussed in this criteria paper above, due consideration to implementation risk is also given. 

For evaluating detailed project implementation risk, CareEdge Ratings follows its standard methodology to assess project implementation (please refer to CareEdge Ratings’ criteria for Infrastructure Sector Ratings on CARE Rating’s website <u>www.careratings.com.</u> 

# **6. Environmental Social and Governance Risk** 

In the last few years, Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) risks have started gaining importance across the globe and are increasingly influencing investment decisions. The companies may have to incur operational or capital costs towards mitigating these risks. CareEdge Ratings analyses the impact of ESG risks on the credit profile of an entity by assessing the expected impact of these costs on the future earnings/revenue/cash flows of rated entities including airport companies. Considerations with respect to ESG aspects are an integral part of assessing credit risk and get addressed under parameters, wherever relevant. Importance of each risk may vary across sectors. 

Environmental Risk: Airport sector has low direct exposure to environmental risk. Environmental risk for this sector could indirectly manifest if more stringent air emission and carbon regulations on airlines cause increase in airfares, influencing passenger traffic and resultant throughput at airports. 

Social Risk: Social risk emanates from requirement of large manpower and risk of protest with local communities for higher noise pollution among others. 

Governance Risk: Similarly, governance parameters such as transparency, adherence to applicable regulations, public disclosures, and costs towards these objectives form part of the credit risk analysis.
