## **Rationale and key rating drivers** 

Ratings reaffirmation for bank facilities and instruments of KEI Industries Limited (KEI) factors in the company’s strengthening business risk profile, characterised by growing scale of operations from robust end user industries demand and recently concluded capacity additions while maintaining profitability margins. CARE Ratings Limited (CareEdge Ratings) envisages that KEI will continue to grow while registering mid-teens growth in the near-to-medium-term. This is expected to gradually improve operating margins benefitting from enhanced geographical diversification in exports, improved operational efficiencies and increased contribution from high-margin segments. 

Ratings also draw strength from KEI’s long track record of operations and established position in the industry with a diversified product portfolio. The company’s diversified and reputed customer base, geographically distributed dealership network, adequate order book and presence in the growing wires and cables industry are supported by experienced promoters. Ratings also factor in strong financial risk profile, reflected in robust capital structure and coverage indicators, which is expected to sustain considering no debt-laden capital expenditure planned. 

CARE Ratings Limited (CareEdge Ratings) also takes cognisance of KEI’s large greenfield expansion plans of ₹1,700-1,800 crore at Sanad, which is being funded through a mix of internal accruals and qualified institutional placement proceeds. Phase I of the project has already commenced in December 2025 (with a slight delay of three months) and the entire project is expected to be commissioned by Q4FY27 (FY refers to April 01 to March 31). This greenfield expansion is critical for the company to tap growing demand from domestic and export markets. CareEdge Ratings believes KEI will commence this project in a timely manner and draw envisaged benefits, which are critical for its overall credit risk profile. 

However, rating strengths are tempered by KEI’s working capital intensive operations, susceptibility of operating margins to adverse movement in raw material prices, foreign currency fluctuations, and intense competition in the wires and cables industry.

# **Outlook:** Stable

# **Key strengths** 

## **Diversified product portfolio supporting its healthy business profile** 

KEI’s cable division has a wide range of products (portfolio of over 400 products), including power cables (comprises low tension [LT], high tension [HT] and extra high voltage [EHV] power cables) up to and including 400 kV grade, control and instrumentation cables, rubber cables, winding, flexible and house wires, specialty cables, submersible cables, OVC/poly-wrapped winding wires and stainless-steel wires. The cable segment has been the major contributor to revenues constituting ~62% of KEI’s net sales in FY26 (~64% in FY25), house wire division and SS wires contributed ~35% in FY26 (~32% in FY25), while engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC) division contributed the remaining ~3% in FY26 (~4% in FY25). KEI has a technical collaboration with BruggKabel AG, Switzerland, for manufacturing EHV cables. Its presence across diverse products and geographies, tailormade products for institutional sales and strong penetration towards retail sales enables it to cater to a wide range of customer requirements across sectors and provides insulation from slowdown in sector or product segments, which is expected to continue going forward. 

<mark>Due to intense competition and long working capital cycle, KEI has curtailed its EPC business and simultaneously increased the share of retail and export sales over the years, which is expected to lead to continuous improvement in operating profitability margin and collection period. The share of retail sales in the revenue mix improved significantly from 29% in FY20 (FY refers to April 01 to March 31) to 54% in FY26, while the share of EPC business declined from 16% to ~3% in the same period. Cumulatively, these steps have enabled the company to reduce its gross operating cycle from 201 days in FY21 to 132 days in FY26.</mark> 

<mark>KEI has strengthened its dealership network, with over 2125 dealers as on March 31, 2026 (2,090 dealers as on June 30, 2025), and over 20,000 retailers in its network, reflecting its growing retail footprint.</mark> 

## **<mark>Growth in scale of operations and profitability and further increase in capacities to support business growth</mark>** 

<mark>KEI’s total operating income (TOI) grew at a compounded annual growth rate (CAGR) of 22% in FY21-FY26, while it grew by ~21% y-o-y in FY26 supported by 25% growth in retail sales and 45% growth in exports sales and over 8% growth in institutional sales and de-growth in EPC segment of ~9%.</mark> 

<mark>KEI’s PBILDT margin stood stable at 10.71% in FY26 (PY: 10.03%). Company continued to shift toward the dealer/distribution (B2C/retail) business, which is structurally better for working capital and margins. With increasing focus of KEI on growing export & retail sales and steady curtailment of lower margin EPC business, KEI’s PBILDT margin is expected to improve by ~25-30 bps every year in the medium term. KEI’s ROCE has remained healthy, although moderated to ~19.98% in FY26 (PY: 21.11%) considering increase in capital employed due to raising of ₹2,000 crore from QIP. ROCE is likely to further moderate and expected to remain at the level of 15-18% in the next two years until profitability and cash accruals started flowing through the optimum utilisation of new Sanand plant. Currently, the company is operating at almost optimum capacity utilisation level of ~80% across all major segments, although capacity utilisation moderated from over 90% in FY24 due to brownfield expansion undertaken in FY25 and greenfield expansion undertaken at Sanand Plant (Phase 1). KEI is already undergoing a greenfield expansion in its cable manufacturing capacity (LT, HT and EHV) at Sanand, Gujarat with a capex of ~₹1700-1800 crore, which is being funded from ₹1,450 crore of qualified institutional placements (QIP) proceeds and balance through internal accruals and available liquidity. The company has already spent ~₹1,065 crore till Q4FY26.</mark> 

<mark>In addition to Sanand, the company also plans to set up new LT/HT capacities, with proposed capex of ₹1,500 crore and ₹500 crore, respectively. In the next 2–3 years, the company is expected to incur annual capex of ~₹600–700 crore, which is likely to be funded primarily through internal accruals and equity raise.</mark> 

<mark>KEI’s brownfield expansions in FY26 and this greenfield project are likely to provide additional capacity to grow its TOI at the annual rate of 12-15% in the medium term. The company is witnessing robust demand for its products from end-user industries such as renewable power generation, telecommunication, infrastructure projects including National Infrastructure Pipeline and metro railways, and enhancement of manufacturing capacities supported by Production-Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme among others. CareEdge Ratings believe that KEI will significantly benefit from this capex once it is completed, and will enable KEI to grow at 12-</mark>

## **<mark>Strong financial risk profile</mark>** 

The company’s capital structure, marked by overall gearing ratio, remains strong at 0.15x as on March 31, 2026 (PY: 0.08x). Debt coverage and leverage indicators, marked by interest coverage ratio and total debt to PBILDT, also continued to remain strong at 20.22x (PY: 17.54x) and 0.78x (PY: 0.48x). Improvement in leverage in FY26 (FY refers to April 01 to March 31) was due to reduction in letter of credit-backed creditors, driven by higher cash purchases and improved profitability. 

KEI is net debt free as on March 31, 2026. The company is expected to generate healthy cash accruals, which are expected to be utilised towards capital expenditure and incremental working capital requirements. The company plans to incur capital expenditure of ~₹600-700 crore every year in FY27-FY29 towards the Sanand project and LT/HT capacities at new plants. This is expected to be funded through QIP proceeds, internal accruals, and available liquidity. In the absence of debt-laden capital expenditure, KEI’s financial risk profile, marked by leverage and coverage indicators, is expected to remain strong. 

## **Healthy order book position providing medium-term revenue visibility** 

KEI had an order book of ₹3,731 crore as on May 31, 2026 (₹4,012 crore as on June 30, 2025). The order book comprises 73% orders in the cables division (including exports, to be executed over three-four months), 19% in the extra high voltage (EHV) cables division (to be executed over 12-15 months), and 8% from the EPC division (to be executed in ~18 months). The order book excludes retail orders, which are executed immediately upon receipt. CareEdge Ratings expects the order book to be sustained and renewed in a timely manner, supported by the company's strong market position and long-standing relationships with its clients. 

## **Long track record of operations & Diversified and reputed customer base** 

KEI has over five decades of track record in the cable and wires business with established relationship with vendors and customers, providing stability to its business operations. Anil Gupta, KEI’s promoter, has been associated with the cable industry for over four decades, and has extensive experience in the manufacturing of cables. The top management comprises experienced professionals. The second generation is also involved in the business as Akshit Diviaj Gupta is handling the company’s retail business and EPC projects. 

KEI has wide sectoral and geographical diversification with its presence across over 60 countries and caters to majority core industries, including power, oil refineries, railways, automobiles, cement, steel, fertilizers, textiles and real estate. Domestically, KEI’s retail sales were fairly diversified with north, south, east and west regions contributing 39%, 17%, 17%, 27% of its sales, respectively, in FY26 (refers to April 01 to March 31). In FY26, exports accounted for ~15% of the total income (PY: 13%). This was due to lower EPC exports as KEI was executing a EPC project in Gambia in the last year. KEI has a diversified customer base, as top five customers contribute only 8% of total sales in FY26 (PY: 7%). 

# **Key weaknesses** 

## **<mark>Working capital intensive operations though improving</mark>** 

<mark>KEI’s operations are working capital intensive, considering high collection period associated with the EPC projects, which take 2-3 years to finalise. The realisation in the EPC projects is based on milestones achieved and ~10% retention money is released post successful completion of the project. The company’s gross operating cycle is at 132 days in FY26 (PY: 128 days) compared to 201 days in FY21. However, KEI’s operations still remain working capital intensive. Company’s receivable days improved to 55 days in FY26 (PY: 61 days). Payable days stood at 37 days in FY2 (PY: 37 days) due to higher cash purchases and, because of which, net operating cycle stood at 94 days in FY26 (PY: 91 days). CareEdge Ratings expects working capital cycle to remain at the similar levels in the medium term, supported by low share of the EPC business and timely release of retention money.</mark> 

## **<mark>Volatility in raw material prices and competitive nature of industry</mark>** 

<mark>KEI’s business is highly raw material intensive, with raw materials forming ~75-77% of the total operating costs. Main raw materials used are copper, stainless-steel strips and rods, GI wires, PVC & DOP and aluminium with purchases backed by letters of credit (LCs) or cash. Orders generally have a mix of variable and fixed-price contracts. The company maintains 2-2.5 months’ inventory, where pricing is already fixed. Since most orders are executed within three months’ time, the company is insulated against adverse raw material movement to a large extent. KEI’s forex risk is largely mitigated with natural hedge. KEI generally does not hedge its exposure, and hence, its profitability remains vulnerable to sharp movements in foreign currency rates, but it generally benefits from rupee depreciation.</mark> 

<mark>The West Asia crisis also led to uncertainties in global supply chains and commodity markets; however, the impact on KEI remained largely mitigated due to its diversified geographical presence and broad-based customer portfolio.</mark>

# **Liquidity** : Strong 

KEI has a strong liquidity profile, marked by expected cash accruals of ~₹1,000-1,100 crore in FY27, and nil term debt obligations (apart from minor lease liabilities). Fund-based limits was largely unutilised for 12 months ended March 2026 while non-fund-based limit utilisation stood at ~52% in the same period, providing cushion to the company’s liquidity profile. KEI had a cash and bank balance of ₹1,513 crore as on March 31, 2026, including ₹467 crore from QIP proceeds, which are expected to be utilised in the ongoing capital expenditure. Capacity expansion plans, including maintenance capital expenditure in FY27-29 entail capital expenditure of ~₹600-700 crore every year, which is expected to be sufficiently funded through qualified institutional placement proceeds, internal accruals and available liquidity. KEI’s cash flow from operations, unutilised working capital limits and available liquidity in the form of cash and liquid investments are adequate to support expansion projects, routine capital expenditure and incremental working capital requirements 

# **Environment, social, and governance (ESG) risks** 

**Environment:** The company is increasing use of renewable energy to reduce carbon footprint and ensuring zero discharge facility at all units. The company has recycled 3053.65 MT of PVC Scrap in FY25. The company is also planting trees around their facilities and using steel drum instead of wooden drum to conserve natural resources. KEI continues to increase its investments towards meeting regulatory standards and reduce environmental risk. 

**Social:** The company ensures healthy and safe environment for the employees and product end-users. The company also provides ESG related training to employees, to ensure ESG compliance across the value chain. Thus, social risk of the company remains low and does not materially affect its credit profile. 

**Governance:** The company is managed by professional board of directors, who have extensive experience in industry. The Board comprises eight Directors including two women directors as on March 31, 2026. Independent Directors are over 50% of the total number of Directors. There is audit committee, nomination and remuneration committee, stakeholder relationship committee; risk management committee, which reduces governance risk.
