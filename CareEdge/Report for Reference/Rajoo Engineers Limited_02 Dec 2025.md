## **Rationale and key rating drivers** 

Ratings assigned to bank facilities of Rajoo Engineers Limited (REL) have been removed from ‘rating watch with developing implications (RWD)’, as REL has successfully acquired 60% stake of Kohli Printing And Converting Machines Private Limited (Kohli), which was funded through fresh equity issue of ₹160 crore raised via qualified institutional placement (QIP) route. 

Ratings assigned to bank facilities of REL continue to derive strength from its established presence in the plastic extrusion machinery industry marked by track record of operations of more than three and a half decades, wide product offering, diversified geographical presence, and experienced promoters. Ratings also factor in the company’s growing albeit moderate scale of operations, healthy profitability, strong financial risk profile, and adequate liquidity. 

However, ratings continue to be constrained by REL’s working capital intensive operations, profitability susceptible to fluctuation in raw material prices and foreign exchange rates, vulnerability to inherent cyclicality in demand from end-user flexible packaging industry, technology obsolescence risk, and presence in a competitive engineering capital goods industry. 

## **Rating sensitivities: Factors likely to lead to rating actions** 

### **Positive factors** 

- Significant increase in scale of operations supported by diversification of product portfolio along with improvement in profit before interest, lease rentals, depreciation and taxation (PBILDT) margin above 15% on a sustained basis. 

- • Improvement in operating cycle below 90 days. 

### **<mark>Negative factors</mark>** 

- Significant decline in scale of operations along with PBILDT margin on a sustained basis. 

- Deterioration in the capital structure marked by overall gearing ratio above 1.00x on a sustained basis. 

- Elongation in operating cycle beyond 180 days on a sustained basis.

# **Established track record of operations with diversified geographical presence** 

Incorporated in 1986 as a manufacturer of plastic extrusion machinery, REL has a long operational track record of more than three and a half decades in this industry. With its long track record, it has established presence in the industry and has developed good relationship with its customers as well as suppliers. It has a wide product basket, including monolayer machines, multi-layer machines, lamination lines, vacuum forming and sheet manufacturing machines among others. REL’s revenue profile is geographically diversified marked by export sales forming ~50% of its total operating income (TOI) in the last five years ended FY25. REL exports to more than 70 countries with major focus on Africa, Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS) region, Gulf and South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation (SAARC) region. In case of export sales, REL generally undertakes sale against advance payment, however it also covers sales against sight letter of credit (sight LC). Moreover, the company’s customer base remained diversified with top 10 customers accounting for ~35% of its TOI in FY25 [PY: 35%]. Being in the capital goods industry, its top customer base keeps on changing based on capex plans at the customers’ end. 

With acquisition of majority stake in Kohli, which is engaged in manufacturing printing, coating, laminating, and other converting machines for flexible packaging and other industrial applications, REL is now able to cater to multiple product requirements of its existing customers. 

# **Growing scale of operations with healthy profitability** 

REL is engaged in manufacturing plastic extrusion machineries, which find application across industries such as packaging, construction and building materials, automotives, electrical and electronics, medical and healthcare, consumer goods, and industrial applications among others. REL’s TOI grew by 28% Y-o-Y to ₹254 crore in FY25 (PY: ₹198 crore) and further by 65% Y-o-Y to ₹182 crore in H1FY26 (PY: ₹110 crore) on the back of higher demand and timely execution of orders on hand. Moreover, the outstanding order book increased from ₹244 crore as on December 17, 2024 to ₹308.46 crore as on November 19, 2025, providing medium-term revenue visibility. 

REL commands healthy profitability owing to its presence in a niche machinery manufacturing segment. Operating profit of the company as marked by its PBILDT margin improved in FY25 as well as in H1FY26, as the company benefited from execution of higher customised orders and increasing export sales proportion. REL’s PBILDT margin, and subsequently, profit after taxation (PAT) margin improved by 480 bps and 393 bps Y-o-Y, respectively, to 18.44% and 13.90% in FY25 [PY: 13.64% and 9.22%, respectively]. In H1FY26, PBILDT margin and PAT margin further improved to 23.07% and 15.35%, respectively. 

CareEdge Ratings expects REL’s scale of operations to register healthy growth on the back of order book on hand while maintaining its prevailing profitability margins. 

# **Strong financial risk profile** 

REL’s capital structure remained strong with zero debt as on March 31, 2025, with nil overall gearing owing to minimal reliance on debt. Moreover, REL had liquid funds in excess of total outstanding debt, resulting in net debt free position over last five years ended FY25. Also, net worth base augmented from ₹152 crore as on March 31, 2025  to ₹340.47 crore as on September 30, 2025, post funds raised via QIP issue in September 2025. These funds were utilised for acquisition of 60% equity stake in Kohli. Total outside liabilities to tangible net worth (TOL/TNW) also remained comfortable at 1.04x as on March 31, 2025 and 0.80x as on September 30, 2025. 

With minimal reliance on debt, debt coverage indicators of the company remained comfortable marked by PBILDT interest coverage ratio of 50x [H1FY26: 50x] and total debt to gross cash accruals (TD/GCA) of 0.01x in FY25 [H1FY26: 0.29x]. 

CareEdge Ratings expects REL’s strong financial risk profile to sustain in absence of planned major debt-funded capex and healthy profit accretion to reserves.

## **Key weaknesses** 

## **High working capital intensity of operations** 

The company’s operations are working capital intensive in nature mainly due to requirement of high inventory holding to cater to various products. Lead time of machines is four to nine months resulting in inventory holding period of ~140-180 days. Furthermore, machine delivery is linked to the project status at client site, and hence till then, same would be under work in process (WIP) of REL. The collection period remained short and in the range of 20-30 days as it mainly takes orders against advance payment/Sight letter of credit (LC). Against this, REL receives credit period of ~45-90 days from its suppliers. Operating cycle elongated from 157 days in FY24 to 189 days in FY25 mainly on account of increase in WIP inventory from 58 days in FY24 to 106 days in FY25 to support high order execution scheduled for H1FY26. Despite high working capital intensity, working capital borrowings remained low as it is largely funded through customer advances and internal accruals. 

## **Vulnerability to inherent cyclicality in demand from end-user industry, technology obsolescence risk, and presence in competitive industry** 

REL's customers are mainly from the flexible packaging industry and the addition of new facilities or expansion of the existing facilities by these players depends on consumer demand and the country's economic condition. REL's fortunes are thus tied to the capital expenditure cycle in this industry. 

Plastic extrusion machinery segment is highly competitive characterised by presence of large-established players along with competition from overseas players with established brand names. Moreover, REL operates in a technology-intensive segment and timely updation to emerging technology becomes inevitable, hence, there is inherent technology obsolescence risk associated with this industry. 

## **Susceptibility of profitability to fluctuation in raw material prices and foreign exchange rate** 

The key raw materials required in manufacturing of machines are stainless steel (SS), mild steel (MS) and SS/MS parts, which REL procures from the domestic market, whereas it imports the automation parts (control panel and others). Steel prices are volatile in nature exposing the profitability to fluctuation in raw material prices. However, the same is partially mitigated as REL has order-backed manufacturing and it books major raw material post receipt of orders. 

REL generates ~40-50% revenue through exports and imports ~15-25% of its raw material, which provides natural hedge to some extent. For balance unhedged portion, in absence of active hedging policy, REL’s profitability is susceptible to adverse foreign exchange rate fluctuations. The company has registered forex gain of ₹0.75 crore in FY25 against ₹0.60 crore in FY24. 

# **Liquidity:** Adequate 

REL’s liquidity was adequate marked by sizable liquid funds, healthy cash flow from operations (CFO), negligible utilisation of working capital limit and moderate cash accruals against low scheduled debt repayment obligations. 

The company holds liquid funds to the tune of ₹132 crore as on September 30, 2025 [₹90 crore as on March 31, 2025] which increased from ₹34 crore as on March 31, 2024, supported by increased operating profit and high receipt of customer advances. CFO of the company increased significantly over the previous year to ₹67 crore in FY25 (PY: negative ₹7 crore) in tandem with increase in operating profit in FY25. REL generated net cash accruals of ₹38 crore in FY25 and ₹30 crore in H1FY26 and expected to generate annual cash accruals to the tune of ₹50-75 crore in FY26-FY28 period against low scheduled debt repayment obligations. Average utilisation of fund-based working capital facilities remained negligible in trailing 12 months ended in September 2025.

Incorporated in 1986 by C.N. Doshi and R.N. Doshi, REL (CIN: L27100GJ1986PLC009212) is engaged in the manufacturing of plastic extrusion machinery. The company designs in-house extrusion machinery and offers customised solutions as per customers’ specific needs. REL sells its products in the domestic market as well as exports to over 70 countries. REL sells its products under the brand name ‘Rajoo’ and operates through its sole manufacturing facility at Shapar, Rajkot (Gujarat), with a built-up area of 20,000 sq. mtrs. 

In 2011, REL entered a JV with Italy-based Bausano & Figli SPA (B&F) and formed a JV entity named Rajoo Bausano Extrusions Private Limited (RBEPL) to manufacture polyvinyl chloride (PVC) pipe extrusion machinery as well as wooden plastic composite (WPC) profile and board lines. In H1FY26, REL acquired 60% stake in Kohli, which is engaged in manufacturing printing, coating, laminating and other converting machines for flexible packaging and other industrial applications. 

The Doshi family has also set up associate concern named Essen Speciality Films Limited (ESFL; rated ‘CARE BBB+; Stable / CARE A2’), which is engaged in manufacturing ethylene vinyl acetate (EVA) and low-density polyethylene (LDPE)-based articles for various applications in bathroom, kitchen and dining, and home décor among others.
