## **Rationale and key rating drivers** 

Reaffirmation of ratings assigned to bank facilities of Ador Welding Limited (AWL) continues to factor in its strong business risk profile supported by its well-established position in the domestic welding industry, strong brand recall, and diversified product portfolio. The business risk profile is further aided by the company’s comprehensive sales and distribution network, which has helped it establish itself as a key player in the organised market. 

In FY22, AWL had announced proposed scheme of amalgamation of Ador Fontech Limited (AFL) into/with itself through merger by absorption. The scheme of amalgamation was approved by the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), Mumbai Bench through order dated August 20, 2024. The scheme became effective on September 25, 2024, on filing certified copy of the order with the Registrar of Companies from the appointed date of April 01, 2022. 

The company reported total operating income (TOI) of ₹1,130 crore in FY25 (FY refers to April 01 to March 31) compared to ₹1,079 crore in FY24. The welding division reported muted volume growth due to slowdown in capex and declining steel prices in Q2 and Q3 of FY25. In H1FY26, the company reported 2.20% growth in TOI to ₹508 crore despite stagnant volumes but improved realisations due to price discipline and better product mix. 

Under the Flux and Process (F&P) division, the company is executing a project for Oil and Natural Gas Corporation Limited (ONGC) worth ₹123 crore signed in July 2022. The division has been loss-making since FY23, with cumulative losses of ₹61.26 crore up to H1FY26. The company recognised expenses of ₹27.92 crore towards cost overrun and liquidated damages related to the project in H1FY26. 

The project is expected to be completed by end of FY26. As per the management, the entire loss on the project has already been provided for in H1FY26, and therefore, profitability in H2FY26 is not expected to be adversely impacted by this project. Going forward, the company plans to undertake smaller-sized projects to improve execution efficiency and profitability. 

Capital structure remains comfortable with overall gearing at 0.04x and interest coverage of 33x. The company’s liquidity position is supported by moderately utilised bank lines and cash and cash equivalent of ₹86 crore as on September 30, 2025. Ratings remain constrained by susceptibility to raw material price volatility, foreign exchange risk, and intense competition in the welding industry from domestic and international players. 

## **Rating sensitivities: Factors likely to lead to rating actions** 

### **Positive factors** 

- Increase scale of operations with improvement in operating margins by over 13% on a sustained basis and/or improved profitability from the Equipment, Flares and Process Equipment division. 

- Maintain operating cycle below 60 days with improved receivable management, thereby reducing reliance on external borrowings. 

- Maintain return on capital employed (ROCE) above 25% on a sustained basis. 

### **Negative factors** 

- Deterioration in performance due to decline in total operating income and segmental losses, resulting in operating margin remaining below 8% on a sustained basis and interest coverage ratio below 6x. 

- Unprofitable projects in the Flares and Process Equipment division impacting blended operating profitability margin. 

- Total debt to profit before interest, lease rentals, depreciation, and taxation (TD/PBILDT) above 1x on a sustained basis.

# **Key strengths** 

## **Well-established and experienced promoter group** 

Incorporated in 1951, the company is promoted by J. B. Advani and Company and is a flagship company of the Ador Group. The company has over seven decades of experience in welding products and services business. The company’s day-to-day operations are undertaken by a team of qualified and experienced professionals led by Aditya T Malkani, Managing Director, AWL, who belongs to the promoter family. J. B. Advani and Company was established in 1908 as a small trading company and has become the Ador Group’s promoter and holding company. The promoter group’s shareholding in AWL stood at 53.72% as on September 30, 2025. Presently, the Ador Group comprises companies operating in engineering products and services, cosmetic products, and green energy solutions. 

## **Well-established brand offering, diversified product portfolio supported by comprehensive sales and distribution network** 

The company is engaged in manufacturing welding consumables, welding and cutting equipment, gas cutting products, welding automation products and systems (WAPS), personal protective equipment and accessories for safety, and has Flux and Process (F&P) business. It offers a wide variety of over 200 types of electrodes, fluxes, flux-cored wires, and specially customised electrodes and services such as design, manufacture, erection and commissioning, mechanical, electrical, and instrumentation of process packages, process equipment, flare system, and components. The company’s annual production of welding consumables in the electrodes and wire division stood at ~90,000 metric tonne and equipment at ~30,000 units annually. The company has a strong distribution network with over 400 distributors selling its products across countries. 

## **Comfortable financial risk profile** 

The financial risk profile remains comfortable with low utilisation of working capital limits and no long-term borrowing, leading to comfortable gearing and interest coverage ratios. Overall gearing stood at 0.04x on March 31, 2025, and interest coverage ratio measured as PBILDT/interest remained strong at 33x in FY25. Financial risk profile is envisaged to remain comfortable, supported by low gearing with no long-term borrowings and strong interest coverage ratio. 

## **Muted growth in TOI in FY25 and H1F26 with decline in operating profit** 

The company reported TOI of ₹1,130 crore in FY25 compared to ₹1,079 crore in FY24. The welding division reported muted volume growth due to slowdown in capex and declining steel prices in Q2 and Q3 of FY25. In H1FY26, the company reported 2.20% growth in TOI to ₹508 crore despite stagnant volumes, supported by improved realisations due to better product mix and price discipline. 

Under the F&P division, the company is executing a large project for ONGC worth ₹123 crore signed in July 2022. The division has been loss-making since FY23, with cumulative losses of ₹61.26 crore up to H1FY26. The company recognised expenses of ₹27.92 crore towards cost overrun and liquidated damages related to the project in H1FY26. 

The project is expected to be completed by end of FY26. As per management, the entire loss on the project has already been provided for in H1FY26, and therefore, profitability in H2FY26 is not expected to be adversely impacted by this project. Going forward, the company plans to undertake smaller-sized projects to improve execution efficiency and profitability. 

The company has launched newer, margin-accretive products such as data mapping/reading solutions, electric battery welders, and improved fluxes, which have begun to gain market traction. Export sales stood at ₹153 crore in FY25, supported by a strong presence in the UAE. The company currently exports to over 15 countries, reflecting a diversified geographic footprint.

# **Key weaknesses** 

## **Susceptibility to raw material price volatility and foreign exchange risk** 

Steel is the key raw material required by the company for its manufacturing activities, and the company procures it domestically from multiple players. Inventory levels are planned based on production requirements. Raw material price volatility is passed on to the end-users to some extent, mitigating input cost pressure. However, time lag in passing on the cost escalation will impact the company’s operational performance, which may affect operating margins. 

Given the presence of export sales, the company enters into forward cover contracts to hedge its foreign exchange exposure. However, the company does not undertake any hedging for commodity price risks. 

## **Prevalent competition in welding industry from domestic and international players** 

The welding industry comprises welding equipment, consumables, and welding services. The total industry size is estimated to be ~₹10,000-₹12,000 crore with a share of welding consumables (70%), welding equipment (15%), cutting (10%), and automation (5%). Of this total industry size, the organised sector forms 50-55% and the unorganised sector forms 45-50% of the market size, and within the organised players, AWL has a strong presence in the consumables space. The welding consumables market accounts for a significant share in the welding industry compared to welding equipment and welding services. Electrodes, wire, and filler metals are the key consumables used across welding processes. Some key product types of welding consumables include stick electrode, solid wires, flux-cored wires, SAW wires, and fluxes. Stick electrodes and solid wires are the key welding consumables produced by most welding companies in India. Welding finds wide application across industries such as automobiles, building and construction, pipelines, and oil and gas. Growth of the welding industry depends on consumption of steel across end-use industries. 

# **Liquidity** : Strong 

The company’s liquidity position of the company remains strong, supported by healthy cash and liquid investments of ₹101 crore on March 31, 2025, and ₹85.66 crore on September 30, 2025. The liquidity profile is further bolstered by substantial undrawn bank facilities, with fund-based working capital limits utilised at only ~5% for 12 months ended September 2025. Overall, working capital limits were utilised to the extent of ~40% in the same period. The company has a capital expenditure plan of ~₹40 crore in FY26, which is expected to be funded entirely through internal cash accruals. The company has no longterm borrowings, providing additional financial flexibility.

# **Environment, social, and governance (ESG) risks** 

On the environmental risk aspect, potential material risks associated with the welding business include exploitation of natural resources, change in existing regulations or new regulations impacting sourcing of materials, emissions, waste generation, storage and use of materials, quality of finished goods, issues in handling hazardous and waste materials, and use of plastic products for packaging. To mitigate these risks, AWL eliminated the usage of plastic bags for packing the SAW flux and has started 100% usage of recyclable paper bags for packaging. It has also reduced its energy expenditure by ~15% by reducing the testing time of individual machines. The company has installed solar power plants in Silvassa factory in 2021-22 of 115 KWP and 518 KWP in 2019-20 capacity at Raipur facility, which effectively reduced greenhouse gas emission by 330 MT. 

On the social risk aspect, AWL is covered under CSR provisions stipulated by Companies Act, 2013. All provisions such as constitution of CSR committee and prescribed minimum amount of CSR expenditure have been duly complied with and details included in CSR annexure to director’s report as required to be reported. 

On governance risk aspect, AWL has complied with all applicable SEBI guidelines and its policies are in line with best corporate governance practices, including appointment of minimum number of independent directors on its board as mandated by guidelines. The corporate governance report has been annexed to the director’s report.
