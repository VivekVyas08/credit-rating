## **Rationale and key rating drivers** 

Ratings assigned to bank facilities and non-convertible debenture issue of Tata Chemicals Limited (TCL) continue to derive significant strength from its robust business profile, highlighted by TCL’s leadership position in the global soda ash industry, geographical diversification across India, North America, Europe and Africa, and a diversified product portfolio spanning basic chemistry and specialty products segments. The company’s substantial natural soda ash capacity supports cost-efficient production. Ratings also reflect TCL’s strong financial flexibility and refinancing capability, underpinned by the Tata Sons Private Limited’s (TSPL) parentage, comfortable financial risk profile and strong liquidity. 

These strengths are partly offset by moderation in total operating income (TOI) and profitability in FY25, driven by global soda ash oversupply, lower realisations and margin pressures across key markets. H1FY26 also reflected subdued demand conditions, although operational efficiencies supported margin improvement. Ratings consider the inherent risks of the soda ash business, including sensitivity to end-user demand cycles linked to economic growth, price volatility, foreign exchange fluctuations and competition from imports. 

## **Rating sensitivities: Factors likely to lead to rating actions** 

### **Positive factors** 

- Sustainability of revenue growth and improvement in profit before interest, lease rentals, depreciation and taxation (PBILDT) margin to 22% on a sustained basis. 

- Consolidated net debt/PBILDT below 1.50x on a sustained basis. 

### **Negative factors** 

- Large debt-funded capex/ acquisition, if any, undertaken by the company thereby deteriorating the consolidated net debt/PBILDT beyond 3.0x on a sustained basis. 

- Substantial decline in profitability or substantial increase in debt leading to deterioration in interest cover below 5.0x on a sustained basis. 

## **Analytical approach:** Consolidated 

CARE Ratings Limited (CareEdge Ratings) has adopted a consolidated analytical approach, factoring in the strong operational and financial linkages between TCL and its subsidiaries. Ratings also draw comfort from TCL’s strategic importance within the Tata Group and the support derived from its ultimate parent, TSPL, which holds a significant shareholding in the company. Refer to Annexure-6 for list of entities consolidated with TCL. 

## **Outlook:** Stable 

The stable outlook reflects the company’s ability to maintain a comfortable operating and financial risk profile, supported by its leadership position in the global soda ash industry and absence of significant debt-funded capital expenditure or acquisition plans in the near-to-medium term.

# **Detailed description of the key rating drivers:** 

# **Key strengths** 

## **<mark>Experienced promoters and well-established presence in global soda ash industry</mark>** 

<mark>As part of the Tata Group, TCL is promoted by Tata Sons Private Limited (TSPL), which holds 31.90% stake in the company, with other Tata Group entities holding 6.08%. TCL benefits from its strategic importance within the Tata Group and financial flexibility and support derived from TSPL. TCL is the world’s third-largest producer of soda ash, with an annual soda ash production capacity of 3.98 million metric tonne (MMT), ~two-thirds derived from natural soda ash operations, supporting lower operating costs and efficient production. The company’s soda ash operations are diversified across natural soda ash plants in North America and Kenya, and synthetic soda ash production at Mithapur, Gujarat. Its manufacturing footprint spans four continents—Asia, Europe, Africa and North America—providing geographic diversification and a strong global presence. TCL is investing in capacity enhancement through debottlenecking, salt improvement projects and other operational optimisation.</mark> 

## **<mark>Geographically diversified operations and presence in both basic and specialty chemicals</mark>** 

<mark>TCL operates a global distribution network supported by 15 manufacturing sites across five countries, with a strong presence in the United States, Europe, East Africa and Asia. Its portfolio comprises Basic Chemistry products—soda ash, sodium bicarbonate and salt—and Specialty Products, including agrochemicals, seeds, silica and prebiotics. In FY25, 81% of TCL’s revenue came from Basic Chemistry, led by soda ash (56%), salt (16%) and bicarbonate (5%), while Specialty Products contributed 19%, with crop protection at 13%, seeds at 3% and other specialty products at 3%. Basic Chemistry remains the primary revenue driver, while Specialty Products offer a steady, smaller contribution. TCL’s products serve diversified industries such as glass, detergents, fastmoving consumer goods (FMCG) and automobiles, mitigating business risk</mark> **<mark>.</mark>** 

## **Stable operational performance** 

In FY25, TCL’s TOI declined by 3.4% compared to the previous year and PBILDT margin contracted, primarily due to global soda ash oversupply and resultant price pressure. Despite this moderation, the company maintained stable operational performance across regions and product lines. Strong demand in key markets such as India and China supported volumes, while efficiency initiatives, high-capacity utilisation and a diversified product and geographic portfolio helped mitigate the impact of weaker pricing. 

In H1FY26, TOI moderated by 2.47% compared to H1FY25; however, PBILDT margin improved to 18.14% (H1FY25: 16.96%), driven by softer coal prices, operational efficiencies and disciplined cost control. India benefited from higher throughput, the US operations experienced temporary margin support from an improved domestic sales mix, the UK business stabilised postreconfiguration and cessation of loss-making operations, while Kenya saw improved domestic performance supported by additional calciner capacity. Overall, the company demonstrated operational resilience in H1FY26 despite persistent global headwinds. 

## **<mark>Comfortable financial risk profile</mark>** 

<mark>Being one of the strategically important companies within the Tata Group, TCL continues to benefit from strong financial flexibility and refinancing ability. The company’s total debt increased from ₹5,859 crore in FY24 to ₹7,343 crore in FY25 (FY refers to April 01 to March 31), and further to ₹7,495 crore in H1FY26, largely reflecting increased working capital and currency translation impact on foreign debt. TCL issued ₹1,700 crore of non-convertible debentures (NCDs) in FY25 to refinance its Singapore loan, reducing exposure to foreign currency volatility. In FY26, the board approved an additional ₹1,500 crore NCD issuance to prepay term loans in its UK subsidiaries, replacing overseas borrowings with rupee-denominated debt and improving the currency risk profile of the consolidated entity. Despite the temporary rise in borrowings, TCL’s liquidity remains comfortable, supported by healthy internal accruals, completion of the capex cycle, and absence of large debt-funded projects in the near term.</mark> 

# **<mark>Liquidity:</mark>** <mark>Strong</mark> 

TCL maintains a strong liquidity profile, supported by consolidated cash and liquid investments of ₹1,136 crore as at end-H1FY26. Working capital utilisation remained moderate, with average fund-based and non-fund-based limits utilised at 30% and 35%, respectively, for the 12 months ended October 2025, providing adequate liquidity headroom. The company also benefits from strong financial flexibility owing to its Tata Group parentage, which enhances its ability to refinance and access capital markets. TCL is expected to comfortably meet its upcoming debt obligations through a combination of internal accruals, existing cash balances and stable operating cash flows. In addition, the company holds investments of ₹1,359 crore in affiliates and ₹7,522 crore in non-current investments as of H1FY26, further strengthening its overall liquidity position.

# **Key weaknesses** 

## **Inherent risks associated with soda ash business** 

The soda ash business is inherently exposed to cyclicality arising from global demand-supply imbalances, capacity additions and regional trade flows. Prices rose sharply in FY22 due to strong demand, and declined significantly FY23 onwards, as global supply expanded, particularly from China, Mongolia and Turkish exporters, while demand growth normalised. This resulted in a steep decline of over 25% in realisations in FY25 compared to the previous year, compressing margins. Pricing and demand weakness continued into H1FY26 as global oversupply persisted and Chinese inventory levels remained elevated, keeping realisations under pressure. Sectoral demand risks persist, as soda ash depends on end-use industries such as glass, detergents and lithium for electric vehicle (EV) batteries. Weakness in container and flat glass in Europe, slower ramp-up in solar glass capacity in India and demand moderation in lithium carbonate globally contributed to subdued off-take. On the supply side, incremental global capacities, especially from Inner Mongolia, and higher exports from Turkey created oversupply in Asian markets, adding to price pressures. Soda ash operations are energy-intensive, exposing producers to volatility in coal, natural gas and freight costs, which directly impact margins. Regional demand variations and policy-linked disruptions, combined with TCL’s significant exposure to the soda ash business (~56% of FY25 revenues), amplify the segment’s inherent risks and make its performance sensitive to global commodity cycles.
