## **Rationale and key rating drivers** 

Ratings reaffirmation factors in the steady performance of Genesys International Corporation Limited (GICL) in FY25 (refers to April 01 to March 31) and Q1 FY26, driven by increasing revenue and sustained profitability on the back of execution of sizeable orders in the light of increasing demand for geospatial services. The company’s total operating income (TOI) grew by ~57% YoY to ₹311.03 crore in FY25 with profit before interest, lease rentals, depreciation, and taxation (PBILDT) margin sustaining above 40%. However, despite the substantial increase in gross cash accruals to ₹125 crore in FY25, GICL’s net cash flow remained negative, indicating elongation of working capital cycle and pressure on free cash flow generation due to stretched collection period and unbilled revenue. With the increasing scale of operation, the unbilled revenue has increased to ₹199.86 crore as on March 31, 2025, which is 65% of TOI, impacting liquidity buffer. The company’s average utilisation of fund-based working capital limits stood high at 87% in the 12-months ending June 30, 2025, indicating limited buffer. Going forward, while CARE Ratings Limited (CareEdge Ratings) expects the company to sustain its performance in medium term given the increasing demand for geospatial services across multiple industries, improvement in the company’s liquidity position will remain a key monitorable. While reaffirming ratings, CareEdge Ratings also notes that GICL has raised additional equity capital of ₹110 crore in Q1 FY26 through a qualified institutional placement with the objective to fund business growth through investment in setting up of data centres, development of tech platform, updation of map content, building sensors, and strengthening IT infrastructure. 

Ratings continue to favourably factor in GICL’s established market position in the industry and favourable industry growth prospects. The company has a sizeable order book of over ₹350 crore as on June 30, 2025, which provides near term revenue visibility. Ratings are also supported by the company’s adequate capital structure and debt coverage metrics. However, ratings remain constrained by elongated working capital cycle due to stretched collection period and sizeable unbilled revenue. The company is exposed to customer concentration risk with top 10 customers contributing material portion towards the revenue.

The company has a long track record of operations of over two decades. Sajid Malik, Chairman and Managing Director of the company have an extensive experience of over two decades in the same line of business. GICL’s leading market position in digital mapping and its proven track record enables it to bag more orders. GICL has completed ‘All India Navigation Map’ covering extensive road network of all cities and villages. 

## **Comfortable capital structure and coverage metrics** 

The debt profile of the company majorly comprises working capital facilities, unsecured loan from related parties, lease liabilities followed by equipment and vehicle loan. The leverage position of the company continues to remain comfortable with overall gearing and total outside liabilities to total net worth (TOL/TNW)  at 0.22x and 0.42x, respectively, as on March 31, 2025 (PY: 0.12x and 0.32x, respectively). Its PBILDT interest coverage stood healthy at 15.26x in FY25 (FY24: 19.55x). Despite the enhancement in working capital limits, the debt metrics of the company are expected to remain comfortable due to healthy accruals and newly infused funds strengthening the net worth. 

# **Key weaknesses** 

## **High working capital intensity of operations** 

The working capital cycle of the company continues to remain elongated owing to high collection period and sizeable unbilled revenue and material portion of revenue being booked in second half of financial year. The company operates on two financial models, where revenue is either booked on milestone basis or subscription basis. As revenue contribution from milestone basis accounts for over 60% of revenue, the receivables remain high. Also, the general credit terms extended to customers is in the range of 90-100 days. In FY25, the average working capital cycle stood at 310 days (PY: 275 days) with average collection period swelling to 382 days, translating into weak PBILDT to cash flow from operations conversion. Dependence on government entities for majority revenue further exacerbates the collection period. The elongated working capital cycle puts pressure on the company’s liquidity position and could create cash flow mismatches and remains a key monitorable. 

## **Customer concentration risk** 

GICL’s clientele includes government entities, international organisations, companies from infrastructure, telecom, and logistics, among others, with government entities being the major revenue contributors. The customer concentration risk continues to remain high with top 10 customers contributing ~94% towards the total revenue of the company in Q1FY26 (FY25: 73%; Q1FY25: 99%). Loss of major customer or reduction in new orders from key customers could impact its business growth. 

## **Competitive nature of ITES industry and tender-based government contracts** 

GICL is engaged in ITES (providing GIS-embedded services), which is highly competitive with presence of big players with established marketing and distribution network. Growing competition exposes the company to typical industry risks such as ability to bag large-sized contracts for continuous cash flow and challenge of retaining technical personnel as it is a knowledge-based industry, which may result in lower growth rates. The company’s revenue profile is exposed to risks related to tender-based government projects and their successful and timely execution. 

# **Liquidity** : Adequate 

The liquidity is adequate with expected net cash flow from operations of ₹25-30 crore p.a. over the next 1-2 years (expected gross cash accruals of ₹130-160 crore p.a.) against scheduled debt repayment obligations (including lease liabilities) of ~₹21 crore in FY26 (including repayment of unsecured loan from non-banking financial company [NBFC]) and ~₹12.94 crore in FY27. The average utilisation of fund-based limits stood at 87% in the 12-months ended June 30, 2025. The free cash and bank balance stood modest at ₹3.93 crore as on March 31, 2025. However, the cash flow from operations stood negative at ~₹53 crore given the increase in receivables and unbilled revenue. The company is expected to incur sizeable capex of ₹30-50 crore p.a. in FY26FY27, which is likely to be largely funded from the proceeds of the recently concluded qualified institutional placements (QIP) of ₹110 crore.

# **Assumptions/Covenants:** Not applicable 

# **Environment, social, and governance (ESG) risks** 

Being in service-oriented business, GCIL’s direct exposure to environmental risks as well as those arising from regulations or policy changes remains low. However, the company is dependent on data centres, both owned and subscribed, for its operations which consume sizeable electricity, thereby indirectly exposing the company to environmental risks. GCIL faces the risk of data breaches and cyber-attacks that could affect the large volume of data it manages. Any material lapses could lead to substantial liabilities and reputational damage. However, the risk mitigates to an extent by its data security and privacy policies in place.
