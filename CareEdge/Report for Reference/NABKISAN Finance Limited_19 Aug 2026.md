## **Rationale and key rating drivers** 

<mark>The rating reaffirmation of the debt instruments of NABKISAN Finance Limited (NABKISAN) continues to be driven by its strong parentage of National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD) and strategic role in supporting agriculture and rural livelihoods through credit to farmer producer organisations (FPOs), agri-startups, non-banking financial companies (NBFCs), and microfinance institutions (MFIs). The completed merger of NABSAMRUDDHI Finance Limited (NABSAMRUDDHI) with NABKISAN in FY26 has strengthened the company's scale of operations, expanded its product offerings, and improved portfolio diversification across agriculture, MSME, microfinance, housing, renewable energy, and water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) financing. Post-merger, the combined entity has assets under management (AUM) of ₹7,066 crore and a tangible net worth (TNW) of ₹1,011 crore as of FY26.</mark> 

<mark>As a subsidiary of NABARD, NABKISAN continues to benefit from NABARD's capital, operational, and managerial support, including board representation that ensures alignment with its strategic objectives. This support was further demonstrated through the successful completion of rights issue of ~₹301.46 crore in Q2FY27, strengthening the company's capital base and supporting future business growth.</mark> 

<mark>The rating also factors in NABKISAN's adequate profitability and capitalisation metrics, and its comfortable asset quality. However, these strengths are tempered by the company's moderate scale of operations and concentrated exposure profile.</mark> 

<mark>CARE Ratings Limited (CareEdge Ratings) also notes that NABKISAN's exposure to the agriculture and FPO segments remains susceptible to risks arising from sectoral cyclicality, limited cash flow buffers, and the relatively early-stage nature of many FPOs. The company's exposure to this segment remains limited, accounting for only 2.85% of the overall portfolio, mitigating associated risks to a large extent.</mark>

# **Detailed description of key rating drivers** 

# **Key strengths** 

## **<mark>Strong parentage and strategic role in development of agriculture and rural sector</mark>** 

<mark>Established in 1997, NABKISAN, a subsidiary of NABARD, plays a strategic role in furthering NABARD’s mandate of promoting rural livelihoods through wholesale lending to FPOs, agri-startups, agri-based corporates, NBFCs, and MFIs. NABARD is India's apex development financial institution for the agriculture and rural sector, with an Advances of ₹9,32,549 crore, total assets of ₹10,98,318 crore, and a TNW of ₹88,793 crore as on March 31, 2026.</mark> 

<mark>The merger of NABSAMRUDDHI Finance Limited has further strengthened and diversified NABKISAN’s portfolio, with the company now having exposure to housing finance, renewable energy, and water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH)-related financing in addition to its traditional agriculture-focused lending segments. Through this, NABKISAN facilitates access to finance for grassroots entities engaged in agriculture and allied sectors. NABKISAN benefits from a strong multi-layered support structure from NABARD, which includes a significant equity holding of 89.12%, access to on-call refinance facilities, and capital support via catalytic capital funds for agri startups and innovation. The company also avails credit guarantee coverage through NABSANRAKSHAN, a fellow NABARD subsidiary, and in coordination across state governments. In addition to financial support, NABKISAN derives substantial managerial and operational backing from NABARD. Key leadership positions, including Managing Director &, Chief Executive Officer, Chief Financial Officer positions, are held by officers deputed from NABARD. Presence of NABARD's nominee director and representation on NABKISAN’s Board of Directors ensures strategic oversight, alignment with policy objectives, and continuity in governance. Operational synergies between NABKISAN and NABARD are further enhanced through shared infrastructure on arm length basis, policy coordination, and the joint implementation of government and state-level FPO financing programs. NABKISAN’s lending to NBFCs and MFIs serving rural and micro borrowers currently not fully served through parent entity, complementing NABARD's developmental mandate and extending outreach to underserved segments. Given NABKISAN’s integral role in executing NABARD’s rural credit and development agenda, CareEdge Ratings believes NABARD will continue to extend financial and managerial support in a timely manner, maintaining strategic control and oversight over NABKISAN.</mark> 

## **<mark>Adequate capitalisation metrics</mark>** 

<mark>NABKISAN continues to maintain healthy capitalisation, reflected in its tangible net worth of ₹1011 crore as on March 31, 2026, compared to ₹881 crore as on March 31, 2025. As on June 30, 2026, the company reported a TNW at ₹1042 crore. The company benefits from access to on-call refinance facilities from its parent, NABARD which constituted 87.70% of its borrowing profile as on March 31, 2026. While the borrowing profile largely depends on NABARD, NABKISAN has initiated measures to diversify its resource base. In this regard, the company secured a working capital line from a public sector bank in April 2025 and subsequently issued Tier II non-convertible debentures (NCDs) in February 2026.</mark> 

<mark>As on March 31, 2026, the company reported a gearing ratio of 6.07x, compared to 4.92x a year earlier. The gearing ratio moderated to 5.53x as of June 30, 2026.To support future growth and maintain adequate leverage, the company has completed ₹301.46 crore rights issue improving its capitalisation metrics. The company also maintains a comfortable capital adequacy position, with a capital adequacy ratio (CAR) of 22.26% as on March 31, 2026 (18.11% as on March 31, 2025) and 22.80% as on June 30, 2026.</mark> 

<mark>CareEdge Ratings expects timely and need-based capital support from NABARD, going forward the leverage is expected to remain in the range of 5-6x levels.</mark> 

## **<mark>Adequate profitability metrics</mark>** 

<mark>The company’s earnings profile remains stable and adequate, supported by portfolio growth, interest spreads, and internal accruals. In FY26, the company reported a profit after tax (PAT) of ₹132 crore on a total income of ₹545 crore, compared to ₹126 crore and ₹472 crore, respectively, in FY25. The profitability remained moderated due to decrease in net interest margin (NIM), as company reduced exposure to high yield MFI loans. Operating efficiency improved, with operating expenses as a percentage of average total assets (ATA) at 0.45% (0.52% in FY25) due to economies of scale post-merger. Credit costs remained stable at 0.05% (FY25: -0.04%) majorly due to the reversal of expected credit loss (ECL) provisions created in earlier years based on historical portfolio performance and risk assessment.</mark> 

<mark>Consequently, the company reported a return on total assets (ROTA) of 2.09% and a return on net worth (RONW) of 13.90% in FY26, compared to 2.57% and 15.34%, respectively, in FY25. In Q1FY27, the company reported a PAT of ₹31 crore. In the same period, the ROTA and RONW was 1.74% and 11.95%, impacted by higher provisions made against incremental NPAs.</mark>

<mark>Going forward, the company’s ability to maintain and enhance profitability while scaling up its portfolio and managing operating costs and asset quality will remain a key monitorable.</mark> 

## **<mark>Comfortable asset quality, susceptible to inherent risk associated in FPO lending</mark>** 

<mark>NABKISAN’s asset quality has shown improvement, with gross stage III assets (GS3) assets declining to 0.85% as on March 31, 2026, compared to 0.86% as on March 31, 2025 (Pre-merger: March 31, 2025: 1.31%; March 31, 2024: 2.00%). NABKISAN reported net stage III (NS3) assets at 0.27% as on March 31, 2026 (0.25% as on March 31, 2025). Improvement reflects better portfolio performance and continued risk management efforts.</mark> 

<mark>As on June 30, 2026, the GS3 assets stood at 0.94% and NS3 assets stood at 0.37%. These exposures are partially de-risked through a credit guarantee mechanism administered by NABSanrakshan Trustee Private Limited (a NABARD subsidiary), which provides government-backed coverage of up to 85% of the loan amount, subject to a cap of ₹2 crore per eligible FPO loan. Loan book is also covered under guarantee funds from Tamil Nadu Government and Odisha Governments.</mark> 

However, the company’s exposure to the agriculture and FPO segment remains susceptible to risks stemming from sectoral cyclicality, limited cash flow buffers, and the early-stage nature of many FPOs. As NABKISAN continues to expand its FPO loan book, maintaining asset quality at scale will remain a key monitorable from a credit perspective. 

# **Key weaknesses** 

## **<mark>Moderate scale of operations with concentrated exposures</mark>** 

<mark>NABKISAN focuses on its book building strategy and lends to NBFCs and MFIs, which are not catered to by NABARD directly. These bulk lending to NBFCs or MFIs is for MSME financing, green financing, agriculture financing, WASH Financing and other under penetrated segments. The company’s AUM demonstrated a significant growth trajectory, with AUM of ₹7,066 crore as on March 31, 2026. The growth in the company's operations was also partially driven by the merger, which resulted in higher disbursements, an expanded product portfolio, and enhanced geographic reach.</mark> 

<mark>However, the portfolio continues to exhibit concentration risk with top 25 exposures accounting for 55.65% of total exposures as on March 31, 2026, against 49.71% as on March 31, 2025. Top exposure accounted for 3.04% of the loan book and 19.95% of the TNW, reflecting concentration risk. The company’s portfolio remains skewed towards the bulk lending segment, with refinance to NBFCs and MFIs forming the dominant share at 87.18%. This is followed by pass-through certificates (PTCs; 6.17%), Direct Lending (2.85%), Direct Assignment (2.62%) lending to agri-corporates (0.65%), on lending to PO (0.28%), and activity-based financing through NGOs, Trusts, and Section 8 Companies (0.25%).</mark> 

<mark>Going forward, NABKISAN aims to rebalance its portfolio by gradually reducing exposure to the bulk lending segment and increasing direct lending through co-lending partnerships and greater focus on FPO and Agri Value Chain financing. This strategic shift is expected to enhance portfolio diversification and improve development impact.</mark> 

<mark>CareEdge Ratings notes that concentration risk is partly mitigated by relatively lower credit risk associated with the onlending portfolio in contrast to direct lending to FPOs or agri-startups.</mark> 

# **Liquidity** : Strong 

<mark>NABKISAN’s liquidity profile remains strong, supported by the structural characteristics of its portfolio and access to stable funding lines. A significant portion of its loan book comprises refinance lending with an average tenure of around three years, which is predominantly funded through corresponding refinance lines from NABARD (NABARD constitutes 87.63% of total company debt), resulting in a well-matched asset-liability profile.</mark> 

The asset and liability management (ALM) statement as on March 31, 2026, exhibited no cumulative mismatches. As on March 31, 2026, cash and bank balances, which included investments in fixed deposits, stood at ₹211 crore. Scheduled collections for the next six months stood at ₹1469 crore, against scheduled debt obligations for the same period of ₹1451 crore. Liquidity comfort is derived from the parentage of NABARD, with on call refinancing facilities and access to need-based financial support. This institutional backing significantly enhances NABKISAN’s financial flexibility and ability to meet short-term obligations.
