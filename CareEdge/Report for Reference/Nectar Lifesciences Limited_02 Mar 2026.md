## **Rationale and key rating drivers** 

Nectar Lifesciences Limited (NLL) has not paid the surveillance fees for the rating exercise as agreed in the rating agreement. In line with the extant SEBI Guidelines, CARE Ratings Limited’s (CareEdge Ratings) ratings on NLL’s bank facilities will now be denoted as CARE B+ (RWN)/ CARE A4 (RWN); ISSUER NOT COOPERATING. 

Users of this rating (including investors, lenders, and the public at large) are hence requested to exercise caution while using the above ratings. 

The revision in the long-term rating assigned to the bank facilities of Nectar Lifesciences Limited (NLL) reflects the weak financial performance reported during 9MFY26. The subdued performance follows the completion of the sale of its core API and formulation business to Ceph Lifesciences Private Limited (Ceph) for ₹1,253.98 crore and the divestment of its menthol segment for ₹19.90 crore in November 2025. The divestments have led to weakening of the company’s operating profile, which is expected to persist over the medium term as the company transitions to its restructured business model. 

CareEdge Rating notes that as per the disclosure made by the company the sale proceeds have been mainly utilised to fully repay its existing debt obligations, resulting in a debt-free status. However, with the divestment of the core business, the ratings remain constrained and continues to be on Rating Watch with Negative Implications due to the uncertainty surrounding the establishment of a viable and scalable new business model. The company’s ability to execute this transition in a timely manner and demonstrate stability in revenue and earnings going forward will be critical to the credit profile. 

## **Rating sensitivities: Factors likely to lead to rating actions** 

## **Positive factors** 

- Establishment of a viable and scalable residual or new business model, with timely execution and demonstrated stability in revenue and earnings post-divestment. 

- Consistent operating performance in the continuing business, with PBILDT margin above 10% and revenue stability demonstrated on a sustained basis. 

- Improved financial metrics, with total debt/PBILDT below 3.00x on a sustained basis. 

## **Negative factors** 

- Weak performance of residual business, with PBILDT margin falling below 6% or continued operating losses. 

- Delay or failure in redeploying sale proceeds, leading to lack of revenue visibility or business direction beyond 12 months. 

- Deterioration in financial metrics, with total debt/PBILDT exceeding 4.5x or weakening liquidity position.

# **Weak operational performance** 

NLL completed the divestment of its API and formulation business for ₹1,253.98 crore and its menthol division for ₹19.90 crore in November 2025. Following these transactions, revenue from continuing operations remained negligible at ₹1.71 crore in 9MFY26, compared to ₹1.69 crore in 9MFY25, reflecting the near-complete exit from its core operating segments. Revenue from discontinued operations declined significantly to ₹395.67 crore in 9MFY26 from ₹1,241 crore in 9MFY25. The company reported an overall net loss of ₹224.97 crore for 9MFY26, as against a net profit of ₹16.41 crore in 9MFY24, largely attributable to onetime adjustments and restructuring impacts arising from the sale of businesses. 

While the monetisation of these businesses has enabled debt reduction and alleviated near-term liquidity pressures, the divestments mark an exit from the company’s principal revenue-generating verticals, leading to a steep fall in operating scale and profitability. 

# **Uncertainty post core business divestment** 

With the divestment of the core business, there is uncertainty around what new business model the company will adopt, how it will be scaled, and whether it can generate stable revenue and earnings in the future. The absence of clarity on diversification plans or a defined roadmap for the residual business adds to this uncertainty, making the company’s future direction and ability to sustain operations an important area to watch. 

# **Succession risk** 

NLL’s promoter Sanjiv Goyal has two sons: Saurabh Goyal and Aryan Goyal. Both were working as Executive Directors and were shareholders. However, both resigned from the company in 2018 after amicable settlement within the family and have started their own ventures. There was a family partition deed between the members according to which, Saurabh and Aryan had issued an advertisement in the newspaper and filed a letter with The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) and Stock Exchanges regarding their disassociation with NLL by October 01, 2020. Though succession risk exists, the management has opined that the company is run by professional management, which will continue going forward.

# **Debt-free status and financial flexibility** 

Following the sale of its core operations, the company has fully repaid its outstanding debt obligations, resulting in a debt-free balance sheet. This clean capital structure provides financial flexibility and reduces near-term liquidity pressures, enabling the company to redeploy resources toward new ventures without the burden of leverage. 

# **Corporate continuity** 

Following the sale of its core business, the company continues its listing status and governance framework. These elements provide continuity and can serve as a base if the company chooses to explore new business opportunities in the future.
