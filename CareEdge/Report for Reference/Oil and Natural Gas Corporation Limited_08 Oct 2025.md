## **Rationale and key rating drivers** 

Ratings assigned to bank facilities and instruments of Oil and Natural Gas Corporation Limited (ONGC) continue to consider its majority ownership by the Government of India (GoI) and the company’s strategic importance to the GoI, as it plays a key role in energy security for the country. Ratings further derive comfort from ONGC’s dominant position, its experienced and professional management, and long track record in domestic exploration and production (E&P) industry. 

Ratings continue to be supported by its sound and resilient profitability margin, backed by robust E&P infrastructure and proven technical capabilities with presence across the hydrocarbon value chain, and the company’s comfortable financial risk profile, marked by low overall gearing ratio, and healthy debt protection metrics. Ratings also factor in sizeable fund infusion by ONGC in one of its erstwhile joint ventures (JV) company, ONGC Petro additions Limited (OPaL), which has now become its subsidiary. 

However, ratings remain susceptible to inherent risk related to the E&P business, regulatory risks, geopolitical risk for overseas operations, and large capital expenditure (capex) requirements to replace reserves.

## **Key strengths** 

### **Strong parentage and strategic importance to GoI** 

A Maharatna public sector undertaking (PSU), ONGC was set up by the GoI in 1956 to plan, promote, and implement programmes for the development of petroleum resources and production and sale of petroleum and petroleum products. ONGC has significant sovereign ownership, with the GoI holding 58.89% stake in the company as on June 30, 2025. ONGC continues to be of high strategic importance for the GoI, as it is the largest oil and gas company of India present across the hydrocarbon value chain,

# **Experienced management** 

ONGC is managed by an experienced management team. Arun Kumar Singh, ONGC’s Chairman, has over 37 years of experience in the oil and gas industry in India and abroad. Before assuming charge as Chairman at ONGC, he was the Chairman and Managing Director of Bharat Petroleum Corporation Limited (BPCL; rated CARE AAA; Stable/CARE A1+). Pankaj Kumar, Director-Production, has extensive 35-year of experience in the oil and gas sector encompassing multifaceted expertise in operations management, well engineering, joint venture management, corporate strategy, and asset management. Om Prakash Sinha, Director-Exploration, with over 37 years of industry experience. Manish Patil, Director-Human Resources, has over 30 years’ experience in operations and supplies, information systems, and HR management services. Vivek Chandrakant Tongaonkar, Director-Finance, has over 37 years of experience in diverse activities across the energy value-chain. Arunangshu Sarkar, Director (Strategy and Corporate Affairs), with over 37 years of experience in diverse activities across the energy value-chain and Vikram Saxena, Director (Technology and Field Services), with an industry experience of over 35 years. 

# **Dominant market position backed by large crude oil reserves** 

India’s largest oil and gas exploration and production (E&P) company, ONGC had a dominant position historically and continues to maintain its position even after the New Exploration and Licensing Policy (NELP), and thereafter, the Hydrocarbon Exploration and Licensing Policy (HELP), which increased private participation in the oil and gas sector of the country. The company has a share of ~63% in India’s total production of crude oil and natural gas (including share of joint ventures [JVs]). It is also a significant producer of value-added products such as liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), superior kerosene oil (SKO), naphtha, ethane, propane, and C2/C3 among others. The company has the largest proven reserves in India, discovered in the last six decades since its inception. Large reserves base provides the company an abundant and stable long-term source of hydrocarbons for crude oil and natural gas production. 

In FY25 (FY refers to April 01 to March 31), the company added 38.19 million metric tonne oil equivalent (MMTOE) reserves (1P) on a standalone basis. The company’s 1P reserves as on March 31, 2025, stood at ~515 MMTOE on a standalone basis. The company had a reserve replacement ratio (RRR) of 1.35x in FY25 basis 2P reserves, which remained at/above unity for the 19th consecutive year, reflecting ONGC’s strong exploratory capability and healthy long-term revenue visibility. 

The company explored 19 of the country’s 26 sedimentary basins for its hydrocarbon potential, having established eight producing basins up to date. It has 1.8 lakh square kilometres (sq km) of acreage as on April 01, 2025, and has plans to increase it to 4 lakh sq km by 2027. 

# **Robust infrastructure and proven technical capabilities** 

The oil and gas industry is capital-intensive, requiring significant time and funds to develop a sound infrastructure. With its long track record of operations, ONGC has been able to develop a robust infrastructure, providing it an advantage over newer players in the industry who entered through NELP and HELP. The company developed significant onshore and offshore production facilities, subsea and land pipelines, gas processing, drilling and workover rigs, storage facilities, well stimulation units, automated hydraulic workover rigs, and other infrastructure throughout the principal oil and gas producing regions of India. 

In FY25 (FY refers to April 01 to March 31), ONGC drilled total 578 wells. In FY25, 107 conventional exploratory wells and 2 CBM assessment wells were drilled (PY: 103). Out of 107 conventional exploratory wells, 70 wells were concluded, and 37 wells are yet to be tested. From the concluded wells, 27 wells proved to be hydrocarbon bearing. This apart, 38 wells from previous years were concluded, of which 20 wells proved to be hydrocarbon bearing. 

# **Presence across the hydrocarbon value chain** 

With its seven direct subsidiaries, five joint ventures (JVs), and three associates, ONGC is present across the hydrocarbon value chain with operations in refining, petrochemicals, power, and liquefied natural gas (LNG), in addition to its exploration and production (E&P) activities. The company has forward integrated into downstream refining and marketing operations in India through successive acquisitions of Mangalore Refinery and Petrochemicals Limited (MRPL; rated CARE AAA; Stable/CARE A1+) and Hindustan Petroleum Corporation Limited (HPCL). Around 87% revenue of ONGC (consolidated) was from the refining and marketing segment in FY25 (FY refers to April 01 to March 31) (PY: 87%).

## **Sizeable scale of operations and resilient profitability margins despite some moderation in FY25** 

The company’s consolidated total operating income (TOI) largely remained steady considering higher crude oil volume sales despite a decrease in average crude oil prices year-on-year (y-o-y). However, operating margin decreased by ~231 bps y-o-y, mainly due to lower margin in Mangalore Refinery and Petrochemicals Limited (MRPL) and Hindustan Petroleum Corporation Limited (HPCL) considering moderation in gross refining margin (GRM), significant under recovery in liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) segment, moderation in product cracks and reduction in retail price of petrol and diesel by ₹2/litre implemented from midMarch 2024. 

In Q1FY26 (FY refers to April 01 to March 31) on a consolidated basis, ONGC reported an improvement in operating profitability at ~15.88% considering better profitability in HPCL due to significant reduction in LPG under recovery aided by upward revision in prices by ₹50 per cylinder, improved product cracks and stable retail fuel prices. While for MRPL, the profitability further moderated considering maintenance shutdown in Q1FY26. 

Going forward, CareEdge Ratings expects profitability to remain healthy in line with FY25 performance, considering normalisation of GRMs and steady crude oil prices. 

## **Liquidity: Strong** 

ONGC’s liquidity remained strong, with a free cash and bank balance of ~₹21,462 crore as on March 31, 2025, and investment in bonds of the GoI of ₹3,253 crore. ONGC has been utilising its fund-based limits of ~₹11,500 crore sparingly with average quarterly utilisation of 22.80% over the last four quarters ended June 2025. The company has envisaged earning healthy cash accruals of ~₹68,000-70,000 crore annually against scheduled term debt repayments of ~₹33,000-36,000 crore in FY26 and FY27. 

ONGC also derives financial flexibility from its low gearing ratio and parentage of the GoI, apart from its dominant market position, which provides it easy access to funds at attractive rates, aiding the funding of its large capital expenditure (capex). 

# **Key weaknesses** 

## **Risk related to E&P business and volatile crude oil prices** 

In addition to being a highly capital-intensive activity, the exploration and production (E&P) business has a long gestation period. Exploration activity involves high uncertainty with respect to the estimation of reserves, as it is a function of the quality of available data engineering and geological interpretation. 

ONGC is also exposed to commodity price risk. Although ONGC as a group is an integrated player in the oil and gas industry, a decrease in crude oil prices may hamper the company’s profitability, as it derives the majority revenue from the sale of crude oil and natural gas. The international crude oil price is a function of dynamic markets and fundamental factors, such as the global demand-supply dynamics, geopolitical stability in countries with oil reserves, the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) policies, foreign exchange rates, and other prices of crude and policy-level changes. 

## **Geopolitical risk associated with international venture** 

ONGC is exposed to geopolitical risk owing to its subsidiary, ONGC Videsh Limited (OVL; rated CARE AAA; Stable/CARE A1+). OVL undertakes exploration and production activities mainly in the Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS) and countries in the Middle East and North Africa (MENA) region. OVL has ~30 projects in 15 countries and ONGC’s investments in OVL are prone to changes in the policy regime, and fiscal law changes, among others, since some countries have a history of unstable regimes. 

Unstable government or unfavourable policies, such as resource nationalisation, add to geopolitical risks in the host countries. 

## **Large capex requirements** 

In last three years ended FY25 (FY refers to April 01 to March 31), the average capital expenditure (capex) of ONGC (standalone) per annum has been between ₹30,000-39,000 crore. In FY25, the company incurred capex of ~₹39,084 crore, with ~27% expenditure on development drilling, ~20% expenditure on exploration drilling, ~39% expenditure on capital projects, and the balance ~14% on surveys, research and development (R&D), integration, and joint ventures (JVs). The company incurred ₹22,973 crore towards integrated projects (Equity infusion in ONGC Petro additions Limited (OPaL) - ₹18,365 crore, Acquisition of PTC Energy - ₹1,179 crore and Ayana - ₹3,124 crore). In FY25, total capital expenditure of ONGC consolidated stood at ₹83,395 crore. 

CareEdge Ratings expects this trend to continue in the ensuing years with an estimated capex plan of ₹35,000 crore planned for FY26 at a standalone level and ~₹50,000 crore at a consolidated level.

Large capex requirements and long gestation periods of exploration and production (E&P) projects have a bearing on the company’s return indicators, although it has a sound financial position to fund its capex requirements. 

## **Regulatory risk** 

GoI’s policy and decisions considering natural gas pricing (APM mechanism), subsidy sharing, windfall taxes, duties, cess, and dividend payments have a significant bearing on ONGC’s profitability, cash flows, and liquidity position. In elevated crude prices, the GoI may choose to pass on fiscal burden through sharing of public sector undertakings (PSUs)’ profits through higher fiscal levies and higher dividend declaration for oil marketing companies (OMCs), which may impact ONGC’s income and accruals. As seen in the past (starting July 2022), the GoI has been imposing the Special Additional Excise Duty (SAED) on production of crude oil, and export of motor spirit (MS), high speed diesel (HSD) and aviation turbine fuel (ATF), which underscores susceptibility of financial risk profiles of companies such as ONGC to government interventions. However, such tax incidences are likely to be selfcorrecting if product margins normalise.
