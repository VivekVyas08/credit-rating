## **Rationale and key rating drivers** 

<mark>Reaffirmation of ratings assigned to bank facilities of Ruchira Papers Limited (RPL) continue to derive strength from experienced promoters and established market position through wide distribution network. Ratings also factor in stable operations despite a 36-days plant shutdown by the company in Q3FY26 (refers to period from October 01 to December 31) for execution of capex programme involving the rebuild of the existing Writing & Printing Paper (WPP) machine, which would lead to an increase in its production capacity from 60,000 MTPA in FY26 to ~71,000 MTPA in FY27, installation of machinery imported from the US for enhancing pulping capacity and installation of CLO2 plant, aimed at improving operational efficiencies. The profitability margins though moderated owing to the industry-wide pressure on WPP realisations in the year however stood comfortable. Ratings continue to derive strength from comfortable financial risk profile with overall gearing below 1x in the last few years and stable debt coverage indicators. However, ratings continue to remain constrained considering working capital intensive operations, margins susceptible to raw material price volatility and highly competitive and cyclical nature of paper industry.</mark>

## **<mark>Stable operational performance</mark>** 

The company’s total operating income remained stagnant while reporting a modest decline of ~2% in FY26 and stood at ₹648.80 crore (PY: ₹659.23 crore). This was considering plant shutdown by the company for execution of the capex programme, involving the rebuild of existing WPP machine, which would lead to an increase in its production capacity from 60,000 MTPA in FY26 to ~71,000 MTPA in FY27, installation of machinery imported from the US for enhancing pulping capacity and installation of CLO2 plant, aimed at improving operational efficiencies. Despite this shutdown, the Kraft Paper unit registered volume growth of ~3,384 MT, enabling the company to maintain overall production volumes broadly in line with the previous year. 

The company’s PBILDT margin stood at 12.50% in FY26 (PY: 16.26%). The company was also impacted by industry-wide pressure on paper realisations in FY26 and relatively higher input costs, which led to lower-than-anticipated net sales realisation (NSR). The profit after taxation (PAT) margin also moderated and stood at 6.80% in FY26 (PY: 10.21%) owing to moderation in PBILDT and increase in interest expenses. 

## **Comfortable financial risk profile** 

The company’s capital structure continued to remain comfortable, where overall gearing ratio was comfortable, however slightly moderated and stood at 0.37x as on March 31, 2026 (PY: 0.18x). This was owing to increase in term loan considering capex undertaken by the company and higher utilisation of working capital borrowings. 

Debt coverage indicators also moderated with Interest coverage (PBILDT/Interest) ratio of 9.56x as on March 31, 2026 (PY: 26.02x) and total debt to gross cash accruals (TD/GCA) of 2.83x as on March 31, 2026 (PY: 1.00x). Moderation was primarily driven by higher debt levels to fund the company's capex program. Profitability was impacted by industry-wide pressure on paper realisations, resulting in a decline in GCA and consequently affecting the leverage metrics. 

# **Key weaknesses** 

## **Working capital intensive operations** 

The company has working capital intensive operations as characterised by operating cycle of 105 days in FY26 (PY: 101 days), primarily driven by higher inventory holding period, which stood at 64 days in FY26 (PY: 64 days). This is necessitated by seasonal availability of agri-residues such as bagasse, prompting the company to maintain adequate raw material inventory for uninterrupted production. It ensures a sufficient stock of finished goods to meet immediate customer demand. The company extends a credit period of ~30 to 50 days to its clients, with the average receivable period standing at 47 days in FY26 (PY: 43 days). Meanwhile, the average creditor period remained low at six days in FY26 (PY: six days). 

## **Highly competitive industry and margins susceptible to volatile raw material prices** 

The paper industry is highly fragmented in nature with stiff competition from a large number of organised and unorganised players. This limits pricing power of manufacturers and puts further pressure on profitability. The industry is also impacted by economic cycles. Other than wastepaper, RPL also uses agro-based raw material, which is purchased from domestic markets. With respect to the agro-based raw materials, there are limitations in their use due to seasonal availability leading to their high price volatility. Therefore, the company’s operating profit remains highly susceptible to raw material price volatility. 

## **Cyclical nature of industry** 

The paper industry is highly competitive in nature with stiff competition from large number of organised and unorganised players and threat from imports. This limits pricing power of manufacturers in terms of flexibility to pass on raw material price fluctuation to its customers. The demand for paper is directly correlated to the level of economic activity, as higher industrial output leads to increased demand for industrial paper for packaging; increased marketing spend benefits the newsprint and value-added segments; and greater education and office activities raises the demand for WPP. 

# **Liquidity** : Adequate 

The company’s liquidity position is adequate as reflected by projected gross cash accruals to the tune of ~₹80.98 crore in FY27 against scheduled repayment of ₹20.49 crore. Average utilisation of working capital borrowings stood at ~71% for 12 months ended May 2026. The company’s current ratio stood at 1.68x (PY: 2.43x) as on March 31, 2026.
