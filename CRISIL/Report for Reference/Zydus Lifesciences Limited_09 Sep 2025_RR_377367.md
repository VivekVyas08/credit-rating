### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the bank facilities, non-convertible debentures, and commercial paper programme of Zydus Lifesciences Ltd (Zydus Life; part of the Zydus group). 

Zydus Wellness Ltd, a subsidiary of Zydus Life, announced on 29<sup>th</sup> August 2025, its acquisition of Comfort Click Ltd (CCL), a UKbased entity, for a cash consideration of £ 239 million. CCL is a digital D2C consumer wellness player, specialising in the vitamins, minerals and supplements (VMS) segment, and having a presence in the European Union (EU), UK, with expansion in the US currently underway. The acquisition will help drive synergies with Zydus Wellness’ existing portfolio, and strengthen its presence in the high growth VMS segment for adults, children and animals, particularly in the overseas markets. The acquisition will be entirely funded through debt. With a net liquid surplus of ~Rs.5,500 crore and strong cash generating ability, Zydus Life remains financially well-positioned, and the acquisition is not expected to have a material impact on its financial risk profile. 

Earlier, Zydus also announced its acquisition of US-based Agenus Inc.’s biologics manufacturing facilities in June, 2025. The transaction, involving an outflow of $75 million upfront and $50 million in contingent payments payable over three years and subject to achievement of revenue milestones, marks Zydus’ foray into the global contract development and manufacturing (CDMO) space. Through the acquisition, Zydus has gained access to Agenus’ lead immune-oncology assets, Botensilimab and Balstilimab, thereby strengthening its oncology portfolio. The deal also secures exclusive commercial rights for the assets in India and Sri Lanka. On its part, Zydus will be responsible for regulatory approval and clinical development. Additionally, Zydus will have the first right to negotiation to manufacture any products developed by Agenus going forward. 

The ratings also continue to reflect the established position of the Zydus group in the branded generics market in India and the expected benefits from growth in the wellness segment. The ratings also factor in the group’s growing presence in international markets and strong financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by exposure to risks related to unfavourable regulatory changes, increasing competition and price erosion in the US generics market. 

Consolidated revenue grew 19% year-on-year in fiscal 2025. Growth was driven by strong all-round growth across markets in formulations. In the US, growth was driven by sustained volume expansion in base business and execution success in new product launches. International markets business, which comprises different countries of emerging markets and Europe, continued its growth momentum with a strong demand led growth across geographies, while revenue from the domestic market grew steadily, backed by traction in pillar brands and innovation products, and a gradual increase in the share of chronic portfolio (43% as per IQVIA MAT on March 31, 2025; 400 basis points (bps) expansion over the last three years). Consolidated operating margin improved to 30.1% during fiscal 2025 due to a better business mix and stable input costs. Zydus continued its steady performance during Q1FY26 as well, where the 6% on-year growth was driven by the domestic formulations business and international markets. Operating profitability remained healthy at 30.9% during the quarter. 

Crisil Ratings expects the business risk profile of Zydus Life to continue improving, supported by a double-digit  revenue growth this fiscal and the next, led by continued traction in the domestic and international markets, ramp-up in sales of new chemical 

entities and biosimilars, and inclusion of operational benefits arising from recent acquisitions while sustaining healthy operating margin at 25-26%, leading to higher cash accrual. 

Gross debt stood at Rs 3,213 crore as on March 31, 2025 (Rs 804 crore as on March 31, 2024), on account of higher working capital requirements. Liquidity was superior at Rs 5,681 crore as on March 31, 2025. The ratings also factor in the expected sustenance of Zydus Life’s healthy financial risk profile over the medium term, with moderate reliance on external borrowing, which will be serviced through healthy accrual, leading to net debt to Ebitda (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation) ratio of less than 0.5 time after factoring in organic capital expenditure (capex) of ~Rs 1,000-1,200 crore and moderate inorganic growth. 

# **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of Zydus Life and its various subsidiaries and step-down subsidiaries, collectively referred to as the Zydus group, as all these entities operate in the pharmaceutical and related industries, and have significant operational linkages and common management. For joint ventures (JVs), Crisil Ratings has followed a moderate integration approach, factoring in share of profit from the JVs and any investment required by them. Also, Crisil Ratings has amortised intangible assets and goodwill consolidated on earlier acquisitions over five fiscals and on the acquisition of Heinz India Pvt Ltd over 10 years. Both profit after tax (PAT) and networth are adjusted to that extent.

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description</u>** 

# **Strengths:** 

- **Established market position in the domestic market:** The Zydus group is one of the leading players in the domestic formulations market, and its domestic sales accounted for 26% of revenues during fiscal 2025. The group holds leading positions in cardio-diabetology, respiratory, gynaecology, oncology, nephrology and hepatology, which collectively account for around half of the domestic formulation revenue, as per IQVIA MAT 2025. It has strengthened its marketing team over the past few years, putting greater thrust on market strategies such as growth in categories, integration of channel partners, supply chain and procurement to improve revenue and cost synergies. The group has a healthy pipeline in the innovation portfolio, which will be growth drivers over the medium term. 

- **Growing presence in international markets:** The business is supported by growing presence in the US generics markets, which accounted for 49% of revenue in fiscal 2025. The Zydus group was the fifth-largest pharmaceutical company in the US generics market as per IQVIA MAT June 2025. It had 419 approvals (including 26 tentative approvals) and filed 481 abbreviated new drug applications (ANDAs) as on June 30, 2025. Healthy pace of filings and approvals in the US, reflected in the strong ANDA pipeline, will strengthen the US business. The group also has established presence in the Rest of World markets such as Brazil, Mexico, Sri Lanka, South Africa, France and Spain, where it reported healthy revenue growth despite geopolitical challenges and adverse macro environment. The Rest of the World segment accounted for 10% of the consolidated revenues fiscal 2025, and is expected to give a leg-up to revenue growth for the year. 

- **Strong financial risk profile:** The financial risk profile is supported by healthy capital structure and debt protection metrics. Adjusted gearing was strong at less than 0.2 time as on March 31, 2025, while interest coverage ratio was over 40 times in fiscal 2025 due to minimal debt. The company availed long-term loan to finance the acquisition of CCL, which will be repaid through cash accrual generated by the acquired entity. Nonetheless, the financial risk profile is expected to remain strong, with net debt to Ebitda ratio and gearing sustained at low levels over the medium term, after factoring in moderate capex and inorganic growth. Any sizeable debt-funded acquisition or investments may adversely impact the debt metrics and liquidity and hence will remain monitorable. 

# **Weaknesses:** 

- **Exposure to regulatory risks:** The Zydus group remains exposed to regulatory risks in both domestic and international markets, particularly the US. In October 2019, a warning was issued by the US Food and Drug Administration (US FDA) for the group’s Moraiya plant, which impacted new product approvals. While the US FDA cleared this warning letter in November 2022 and the company has started getting new product approvals, another warning letter was received in the first half of fiscal 2025 for the injectables facility at Jarod, for which the company is undertaking corrective action. Any regulatory issues related to the facilities could impact revenue and profitability. The ongoing litigation by the anti-trust division of the US Department of Justice on industry generic players regarding price-collusion allegations is monitorable. Furthermore, any price-control measures of the government in the branded domestic formulations segment may weaken growth. 

- **Susceptibility to competition and stretched working capital cycle:** The group faces intense competition in regulated markets, where innovator companies engage in aggressive defence tactics and there are several cost-competitive Indian players. Generics players in regulated markets are affected by severe price erosion given the commoditised nature of products and government pressure to lower prices. Ability of the company to keep launching new products mitigates this risk. Strong bargaining power of distributors in the US leads to large working capital requirement; gross current assets were at 189 days as on March 31, 2025. However, ample liquidity and high financial flexibility will sufficiently cover working capital requirement. 

# **<u>Liquidity: Superior</u>** 

Healthy net cash and equivalent (including investments in mutual funds) of Rs 5,681 crore as on March 31, 2025 and strong annual cash generation support liquidity. Besides, the company also has fund-based bank limit of about Rs 3,400 crore, which 

was moderately utilised at 22% for six months through July 2025. Annual cash accrual of over Rs 4,000 crore will be more than sufficient to meet yearly organic capex of ~Rs 1,000-1,200 crore and part fund inorganic capex of the company. 

# **<u>Environment, social and governance (ESG) profile</u>** 

Crisil Ratings believes Zydus Life’s ESG profile supports its already strong credit risk profile. 

The pharmaceutical sector can have a significant impact on the environment on account of greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, water use and waste generation. The social impact of the sector is characterised by impact on the health and wellbeing of its consumers on account of its products, and on employees and local community on account of its operations. 

# **Key ESG highlights:** 

- ZLL aims to achieve carbon neutrality for direct emissions in its Indian operations by fiscal 2035. In alignment with this target, the company nearly doubled its renewable power consumption in fiscal 2025 (rose to 82,785 MWh from 43,393 MWh in fiscal 2024) as it transitioned from coal to biomass briquettes as fuel in its steam boilers 

- ZLL aims to achieve net water neutrality in its Indian operations by 2028, balancing water consumption with replenishment. To achieve this goal, the company, in fiscal 2025, expanded wastewater reuse, enhanced water conservation across its sites and supply chain, and upgraded facilities to achieve zero liquid discharge. 

- To enhance inclusivity within the workplace, ZLL aims to achieve a gender diversity goal of 12% female representation by the year 2026. In line with this, female representation in workforce stood at ~9% 

- The company’s lost time injury frequency rate (LTIFR) continues to be relatively low at 0.07 (per one million-person hours worked) for employees and it reported zero fatalities among workforce, in fiscal 2025 

The company has an adequate governance structure, with independent directors making up more than half its Board, ~22% being women, presence of investor grievance redressal mechanism, whistle-blower policy, and extensive disclosures. Further, it also has a board-level ESG committee to provide oversight and to monitor its ESG strategy and action plansThere is growing importance of ESG among investors and lenders. The commitment of the company to ESG principles will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence and ensure ease of raising capital from markets where ESG compliance is a key factor. 

# **<u>Outlook: Stable</u>** 

The Zydus group will maintain diversified revenue profile across geographies and healthy cash accrual over the medium term. The financial risk profile will likely remain strong supported by healthy capital structure and debt protection metrics. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Downward factors:** 

- Sharp decline in operating margin due to increased competition, unfavourable regulatory developments or lower-thanexpected sales across business segments 

- Higher-than-expected debt undertaken to fund large acquisitions, capex and working capital cycle, with net debt to Ebitda ratio above 0.5 time on a steady-state basis 

- Any significant payout towards the ongoing anti-trust litigation or any other adverse regulatory developments impacting the liquidity and debt protection metrics. 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Cadila Laboratories was set up in 1952 by Mr Raman Patel and Mr Indravadan Modi. Cadila Healthcare was incorporated in 1995 following the split of Cadila Laboratories, with Mr Modi and his family's share being moved to a new company. The division that was managed by Mr Raman Patel’s son, Mr Pankaj Patel, was renamed Cadila Healthcare, which was renamed Zydus Life in February 2022. The company is under the leadership of Dr Sharvil Patel, a third-generation promoter. Zydus Life got listed on the Bombay Stock Exchange in 2000. Over the years, Zydus Life has grown to become one of the leading pharmaceutical companies in India. It also has growing presence in the regulated markets, particularly the US, and is one of the top five players in the US generics market in terms of prescriptions. Other segments include emerging markets formulations, consumer wellness, animal healthcare and bulk drugs. The group has a network of manufacturing facilities comprising 19 formulation manufacturing units, 1 animal health unit, 6 bulk drug manufacturing units, 3 biologics units, 5 vaccine units, 1 medical devices facility and 4 consumer product facilities. Moreover, the company has seven R&D centres. As on June 30, 2025, 75% stake in Zydus Life was held by the promoters. 

For the June 2025 quarter, the company reported PAT of Rs 1,521 crore on total income of Rs 6574 crore (Rs 6208 crore a year earlier).
