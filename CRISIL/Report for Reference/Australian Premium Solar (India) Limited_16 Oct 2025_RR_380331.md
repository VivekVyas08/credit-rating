### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil BBB/Stable/Crisil A3+’ ratings on the bank facilities of Australian Premium Solar (India) Ltd (APSIL). 

Crisil Ratings had upgraded its ratings to 'Crisil BBB/Stable/Crisil A3+' from 'Crisil BBB-/Stable/Crisil A3' through a rationale dated September 11, 2025. 

The ratings reflect the improved business risk profile of APSIL, with revenue projected to grow more than 50% in fiscal 2026 from Rs 433.5 crore in fiscal 2025 (Rs 151 crore in fiscal 2024); while operating margin is likely to remain in double digits (13% in fiscal 2025 and 6.6% in fiscal 2024). The working capital cycle is supported by efficient receivables and inventory management and credit from suppliers. 

The financial risk profile is backed by healthy networth of Rs 87 crore, and strong gearing and total outside liabilities to adjusted networth (TOLANW) ratio of 0.09 time and 1.39 times, respectively, as on March 31, 2025. The debt protection metrics remained healthy. The liquidity will remain comfortable with healthy expected net cash accrual against moderate debt obligation, nil utilisation of bank limit and high unencumbered cash balance. 

The ratings continue to reflect the favourable demand outlook for the solar industry, the extensive experience of APSIL's promoter, and healthy financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by susceptibility to increasing competition and to volatility in raw material prices, regulatory changes, and fluctuations in foreign exchange (forex) rates. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of APSIL and its subsidiary, APS Rooftop Solar Pvt Ltd.

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Favourable demand outlook for the solar industry:** APSIL will benefit from the growing emphasis on solar power in India and the long-term plans of the government to increase generation from renewable sources. Introduction of protectionist measures by the government—such as basic customs duty (BCD) of 40% and 25% on imported solar modules and solar cells, respectively, from April 2022, and implementation of Approved List of Models and Manufacturers (ALMM), along with incentives for domestic players under the Production-Linked Incentive scheme—increases the cost competitiveness of domestic modules compared with imported ones. Government-approved schemes such as Kisan Urja Suraksha Utthan Mahabhiyan, Central Public Sector Undertaking, PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana and rooftop scheme are also expected to drive demand. APSIL plans to expand its footprint in engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) services for large projects for the commercial and industrial segment, which will support growth in business. 

**Extensive industry experience of the promoter:** The promoter has been in the solar business since 2008. The company has solar photovoltaic module manufacturing capacity of 600 megawatt (MW). APSIL commenced the solar module segment in fiscal 2013 and has since ramped up operations significantly. The company generated revenue of Rs 433.5 crore in fiscal 2025 and has sustained growth on the back of capacity expansion and healthy utilisation, growth across business segments, significant ramp-up in the solar pump installation segment, and robust demand. 

**Healthy financial risk profile:** Capital structure has been strong due to low reliance on external funds, as indicated by gearing of 0.09 time and TOLANW ratio of 1.39 times, as on March 31, 2025. This, along with healthy profitability, led to robust debt protection metrics, with interest coverage and net cash accrual to total debt ratios of 47.27 times and 5.42 times, respectively, for fiscal 2025. The metrics are expected at similar levels over the medium term. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Susceptibility to increasing competition and to volatility in raw material prices and regulatory changes:** The operating margin remains vulnerable to sharp fluctuations in raw material prices (raw material cost accounts for 78-84% of the operating income). The prices of key inputs such as solar cells are volatile and depend on external factors, and dipped significantly in fiscal 2024 and the first half of fiscal 2025. Moreover, the company is exposed to increasing competition from domestic as well as imported modules on account of large ongoing and planned capacity additions in the domestic market with various degrees of integration. Furthermore, Indian manufacturers face competition from Chinese imports, and module and cell prices reduced significantly in fiscals 2024 and 2025 due to supply glut in China amid US restrictions on Chinese imports. However, increasing integration of operations with enhancement of module capacities and implementation of BCD will help APSIL to be cost-competitive against Chinese imports. That said, the risk of significant price reduction by Chinese players to lessen the impact of BCD persists. Additionally, implementation of ALMM, which is expected to boost demand for domestic modules, will also be monitorable. 

Prices of key raw materials such as polysilicon, aluminum and copper and, in turn, solar cells have been volatile over the past few quarters. Though APSIL has price-variation clauses for most raw materials and undertakes order-backed procurement to mitigate this risk, any sharp rise in input cost may impact demand in the domestic industry. APSIL has an established market presence in the module business, which supports the operating margin and enables the company to cater to the domestic content requirement module tenders. The capacity addition planned by other domestic players over the medium term and intensification of competition will need to be monitored. 

The company’s operating margin improved to 13.1% in fiscal 2025 from 6.6% in fiscal 2024, aided by the sharp fall in raw material prices—solar cell prices fell in tandem with silicon wafers as well as polysilicon amidst overcapacities in China. Moreover, better economies of scale on the back of healthy volume growth also supported the improvement in the margin. 

Growth remains vulnerable to changes in government policies. However, the central government's focus on achieving a steep target of ~280 gigawatt solar power by 2030 is expected to support APSIL in the long run. 

**Vulnerability to fluctuations in forex rates:** Since the company imports its raw material, any sharp fluctuation in forex rates affects  profitability and accrual. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Adequate 

Bank utilisation was nil for the 12 months through August 2025. Annual cash accrual is expected to be Rs 60-65 crore against annual term debt obligation of Rs 3-5 crore, over the medium term, and the surplus will cushion liquidity. The current ratio was healthy at 1.56 times on March 31, 2025. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes APSIL and its subsidiary will continue to benefit from the favourable demand outlook for the solar industry, the extensive experience of the promoter, and established relationships with clients.

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

APSIL was incorporated in 2013 as a private limited company and was reconstituted as a public limited company in 2023. Promoted by Mr Nikunjkumar Chimanlal Patel (chairman and executive director), the company, along with its subsidiary, manufactures monocrystalline and polycrystalline solar panel modules, and provides EPC and solar pump installation services. These solar modules are for residential, commercial and industrial off-grid/on-grid tied applications. 

The manufacturing facility is at Tajpur in Sabarkantha, Gujarat, with total installed capacity of 600 MW per annum. 

The company is listed on the SME Platform of the National Stock Exchange of India Ltd (NSE Emerge).
