### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has removed its rating on the long-term bank facilities and debt instruments of Nuvoco Vistas Corporation Ltd (NVCL) from ‘Rating Watch with Developing Implications **’** and has reaffirmed the ratings at **‘Crisil AA/Crisil AA-’** and assigned a **‘Stable’** outlook. Crisil Ratings has also reaffirmed its 'Crisil A1+' rating on the short-term bank facilities and commercial paper programme of NVCL. 

The rating action follows the successful refinancing of the first tranche of the Rs 1,200 crore bridge loan, which was drawn to fund the acquisition of Vadraj Cement Ltd (VCL), through issuance of compulsory convertible debentures (CCDs) having equity-like features. 

The Rs 600 crore CCDs issued by VCL on private placement basis in November 2025 have a committed internal rate of return (IRR), with minimal annual coupon payout. This instrument has a tenure of seven years, with a call option available to NVCL from the fifth year from the date of allotment, giving NVCL the right to acquire these CCDs from investors. Exercise of this option will depend on the financial leverage of the company at that point in time. If the call is not exercised, the investors have a put option to sell these CCDs to Nirma Ltd. Crisil Ratings has treated the CCDs as equity, in line with its criteria. The company is also in final stages of refinancing the balance bridge loan through CCDs with broadly similar terms and the transaction is likely to be completed shortly. 

Issuances of equity like instruments coupled with improved operating performance of the company should result in net debt to earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortization (Ebitda) of NVCL to improve to below 2.5 times in fiscal 2026 from around 2.7 times in fiscal 2025 and decline gradually thereafter. This is despite higher-than-expected capex of ~Rs 2,000 crore to be incurred over fiscals 2026-2027 towards operationalization of the VCL assets, acquiring a captive power plant and organic capacity additions in eastern region through debottlenecking. These leverage expectations also build in equity-content for the yet-to-be issued CCDs and final terms of the instrument will remain monitorable. 

During the first half of fiscal 2026, NVCL’s consolidated operating income increased by 9% year-on-year, driven by improved realisations. This has also supported improvement in profitability, with Ebitda per tonne rising to Rs 942 from Rs 625 in the corresponding period of the previous year. With the improvement in pricing and demand scenario during fiscal 2026, the operating performance is expected to be much better than the previous fiscal, which is also supported by higher operating 

leverage and efficiency improvement initiatives being undertaken by the company. Over the medium term, Crisil Ratings expects healthy demand growth in eastern region resulting in increased utilisation levels and scale of operations. 

The ratings continue to reflect the company’s strong market position in eastern region and its diversification into the northern regions. Cost-optimisation initiatives such as setting up alternative fuel facilities and railway sidings and business synergies such as product premiumisation and logistic synergies, are expected to support cash flow. NVCL also enjoys healthy financial flexibility, being a part of the Nirma group. These strengths are partially offset by the company’s modest financial risk profile, susceptibility to variations in input costs and cyclicality in the cement industry. 

# **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of NVCL with its subsidiary and joint venture. 

Crisil Ratings has treated Rs 600 crore of CCDs as equity in line with the criteria for rating corporate sector hybrids. 

Crisil Ratings has treated the outstanding portion of perpetual non-convertible debentures (NCDs) of Rs 300 crore as 100% debt, given the exercise of call option by the company for repayment of part of the original quantum (of Rs 600 crore). Crisil Ratings has rated the perpetual NCDs one-notch lower than the other traditional long-term bonds, in line with its criteria for rating corporate sector hybrids. This is based on the instrument's feature that allows flexibility to defer distribution payments and the likelihood of deferral, if required. 

Crisil Ratings has adjusted NVCL’s networth for amortisation of goodwill on account of acquisitions.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Strong market position following the acquisition of NVL:** NVCL, on a standalone basis, had high utilisation levels in the past. With the acquisition of NVL, the company increased its installed capacity and became a leading player in the eastern region. Post acquisition, the market position of NVCL strengthened with an additional capacity of 8.3 million tonne per annum (MTPA) in eastern region across plants in Chhattisgarh, Bihar, Odisha and West Bengal. The presence of the split grinding units of NVL across eastern states and an integrated unit in Chhattisgarh complements the plants of NVCL in the eastern region. 

Furthermore, in the northern region NVCL has an installed capacity of 6.1 MTPA spread across an integrated plant in Chittorgarh (Rajasthan), a split grinding unit in Haryana and an integrated unit in Nimbol (Rajasthan). These units shall benefit from synergies, including rationalisation of the marketing network and cost savings because of the ramp-up in scale of operations. 

NVCL has its own captive limestone mines and clinker capacity along with captive power plants and waste heat recovery systems across various plants. The established market position is supported by strong brands, such as Duraguard, Double Bull, Concreto and Infracem, and an extensive network of dealers and sub dealers of NVCL and NVL. Strong brand equity provides the ability to command a premium for products. 

The acquisition has given NVCL access to adequate limestone reserves, which shall aid business over the medium term. The presence of grinding units in key states will help with product optimisation and will increase market reach over the medium term. 

Furthermore, the acquisition of VCL (which has a 3.5 MTPA clinker unit in Kutch (Gujarat) along with limestone reserves plus 6 MTPA grinding unit in Surat (Gujarat)) has taken NVCL’s total installed grinding capacity to 31 MTPA. Additionally, NVCL has also announced 4 MTPA capacity expansion in eastern region through debottlenecking, process modification and equipment upgradation which is expected to get concluded by fiscal 2027. Operationalisation of these capacities will enhance NVCL’s market share in the west, north and east regions. 

**Healthy operating efficiency:** Operating efficiency has remained healthy (barring the decline in profitability during fiscal 2023 owing to rise in input costs on account of power and fuel costs), driven by operational synergies with NVL, superior brand positioning, high utilisation of around 75% and increase in captive capabilities for meeting power requirements. 

Operating profitability moderated during fiscal 2025 due to weak macro environment for majority part of the period which also had an impact on cement prices, however it is expected to recover going forward driven by expected improvement in demand, price hikes and efficiency improvement measures implemented. The company’s Return on Capital Employed (RoCE) has remained subdued in the past majorly owing to large acquisitions undertaken in the past resulting in recognition of intangibles. This is expected to gradually improve over the medium term. 

During the first half of fiscal 2026, NVCL’s consolidated operating income increased by 9% year-on-year to Rs 5,330 crore driven by improved realisations due to price hikes undertaken during the quarter. Ebitda per tonne improved to around Rs 942 for the same period (against Rs 625 in the first half of fiscal 2025), owing to improved realisations as well as moderation in input costs, particularly raw material and power and fuel costs. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Moderate, but improving, financial risk profile:** The financial risk profile has been constrained by the debt-funded acquisition of NVL. While leverage increased sharply in fiscal 2021, it progressively corrected through the initial public offering (IPO) proceeds and healthy internal cash accrual. While the net debt continued on a downward trajectory and stood at Rs 3,640 crore as on March 31, 2025, net debt to Ebitda rose to around 2.7 times in fiscal 2025 (against 2.5 times for fiscal 2024) owing to moderate profitability as a result of tepid demand and weak cement prices. 

NVCL completed the acquisition of VCL with an upfront payout of Rs 1,800 crore, initially funded entirely through debt. NVCL is likely to undertake capex of ~Rs 2,000 crore over fiscals 2026-2027 towards operationalising the VCL assets, acquiring a captive power plant, and organic capacity addition in the eastern region through debottlenecking, which will be partly debt-funded. This includes regular maintenance capex of Rs 100-150 crore annually. 

However, with recent refinancing of part of the bridge loan with CCDs (treated as equity) and improved operating performance, the net debt to Ebitda ratio is expected to improve to below 2.5 times in fiscal 2026 and decline gradually thereafter with no further large-scale capex expected to be announced in the near-term. This will remain monitorable. Crisil Ratings takes comfort from the capex prudence exhibited by the company in the past and expects it to continue to ensure that net debt to Ebitda ratio remains within an acceptable threshold going forward. 

**Susceptibility to variations in input costs and cyclicality in the cement industry:** Capacity addition in the cement industry tends to be sporadic because of the long gestation period for setting up a facility and numerous players adding capacity during the peak of a cycle. This led to unfavourable price cycles for the sector in the past. Moreover, profitability remains exposed to volatility in input prices, including raw material, power, fuel and freight. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Liquidity is strong, backed by the ability to raise short-term and long-term debt at short notice at competitive rates. NVCL (at consolidated level) has a sanctioned limit of about Rs 1,900 crore, which is moderately utilised; Unencumbered cash and equivalents of around Rs 227 crore as on September 30, 2025, also support liquidity. The company is likely to generate cash accrual (excluding capex) of about Rs 2,700 crore over two fiscals through 2027, which will support timely debt servicing. 

# **<u>ESG Profile</u>** 

The environment, social and governance (ESG) profile of NVCL supports its healthy credit risk profile. The cement sector has a significant impact on the environment owing to higher emissions, waste generation and water consumption. This is because of the energy-intensive cement manufacturing process and its high dependence on natural resources such as limestone and coal as key raw materials. The sector has a social impact due to its nature of operations affecting local community and health hazards involved. NVCL has focused on mitigating its environmental and social risks. 

# **<u>Key ESG highlights</u>** 

- The company’s Scope 1 emissions intensity at ~0.454 tCO2E per tonne of cementitious material produced and energy consumption intensity at ~2.44 GJ per tonne of cementitious material is lower compared with its peers. The company’s hazardous and non-hazardous waste generation intensity is also lower compared with its peers and the waste recycling rate is also high at ~100%. 

- Its gender diversity (~3% female employees and ~4% female workers) and attrition rate are broadly in-line with the peer average. 

- The company’s lost time injury frequency rate (LTIFR) for employees stood at 0.81. 

- NVCL’s governance structure is characterized by 50% of its board comprising of independent directors, ~17% women directors, split in the positions of chairman and managing director, dedicated investor grievance redressal system, and extensive financial disclosures. 

There is growing importance of ESG among investors and lenders. The commitment of NVCL to ESG will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence, given access to domestic capital markets. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

The stable outlook reflects strong business risk profile of the company supported by its leading position in the eastern market and expectation of improvement in the financial risk profile. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

- Sustained improvement in cash accrual, supported by improvement in operational efficiency and realisation of synergies. 

Reduction in debt levels, resulting in net debt to Ebitda below 1.5 times on a sustained basis. 

# **Downward factors** 

Delay in improvement in net debt to Ebitda ratio to around 2.5 times. Significant debt-funded growth plans. 

**<u>About the Company</u>** 

NVCL is currently the fifth largest cement manufacturer in India with a total installed capacity of 31 MTPA on consolidated basis including that of VCL. Further, it targets to achieve a total cement capacity of 35 MTPA by the end of fiscal 2027. It also operates 58 ready-mix concrete plants across India.  The company has integrated cement plants, grinding units and a ready-mix concrete and modern building materials (MBM) business. Cement manufacturing operations are spread across West Bengal, Bihar, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Haryana, Rajasthan, Gujarat. The main brands are Concreto, Duraguard, Double Bull and Infracem. NEPL and promoters of the Nirma group hold about ~72% in NVCL post IPO and NVL is a 100% subsidiary of NVCL.
