### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil BBB/Stable/Crisil A3+’ ratings on the bank facilities of Jinkushal Industries Ltd (JKIL). 

The ratings reflect the extensive industry experience of the promoters of JKIL, and the company’s comfortable financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by its exposure to cyclicality in the end-user industries and working capitalintensive operations. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has consolidated the business and financial risk profiles of JKIL with its overseas subsidiary with effect from 1<sup>st</sup> April, 2024. 

_Please refer Annexure - List of Entities Consolidated, which captures the list of entities considered and their analytical treatment of consolidation._ 

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Extensive industry experience of the promoters:** The promoters have over three decades of experience in the heavy construction equipment industry through contract mining work. The promoters, Mr Anil Jain and his son Mr Abhinav Jain are mechanical engineers by qualification and have built an extensive network across the country for the supply of construction and mining equipment. The promoters provide machines on rental basis as well and have a supply chain network for spare parts. Having started the business of exporting refurbished construction equipment only in 2017, it has displayed speedy ramp up and touched a topline of Rs 380 crore in fiscal 2025. Going by half yearly topline of around Rs 122 crore on a consolidated basis, scale of operations is expected to grow over the medium term. 

**Comfortable financial risk profile:** JKIL's financial risk profile is moderate, with a stable capital structure and comfortable debt protection metrics. As of March 2025, the company's gearing ratio is 0.58 times, with no term debt. The company's debt protection metrics are comfortable, with a healthy interest cover of 6 times. The recent IPO equity infusion of ₹104 crore is expected to further strengthen the company's financial position, with plans to utilize ₹72.67 crore for working capital, which is expected to improve debt protection metrics and capital structure over the medium term, with a likely reduction in gearing to around 0.07-0.10 times and improvement in interest cover to more than 9 times. 

### **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Exposure to cyclicality in the end-user industries:** The entity caters to the real estate, construction, mining and infrastructure industries. The end-user industries are cyclical and strongly correlated to economic cycles. Furthermore, the company generates more than 95% of its revenue from the export of refurbished and value-added construction and mining equipment. Cyclicality of the industries as well as exposure to risks related to global trade relations will remain key monitorable. 

**Working capital-intensive operations:** The company's gross current assets (GCAs) have increased significantly from 46 days as of March 31, 2023, to 132 days as of March 31, 2025. This substantial rise in working capital requirements is primarily attributed to a shift in the business model, where the company now receives partial upfront payment, with the balance due upon delivery, as opposed to the previous practice of receiving full payment in advance. This change is a strategic move to expand the company's market presence. Going forward, the operations are expected to remain working capital-intensive, with GCAs projected to further increase over the medium term.
