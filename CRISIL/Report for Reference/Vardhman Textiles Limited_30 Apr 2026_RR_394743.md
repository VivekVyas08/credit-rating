## **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its 'Crisil AA+/Stable/Crisil A1+' ratings on the bank facilities, fixed deposits and commercial paper of Vardhman Textiles Limited (VTXL; part of the Vardhman group). 

The ratings continue to reflect the strong financial and liquidity risk profiles of the Vardhman group, its diversified business risk profile, especially in the textile business, and healthy operating capabilities. These strengths are partially offset by large working capital requirement, modest market position in the steel industry and susceptibility to volatility in input prices. 

The ratings factor in the ongoing capital expenditure (capex) of Rs 5,400-5,500 crore in the Vardhman group. VTXL had announced capex of ~Rs 3,000 crore between fiscals 2025 and 2027 to expand the yarn division, install nylon and polyester fabric division, and digitise and modernise the existing facility and in the green energy vertical. Vardhman Special Steels Ltd (VSSL) shall invest Rs 2,400–2500 crore over fiscals 2027-2030 (with major capex on plant and machinery beginning in fiscal 2028) for setting up a forging plant and a greenfield project for melting capacity of 500,000 tonne in Punjab. The capex will be funded through equity and debt. Timely completion and operationalisation of the additional capacities shall remain monitorable. 

At the group level, operating income is expected at Rs 12,000–12,200 crore in fiscal 2027, compared with an estimated Rs 11,600–11,700 crore in fiscal 2026, following an increase of 3% in fiscal 2025 from Rs 11,615 crore in fiscal 2024. The revenue is likely to increase over the medium term backed by timely completion of capex, healthy capacity utilisation and improvement in realisations of cotton yarn. Consolidated earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (Ebitda) margin is expected to improve to 13.5–14.0% in fiscal 2027 from an estimated 12.75–13.00% in fiscal 2026 owing to increase in spread between cotton and yarn and improved profit in the fabric business. In the first nine months of fiscal 2026, VTXL reported revenue of Rs 7,371 crore and Ebitda margin of ~12.8%, while VSSL reported revenue of Rs 1,297 crore and Ebitda margin of ~9.3%. 

Strong consolidated cash accrual of Rs 1,150–1,200 crore in fiscal 2026, which may increase to more than Rs 1,250 crore over the medium term, driven by healthy profitability and strong market position, will sufficiently cover annual debt obligation of Rs 200–500 crore in the medium term. 

At the group level, the financial risk profile will likely remain strong, with net debt expected to increase however, remain below Rs 1000 crore over the medium term (net debt was Rs 350–400 crore as on March 31, 2026). However, this is contingent on the group’s ability to maintain heathy profitability. The gross debt increased to ~Rs 2,000 crore as on March 31, 2026, from ~Rs 1,358 crore a year earlier and is expected at Rs 2,100–2,200 crore over the next two fiscals, despite the ongoing capex. Debt protection metrics will remain comfortable, with interest coverage and net cash accrual to adjusted debt (NCAAD) ratio above 12.0 times and below 0.6 time, respectively, for fiscal 2026, as against 13.66 times and 0.91 time for fiscal 2025. This will be supported by gradual repayment of debt and improvement in profitability. The capital structure will moderate slightly, but remain comfortable, with gearing and total outside liabilities to tangible networth (TOLTNW) ratio 

expected at 0.20–0.25 time and 0.25–0.35 time, respectively, over the next two fiscals, as against 0.13 time and 0.26 time as on March 31, 2025. 

Liquidity remains strong, with consolidated cash and equivalents of ~Rs 1,500 crore as against debt repayment of Rs 180– 300 crore over medium term. Furthermore, VTXL had total fund-based limit of Rs 1,920 crore with utilised limit of Rs 476 crore as on January 31, 2025, with 12-month average utilisation of 12.3%. 

# **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of VTXL and its subsidiaries, Vardhman Acrylics Ltd (Vardhman Acrylics) and Vardhman Spinning & General Mills Ltd. This is because the three entities, collectively referred to as the Vardhman group, are in the same business and have common treasury and strong operational linkages, including procurement of cotton. The business and financial risk profiles of the subsidiary, VTL Investments Ltd, and its associate, VSSL, have also been combined because of history of support from the group. The business and financial risk profiles of Vardhman Yarns and Threads Ltd (VYTL) have not been included since fiscal 2017 as VTXL has divested most of its stake in VYTL and is now a minority shareholder.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Diversified business risk profile, especially in the textile segment:** The Vardhman group operates across the textile, acrylic fibre and steel segments, which accounted for 82%, 3% and 15% of revenue, respectively, in fiscal 2024, Though the group is a small player in the steel business, it has strong market position in the cotton yarn and fabrics segment, backed by its large capacity and established relationship with leading global apparel manufacturers. It is one of the largest spinners in the domestic market, with installed capacity of 12.3 lakh spindles, accounting for 2% of the installed spindles in India. 

The group is also among the top three woven fabric manufacturers in India, with grey and processed fabric capacity of 220 million metre per annum (MMPA) and 210 MMPA, respectively. It is an approved supplier to large retailers, such as Walmart (rated 'AA/Stable/A-1+' by S&P Global Ratings), Hennes and Mauritz, and Aditya Birla Fashion and Retail Ltd ('Crisil AA+/Stable/Crisil A1+'). The Vardhman group is also one of the largest players in the domestic acrylic fibre market with capacity of 21,000 tonne per annum (TPA). 

**Healthy operating capabilities:** The strong position of the group in the textiles business is reinforced by its healthy operating capabilities. The group has continuously invested towards enhancing its spinning productivity. Being one of the largest consumers of raw cotton in the country—it procures over 15 lakh bales per annum—the group enjoys preferredbuyer status and pricing benefits. 

After a steady growth in operating income by 3% and recovery in operating margin to 12.8% in fiscal 2026 due to stable cotton price, the operating income is expected to grow by 4-6% and operating margin to improve to 13.0–14.0% over the medium term, leading to net cash accrual of more than Rs 1250 crore. 

**Strong financial risk profile:** The financial risk profile was robust despite capex of Rs 5,400–5,500 crore over fiscals 20252030, supported by healthy cash accrual, improving profitability and longer gestation period. 

Net debt is expected to increase, however remain below Rs 1,000 crore in the medium term. However, it remains contingent on the group’s ability to maintain heathy profitability. Debt may increase owing to sizeable capex. Debt protection metrics will remain comfortable, with interest coverage and NCAAD ratio estimated at 14.0–15.0 times and below 0.6 time, respectively, for fiscal 2026, as against 13.66 times and 0.91 time for fiscal 2025. The debt protection metrics will be supported by gradual repayment of debt and improvement in profitability. The capital structure will moderate slightly, but remain comfortable, with gearing and TOLTNW ratio expected at 0.20–0.25 time and 0.25–0.35 time, respectively, over the next two fiscals, as against 0.13 time and 0.26 time as on March 31, 2025. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Vulnerability of operating profitability to volatility in input prices:** The group remains susceptible to volatility in the prices of key raw materials, such as cotton (which accounts for 50% of the cost of yarn) and steel. Cotton prices are exposed to risks such as unfavourable monsoon or pest attacks and are linked to international demand and supply. Though the group benefits from the large procurement and adequate risk management systems of VTXL, profitability remains susceptible to volatility in raw material prices. Operating margin fluctuated between 10% and 23.1% in the past decade and was adversely affected in fiscal 2025 and in fiscals 2020, 2018, 2015 and 2012, because of slowdown in demand from China and government intervention. In the steel business, the operating margin is vulnerable to the prices of sponge iron, manganese and nickel. 

**Large working capital requirement:** Cotton is a seasonal crop and its availability and quality are typical issues that come up after the cotton season. Driven by the Vardhman group’s commitment to deliver quality products, it procures cotton during the cotton season and maintains large inventory. Inventory falls by September as the stock is consumed in the first half of the fiscal and starts increasing once the cotton season begins from October. In the steel business, dependence on the automotive industry has resulted in sizeable working capital requirement with receivables of ~50 days against payables of 25-30 days. Gross current assets, for VTXL, were large at 213 days as on March 31, 2025. 

**Modest, though improving, market position in the steel business:** The group has a relatively small presence in the steel business through VSSL. Moreover, as it derives over 85% revenue from the automotive sector, it remains susceptible to cyclicality in this segment. That said, better utilisation and focus on cost optimisation have ensured steady performance, as reflected in the operating margin rising to 9.5% in fiscal 2025 from 2.4% in fiscal 2015. The operating margin of VSSL is estimated at 10–11% in fiscal 2026. The business risk profile will likely remain healthy, with Ebitda per tonne expected at Rs 8,000–10,000 over the medium term. Cost-control measures and initiatives for better procurement will help keep debt protection metrics comfortable over the medium term. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Liquidity will likely remain strong, supported by expected cash accrual of Rs 1,200–1,250 crore against debt obligation of Rs 200–300 crore over the medium term. Consolidated cash and equivalents were ~Rs 1,500 crore as on 31<sup>st</sup> March 2026. Furthermore, VTXL has total fund-based limit of Rs 1,920 crore with utilised limit of Rs 476 crore as on January 31, 2025, and 12-month average utilisation of 12.3%. 

# **<u>ESG Profile</u>** 

The Vardhaman group has set robust sustainability targets across various aspects. These include: 

- 100% ZDHC MRSL (Zero Discharge of Hazardous Chemicals Manufacturing Restricted Substances List) compliance by 2030 

- Net positive impact on biodiversity by 2050 with no gross deforestation by 2045 

- Reducing virgin plastic in packaging by 20% and transitioning to 100% sustainable packaging by 2030 Reducing Scope 1 and 2 emissions by 42% by 2030 and net zero by 2045 

- Reducing freshwater consumption by 30% and waste to landfill by 50% by 2030 

The group reported a moderately high waste recycling rate (~67% of total waste generated), higher-than-the-peeraverage share of renewable energy consumed in the total energy mix (~23% ), high proportion of water reused (~56% of total water drawn), relatively low proportion of water withdrawn from stressed areas (~11% of total water drawn), and no operations in ecologically sensitive areas. 

- The Vardhaman group’s diversity and health and safety targets include achieving 20% women representation in management and 40% overall gender diversity by 2030, along with zero fatalities, zero occupational diseases, and a 25% on-year reduction in LTIFR and first-aid incidents. 

- The group has reported higher-than-the-peer-average gender diversity among employees and workers (~11% and ~37%, respectively), nil workforce fatalities and nil lost-time injury frequency rate (LTIFR) of employees. Its governance structure is characterized by adequate size of the board (12 members) with ~50% independent directors, high representation of women directors (~33%), majorly independent audit and nomination and remuneration committees, high attendance of independent directors at board and committee meetings (~93%), 100% investor complaints redressal rate and nil whistle blower complaints. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

The Vardhman group will sustain its healthy credit risk profile, backed by its strong market position in the textile business, diversified product portfolio and strong liquidity. 

# **<u>Rating Sensitivity Factors</u>** 

# **Upward Factors** 

- Substantial increase in revenue and market share, driven by product diversity, with operating margin remaining steady over 15% 

- Debt to Ebitda ratio declining below 1.0 time on a steady-state basis Healthy liquid surplus of at least Rs 800 crore 

# **Downward Factors** 

- Net debt to Ebitda ratio exceeding 1.2 times on a sustained basis due to large capex, stretched working capital cycle or sharp decline in Ebitda 

Sizeable reduction in liquidity due to buyback of shares, sizeable dividend payout and additional capex. 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

The Vardhman group, headed by S P Oswal, is a leading textile group in India, with operations across the yarn, fabric, sewing threads, fibre, special alloys and garment sectors. In fiscal 2025, the textile business accounted for 82.4% of the consolidated operating income, followed by the acrylic segment (2.4%) and the steel alloy segment (15.2%). The group has 15 manufacturing units across four states. 

VSSL has capacity to manufacture 3 lakh TPA of steel billets, 2 lakh TPA of steel rolled products and 48 thousand TPA of bright bards. 

Vardhman Acrylics manufactures acrylic fibre and has capacity of 21,000 TPA.
