### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its 'Crisil A-/Stable/Crisil A2+' ratings on the bank facilities of Gufic Biosciences Ltd (GBL). 

The ratings continue to reflect the established market position in the pharmaceutical industry, its well-established clientele and a comfortable financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by vulnerability to regulatory changes, large working capital requirement and exposure to stabilisation risk. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profile of GBL and its subsidiaries, Gufic Prime Pvt Ltd (GPPL), Gufic UK Ltd (GUL), Gufic Ireland Ltd (GIL) and Veira Life FZE (VLF).

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Established market position in the pharmaceutical industry:** The promoters have experience of over five decades in the pharmaceutical industry. Their strong understanding of the market dynamics and healthy relationships with customers and suppliers will continue to support the business. GBL has various certifications and approvals for its manufacturing facilities and has diversified its product portfolio through continuous research and development (R&D). The product portfolio is diversified, with the top five products contributing to over 13% of revenues in fiscal 2025. Revenues increased to around Rs 820 crore in fiscal 2025 from Rs 690 crore in fiscal 2023, and the momentum in revenues growth is expected to continue backed by healthy demand, continuous R&D and diversified product portfolio, along with steady ramp up of the newly established Indore unit, which commenced commercial production in January 2025. The company achieved revenues of Rs 224 crore in the first quarter of fiscal 2026. Established market position in the pharmaceutical industry should continue to support the business risk profile over the medium term. 

**Well-established clientele:** Clientele comprises large players such as Glenmark Pharmaceuticals Ltd, Lupin Ltd, Abbott Healthcare Pvt Ltd and Zydus Healthcare Ltd. Healthy relationships with reputed players led to repeat orders, contributing to steady revenues growth. The company has a network of 25 carrying and forwarding agents and more than 500 stockists across India, through which it has access to over 1 lakh retailers. The top five customers contributed to 23% of revenues in fiscal 2025. Benefits from longstanding relationships with the well-established customer base will persist over the medium term. 

**Strong financial risk profile:** Networth was strong at Rs 595 crore as on March 31, 2025, an increase from Rs 527 crore a year earlier, backed by steady accretion to reserve and equity infusions. Total outside liabilities to adjusted networth ratio and gearing stood at 0.96 time and 0.52 time, respectively, as on March 31, 2025 (1.06 times and 0.60 time, respectively, as on March 31, 2024), owing to moderate reliance on external debt due to large working capital requirements and term debt availed for the recent capex. Debt protection metrics were robust, as reflected in interest coverage and net cash accrual to adjusted debt ratios of 5.7 times and 0.3 time, respectively, in fiscal 2025. The financial risk profile will likely remain comfortable over the medium term backed by stable profitability, incremental cash flows from the Indore unit and no further debt-funded capex plans. 

**<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Vulnerability to adverse changes in government regulations:** The pharmaceutical industry is highly regulated by state governments and government agencies, which approve new drugs and clinical trials, control the quality of imported drugs and set prices for many critical drugs; while state authorities regulate manufacture, sales and distribution. Unfavourable regulations adversely impacting the business risk profile will remlain a key monitorable. 

**Large working capital requirement** : Gross current assets (GCAs) were sizeable and stood at around 271 days as on March 31, 2025, driven by receivables of 144 days (155 days as on March 31, 2024) and inventory of 116 days (111 days as on March 31, 2024). Receivables have increased owing to the credit period of up to 135 days offered to certain customers. Furthermore, due to business need, the company holds large raw material and work-in-process inventory of 90-120 days. Operations will remain working capital intensive over the medium term. Management of the working capital cycle amid increasing scale of operations will remain monitorable over the medium term. 

**Exposure to stabilisation risks of the new plant:** GBL has undertaken a greenfield project in Indore, Madhya Pradesh, to expand capacities of existing formulations/injectables and incorporate a new product, which achieved commercialised production in January 2025. As the company incurred expenditure for validation checks and other expenses, without any major cash inflow as the plant was operating at less capacity, the operating margin fell from 18.6% in fiscal 2024 to 16.6% in fiscal 2025 and further to 14.9% in the first quarter of fiscal 2026. Timely stabilisation and ramp up of the capex, resulting in cash flow generation and increase in operating margin, remain monitorables over the medium term. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Cash accrual is projected at Rs 95-115 crore in fiscals 2026 and 2027, which is sufficient to meet yearly debt obligation of Rs 25-26 crore. Fund-based bank limit of Rs 280 crore (enhanced from Rs 200 crore in September 2025) was utilised 80% on average over the 12 months through August 2025. Utilisation has been higher over the past 2-3 months, however the same is expected to moderate in the near term. While the recent enhancement of the working capital limit provides liquidity to cover the working capital requirement, moderate utilisation of the working capital limits remains monitorable. Unencumbered cash and equivalents was Rs 12-13 crore as on March 31, 2025. Crisil Ratings expects internal accrual, cash and equivalent and unutilised bank lines will be sufficient to meet the capex, debt obligation and working capital requirement. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes GBL’s business risk profile of GBL will continue to strengthen over the medium term driven by its increasing scale of operations and stable operating margin. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Steady revenues growth and increase in operating margins sustained above 15% leading to higher-than-expected cash accruals 

Improvement in the working capital cycle and sustenance of healthy financial risk profile 

# **Downward factors** 

Decline in revenues or fall in operating margins leading to cash accruals below Rs 40-45 crores 

Further stretch in the working capital cycle or large capex weakening the liquidity resulting in continued higher utilization of bank lines 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Incorporated in 1984 by the Choksi family, GBL manufactures formulations across various therapeutic segments such as antifungal, anesthetics and immunosuppressants. Its key business segments are pharmaceutical products (92% of revenues) and bulk drugs (8%). Its facilities are at Navsari, Gujarat; Belgaum, Karnataka; and Indore, Madhya Pradesh. Mr Jayesh P Choksi (chairman and managing director) and Mr Pranav J Choksi (CEO) manage the business. GBL is listed on the Bombay Stock Exchange and the National Stock Exchange. 

The group’s subsidiaries, GPPL, GUL, GIL and VLF, are engaged in similar businesses
