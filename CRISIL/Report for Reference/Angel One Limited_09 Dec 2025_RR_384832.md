### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ratings on the bank facilities of Angel One Limited (Angel One; part of the Angel group) and subsequently **withdrawn** the ratings at the company's request and on receipt of a no-objection certificate from the banker. The rating on the non-convertible debentures (NCDs) has also been **withdrawn** upon receiving confirmation of no issuance done under the instrument. Further, the rating on the commercial paper has also been **withdrawn** basis confirmation provided by issuing and paying agent (IPA) of non-utilisation of Crisil Ratings’ rating for raising funds under this instrument. The withdrawal is in line with Crisil Ratings’ policy on withdrawal of bank loan ratings and debt instruments. 

The ratings continue to factor in the Angel group’s strong market share and longstanding presence in the equity broking segment, along with the extensive experience of its promoters in the capital markets business. The ratings also take into consideration the sound risk management systems and comfortable capital position of the group. These strengths are partially offset by high dependence on broking income, increasing competition in the segment and susceptibility to uncertainties inherent in the capital markets business. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has consolidated the business and financial risk profiles of Angel One with its wholly owned subsidiaries, Angel Fincap Pvt Ltd, Angel Financial Advisors Pvt Ltd, Angel Securities Ltd, Angel Digitech Services Pvt Ltd and Mimansa Software Systems Pvt Ltd. This is because all these companies, collectively referred to as the Angel group, have highly integrated operations and common directors and senior management. Moreover, the management has articulated that in case of distress in any of these companies, other group companies will provide financial support, including transfer of funds, on a timely basis.

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

### **Strong market share in the equity broking segment:** 

The Angel group has a well-established presence of over three decades in the broking industry. As on September 30, 2025, the group had 69 lakh active customers on the National Stock Exchange (NSE). The company’s market share within the active client space (NSE) stood at 15.2% as on September 30, 2025 (15.4% as on March 31, 2025). The group has focused on digitising its operations, thereby offering all its products through mobile application. This easy-to-use trading platform has helped the group reach new clients. Also, these initiatives have helped the Angel group to position itself among the top three digital brokers and garner major clients, resulting in significant increase in broking income. 

### **Longstanding presence with extensive experience of the promoters in the capital markets business:** 

The Angel group has been operating for three decades and is led by Mr Dinesh Thakkar (Managing Director [MD] and Chairman), a first-generation entrepreneur. The management has been instrumental in transforming the group from a traditional to a digital broking set up. This has helped the Angel group to successfully pivot its business model in response to a changing environment within the broking industry. It has proactively embraced the shift in industry trends by offering trading through mobile application and use of e-KYC (know your customer) and a flat-fee-based pricing model. Furthermore, with a technology-driven model, the management has redefined the Angel group to further strengthen and cover all its digital services under one platform and to monetise the core brokerage platform through additional products and services. 

### **Sound risk management systems:** 

The risk management systems are adequate in accordance with the group’s current and planned scale of operations. Across segments, the group has a granular portfolio and relatively stringent margin collection policy to partially offset the market volatility risk. On top of the exchange specified minimum value at risk plus extreme loss margin, the group charges additional margin to scrips based on its categorisation as blue chip, good and average scrips. The group’s sound risk management systems are reflected in the absence of major losses or bad debt (excluding two exceptional incidents) in the last several years. 

### **Comfortable capitalisation:** 

The Angel group’s capitalisation has remained comfortable for the past several years owing to steady internal accrual and capital raising at regular intervals. As on September 30, 2025, the group’s networth improved to Rs 5834 crore from Rs 5639 crore as on March 31, 2025. Apart from capital raising, the group’s steady internal accrual continues to support its capital position. As far as gearing is concerned, apart from meeting its working 

capital requirement, the group has also expanded its margin trade financing (MTF) book. In line with increasing demand for MTF, the group proposes to leverage funding opportunities towards its client base. While the group has a huge opportunity in this segment, gearing is expected to remain below 3 times even during peak demand. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **High dependence on revenue from broking services:** 

The group’s revenue has been highly skewed towards broking income, which accounts for two-thirds of total income. Given the higher reliance on broking income, any significant volatility in the markets can directly exert pressure on the group’s overall income profile. Also, compared to other equally established large capital market entities, the group’s share of broking income is high. 

Nevertheless, as part of its diversification strategy, the group continues to offer multiple investment services such as wealth management, mutual funds and distribution of insurance products. Within wealth management, the group has built assets under management (AUM) of Rs 6,140 crore as on September 30, 2025. The group also launched its own mutual fund schemes in February 2025 to enhance its presence in the capital markets segment by offering more investment products. With regards to the lending business, the group has also substantially enhanced its client funding (margin funding) book to Rs 5,947 crore as on September 30, 2025, from Rs 3,859 crore as on March 31, 2025. Crisil Ratings believes the steps taken by the group towards diversification have been yielding some benefits. On the costs front, in the second half of fiscal 2025, the overall cost to income ratio saw some correction due to the impact on revenue profile owing to application of ‘True to label’ guidelines. Nevertheless, with some improvement in the topline and non-applicability of one-time expense, the cost to income ratio improved to 76% and 69% on gross and net basis, respectively, in the second quarter of fiscal 2026. 

**Highly competitive capital market industry with every player expanding towards the digital acquisition model:** The group’s businesses, confined within the capital markets industry, face intense competition as multiple players offer low-cost products to clients. The industry has seen a continuous transformation with technology-based discount brokers entering and dominating the market. The competition, therefore, is expected to remain high, thereby intensifying the price war. Accordingly, the group’s key broking business remains exposed to market, economic, political and social factors that drive investor sentiment. Crisil Ratings believes the maintenance of active clients in total user base, along with continuous engagement of first-time investors in trading activity, will remain key rating sensitivity factors. 

# **Susceptibility to regulatory changes:** 

Over the last couple of years, the broking industry has witnessed continuous regulatory revisions. While most have not affected the performance of Angel group, the regulation on ‘True to label’ has affected its revenue profile. As per this framework (effective October 1, 2024), there has now been standardisation in exchange charges with a flat fee structure regardless of the size of turnover. For Angel group, the income generated from this source was 8-9% of topline, which reduced to nil from the third quarter of fiscal 2025. A similar trend has been observed industry-wide; revenue profile of industry players having large volumes either through client business or proprietary trading was impacted due to this regulation. Following which, the group has now been using other levers making revisions in its pricing model. The group is focusing on generating revenue from other sources such as interest from MTF book, distribution of investment products, services such as mutual funds and wealth management. The Angel group has shown its ability to manage such regulatory changes without any major impact on its revenue profile or market position. However, capital markets remain susceptible to such regulatory changes and the group’s ability to manage them with minimal impact on its credit profile will remain monitorable. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Liquidity is comfortable because of the agency nature of the business and healthy unutilised bank overdraft (OD) facilities. All the bank facilities are working capital and are matched against exposures extended to clients. As those instruments are for a short tenure of 15 - 90 days, the company can match its client recoveries with its debt obligation. Furthermore, the company starts raising incremental funds once the overdraft utilisation reaches 75-80%. The company maintains margins in the form of fixed deposits (FDs) at exchange level. The overall fixed deposits of the company stood at Rs 2,500-3,000 crore on a steady-state basis and will increase or decrease depending on client activity. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** <u>Stable</u> 

Crisil Ratings believes the Angel group will sustain its market position with adequate capitalisation and sound risk management practices over the medium term. 

# **<u>Rating Sensitivity Factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Sustenance of market position in terms of growing share in active clients to over 16% Increase in revenue diversity with rise in share of income from non-broking activities Improvement in cost-to-income ratio on steady-state basis 

# **Downward factors** 

Weakening of business risk profile, as indicated by drop in market share, affecting revenue from broking services Decline in the earnings profile or sustained increase in cost-to-income ratio over 75% 

# **<u>About the Group</u>** 

Angel One (erstwhile Angel Broking) was incorporated in 1997 by Mr Dinesh Thakkar, the chairman and MD of the group. The Angel group is engaged in retail broking in equity, commodity and currency segments. It is a member of the Bombay Stock Exchange, NSE, Metropolitan Stock Exchange of India Ltd, Multi Commodity Exchange of India Ltd and National Commodity and Derivatives Exchange Ltd. As part of its diversification strategy, the group continues to offer multiple investment services such as wealth management, mutual funds and distribution of insurance products. Furthermore, the group leverages cross-selling opportunities by offering non-capital market-linked products (only distribution) such as FDs and personal loans
