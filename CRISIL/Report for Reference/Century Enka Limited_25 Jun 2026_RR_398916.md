#### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A+/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the bank facilities of Century Enka Limited (CEL, an Aditya Birla Group Company). 

The ratings continue to reflect the healthy financial risk profile and established market position in the nylon tyre cord fabric (NTCF) and nylon filament yarn (NFY) segments. These strengths are partially offset by susceptibility of operating margins to volatility in input prices (particularly caprolactam), structural headwinds from increasing radialisation, and continued pricing pressure from low-cost imports, especially in the NFY segment. 

CEL reported ~15% on-year decline in revenue to Rs 1,705 crore in fiscal 2026 (Rs 2,002 crore in fiscal 2025), primarily due to lower realisations linked to moderation in raw material prices and subdued demand during the year, and persistent pressure from imports. However, demand recovered in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026, supported by rationalisation in the Goods and Services Tax (GST) rates, improved rural demand, and traction in commercial vehicles, tractor and two/threewheeler segments. 

Despite lower revenue, the operating margin rose to ~8.7% in fiscal 2026 from ~6.1% in fiscal 2025, driven by cost optimisation measures, higher share of value-added products (over 35% of NFY portfolio during fiscal 2026), effective passthrough of raw material prices and increased use of renewable energy. The margin was also supported by inventory gains during the last quarter of the fiscal 2026. The margin is expected at 7–10% over the medium term, supported by continued focus on cost efficiency and product mix improvement, along with gradual contribution from the polyester tyre cord fabric (PTCF) segment from the fourth quarter of fiscal 2027. 

CEL has undertaken power cost optimisation through increased use of renewable energy, with the renewable share reaching ~36% in fiscal 2026. The company continues to focus on process optimisation and energy efficiency measures to manage costs. It plans to expand renewable capacity under Phase 2 at Bharuch by the second half of fiscal 2027 and through the group captive model. The incremental capacity will result into power cost optimisation and full benefits will accrue from fiscal 2028. 

The financial risk profile remains healthy, with networth of ~Rs 1,500 crore and gearing at ~0.02 time as on March 31, 2026, and interest coverage at ~66 times for fiscal 2026. Cash accrual is ~Rs 135 crore in fiscal 2026. The company had cash and investments of ~Rs 359 crore and low debt of ~Rs 20 crore as on March 31, 2026. Planned capex is expected to be funded through internal accrual. 

#### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has evaluated the standalone business and financial risk profiles of CEL. 

#### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Healthy financial risk profile:** The financial risk profile is supported by low leverage and strong debt protection metrics which is expected to remain strong over the medium term. The capital structure is underpinned by healthy networth of ~Rs 1,500 crore and gearing of ~0.02 time as on March 31, 2026, indicating minimal reliance on external debt. 

The company plans capex of ~Rs 120 crore in fiscal 2027, primarily towards value-added products and routine capex. The capex is expected to be funded through internal accrual and existing liquidity. Liquidity remains strong, with cash and investments of ~Rs 359 crore. Expected annual cash accrual of Rs 130–170 crore over the medium term will be adequate to meet the capex and operational requirements, without affecting the financial risk profile. 

**Market leadership in the NTCF segment with evolving product diversification in the reinforcement segment:** CEL is among the top three players in the domestic NTCF segment, with a market share of ~25%, supported by longstanding relationships with leading tyre manufacturers such as MRF Ltd, Apollo Tyres Ltd and JK Tyre Ltd. CEL benefits from its integrated manufacturing capabilities and established presence across the reinforcement value chain. Demand for NTCF is expected to remain stable over the medium to long term, with the impact of increasing radialisation in commercial vehicles partially offset by growth in the underlying tyre market and in replacement demand. 

The company has entered the PTCF segment, catering to the passenger vehicle and electric vehicle (EV) tyre market, thereby expanding its presence within the reinforcement segment. The PTCF facility is in advanced stages of customer approvals, and is likely to be commissioned in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2027, supported by relationships with tyre manufacturers and aiding product diversification over the medium term. 

**Product portfolio optimisation in NFY with increasing shift towards value-added segments:** CEL maintains a strong position in the NFY segment with a market share of ~24% supported by its established brand. The company has undertaken a strategic shift in the NFY segment from commodity products to value-added products in response to increasing competition from low-cost imports. The company has scaled up offerings such as mother yarn, DTY (draw textured yarn) and ATY (air textured yarn) catering to specialised applications in athleisure, technical textiles and premium fabrics. The share of value-added products has increased to over 35% of the NFY portfolio, and is expected to increase over 50% over the medium term. This shift has been supported by targeted capex and modernisation of manufacturing facilities, including investments in yarn conversion and advanced processing capabilities. Value-added products offer higher realisations compared with commodity products, and continue to contribute materially to the product mix. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Susceptibility to volatility in input prices and import-linked pricing dynamics:** The cost of production and the operating margin remain exposed to volatility in crude-linked raw material prices, particularly caprolactam, which constitutes 50–60% of the cost base and has a direct pass-through linkage to revenue, thereby impacting realisations. While CEL follows a pass-through mechanism for raw material price movements, profitability remains sensitive to the lag in pass-through and to inventory, especially during periods of sharp price fluctuations. 

In the NFY segment, domestic prices are linked to import parity and the sharp increase in low-cost imports from China and Vietnam continues to exert pressure on pricing and margins in commodity products. Although CEL has reduced exposure to commodity segments (15–20% of volume) and increased the share of value-added products (~35%), and anti-dumping measures are under consideration, the ability to sustain operating profitability amid elevated import competition and raw material volatility remains monitorable. 

**Exposure to cyclical demand and structural shift towards radial tyres** : The performance of CEL remains linked to demand from the tyre industry, with nearly 75% of demand driven by the replacement market and the balance linked to new vehicle sales. The demand for tyres is influenced by trends in the automotive sector and overall economic conditions, while competitive intensity and cheaper imports continue to pose a challenge. 

The increasing adoption of radial tyres in the truck and bus segment has affected demand for NTCF, leading to pricing pressure and moderation in margins across the industry. Demand from the replacement market is expected to remain relatively stable over the medium term. CEL’s entry into the PTCF segment, catering to the radial tyre market, with revenue contribution expected from fourth quarter of fiscal 2027, will support the business. However, the pace of ramp-up in the PTCF segment remains monitorable. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

CEL’s liquidity is supported by healthy cash accrual, and cash and equivalent of Rs 359 crore as on fiscal 2026. Net cash accrual improved to ~Rs 135 crore in fiscal 2026 vis-à-vis annual debt obligation of Rs 14 crore. Net cash accrual is expected to sustain at Rs 130–170 crore over the medium term. Planned capex of ~Rs 120 crore in fiscal 2027, primarily toward value-added products and routine capex, is expected to be funded through internal accrual and existing liquidity, without reliance on incremental borrowings. The financial flexibility is supported by low to moderate utilisation of the bank limit. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

CEL will continue to benefit from its established market position and healthy financial risk profile over the medium term.

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Set up in 1965 by the BK Birla group and Enka International (part of the Netherlands-based Akzo Nobel group), CEL manufactures industrial and textile yarn and fabric such as NTCF and NFY. CEL is part of the Aditya Birla Group. NTCF is used as a reinforcement material in bias/cross ply tyres primarily used in trucks, buses, two/three-wheelers, and off-the-road (OTR) vehicles used in mining, forestry, farming and heavy earthmoving. NFY is mainly used in sarees, dupattas, dress material and ethnic dresses. CEL entered the PTCF segment with the commissioning of its PTCF plant in the last quarter of fiscal 2024.
