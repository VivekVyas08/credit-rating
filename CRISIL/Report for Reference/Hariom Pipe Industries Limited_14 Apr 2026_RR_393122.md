### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has placed its ratings on the bank loan facilities of Hariom Pipe Industries Limited (HPIL; a part of the Hariom group) on **'Rating Watch with Developing Implications'.** 

The rating action follows the announcement by HPIL on April 2, 2026, that it had received directions from the Tamil Nadu Pollution Control Board (TNPCB) for temporary shutdown of its unit in SIPCOT, Perundurai. This unit accounted for 40–45% of the company's revenue in fiscal 2026. 

The shutdown is expected to be temporary, and the company has measures in place to mitigate the impact on its business performance. Its Mehboobnagar unit has excess capacity, which will be utilised to continue manufacturing products. Additionally, HPIL has engaged with a third-party manufacturer to produce products on a jobwork basis, ensuring continuity of supply to customers. 

While HPIL expects to resume operations at the affected unit within this quarter, the situation will be closely monitored. Any significant impact on production, sales volume or profitability could potentially affect the company's credit profile. 

The ratings continues to reflect the extensive experience of the promoters in the steel industry along with the Hariom group’s integrated operations leading to healthy operating efficiency. The ratings also factor in the rising contribution of value-added products and comfortable financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by exposure to intense competition, and susceptibility to cyclicality, volatility in input cost and project risk associated with the solar capacity project. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of HPIL and its subsidiary, Hariom Power and Energy Pvt Ltd (HPEL), and newly formed subsidiary Metal Mart Pvt Ltd (MMPL), while arriving at the ratings. HPEL is consolidated on account of the support it is likely to receive from HPIL for the execution of its project and the potential cash flow fungibility between the entities. Also, HPIL has provided corporate guarantee for the debt availed by HPEL. HPIL and MMPL have higher cash flow fungibility and common business. All the companies are collectively referred to herein as the Hariom group.

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Extensive experience of the promoters:** The promoters bring over three decades of experience in the steel industry, leveraging their deep understanding of the market dynamics to successfully diversify the group’s product offerings and streamline operations. Their extensive industry expertise has also enabled them to establish strong relationship with steel traders and manufacturers in multiple states, including Maharashtra, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and Tamil Nadu. 

In recent years, the group has significantly expanded its capacities and operations through strategic acquisitions of units, which has had a positive impact on its business performance. This expansion has not only increased production capabilities but also enhanced market presence, positioning the group for continued growth and success. 

**Integrated operations, leading to healthy operating efficiency:** HPIL started with a furnace to manufacture mild steel (MS) billets and has integrated forward over the years into hot-rolled (HR) strips, MS tubes, galvanised pipes and galvanised iron pipes and scaffolding. The company integrated backward and started manufacturing MS sponge iron in fiscal 2021. The integrated operations helped limit the impact of downcycles and sustain better profitability (operating margin above 12%) compared with other steel players. Moreover, savings in power cost because of inhouse solar power capacity and furnace unit aid the operating margin. The power cost declined significantly to 5% of revenue during fiscal 2025 from 10.5% in fiscal 2023, which helped maintain healthy earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation (Ebitda) per tonne. 

**Rising contribution of value-added products:** Value-added products (VAPs), such as galvanised pipes and coils, have consistently gained traction, accounting for 52.24% of sales in the first nine months of fiscal 2026, up from 51.68% in fiscal 2025 and 33.28% in fiscal 2024. The shift towards higher-value products has helped mitigate the impact of moderating realisations. Although the temporary shutdown of the Perundurai unit, which produces GP pipes and coils, may pose some challenges, the company has surplus capacity in other units which can compensate for the lost production. As a result, no significant drop in sales is expected. 

While Ebitda per tonne declined in fiscal 2026 due to higher raw material costs, particularly for the VAP segment, which sources materials from external suppliers, it remains better than that of peers. The Ebitda margin is expected to remain healthy, above 11%, despite the temporary shutdown of the Perundurai unit. 

**Healthy financial risk profile:** The capital structure remains healthy, with gearing below 1 time, despite the subsidiary availing of term debt of Rs 194.66 crore for the development of a solar project. Additionally, HPIL has started utilising vendor bill discounting limits since fiscal 2025, which has not had a significant impact on its capital structure. 

The debt protection metrics are expected to remain strong, with interest coverage above 3.5 times in fiscal 2026. The total outside liabilities to tangible networth (TOLTNW) ratio has consistently been below 1.3 times, indicating comfortable debt relative to networth. The networth strengthened in fiscal 2026, driven by strong accretion to reserve. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Exposure to intense competition and cyclicality in the steel industry:** The Indian steel industry is highly fragmented due to low entry barriers. The consequent intense competition along with limited product differentiation may continue to constrain scalability, pricing power and profitability. Moreover, the industry is inherently strongly correlated to the economic environment. Also, while large players benefit from economies of scale, high power costs and limited ability to control market prices restrict the profitability of small players. However, these risks are partially offset by the benefits HPIL derives from its backward-integrated operations. 

**Exposure to volatility in steel prices:** Operating profitability is susceptible to volatility in the cost of raw materials, such as sponge iron and steel scrap, along with power and steel prices. The rates are market driven and individual players are price takers. Hence, any sharp fluctuation in steel prices will significantly impact the operating margin, more so, as HPIL does not have price contracts with suppliers or customers. 

**Project risk associated with the solar project:** HPIL has received a letter of acceptance from Maharashtra State Electricity Distribution Company Ltd for the development of a 60 megawatt (MW) solar power generating station, which will be executed through its subsidiary, HPEL. The project has a capital commitment of Rs 66 crore, which will be funded by HPIL, and a term debt of Rs 194.66 crore has been sanctioned. 

The project is in an initial stage, with scheduled commercial operation date (COD) of December 2026, as per the sanction terms. Ability to execute the project within the planned timeline and budgeted cost will be closely monitored. Successful execution of the project will be crucial for achieving the company's growth objectives and expanding presence in the renewable energy segment. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

The liquidity profile will remain adequate with net cash accrual expected above Rs 100 crore per annum against yearly debt obligation of less than Rs 20 crore over the medium term. The cash credit and working capital demand loan were utilised moderately, at 65% on average in the 12 months through February 2026, while the vendor bill discounting facility was utilised 71% on average. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Increased utilisation of newly added capacities and better demand, resulting in heathy revenue and net cash accrual more than Rs 140 crore 

Limited reliance on debt and sustenance of strong liquidity. 

# **Downward factors** 

Large, debt-funded capital expenditure or stretch in the working capital cycle, leading to TOLTNW ratio above 1.5 times Steep fall in revenue or profitability on account of the temporary shutdown of the Perundurai unit 

Delay in completion of the solar project, resulting in cost overrun or substantial increase in debt 

# **<u>About the Group</u>** 

Incorporated in 2007 by Rupesh Kumar Gupta and his family members, HPIL manufactures MS sponge, billets and tubes, HR strips and scaffolding systems. The products are sold under the Hariom Pipes brand. The company is listed on the National Stock Exchange and Bombay Stock Exchange. 

HPEL was established on March 19, 2025, to undertake the development of a 60-MW solar power generating station. HPEL is a 80% subsidiary of HPIL. 

Metal Mart Private Limited was incorporated in fiscal 2026 and HPIL holds 70% stake in the company and is into trading of metal and steel allied products.
