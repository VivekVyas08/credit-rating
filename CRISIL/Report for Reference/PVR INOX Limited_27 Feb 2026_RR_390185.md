### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AA/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the non-convertible debentures (NCDs), commercial paper programme and bank facilities of PVR INOX Ltd (PVR). Crisil Ratings has also **withdrawn** its ratings on the proposed bank facilities of Rs.683.76 crore, as requested by the client, and in line with the policy of withdrawal. 

The reaffirmation factors in strong revenue rebound of 16–18% expected this fiscal, with operating margin of 12–13%, improving the return on capital employed (ROCE) ratio to 10–11% for fiscal 2026. This healthy performance is expected to continue on account of a strong content line up for calendar year 2026. Moreover, the company has shown improvement in operating margin to 13.9% in the first nine months of fiscal 2026; a strong rebound from 8.5% in the first nine months of fiscal 2025; in line with the expectations of Crisil Ratings. The improvement comes on the backdrop of increased occupancy to 26.6% in the first nine months of fiscal 2026 as against 23.9% in the first nine months of fiscal 2025, aided by significant uptick in gross box office collections across languages and steps taken by the company for improving operating efficiency such as re-releases, alternate content screening, live sports screening, passport programme, and focus on optimising the operating cost. 

Moreover, the company is actively reducing upfront capital expenditure (capex) by transitioning to new capital-efficient growth models such as FOCO (franchise-owned and company-operated — 100% investment by the developer) and assetlight (40–80% investment by the developer). Gross addition of 120-150 screens annually will be mostly undertaken through the new model. The company is likely to incur overall capex, including addition of new screens, maintenance of existing ones, and IT-driven initiatives, of Rs 300–400 crore for fiscals 2026 and 2027, funded through internal accrual of the company. 

The ratings also take cognizance of the sale of the brand 4700BC (Zee Maize Pvt Ltd) for an all-cash consideration of Rs 226.8 crore aimed to focus on core business. The proceeds were largely utilised towards prepayment of debt and net debt has thus reduced from Rs 952 crore as on March 31, 2025, to Rs 365 crore as on December 31, 2025. Crisil Ratings expects the net debt and gross debt to continue on the declining trajectory over the medium term. 

The reaffirmation also reflects the strong market position with an established brand, expectation of improvement in operating efficiency, led by synergistic benefits and premiumisation, and the above-average financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by exposure to content risks inherent in the film exhibition business and rising competition from alternate media and broadcasting mediums.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Strong market position with an established brand** 

PVR is the largest multiplex player in India, with 1,774 screens across 112 cities as on December 31, 2025. The second largest player in this segment is one-fourth the size of PVR. The merged entity has a geographically diversified screen portfolio across India. Going forward, the company will continue to benefit from its strong and established market position, via optimisation of the screen portfolio and better pricing power with stakeholders. 

# **Improving operating efficiency, aided by benefits of synergy and scale** 

PVR posted revenue of Rs 5,172 crore during the first nine months of fiscal 2026, a growth of 14.2% on-year aided by improved occupancy. The operating margin also increased to 13.9% in the first nine months of fiscal 2026 against 8.5% in the first nine months of fiscal 2025 on account of improved revenue and cost rationalisation measures taken by the company. Going forward, Ebitda (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation) margin is expected to be 12–13% for fiscals 2026 and 2027 due to stronger content line-up for calendar year 2026. 

# **Above-average financial risk profile expected to improve further over the medium term** 

Financial risk profile is characterised by continued reduction in net debt; which has reduced from Rs 952 crore as on March 31, 2025, to Rs 365 crore as December 31, 2025. The company is actively reducing upfront capex by transitioning to new capital-efficient growth models such as FOCO (franchise-owned and company-operated – 100% investment by the developer) and asset-light (40–80% investment by the developer). Gross addition of 120-150 screens annually will be mostly undertaken through the new model. The company is likely to incur overall capex, including addition of new screens, maintenance of existing ones, and IT-driven initiatives, of Rs 300–400 crore for fiscals 2026 and 2027, funded through internal accrual of the company. 

Furthermore, Crisil Ratings expects that the net debt and gross debt will continue a reducing trajectory over the medium term. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Exposure to risks inherent in the film exhibition business** 

The film exhibition business remains inherently susceptible to fluctuations in profitability, due to variability in performance of content. Such fluctuations impact multiplex players, given their high fixed cost, and high dependence on occupancy, which is driven by success of films. Extent of fluctuation in operating margin has increased post pandemic, vis-à-vis pre-pandemic, when even films with poor reviews delivered an average box office collection, supporting occupancy. The harsher impact of weak content on occupancy post-pandemic has made the film exhibition business much riskier. 

# **Rising competition from alternative media and broadcasting mediums** 

Other forms of entertainment and new content distribution platforms, including over-the-top, have emerged as competitors for content distribution, especially for small- and medium-budget movies and longer form content. This trend will pose challenges in sustaining profitability and growth for PVR. Hence, material changes to consumption patterns from current levels by audiences over longer term will be monitorable. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Cash and equivalents were around Rs 728 crore as on December 31, 2025. Cash accrual is expected to be healthy over Rs 800 crore for fiscal 2026, against term debt obligation of Rs 422 crore. Commercial paper of Rs 150 crore is due for redemption by the end of fiscal 2026. Fund-based limit of around Rs 245 crore remained unutilised as on December 31, 2025. Capex plans will be funded prudently via debt and internal accrual such that debt does not increase materially. 

# **<u>ESG Profile</u>** 

Crisil Ratings believes the ESG profile of PVR supports its strong credit risk profile. 

The media and entertainment sector has a low impact on the environment because of low greenhouse gas emissions in core operations and lower generation of hazardous waste. The sector has a social impact because of its large workforce. PVR has continuously focused on mitigating its environmental and social impact. 

# **Highlights:** 

Adoption of energy-efficient equipment and waste management protocols at cinemas. Green initiatives like solar power, water conservation and paperless ticketing 

ESG performance tracking and sustainability disclosures aligned with global frameworks 

As part of its corporate social responsibility (CSR) initiatives, PVR is constantly working in areas such as climate change and health and safety for women and children. 

The board of directors comprises 50% independent directors with a split in positions of Chairman and CEO, a healthy investor grievance redressal mechanism and extensive disclosures. 

There is growing importance of ESG among investors and lenders. PVR’s commitment to ESG principles will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence, given the high shareholding of foreign portfolio investors in the company. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes PVR will continue to benefit from its established market position and brand equity, while the financial risk profile should remain supported by healthy cash accrual. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Sustained increase in occupancy, leading to higher revenue and sustenance of operating margin above 16-18% and RoCE (net of cash) at 25-30% 

Sustained improvement in the financial risk profile 

# **Downward factors** 

Delayed traction in improvement of occupancy, with profitability and/or ROCE (net of cash) remaining lower than 11% and 12%, respectively 

Delayed deleveraging than expected 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

PVR was established in 1995 as a 60:40 joint venture (JV) between Priya Exhibitors Pvt Ltd and Village Roadshow Ltd (VRL), a world leader in the multiplex business. In 1995, PVR took a single-screen cinema hall, Anupam, in Saket, Delhi, on lease and converted it into a four-screen multiplex, which started operations in 1997 as PVR Anupam, and was the first multiscreen cineplex in India. As part of its global business strategy, VRL exited the JV in 2002. 

In November 2012, PVR acquired Cinemax, strengthening its presence in west India. Cinemax operated in 39 locations with 138 screens. This acquisition made PVR the largest multiplex operator in India. 

In May 2016, it completed the acquisition of 32 screens (29 operational and three upcoming) from DT Cinemas for Rs 433 crore. 

In January 2017, Warburg Pincus LLC acquired a 14% stake in PVR—9% from current shareholders (Multiples Private Equity Fund I Ltd) and 5% from the promoters. 

In August 2018, PVR acquired SPI Cinemas, adding 76 screens to its portfolio. In January 2023, the Mumbai Bench of the National Company Law Tribunal approved the proposed scheme of amalgamation of INOX with PVR, and the merger became effective as on February 6, 2023.
