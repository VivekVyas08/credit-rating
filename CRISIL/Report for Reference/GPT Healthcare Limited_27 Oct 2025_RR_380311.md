### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A-/Stable/Crisil A2+’ ratings on the bank facilities of GPT Healthcare Limited (GHL). Crisil Ratings has also **withdrawn** its rating on Rs 40.97 crore proposed fund-based bank limits as requested by the company. This is in line with Crisil Ratings' withdrawal policy. 

The ratings continue to reflect the company’s established market position, the extensive experience of its promoters in the healthcare industry, and healthy financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by the modest scale of operations and exposure to intense competition. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has evaluated the standalone business and financial risk profiles of GHL. 

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Established market position and extensive experience of the promoters:** Strong recall for the ILS Hospital brand, particularly in West Bengal and Tripura, through multiple hospitals has helped GHL sustain its scale of operations over the past few years. GHL operates five hospitals (three in Kolkata and one each in Agartala and Raipur). Its Raipur hospital commenced operations in May 2025. While the company has scaled up operations in all the hospitals through increase in average revenue per operating bed (ARPOB) and reduction in average length of stay (ALOS), bed occupancy was limited to 53% for fiscal 2025 and the first quarter of fiscal 2026. 

However, extensive experience of the promoters and strong internal controls have resulted in healthy return on capital employed (RoCE) and operating margins of 26-31% and 20-22%, respectively in three fiscals through March 31, 2025. Cash flow from operations has also been positive historically. Improvement in bed occupancy and timely ramp-up of operations at the recently commissioned Raipur hospital and upcoming asset-light hospitals in Jamshedpur (by the fourth quarter of fiscal 2028) should strengthen GHL’s market position. 

**Healthy financial risk profile:** Healthy networth of Rs 247 crore as on March 31, 2025, along with limited external borrowings, resulted in comfortable leverage ratios with gearing and total outside liabilities to total networth of 0.05 and 0.55 time, respectively. Debt protection metrices were adequate with interest coverage and net cash accrual to adjusted debt ratios of 50.37 and 3.64 times, respectively, for fiscal 2025. Planned capital expenditure of ~Rs 15 crore during fiscal 2026 (for Raipur hospital) and of ~Rs 60 crore during fiscals 2027 and 2028 (for setting up the Jamshedpur hospital), respectively, will be funded largely through internal accrual, thus limiting reliance on external debt over the medium term. However, any acquisition significantly leveraging the balance sheet will be closely monitored. 

### **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Modest scale of operations:** GHL reported 2% on-year growth in operating income to about Rs 407 crore in fiscal 2025, which continues to be modest in comparison with peers. The growth in fiscal 2025 was aided by improvement in ARPOB to Rs 37,180 from Rs 32,947 a year earlier driven by various specialty surgeries conducted across hospitals during the fiscal, while bed occupancy dropped to 53% (59% in fiscal 2024) due to short stay specialty surgeries (Salt Lake & Howrah hospital), political unrest in Bangladesh (Agartala hospital), wide-spread floods in Tripura (Agartala hospital) and R.G. Kar incident (Dumdum hospital). Despite muted scale, prudent internal control has helped operating margin remain healthy at 20-22% in the three fiscals through 2025. In the first quarter of fiscal 2026, operating margin was around 16% against over 

18% for the comparative period in the previous fiscal due to operating loss of about Rs 3-4 crore reported at the Raipur hospital pending breakeven. Improvement in bed occupancy and launch of new specialties driving revenue growth for existing hospitals, and timely commissioning of the upcoming hospital and ramp-up of operations at the recently commissioned hospital resulting in a significant increase in scale of operations, is a key rating sensitivity factor. 

**Exposure to intense competition:** The hospitals of GHL face competition from various large hospitals in their region of operation. Also, GHL faces regulatory risk inherent in the healthcare industry. Government policy on capping prices for medical procedures and devices may impact profitability as seen in the past. Furthermore, GHL generates revenue from West Bengal, Tripura and Raipur, rendering the company susceptible to increase in competition because of the entry of large hospital chain in these regions. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Adequate 

Bank limit utilisation was low, at 4% on average for the 12 months through September 2025. Cash profit before dividend payout is expected to be over Rs 80 crore against term debt obligation of Rs 5-6 crore over the medium term and the surplus will cushion liquidity. The current ratio was modest at 0.74 time as on March 31, 2024. The company declared a dividend payout of 25-40% in the three fiscals though 2025. Free cash and bank balance and liquid investments in mutual funds and debentures were around Rs 34 crore as on March 31, 2025. Low gearing and healthy networth support the company’s financial flexibility to withstand adverse conditions or downturn in the business. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes GHL will continue to benefit from its established market position and healthy financial position. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors:** 

Substantial growth in revenue, driven by rise in bed occupancy in existing hospitals and timely commission and ramp-up of operations in recently commissioned and upcoming hospitals, respectively, strengthening the market position with sustenance of operating margin around 20%. 

Continued healthy financial risk profile and financial flexibility 

# **Downward factors** 

Weakening business risk profile resulting in profit after tax falling below Rs 30 crore 

Larger-than-expected capex/acquisition or huge dividend payout weakening the financial risk profile. 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

GHL was incorporated in 1989 as Jibansatya Printing House Pvt Ltd by the Kolkata-based GPT group. The company was later renamed GPT Healthcare Pvt Ltd and was reconstituted as a public limited company with the present name in September 2021. It operates five mid-sized multispecialty hospitals with an aggregate capacity of 719 beds across Kolkata, Agartala and Raipur under the ILS Hospitals brand. Company’s operations are managed by Dr Om Tantia (Chairman), Mr Anurag Tantia (Whole-time Director) and other line of executives.
