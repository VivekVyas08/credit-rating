### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AAA/Stable' rating on the non-convertible debentures and enhanced bank facilities of NLC India Ltd (NLC). Crisil Ratings has **withdrawn** its rating on non-convertible debenture of Rs 500 crore (see 'Annexure - Details of rating withdrawn') on their redemption and on receipt of independent confirmation. This is in line with the Crisil Ratings policy on withdrawal of ratings. 

The rating continues to factor in NLC’s strategic importance to the Government of India (GoI), as reflected in the dominant government shareholding and the company being a nodal agency for lignite mining. The company has a strong business risk profile, backed by high operating efficiency, regulated cash flow and captive fuel availability for its power plants, and healthy financial risk profile owing to comfortable capital structure and liquidity. These strengths are partially offset by exposure to risks related to weak counterparties and implementation of large capacity expansion projects. 

During fiscal 2025, earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (Ebitda) margin increased to 31.7% from 29.1% during fiscal 2024, owing to higher power generation from existing plants and higher coal and lignite sales. However, the Ebitda margin decreased in the first quarter of fiscal 2026 to 24.4% (32.1% in the corresponding period of the previous fiscal) primarily due to boiler modifications in NLC’s TPS-II expansion plant as well as early onset of monsoon leading to lower power generation across its other plants. 

The financial risk profile is expected to remain healthy despite large planned capital expenditure (capex) over the medium term. While adjusted gearing will increase, it will remain within the Central Electricity Regulatory Commission (CERC) stipulated norm. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of NLC and its subsidiaries and joint ventures (JVs) due to the stated position of the management of providing complete financial and managerial support. 

In case of exigencies, NLC is expected to receive distress support from the government for timely servicing of debt, considering its strategic role in meeting the significant power requirement of several southern states and GoI’s 72.2% ownership in the company.

### **<u>Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description</u>** 

### **Strengths:** 

**Strategic importance to the government:** NLC is the nodal agency for lignite mining in India and is backed by its sizeable reserves. India had total measured proven lignite reserves of 7,964.43 million tonne (MT) with NLC having market share of more than 55% in lignite production. The company is a major provider of power for south Indian states and meets around 10% of the power requirement of Tamil Nadu. The government has granted Navratna status to NLC and is a dominant 

shareholder in the company. The government had provided guarantees for the foreign currency borrowing of NLC in the past and is likely to continue to offer need-based support. 

**Regulated cash flow under the classic two-part tariff structure:** On a consolidated basis, the company owns and operates thermal power plants with combined capacity of 5,300 MW. The plants have long-term power purchase agreements (PPAs) with a regulated two-part tariff structure. This ensures recovery of all fixed expenses — including debt obligation — and fixed return on equity based on the achievement of performance benchmarks mandated by CERC. Moreover, transfer pricing of lignite and coal is determined by CERC on cost plus service model. 

**Healthy operating efficiency and captive fuel availability:** Ownership of lignite mines mitigates risks related to availability of fuel. The captive lignite mines should be sufficient to meet fuel requirement for about 20 years. Fuel requirement for the coal-based plant of NLC Tamil Nadu Power Ltd (NTPL) is met from NLC’s mines in Talabira, Odisha. Moreover, commissioning of remaining two units (660 MW*2) of the plant of Neyveli Uttar Pradesh Power Ltd (NUPPL; JV with Uttar Pradesh Rajya Vidyut Utpadan Nigam Ltd) during fiscal 2026 will support cash flow over the medium term. 

NLC had operational solar and wind capacity of 1,431 MW as on March 31, 2025. These capacities are tied-up under medium-to-long-term PPAs, with adequate tariffs. Furthermore, the company is likely to commission 1,500-2,000 MW over the next two years and increase capacity to 10 GW by fiscal 2030, which will support higher accrual. 

Operating profitability increased to 31.7% during fiscal 2025 from 29.1% during fiscal 2024. However, it remains lower than 35-40% seen in the past. Lower operating profitability is largely owing to technical issues in the TPS-II and TPS-II expansion plants leading to the plants operating at lower than normative plant load factor of 80-85%. The company is undertaking modifications in these plants which are expected to be completed during fiscal 2026. 

**Healthy financial risk profile:** While gearing (including under construction debt) decreased to 1.03 times as on March 31, 2025, from 1.17 times a year earlier, it is expected to increase over the medium term due to additional borrowings for the ongoing capex. That said, the gearing is expected to be well within the CERC-stipulated norm of 2.33 times (debt-equity ratio of 70:30). The company plans sizeable capex of Rs 25,000-30,000 crore over fiscals 2026-2028 towards expanding its thermal and renewable energy (RE) portfolio to more than 10 gigawatt (GW) from 6.7 GW as on March 31, 2025. Despite the capex and expected regular dividend payout, the financial risk profile should remain healthy over the medium term backed by robust capital structure, strong liquidity and sizeable cash accrual. Larger-than-expected capex, adversely impacting the cash position, will be monitorable. 

# **Weaknesses:** 

**Exposure to counterparty risks:** The company is exposed to the risk of irregular cash flow due to the inherent weak credit risk profiles of key consumers, which are primarily state distribution companies. However, consolidated receivables (excluding unbilled revenue and bill discounting) declined significantly over the past four fiscals to 77 days as on March 31, 2025 (239 days as on March 31, 2021). This was mainly due to the Late Payment Surcharge Rules, 2022, under which the company is receiving part of receivables in instalments from multiple discoms, including Tamil Nadu Power Distribution Corporation Ltd (TNPDCL; earlier, Tamil Nadu Generation and Distribution Corporation Ltd, the generation and distribution arm of the erstwhile Tamil Nadu Electricity Board) as well as timely collections from other counterparties. 

However, 55-60% of the power generated is sold to TNPDCL, which accounted for around 65% of the receivables (including CERC recoverable from TNPDCL) as on June 30, 2025. The credit risk profile of the counterparty is weak as poor operating performance and absence of periodic tariff revision have led to substantial loss from operations. The counterparty risk is mitigated by payment security mechanisms, which include letter of credit and the Reserve Bank of India’s tripartite agreement. 

**Exposure to project implementation risks:** NLC is implementing large projects in the thermal, renewable and mining segments. The major ongoing projects are 1,980-MW coal-based thermal power plant under NUPPL (wherein Unit-1 of 660 MW was commissioned in December 2024 while Units II and III are expected to be commissioned in September and December 2025, respectively), 2,115 MW renewable power (wind and solar) projects and 9 MT coal capacity in South Pachwara, Jharkhand. Besides, there are large thermal (4,575 MW), renewable (6,564 MW) and mining (56.25 MT) projects in planning stage, which will be implemented over the medium term. 

The upcoming capacities will be insulated from demand and fuel supply risks due to the long-term PPAs and adequate fuel availability. However, NLC may face risks related to time and cost overruns (as seen in some of its newly commissioned and ongoing projects), primarily due to delay in obtaining clearances, supply of equipment and technical issues during the stabilisation phase. Also, one of the main projects, NUPPL has commissioned Unit I of 660 MW in December 2024 and the remaining two units are expected to be commissioned in September and December 2025, respectively, after multiple delays. Further delays may impact profitability depending on the extent of cost overrun allowed as a pass-through by CERC. Consequently, timely commissioning of the remaining units without time and cost overrun will be monitorable. 

# **<u>Liquidity: Superior</u>** 

Expected cash accrual of Rs 4,000-4,500 crore each in fiscals 2026 and 2027 will comfortably cover debt obligation aggregating Rs 4,000 crore over the two fiscals. NLC had liquid surplus of Rs 320 crore as on March 31, 2025, majority of 

which remained unencumbered. Liquidity is also supported by fund-based bank limit of around Rs 4,000 crore (drawing power of Rs 579 crore as on June 30, 2025), which remained unutilised over the 12 months through June 2025. Overall, cash accrual, cash and equivalent, and unutilised bank lines will sufficiently cover debt obligation, incremental capex and working capital requirement in fiscal 2026. 

# **<u>Environment, social and governance (ESG) profile</u>** 

Crisil Ratings believes NLC’s ESG profile supports its already strong credit risk profile. The power sector has a significant impact on the environment owing to high emissions, water consumption and waste generation. This is because generation of conventional power involves high dependence on natural resources, mainly coal. The sector has a social impact as its operations affect the local community and involve health hazards. NLC is focused on mitigating its environmental and social risks. 

# **ESG highlights:** 

- The company reported a marginal increase in Scope 1 and 2 emissions intensity (up ~1% on-year) to ~1,287 tCO2e/per million units of power generated (MU) in fiscal 2024. 

- As on March 31, 2025, the company’s generation capacity stood at 6.7 GW, of which ~21% comprised RE (solar and wind). It has set a target of 50% capacity share from RE assets by 2030 with RE capacity expanding to 10.11 GW. As part of its sustainability initiatives, the company is entering battery storage, green hydrogen, EV charging stations, among other businesses. 

- NLC has better-than-peers performance on lost time injury frequency rate (LTIFR) among workforce (~0.013 and ~0.065 for employees and workers, respectively), nil employee fatality, and relatively high share of female employees in the workforce (~9%). 

- Its governance structure is characterised by 10 board members, 30% of the board comprising independent directors, and presence of an investor grievance redressal mechanism and extensive disclosures. 

There is growing importance of ESG among investors and lenders. NLC’s commitment to ESG principles will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence, given the high share of market borrowings in its overall debt and access to both domestic and foreign markets. 

# **<u>Outlook: Stable</u>** 

Crisil Ratings believes NLC’s business risk profile will remain strong over the medium term, backed by efficient operations and fuel security. The financial risk profile is expected to remain healthy, driven by conservative gearing and adequate liquidity. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Downward factors** 

- Any change in the support philosophy of the government or any divestment below 51% government shareholding leading to change in control. 

- Significant weakening of the operating performance of power plants and delays in the implementation of capex impacting the business and financial risk profiles 

- Delay in recovery of dues from counterparties impacting liquidity 

Higher-than-expected capex or any sizeable debt-funded acquisition weakening the gearing to more than 2 times 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

NLC operates lignite and coal mines and thermal power stations in Neyveli and Tuticorin in Tamil Nadu; Barsingsar in Rajasthan; Uttar Pradesh and Odisha. It sells power primarily to the state utilities of Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh, Kerala, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh and the Union Territory of Puducherry. The company has four lignite mines and one coal block with combined mining capacity of 50.1 MTPA, and seven thermal power stations with combined generation capacity of 5,300 MW. In addition, NLC operates a 1,380.06-MW solar power plant and a 51-MW wind power plant. It was awarded the Navratna status in fiscal 2011. 

During fiscal 2025, operating income increased 13.2% to Rs 15,283 crore while profit after tax (PAT) stood at Rs 2,714 crore. During the first three months of fiscal 2026, operating income increased by 13.3% to Rs 3,826 crore and PAT stood at 839 crore.
