### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A/Stable/Crisil A1’ ratings on the bank facilities of Elin Electronics Ltd (EEL; part of the Elin group). 

The ratings continue to reflect the established market position of the Elin group and its healthy financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by the low operating profitability due to intense competition and moderate scale of operations. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of EEL and its wholly owned subsidiary, Elin Appliances Pvt Ltd (EAPL). This is because both these companies, collectively referred to as the Elin group, have strong business and financial linkages.

### **<u>Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description</u>** 

### **Strengths:** 

**Established market position:** The Elin group enjoys an established market position in the electronics appliances industry, which has helped the company develop healthy relations with top industry brands such as Philips, Signify, Bajaj, Havells among others and diversify its product mix into manufacturing of lights, motors, small appliances, and fans. In line with expectation, the group has achieved an operating income of Rs 1180 crore for fiscal 2025, which is expected to grow 1518% annually over the medium term despite challenges in realisations across the lighting and fans segment. In the ongoing fiscal, operating income is expected to witness organic expansion driven by the addition of new products such as TPW (table, pedestal and wall) fans, OFR (oil filled radiator) and OTG (oven-toaster-grill), and new customers such as Wipro, Usha domestically and exports to original equipment manufacturers in the USA. Demand is seasonal and accordingly growth is expected to come in the second half of fiscal 2026. Additions of new products and geographies shall continue to support revenue visibility over the medium term and the business risk profile shall remain supported by the established market position of the group. 

**Healthy financial risk profile:** Networth is expected to grow to Rs 550-570 crore as on March 31, 2026 from Rs 520-540 crore as on March 31, 2025 (Rs ~502.52 crore as on March 31, 2024); while accretion to reserve and moderate reliance on debt should lead to strong gearing of 0.05-0.1 time and total outside liabilities to adjusted networth ratio of 0.4-0.5 time as on March 31, 2026 against ~0.05 and ~0.36 time, respectively, a year earlier. Strong gearing demonstrates financial flexibility over the medium term. Debt protection metrics may continue to be healthy owing to adequate operating profitability, as indicated by interest coverage ratio of 6-7 times in fiscal 2025 and projected at more than 5.5 times over the medium term (~5.14 times for fiscal 2024); net cash accrual to adjusted debt ratio is expected at 1.5-2.0 times in fiscal 2026 and will improve further over the medium term. The strong financial risk profile, especially liquidity, will support the working capital requirements and capital expenditure (capex) of Rs ~90-100 crore undertaken for expansion in new territories such as Bhiwadi funded from internal accruals and investments and is likely to strengthen over the medium term owing to steady accretion to reserve and the absence of any further debt-funded capex. 

**Weaknesses:** 

**Low operating profitability due to intense competition:** The domestic consumer electronics market is intensely competitive. The entry of several large players over the past few years has affected the profitability of players such as the Elin group. Fluctuations in raw material prices accentuate the pressure on profitability because of the inability of players to pass on cost increases to customers. Operating margin improved marginally to ~4.4% in fiscal 2025 (from 4% fiscal 2024) due to backward integration. However, the return on capital employed (RoCE) has been lower than expectation of 10-12% due to slow ramp up of operations for new products and lower-than-expected absorption of fixed overheads such as employee costs. However, with alternative power sources in place with a new power purchase agreement of 4MWh and expected launch of new product lines of OTG and OFR to begin in the second half of fiscal 2026, the operating margin and ROCE are expected to increase to 6-7% and 8-10%, respectively, in the medium term. The timely revival of the operating margin to historic levels of 6-8% and its sustenance thereafter shall remain a key rating sensitivity factor. 

**Modest scale of operations:** Operating income posted a compound annual growth rate of ~3% for the three years through 2025; revenue was ~Rs 1180 crore in fiscal 2025. Intense competition from both organised and unorganised players (including Chinese products) and increased volume have led to softening of LED (light-emitting-diode) prices resulting in stagnant revenue growth in the industry over the years.   With ramp up of operations in new segments of OTG, OFR and export markets and healthy demand from the end user industries, operating income is expected to improve to Rs 1350-1500 crore in fiscal 2026. Sustained improvement in the scale of operations, amid stable operating margin, will therefore remain a key rating sensitivity factor. 

# **<u>Liquidity: Strong</u>** 

EEL has adequate liquidity driven by expected net cash accrual of Rs 50-70 crore with no debt repayment obligations along with cash and equivalents of Rs 80-100 crore over the medium term. EEL also has access to fund-based limits of ~Rs 78 crore, which have been utilised at a mere ~6.71% for the 12 months through April 2025. With no term loans proposed to be undertaken and no term debt obligation, Crisil Ratings believes EEL’s internal accrual, cash and equivalents and unutilised bank line will be sufficient to meet incremental working capital requirement. 

# **<u>Outlook: Stable</u>** 

The operating income and margin of the Elin group will continue to benefit from new products and customers over the medium term. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **<u>Upward factors:</u>** 

Sustained improvement in operating income supported by volumetric growth along with sustenance of operating margin at 7-8% leading to higher-than-expected net cash accrual. 

Efficient working capital management and sustenance of healthy financial risk profile and strong liquidity profile. 

# **<u>Downward factors:</u>** 

Decline in operating income or operating margin below 4% on a sustained basis leading to lower-than-expected net cash accrual. 

Working capital intensive operations or significant debt-funded capex weakening the financial risk profile of the company, particularly liquidity. 

# **<u>About the Group</u>** 

Incorporated in 1982, EEL is a part of the Sethia group run by Sethia family, which had 57.4% shareholding in the company as on December 31, 2022. EEL began operations by manufacturing electric motors and diversified into making tape recorders. It currently manufactures universal motors, electrical appliances, LEDs, sheet metals and mouldings, among others. The company has a facility in Ghaziabad, Uttar Pradesh; Baddi, Himachal Pradesh; and Goa. It is listed on the Bombay Stock Exchange and the National Stock Exchange. 

EAPL was incorporated in August 2002 and manufactures small kitchen appliances (mixer grinders, juicer mixer grinder, bar blenders, electric iron, toasters), personal care products (hair straightener and dryer), lighting fixtures and modular switches at its facility in Baddi.
