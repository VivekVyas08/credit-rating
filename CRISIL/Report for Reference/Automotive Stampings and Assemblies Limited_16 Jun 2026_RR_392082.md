### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A-/Stable/Crisil A2+’ ratings on the bank facilities of Automotive Stampings and Assemblies Limited (ASAL). 

The ratings continue to reflect the strong support ASAL receives from its parent, Tata AutoComp Systems Ltd (TACO; rated ‘Crisil AA+/Stable/Crisil A1+’) and the steadily improving business risk profile of the company. These strengths are partially offset by high product and customer concentration in revenue, modest financial risk profile and cyclicality in the automotive industry. 

During fiscal 2026, revenue grew by 15% to Rs 891 crore from Rs 775 crore in fiscal 2025. This scale of operations increased owing to volume growth of primary customers, Tata Motors Ltd (TML, rated ‘Crisil AA+/Stable/Crisil A1+’), Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles Ltd (TMPVL, rated ‘Crisil AA+/Stable/Crisil A1+’) and TACO group companies due to increase in demand for passenger vehicles (PV) and commercial vehicles (CV) post GST rationalisation. ASAL books around 64% of its revenue from the PV segment followed by CV and three wheelers. Increasing share of value-added products such as battery trays for electric vehicles (EV), aluminium cooling tubes, fabrication parts, and newly-launched products like seating structures are likely to aid revenue growth. Increasing share of business with existing customers and addition of new customers for newly launched products, with steady demand across various auto segments, is expected to support topline growth of 7–8% in the medium term. Operating margin marginally rose to 6.6% in fiscal 2026 from 6.2% a year earlier backed by operating leverage. Operating margin is expected to sustain at around 7% over the medium term, supported by healthy proportion of value-added products and better absorption of fixed overheads, with rising scale of operations. 

The financial risk profile, while it continues to be modest, has been improving over the past four fiscals owing to consistent improvement in the operating performance. Networth increased to Rs 36 crore as on March 31, 2026, from Rs 8 crore as on March 31, 2025, with steady accretion to reserve. External borrowing stood lower at Rs 70 crore (including inter-corporate deposits [ICDs] from TACO) as on March 31, 2026, in comparison to Rs 90 crore a year earlier owing to improvement in net working capital cycle (negative 2 days during fiscal 2026 in comparison to 11 days in fiscal 2025). Hence, overall borrowings (including lease liabilities) stood at Rs 124 crore as of March 2026 as against Rs 149 crore as of March 2025. Total borrowings (net of cash) was lower at Rs.105 crore as on March 31, 2026, in comparison to Rs.144 crore as on March 31, 2025. Over the medium term, the financial risk profile will continue to be modest, albeit improving consistently on the back of healthy cash generation of Rs 40–50 crore per annum vis-à-vis yearly capital expenditure (capex) of Rs 40-50 crore, thus limiting the dependance on external long-term borrowings. Furthermore, key debt protection metrics are expected to stay comfortable, as indicated by interest coverage ratio of over 4 times (4.25 times during fiscal 2026). Liquidity is supported by cash surplus of Rs 19 crore as on March 31, 2026. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has evaluated the standalone business and financial risk profiles of ASAL. Crisil Ratings has applied its parent notch-up framework to factor in strong operational, financial and managerial support from TACO, which holds 75% equity stake in ASAL and has a track record of providing financial support by way of unsecured loans and ICDs. 

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Strong business and financial support from TACO and other group companies:** 

ASAL is a key supplier of sheet-metal stampings, welded assemblies and modules for PV and CV manufactured by TMPVL and TML, respectively. TML is one of the leading CV players, while TMPVL has the highest market share of around 40% in the passenger EV segment and is a leading player in the PV segment, with market share of around 14% as of March 2026. TMPVL and TML will maintain their market share over the medium term, aided by improved product portfolio and new product launches. ASAL will benefit from the growth of TML and TMPVL. 

TACO has provided inter-corporate debt to ASAL to ensure timely debt servicing, which is expected to continue, if required. 

# **Improving operating performance:** 

The operating performance of ASAL has consistently been improving over the past six fiscals barring fiscal 2025 because of increased production witnessed in TML and TMPVL owing to positive demand sentiments. Over fiscals 2020 to 2021, the automotive industry was in a downcycle on account of demand contraction owing to introduction of BS-VI emissions norms and the pandemic-induced lockdowns and disruptions, such as curtailment of consumption and infrastructure spending, and labour shortage. Economic activity resumed in fiscal 2022, leading to a revival in demand for automotives, with TML and TMPVL leading the way through new product launches, revamped product portfolio, first-mover advantage in EVs, and high brand value in commercial vehicles. In addition, owing to the expansion of ASAL’s product portfolio towards new valueadded products, such as battery trays, aluminium cooling tubes and seating structures, also resulted in increasing share of business with TML, TMPVL and TACO. Thus, while revenue increased from Rs 339 crore during fiscal 2021 to Rs 891 crore during fiscal 2026, operating margin has expanded to 6.6% from breakeven over the same period. 

Turnover will continue to grow in line with the increase in production by the principal customers, and the operating margin is expected sustain at around7.0% supported by improving operating leverage. The impact on revenue and profitability due to lower discretionary spending, escalating costs of essential raw materials and rising freight expenses owing to ongoing geopolitical instability will remain monitorable. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Susceptibility to inherent cyclicality in the auto industry:** 

ASAL caters to the automotive industry, which is highly sensitive to economic cycles. Automotive original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) were adversely hit in fiscal 2020 owing to drop in consumer spending, weak monsoon, and imposition of BS-VI emission norms, and in fiscal 2021 by disruptions because of Covid-19. Growth recovered only from the second half of fiscal 2021 and performance remains vulnerable to economic downturns. Furthermore, change in regulations can have a bearing on the demand like GST rationalisation in fiscal 2026 translated to rise in demand in the second half of the year. 

# **High customer concentration in revenue:** 

ASAL derived around 78% of its revenue in fiscal 2026 from TMPVL, TML and other TACO group companies, resulting in revenue concentration risk. The same was upwards of 80% in fiscal 2025. The company is engaging with new clients to reduce customer concentration as well  as diversifying its product base. 

# **Weak, albeit improving, financial risk profile:** 

The company’s adjusted networth which remained negative till fiscal 2024 due to continued loss, has remained positive since fiscal 2025. While the overall debt (including lease liabilities) has lowered to Rs 124 crore as of March 2026 from Rs 149 crore as of March 2025 with improvement in net working capital cycle, the gearing continues to be elevated at 3.5 times as on March 31, 2026, albeit substantially lower than 17.9 times a year earlier. The networth is expected to improve over the medium term with steady accretion to reserve, which coupled with stable external debt borrowings will translate to gearing remaining below 2.2 times. Working capital requirement is expected to remain modest on account of sustenance of a low net working capital cycle. 

Over the medium term, the financial risk profile will remain modest, albeit improving consistently on the back of healthy cash generation of Rs 40–50 crore per annum being adequately matched to yearly capex of Rs 40-50 crore. Debt protection metrics are expected to remain healthy over the medium term, with interest coverage ratio of over 4 times (4.25 times during fiscal 2026). 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Adequate 

The company has cash surplus of Rs 19 crore as on March 31, 2026. This, coupled with expected annual net cash accrual of Rs 40–50 crore, shall be adequate to cover yearly capex spends of Rs 50 crore, and shortfall, if any, will be bridged through debt from the parent company or group entities. The company has no long-term debt, hence the repayment obligation remains nil. Furthermore, the company has access to fund-based limits of Rs 136.5 crore, which has an average utilisation of 61% based on the available drawing power for the 12 months through March 2026. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes the business risk profile of ASAL will continue to be supported by growing demand for auto components from its key customers as well as gradually diversifying product basket. The financial risk profile will stay modest, albeit improving with rising profitability and stable external borrowings. Furthermore, ASAL would continue to benefit from timely funding support extended by TACO.

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

ASAL was set up as JBM Tools Ltd (JBM) by SK Arya and Associates (SKAA) in March 1990 and got its current name in August 2003. ASAL manufactures sheet-metal stampings, welded assemblies and modules for passenger cars and CV, largely for TML and TMPVL. The company has manufacturing facilities in Pune, Maharashtra; and Pantnagar, Uttarakhand. 

ASAL went public in March 1994, and TACO, a Tata group company, became a joint venture (JV) partner in 1997. In April 2002, SKAA exited the JV and transferred its entire holding to TACO and Tata Industries Ltd (TIL; rated 'Crisil AAA/Stable'). 

In February 2007, TACO entered into an agreement with Gestamp Servicios S L (Gestamp), under which both the companies were to hold equal equity stakes in ASAL. Consequently, Gestamp acquired 0.01% stake through an open offer, and TACO transferred 37.49% of its stake in ASAL to Gestamp. In February 2007, TACO reduced its stake to 37.5% (same as Gestamp), while the remaining shares were owned by the public and others. With the purchase of Gestamp's stake in December 2010, TACO now holds 75% stake in ASAL. 

# **<u>About the parent</u>** 

TACO was promoted by the Tata group in 1995, and it operates as the vehicle for the group's ventures in the auto components business. TACO is owned by the Tata group companies with Tata Sons holding 38.25%, TIL holding 34.40%, and TML holding 26.00%. TACO's own standalone operations include manufacture of auto plastic products and sheetmoulded composite parts. Furthermore, TACO offers services in engineering and supply chain management and provides centralised corporate services to group companies. The TACO group currently operates in various products through multiple domestic subsidiaries and operating JVs. Furthermore, the TACO group has an overseas operating subsidiary in China and an overseas holding company. The group operates 66 manufacturing facilities strategically located across India, North America, Latin America, Europe and China. TACO has carried out three large acquisitions since March 2025 including Artifex Interior Systems Pvt Ltd, IAC Slovakia and IAC Sweden entities among others. The recent overseas acquisitions have reinforced diversification and deepened TACO's presence in interior systems
