### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has revised its outlook on the long-term bank facilities and corporate credit rating of Astra Microwave Products Ltd (AMPL) to **‘Positive’** from ‘Stable’ while reaffirming the rating at ‘ **Crisil A’** . The rating on the short-term bank facilities has been reaffirmed at ‘Crisil A1’. 

The outlook revision reflects the improvement in the business risk profile of the company supported by healthy operating performance and increased order book. Revenue is expected to grow at 13-14% over the medium term, providing revenue visibility. The first half of fiscal 2026 saw ~8% on-year revenue growth. Ability to secure new orders and manage project execution within timelines will remain monitorable. Operating margin rose to 21.4% in the first half of fiscal 2026 from 19% in the first half of fiscal 2025 and 13.8% in the first half of fiscal 2024. The operating margin is expected at 24-25% over the medium term, driven by improving product mix and favourable geographical mix, with ~90% of revenue coming from the domestic market, which has higher margin. A healthy order book translating into revenue growth and comfortable profitability is likely to result in higher cash accrual over the medium term. 

The financial risk profile was healthy, supported by comfortable capital structure and debt protection metrics. The financial risk profile is expected to improve steadily over the medium term supported by improvement in revenue and profitability, resulting in higher accretion to reserve and improved debt protection metrics. Leverage ratios, such as gearing and total outside liabilities to tangible networth, are expected at less than 0.5 time and 1 time, respectively, as on March 31, 2026 (0.39 time and 0.68 time, respectively, as on March 31, 2025). Timely execution of orders and improvement in profitability will remain monitorables. 

Liquidity remains comfortable despite sizeable working capital requirement, with Rs 100 crore of unencumbered cash and equivalent as on September 30, 2025. Expected annual net cash accrual of Rs 160-200 crore (Rs 170 crore in fiscal 2025) will sufficiently cover yearly debt obligation of Rs 17-19 crore from fiscal 2026. Annual capital expenditure (capex) of Rs 4050 crore for the near to medium term will be funded through a prudent mix of debt and equity. Also, equity raise of Rs 174 crore in the next 12-18 months through private placement of warrants, of which Rs 43 crore has been already raised, will also aid the liquidity. 

The ratings continue to reflect the established market position of AMPL in the microwave radio frequency (RF) applications industry, its strategic importance as a qualified vendor for defence research establishments, strong customer relationships, healthy order pipeline and comfortable financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by large working capital requirement, susceptibility to risks inherent in tender-based business and long gestation period of projects. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profile of AMPL, Astra Defense Technologies Pvt Ltd (erstwhile Bhavyabhanu Electronics Pvt Ltd), Aelius Semiconductors Pte Ltd (ASPL) and Astra Space Technologies Pvt Ltd 

(ASTPL). These entities, collectively referred to as the AMPL group, have common ownership and management and strong business synergies. Also, Crisil Ratings has moderately consolidated the joint venture, Astra Rafael Comsys Pvt Ltd, owing to operational and financial linkages.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Established market position in the defence segment, supported by strong in-house capability in the microwave RF applications domain, reputed clientele and growth prospects for the sector** 

The AMPL group derives strength from its in-house capability to provide customised microwave RF solutions. It has five state-of-the-art facilities around Hyderabad, with research and development (R&D) capability for microwave RF applications, test equipment and environment chambers for space applications. It has set up an R&D facility in Bengaluru to manufacture radars. Over the years, the group has diversified its portfolio by providing microwave applications in the space and civil telecommunication segments. 

It has longstanding relationships with customers and is recognised as a qualified vendor by defence research establishments. Key customers include various defence labs under the Ministry of Defence and DRDO (Defence Research and Development Organisation), Bharat Dynamics Ltd, Bhart Electronics Ltd and Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). Its laboratories for testing space applications are acknowledged by the ISRO. The group has taken steps to move up the value chain from supplying components to systems and system of subsystems, and has identified growth areas such as SATCOM (satellite communication) systems, wind profiler radars, ground surveillance radars, doppler weather radars and anti-drone systems. The company has set up a new facility in Bengaluru to cater to space technologies (such as satellites). Extensive and continued government focus on indigenisation of the defence sector with initiatives such as Make in India and import embargo augurs well for the group. 

# **Healthy order pipeline providing sound revenue visibility** 

As on September 30, 2025, the company had orders worth Rs 1,916 crore which are to be executed over the next 18-24 months. Orders from the domestic market account for more than 90% of the order book, with majority coming from the defense sector (over 70%). The company’s management has been focusing on increasing domestic orders and execution as these enjoy higher profitability compared with export orders. Domestic revenue contribution stood at 90% in fiscal 2025 (88% in the first half of fiscal 2026), compared with 68% in fiscal 2024, and is expected to remain high at 85-90% over the medium term, driven by healthy orders from domestic players. AMPL is focusing on expanding orders and revenue contribution from space projects, for which the company has set up a wholly owned subsidiary with a dedicated team. Furthermore, orders will continue to high over the medium term owing to government initiatives, such as Atmanirbhar Bharath and Make in India, and the announcement of positive indigenous list for defence products. 

Revenue grew 8% on-year in the first half of fiscal 2026 and is expected to grow 13-15% in fiscal 2026 driven by domestic orders from the defense segment. Over 60% of the revenue comes in the second half of the fiscal. The defence segment now contributes over 80% of the revenue. The order book was healthy at Rs 1,916 crore as on September 30, 2025, providing revenue visibility for the next 18-24 months. Over the medium term, revenue is expected to grow by 14-15% onyear driven by domestic defense orders. Operating margin was healthy at 21.4% in the first half of fiscal 2026 (19% for the first half of fiscal 2025) and is expected to be healthy at 24-25% for the full fiscal. Return on capital employed (RoCE) is expected to remain healthy at 15% over the medium term (18.6% in fiscal 2025). 

# **Comfortable financial risk profile** 

The financial risk profile remains healthy, as reflected in the comfortable capital structure and debt protection metrics. The financial risk profile is backed by strong networth of ~Rs 1,100 crore and low gearing of 0.39 time as on March 31, 2025. Networth is expected to improve to above ~Rs 1,400 crore in the near term aided by equity raise through issue of warrants of Rs 174 crore and healthy accrual. The first tranche of Rs 43 crore through the warrants issue has been already raised. The company has maintained total outside liabilities to adjusted networth (TOLANW) ratio below 1 time despite large working capital requirement. The TOLANW ratio is expected below 1 time over the medium term on account of healthy operating profit and steady accrual. Debt protection metrics will likely remain healthy, with interest coverage ratio above 5 times over the medium term. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Large working capital requirement:** Longer gestation period has led to sizeable inventory and receivables owing to a large number of tests and approvals required from customers before clearance of bill. Also, as AMPL has moved up the value chain, supplying more critical and complex systems and subsystems, the inventory and receivables have risen. Gross current assets (GCAs; net of cash) increased to over 500 days as on March 31, 2025, driven by inventory of 286 days and receivables of 273 days. The GCAs (net of cash) are expected ~500 days over the medium term because of increasing complex domestic orders. 

**Susceptibility to risks inherent in tender-based business and long gestation period of projects:** The business depends on successfully bidding for tenders from defence public sector undertakings and research establishments. Establishments such as DRDO invite tenders from qualified vendors for their R&D requirement and commence bulk 

production on successful completion of product development. Long-term revenue visibility is driven by the success of R&D projects at DRDO and mass production. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Adequate 

Expected cash accrual of Rs 170-200 crore over the medium term will sufficiently cover yearly debt obligation of Rs 18-19 crore till fiscal 2028. The company has access to working capital limit of Rs 435 crore, which was utilised 91% on average in the six months through May 2025. AMPL has moderate capex plans of Rs 40-50 crore in the near term, which will be funded through a mix of debt and equity. Free cash and equivalent of Rs 110 crore as on September 30, 2025, aids the liquidity. The working capital requirement is largely funded through internal accrual, customer advances and external debt. Bank limit utilisation remains monitorable. Moreover, equity of Rs 174 crore in fiscal 2026, of which Rs 43 crore has been already raised, will further aid the liquidity and will be used for expansion and working capital purposes. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Positive 

The AMPL group will benefit from continuous improvement in the business risk profile supported by healthy operating performance and increased order book. The financial risk profile is expected to be healthy, driven by comfortable capital structure and debt protection metrics. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors:** 

Higher-than-expected ramp-up in revenue and operating profitability leading to RoCE of 15-16% on a sustained basis Improvement in the working capital cycle 

Sustenance of healthy financial risk profile 

# **Downward factors:** 

- Revenue degrowth, due to delay in execution of existing contracts, or decline in operating margin impacting the business risk profile 

- Weakening of the financial risk profile on account of higher-than-anticipated debt or further stretch in the working capital cycle, with interest coverage ratio below 3.5 times 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

AMPL was incorporated as a private limited company in 1991 and reconstituted as a public limited company in 1993. It is promoted by Mr P A Chitrakar, Ms C Pramelamma and Mr B Malla Reddy. The company designs, develops and manufactures customised subsystems and components for microwave communication systems used in defence, space and telecommunication sectors. 

In fiscal 2014, AMPL floated Astra Defense Technologies Pvt Ltd (erstwhile Bhavyabhanu Electronics Pvt Ltd), which is 100% owned as a captive supplier of raw material for overseas orders. In fiscal 2015, AMPL floated the 100% owned ASPL in Singapore as a supplier of monolithic microwave integrated circuit (MMIC) products for semiconductors. In fiscal 2019, AMPL set up a joint venture, Astra Rafael Comsys Pvt Ltd, with Rafael Advanced Defence Systems for production of communication systems and subsystems for defence. AMPL has incorporated ASTPL as a wholly owned subsidiary, which will cater to the requirement of space and satellite technologies.
