### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A-/Stable/Crisil A2+’ ratings on the bank facilities of Saatvik Green Energy Ltd (Saatvik Green). 

The ratings reflect the increase in Saatvik Green’s scale of operations, adequate capacity utilisation, successful equity infusion on account of initial public offering (IPO) and healthy operating margin, aided by robust domestic demand for solar photovoltaic (PV) modules following favourable government policies. The strengths are partially offset by exposure to intense competition, susceptibility to regulatory changes and volatility in raw material prices, and risks related to timely execution and stabilisation of large upcoming projects. 

The company achieved consolidated revenue of Rs 2,189 crore in fiscal 2025 (Rs 1,097 crore for fiscal 2024), up ~100% on-year, with 70-80% on-year growth expected in fiscal 2026. The growth is backed by significant ramp-up in capacity, from 0.6 gigawatt (GW) in fiscal 2023 to 4.8 GW as of September 2025, of which ~4.2 GW is under wholly owned subsidiary, Saatvik Solar Industries Pvt Ltd (Saatvik Solar; formerly known as S Cleantech Renewables Pvt Ltd), which was incorporated in April 2024. Saatvik Green maintained healthy effective capacity utilisation of ~80% for fiscal 2025, which is expected to continue considering strong order book of over Rs 6,600 crore as of January 2026. In addition, government initiatives to increase adoption of solar energy, such as reimposition of the Approved List of Module Manufacturer (ALMM) policy from April 1, 2024, imposition of basic customs duty (BCD) and schemes such as PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojna, Kisan Urja Suraksha Utthan Mahabhiyan (KUSUM), proposed Approved List of Cell Manufacturers (ALCM) from June 2026, should ensure strong demand for domestic module and cell manufacturers. 

Saatvik Green’s operating margin rose to 16.2% in fiscal 2025 from ~14.4% in fiscal 2024, up from 4.5% in fiscal 2023, led by economies of scale and substantial decline in prices of upstream components such as polysilicon and solar cells, and execution of high-margin engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC) orders. Solar cell prices dropped by over 50% on-year in fiscal 2025, led by global overcapacities. The operating margin should sustain and remain healthy at 15-17% over the medium term. 

Saatvik Green raised Rs 700 crore through a fresh issue by way of an IPO and got listed on September 26, 2025. Of the IPO proceeds, Rs 477 crore will be utilised for setting up 4-GW solar PV module capacity at Odisha, Rs 176 crore for repayment of term debt and the balance for general corporate purposes. 

Saatvik Green is undertaking greenfield capacity expansion in subsidiary, Saatvik Solar, in Odisha. In Phase 1, it plans to add 4 GW module capacity (by the first quarter of fiscal 2027) and 2.4-GW backward integrated cell line capacity (by the third quarter of fiscal 2027). In Phase 2, additional 2.4-GW cell capacity is planned to be added post successful commercialisation of Phase 1 capex, estimated by the end of fiscal 2028. Phase 1 capex should entail a cost of Rs 1,850 crore, funded through debt of ~Rs 1,295 crore (sanctioned in September 2025), IPO proceeds of Rs 477 crore and the balance through internal accrual. Phase 2 capex is estimated at ~Rs 1,300 crore and will likely be funded through debt and own funds in a ratio of 70:30. 

Despite the debt-heavy capex, the financial risk profile of Saatvik Green is expected to remain comfortable with interest coverage expected at 7-8 times and debt to earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (Ebitda) ratio below 

2.5 times over the medium term. The company repaid its consolidated term debt of Rs 176 crore through IPO proceeds in October 2025. Net cash accrual of Rs 400-800 crore expected over fiscals 2026 and 2027 should suffice to meet negligible debt obligation of up to Rs 20 crore, with majority of the debt repayment lined up from fiscal 2028 due to moratorium of 18 months. Moreover, estimated accrual of over Rs 900 crore post fiscal 2027 will suffice to meet the expected debt obligation of Rs 250-300 crore per annum, considering 70% debt funding for Phase 2 capex as well.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Significant growth in scale of operations and healthy operating efficiency** 

Having commenced operations in fiscal 2017, Saatvik Green has demonstrated significant ramp-up in operations. Revenue rose to Rs 2,189 crore in fiscal 2025 from Rs 151 crore in fiscal 2021 and is estimated to cross Rs 3,500 crore in fiscal 2026, led by a bigger order book and increased capacities with healthy utilisation. The growth is led by robust domestic demand, along with favourable government policies. The revenue growth momentum is likely to sustain over the medium term with orders worth over Rs 6,600 crore (~4.8 GW) as of January 2026 providing strong revenue visibility. The order book is ~100% of the company’s current installed capacity. Post-completion of capex in Odisha, the company will have total module capacity of 8.8 GW by the end of the first quarter of fiscal 2027, and cell manufacturing capacity from the third quarter of fiscal 2027, although stabilisation may take a few more months and will be monitorable. All capacity additions are of Topcon modules, which is the latest technology offering higher efficiency. 

Operating margin improved to 16.2% in fiscal 2025 from ~4.5% in fiscal 2023, led by economies of scale and decline in prices of key inputs such as polysilicon and solar cells. Return on capital employed remained healthy at 56% in fiscal 2025 and is expected to remain healthy despite moderating to 25-30% over the medium term because of capacity expansions lined up and the time needed for stabilisation of operations. 

# **Diversified segments and client base** 

Saatvik Green caters to reputed clients across segments. Steady focus on adding customers has led to high growth in the order book. While ~70% of its sales are to utility players and ~25% to retail and commercials and industrial (C&I) consumers, ~3% comes from EPC and the balance ~1% from exports. Although the top five customers contributed ~50% to revenue in fiscal 2025, the company has added customers every year, depicting continuous diversification in the clientele. Saatvik Green has also diversified its product portfolio by adding products such as solar pumps, battery energy storage systems (BESS) and inverters from fiscal 2026 and plans to move into round-the-clock projects across the solar value chain; this will account for 10-15% of revenue. 

# **Favourable demand outlook for the solar industry** 

Amid the growing emphasis on the use of solar power in India, Saatvik Green is well-positioned to benefit from the government’s long-term plans to increase generation from renewable sources to 500 GW, of which 280 GW will be solar. Applicable from April 2022 and simplified in February 2025, the BCD of 20% on import of cells with Agriculture Infrastructure and Development Cess (AIDC) of 7.5%, and 20% on modules with AIDC of 20%; and reimposition of ALMM for government projects and projects using open access or net-metering, along with incentivising domestic players under the Production Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme will make domestic modules more cost competitive vis-à-vis imported ones. Governmentapproved schemes such as KUSUM, Central Public Sector Undertaking (CPSU) and the rooftop scheme (PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana) are also driving demand. Furthermore, the Ministry of Renewable Energy has plans for approved list of models and manufacturers for solar PV cells (ALCM) from June 2026, wherein, all modules approved under ALMM will be required to use cells from the ALCM list. Furthermore, all projects where ALMM is applicable also need to use cells from the ALCM list. This will support demand for domestic cells. The government also plans to introduce such policies for other upstream components such as wafers and ingots, which shall further solidify the domestic supply chain. 

# **Adequate financial risk profile** 

The financial risk profile remains comfortable, with networth rising to Rs 1,203 crore as on September 30, 2025, from Rs 338 crore as on March 31, 2025, on account of successful fund raising by way of IPO and increased scale and profitability. Despite debt-funded capex, the debt protection metrics should remain comfortable, with interest coverage and net cash accrual to adjusted debt ratio estimated at 7-8 times and 0.4-0.50 time, respectively, over the medium term. Gross current assets are expected to remain high at 200-220 days, led by large inventory, especially in the last quarter of the fiscal, when majority of sales take place. Net cash accrual to debt obligation ratio should be comfortable at 3-4 times, despite the debtfunded capex. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Susceptibility to volatility in commodity prices and foreign exchange (forex) rates and global trade uncertainties** 

Profitability remains vulnerable to sharp fluctuations in raw material prices, which form 75-80% of the operating income. While prices of key inputs such as polysilicon and solar cells declined significantly in fiscal 2025, they are dependent on 

global factors, a key rating sensitivity factor. Saatvik Green also remains exposed to fluctuations in forex rates as majority of raw material is imported. 

Also, global trade is vulnerable to frequent changes in trade restrictions, as indicated by the US tariffs, which are unfavourable for the domestic solar industry as more than 95% of solar exports (accounting for 20-25% of domestic solar capacity) are to the US. Although Saatvik Green has negligible exports and strong order book, any impact of the US tariffs leading to a supply glut in the domestic market will be monitorable. 

# **Increased competitive intensity** 

Saatvik Green is exposed to increasing competition from domestic as well as imported modules, given the large capacity additions planned in the domestic market to meet increasing demand. Indian manufacturers face competition from Chinese imports. Prices of modules and cells have dipped significantly in China due to overcapacity led by supply glut on account of global trade imbalances. 

BCD (implemented from April 2022, simplified in February 2025) of 20% on import of cells with AIDC of 7.5%, and 20% on modules with AIDC of 20%, makes Indian players cost-competitive. That said, cost of inputs and ability to pass on changes in cost to customers will be monitorable, considering the proposed ALCM for cell manufacturing from June 2026, which could make cells manufactured in India costlier. 

# **Exposure to project execution and stabilisation risk** 

Saatvik Green is vulnerable to timely execution and stabilisation of new projects, more specifically the cell line capacity, which is being undertaken for the first time by the company. Timely completion of the project and commensurate ramp-up of expanded capacities will be monitorable. That said, Saatvik Green’s record of successful execution of capacity expansion lends support — the company expanded module line from 0.6 GW in fiscal 2023 to 4.8 GW as of September 2025 (of which 4.2 GW is in Saatvik Solar). 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Adequate 

Net cash accrual is expected over Rs 400 crore in fiscal 2026 and at Rs 700-900 crore per annum over the medium term, and should suffice to cover term debt obligation of Rs 250-300 crore from fiscal 2028 and fund part of the ongoing capex and working capital requirement. Fund-based bank limit utilisation averaged 78% over the 12 months through December 2025, with unutilised bank lines of Rs 363 crore available as of December 2025. Furthermore, need-based financial support is available from the promoters and the Shree Ganesh group. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Saatvik Green will likely sustain the improvement in its business risk profile amid healthy demand and order book, favourable government policies and increasing operational capacities. The financial risk profile should remain comfortable despite debt-funded capex, with comfortable debt coverage and debt to Ebitda ratio. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Healthy ramp-up of existing and upcoming capacities, along with sustenance of operating margin above 15% at the consolidated level, resulting in significant improvement in operating cash accrual 

Improvement in debt coverage indicators and capital structure backed by increased accrual or faster-than-expected debt reduction 

# **Downward factors** 

Slower-than-expected ramp-up in operations resulting in decline in operating margin and weakening of debt coverage indicators; for instance, interest coverage ratio below 3 times on a sustained basis Substantial delay in project execution resulting in material time and cost overruns 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Set up in 2015 by the promoters of the Shree Ganesh group, Saatvik Green manufactures solar PV modules for residential, commercial and industrial off-grid/on-grid applications. The company had installed capacity of 4.8 GW as on September 30, 2025, at its facilities in Ambala (Haryana), of which 4.2 GW is under Saatvik Solar, a 100% subsidiary of Saatvik Green. 

The Shree Ganesh group comprises Shree Ganesh Fats Pvt Ltd ('Crisil A/Stable'), Kamla Oleo Pvt Ltd ('Crisil A/Stable'), Kamla Organics Pvt Ltd, M K Proteins Ltd ('Crisil A/Stable') and SGF Industries Pvt Ltd ('Crisil A/Stable').
