### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has placed its rating on the long-term bank facilities of Inox Green Energy Services Limited (IGESL) on ‘ **Rating Watch with Developing Implications’** and has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A1’ rating on the short-term bank facilities. 

The rating action on IGESL reflects the similar rating action on its parent, Inox Wind Ltd (IWL, ‘Crisil A/Watch Developing/Crisil A1’). The ratings of IWL have been placed on watch following the announcement by Inox Clean Energy Ltd (ICEL) to acquire renewable energy (RE) assets of around 6 GW from Vena Energy India. This is because the ratings of IWL factor in its strong group linkages as part of the INOXGFL group. The INOXGFL group has presence in specialty chemicals through Gujarat Fluorochemicals Ltd (GFL; ‘Crisil AA+/Stable/Crisil A1+’), and supply of wind turbine generators and associated turn-key engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) contracts, along with operations and maintenance (O&M) of RE assets, through IWL and its subsidiaries. The group has ventured into the renewable sector as an independent power producer by developing and operating RE projects as well as manufacturing solar cells and modules, through ICEL. 

ICEL has articulated targets of 14 GW in RE generation by the end of fiscal 2029 and manufacturing capabilities of 11 GW each for solar cells and modules over the medium term, requiring significant capital. In addition to the proposed acquisition of RE assets from Vena Energy India, ICEL, in the past 12 months, has acquired Macquarie-owned Vibrant Energy, Indian assets of SunSource Energy, and CalPERS-backed SkyPower. ICEL has also announced acquisition of US-based Boviet Solar’s manufacturing assets, including its Africa business. The impact of the INOXGFL group’s capital allocation strategy in the clean energy vertical and the credit risk profiles, including capital structures, of these assets on the group’s credit profile is critical. Crisil Ratings will continue to engage with the group’s management and resolve the watch once it obtains clarity on the capital allocation. 

IGESL handles O&M for the projects of IWL, post commissioning, and is strategically important to the parent. IGESL had a healthy O&M portfolio of 13+ gigawatt (GW) as of March 2026 (around 10.5 GW of wind assets and the balance solar assets), along with machine availability of around 96.5% for fiscal 2026. The O&M portfolio includes investments to acquire 6.5 GW of operational wind O&M portfolio of two companies (acquisition expected to conclude in fiscal 2027). Revenue visibility is supported by the healthy order book of IWL. 

IGESL achieved revenue of Rs 281 crore with earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation (Ebitda) of Rs 23 crore (operating margin: 8.2%) in fiscal 2026, compared with Rs 220 crore and Rs 37 crore, respectively, (operating margin: 17%) in fiscal 2025. As per the company’s management, the operating margin declined on account of upfront scheduled maintenance expense in a few projects. While recovery in the operating margin needs to be monitored, it is expected to remain healthy over the medium term, given the company’s stable annuity-based business. The overall consolidated margin at IWL remains comfortable. 

The ratings of IWL continue to reflect its healthy business risk profile, driven by its improved operating performance as reflected in healthy order execution over the past two fiscals. The financial risk profile of IWL remains healthy driven by the company’s deleveraging efforts through successful equity raises during fiscals 2023-2025, and around Rs 1,600 crore raised in fiscal 2026. The working capital intensity, however, remains high as reflected in large receivables, underpinning the business risk profile. The management expects the working capital cycle to moderate in the near term, which will be monitorable. 

IWL reported consolidated revenue of Rs 4,397 crore and operating profit of Rs 891 crore (operating margin of 20.3%) in fiscal 2026, against Rs 3,564 crore and Rs 757 crore, respectively, with operating margin of 21.2% in fiscal 2025. The increase in revenue was driven by ramp-up in order execution while profitability was affected because of supply chain challenges on account of geopolitical issues, mainly in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026. 

The overall order book of IWL stands at around 3.1<sup>1</sup> GW as on March 31, 2026, providing healthy medium-term revenue visibility. The order book mostly comprises high-margin 3.3-MW turbines, which shall result in healthy Ebitda margin. Timely execution of the order book remains monitorable. 

IWL, through its subsidiary Inox Renewable Solutions Ltd (IRSL; ‘Crisil A/Watch Developing/Crisil A1’), has taken measures to improve backward integration, such as crane services, which shall help improve margins for the EPC and O&M segments. The ability to maintain profitability while scaling up operations will be monitorable. 

The working capital intensity of IWL remains high with receivables increasing to 353 days and inventory to 186 days as on March 31, 2026, from 280 days and 145 days, respectively, a year earlier. The receivables increased to Rs 4,249 crore as on March 31, 2026, from Rs 2,687 crore as on March 31, 2025, mainly due to delayed collections from customers, linked to project milestones. The company’s management expects these dues to be recovered in the near term, which should improve cash flow. Timely collection of receivables and smooth execution of orders will be critical factors in assessing IWL’s credit profile. 

Inventory remains high on account of stock maintained for the common infrastructure business. With significant increase in sales, rationalisation of working capital, including receivables and inventory, will be monitorable. 

The financial risk profile of IWL remains healthy supported by net debt to Ebitda ratio of 0.43 time for fiscal 2026 compared with 3.44 times at the end of fiscal 2024. The improvement over the last two fiscals was supported by increase in scale and equity raise through rights issue of ~Rs 660 crore (net of Rs 590 crore redeemed to promoters for cumulative redeemable preference shares issued earlier). With turnaround in operating profitability and reduction in debt, interest coverage improved sharply to above 5 times through fiscal 2026 and is expected to remain healthy over 5 times over the medium term. 

Crisil Ratings also notes the proposed demerger of the substations business from IGESL into IRSL. This will result in an assetlight balance sheet for IGESL and streamline business activities. 

The ratings of IGESL continue to reflect its strong linkages with IWL, the healthy portfolio of IGESL and the extensive experience of its promoters in the wind energy business. These strengths are partially offset by exposure to intense competition.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Linkages with IWL** 

IGESL undertakes O&M activities for the projects of IWL post commissioning. The companies have strong operational linkages as the projects often have all three components: material supply, EPC and O&M. IGESL has contributed significantly to the operating profitability of IWL, and has received strong financial support from the parent through intercorporate deposits and optionally convertible debentures in the past. Moreover, the companies have a common treasury. The strong linkages and importance of IGESL to IWL will likely continue. 

# **Strong linkages with the INOXGFL group** 

The promoter group holds around 44% stake in IWL, with the INOXGFL group maintaining complete control over operations. The INOXGFL group has extended support to IWL and IGESL through group entities by way of non-convertible debentures, term debt and working capital facilities as and when required. Moreover, group entities have provided support through capital advances and intercorporate deposits in the past. Given the improvement in profitability and deleveraging over the past two fiscals, Crisil Ratings believes IWL will service its debt through its own accrual. The INOXGFL group has entered the renewable sector as an independent power producer by developing and operating RE projects as well as manufacturing solar cells and modules. This business is held by ICEL. While execution risk persists for the projects under ICEL, IWL and its entities will benefit from ICEL’s plans to develop capacities in the wind sector. 

# **Healthy portfolio and experienced promoters of IGESL** 

IGESL has an O&M portfolio (including capacities under pipeline) of 13 GW+ (around 10.5 GW of wind assets and the balance solar assets), up from 3.2 GW as on March 31, 2024, driven by inorganic expansion. The company plans to acquire 6.5 GW of operational wind assets through investments in two companies, expected to conclude in fiscal 2027. Backed by the extensive experience of the promoters and tailwinds in the wind sector, the operating performance is likely to remain healthy in the near term. 

# **Stable and strong profitability** 

Owing to the annuity-based business, IGESL enjoys healthy and stable cash flow built into the O&M contracts for a specified period. This is supported by healthy machine availability, which was above 96% in fiscal 2026. Hence, the operating margin is expected to remain healthy over the medium term. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Exposure to intense competition:** The business environment for the wind energy sector continues to be challenging. While IGESL is one of the prominent domestic players, it remains exposed to intense competition from other domestic players and 

foreign players. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Adequate 

The liquidity position of IGESL remains adequate, as reflected in healthy cash and equivalents of Rs 505 crore as on March 31, 2026. Furthermore, term debt obligation is negligible and healthy internal accrual will support cash flow. The liquidity is enhanced by unutilised fund-based working capital limit of Rs 5.84 crore as on March 31, 2026. 

IGESL benefits from the financial flexibility of its parent, IWL, as part of the INOXGFL group. The group companies have provided direct funds in the form of intercorporate deposits and advances for supplies and have helped IWL avail of funds from banks, supported by guarantees, letters of comfort or by pledging their own funds. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Track record of high execution with sustenance of operating margin over 15% for IWL Streamlining of working capital, strengthening the financial risk profile for IWL Significant increase in the portfolio of IGESL and sustenance of healthy operating margin 

# **Downward factors** 

Lower-than-expected execution with operating margin sustaining below 12% for IWL Change in the shareholding of, or support from, the INOXGFL group 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

IGESL was incorporated as a wholly owned subsidiary of IWL in May 2012. The company offers O&M and common infrastructure facility services for wind turbine generators manufactured and supplied by IWL. 

# **<u>About the IWL</u>** 

Incorporated in April 2009, IWL is a part of the INOXGFL group. The company manufactures nacelles, hubs, rotor blades and towers used to make wind turbines. It also provides associated services, such as O&M of wind turbines, project execution and infrastructure development for wind farms. The company has four units: at Una in Himachal Pradesh for nacelles and hubs, at Rohika in Gujarat for blades and towers, at Barwani in Madhya Pradesh for nacelles, hubs, blades and towers, and a newly tiedup nacelle manufacturing facility at Bhuj in Gujarat. IWL has a technical tie-up with AMSC Windtech, which provides control systems and vets suppliers for other parts from across the world. 

# **<u>About GFL</u>** 

GFL, which houses the chemicals business of the INOXGFL group, has a diverse product portfolio, including caustic soda, chloromethanes, polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE), hydrochlorofluorocarbons (HCFCs) and value-added products. The company is one of the largest chemical players in India, with installed capacity of 65,000 tonne per annum (TPA) of HCFC, 16,200 TPA of PTFE, 134,750 TPA of caustic soda and 108,500 TPA of chloromethane.
