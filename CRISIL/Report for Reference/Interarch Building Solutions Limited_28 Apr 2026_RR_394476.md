### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A/Stable/Crisil A1’ ratings on the bank facilities of Interarch Building Solutions Limited (IBSL); Formerly known as Interarch Building Products Limited (IBPL). 

The operating income grew ~12% to Rs 1,454 crore in fiscal 2025 from Rs 1,293 crore in fiscal 2024. The revenue further rose ~41% to Rs 1,394 crore in the nine months ended December 31, 2025, compared with Rs 990 crore in similar period last fiscal owing to strong order book, and better execution capabilities. Revenue is expected to grow further at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 13–14%, supported by increasing order book position and enhancement in capacity. This will benefit the profitability of the company over the medium term, with better operating leverage. 

The operating margin stood at 8.9% in the nine months ended December 31, 2025, and is expected to sustain at 9–10% in the medium term. The rise in steel prices may slightly impact on the gross margin for orders under execution mainly due to timing difference between order bidding and execution. However, the impact on overall operating profit is expected to be limited with management taking steps to improve profitability by bulk procurement of raw materials at relatively better pricing, reduction in wastages and better absorption of fixed costs owing to improving scale. That being said, a sharp and unexpected increase in steel prices could potentially erode the operating margin. 

The financial risk stands comfortable, with healthy capital structure and negligible debt. With limited reliance on debt, the gearing is expected to remain less than 0.05 time over the medium term. The debt protection metrics continue to be strong, with the interest coverage ratio estimated to have remained above 16 times for fiscal 2025 and expected to stay above 16 times over the medium term as well, driven by better profitability and negligible debt. 

The ratings continue to reflect IBPL's established position in the pre-engineered buildings (PEB) industry, and a comfortable financial risk profile driven by negligible debt. These strengths are partially offset by exposure to cyclicality in the end-user industry and raw material prices resulting in volatile operating margin. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

For arriving at the ratings, Crisil Ratings has analysed the standalone business and financial risk profiles of IBSL. 

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Established position in the PEB industry:** IBSL is one of the top players in the Indian PEB industry indicated by second largest integrated capacity. Strong market position with established customers, such as Reliance Industries, the Tata group and the Aditya Birla group, which also provide repeat orders, should continue to support the business. The company has facilities in north and south India, which helps it maintain geographical diversification in orders as well as cater to clients spread across India. 

With good relationships with customers, and wide presence across the country, the order book of the company is on an increasing trend, that is expected to support the growth with high execution capabilities. 

**Comfortable financial risk profile:** The company has a comfortable capital structure, with gearing below 0.02 time and total outside liabilities to tangible networth (TOLTNW) ratio at 0.5 time expected for fiscal 2026, owing to negligible debt. 

The working capital cycle remains comfortable with gross current assets (excluding cash and equivalents) of 140–150 days estimated for March 31, 2026. Given the efficient working capital management, the TOLTNW ratio has remained below 1 time over the past ten fiscals. The financial risk profile is expected to remain comfortable in the medium term in absence of any large, debt-funded capital expenditure (capex) and moderate working capital requirement. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Susceptibility to cyclicality in the end-user industry and raw material prices:** The business is susceptible to economic cycles and capex plans of its clients. Operating margin has been fluctuating over the years largely on account of high fixed costs and the company’s inability to pass the increase in raw material prices to its customers. With improved scale and better bargaining power, the volatility in profitability has reduced and is expected to further come down. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Liquidity is strong, supported by minimal bank limit utilisation, healthy cash and bank balance, with improved net cash accrual due to improved profitability. The company has unencumbered cash and equivalents of ~Rs 150 crore as on December 31, 2025. Apart from that it had proceeds from IPO parked in FDs of Rs. 29.95 crore and lien marked FDs (against Non fund based facilities) of Rs. 55.47 crore. Utilisation of fund-based limits was minimal over 12 months. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes IBSL will continue to increase its revenue backed by its market position in the PEB segment and maintain steady profitability by operating leverage. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Sustained double-digit growth in revenue, with Ebitda (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation) margin of 10% 

Sustenance of healthy capital structure and working capital cycle 

# **Downward factors:** 

Decline in profitability, with operating margin falling below 8% on a sustained basis 

Sustained and significant decline in revenue or order book 

Large working capital requirement or significant, debt-funded capex weakening the financial risk profile 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Noida (Uttar Pradesh)-based IBSL, incorporated in 1983, is promoted by Gautam Suri and Arvind Nanda. The company started operations by manufacturing PEBs, metal ceilings, roofing and claddings. It has two plants in Tamil Nadu, two plants in Uttarakhand and one in Andhra Pradesh.
