### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A-/Stable/Crisil A2+’ ratings on the bank facilities of EMS Limited (EMS). 

The ratings continue to reflect the extensive experience of the promoters and the established market position of group in the water engineering procurement and construction (EPC) industry, the company’s healthy order book providing revenue visibility and its healthy financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by exposure to risks inherent in tenderbased business and working capital-intensive operations. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of EMS and its five subsidiaries and three joint ventures (JVs). This is because all these entities, together referred to as the group, operate in the same industry and have operational and financial links.

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

### **Extensive promoter experience and established market position** 

The promoters have more than three decades of experience in the water EPC industry which has given them an understanding of market dynamics and helped establish healthy relationships with customers and suppliers which will continue to support the business. With specialization in turnkey services in water, wastewater collection and treatment and disposal, the group has delivered numerous projects over the years, including a recent completion of a hybrid annuity model (HAM) project, thus enhancing its execution capabilities. Till December 2025, the Group has marked an operating income of Rs 612 crore (Against Rs. 684 crore in the 9MFY25) with expectation to close the year at around Rs 950 to 1000 crore in FY 26 which is slightly lower than Crisil Rating’s expectations as the business remained impacted during Q2 and Q3 due to the heavier than expected rains leading to slower execution.  The company has an order book of more than Rs 2200 crore as on December 2025, to be executed over the next 18–24 months, providing revenue visibility and has a strong bidding pipeline of more than Rs 4000 crore. The increase in the order book, along with its timely execution and geographical diversity, will remain monitorable. 

**Healthy financial risk profile:** Group’s capital structure has been healthy due to its low reliance on external funds, as indicated by an estimated gearing of 0.09 times and a total outside liabilities to adjusted net worth (TOLANW) ratio of ~0.19 times as of March 31, 2025. 

The net worth is estimated to have increased to over Rs.1100 crore as of March 31, 2026, from Rs.972 crore in the previous fiscal year. Debt protection metrics have been above average due to moderate leverage and healthy profitability. The interest coverage and net cash accrual to total debt (NCATD) ratio are estimated at 37times and 1.05 times, respectively, for fiscal 2026, and are expected to improve over the medium term. Absence of sizeable debt funded capital expenditure and expected accretion to reserves shall continue to strengthen the financial risk profile over the medium term as well. 

### **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Exposure to risks inherent in tender-based business:** EMS undertakes construction, where projects are secured through tenders floated by government authorities, revenue and profitability thus depend on success in bidding for such 

tenders. Aggressive bidding and tendering nature of business and large number of players in the mid-sized segment intensify competition which may continue to constrain scalability and pricing power. Operating margins declined to 20 % in 9MFY26 (from 27.7% in 9M previous fiscal) and are expected to remain at around 20% for the full fiscal 2026, as company’s core work involves underground sewerage and pipeline laying. Intense and early rainfall in regions like Uttarakhand and Uttar Pradesh during the year. significantly hampered ground-level execution, leading to lower revenue recognition against fixed mobilization costs. Moreover, this has resulted in increase in miscellaneous expense like site clearance. However, with the technical nature of business the margins are expected to improve,the sustained improvement in the operating margins will remain the key monitorable. 

**Working capital-intensive operations:** The group's gross current assets (GCA) have increased to 237 days as of March 31, 2025, from 234 days the previous year, primarily due to stretched receivables resulting from large order executions concentrated in the last quarter. Retention money withheld by the government authorities also increases the average collection cycle for the group. High inventory levels of raw materials and work-in-progress also contribute to the increase in GCA days, thereby escalating working capital requirements. The group has been able to manage its working capital through mobilization advances, routine cash flows and available liquid reserves, however, given the nature of the business, the working capital requirements are expected to increase with expected growth in revenue. The group’s ability to efficiently manage its working capital amidst business growth, leading to sustained improvement in return on capital employed will remain imperative and a key rating sensitivity factor. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Liquidity is expected to remain strong with nil bank limit utilization against sanctioned cash credit limit of Rs 46.5 crore as the company has sufficient net cash accrual to manage working capital requirement. 

The group is expected to generate sufficient cash accrual of Rs 140-160 crore over the medium term against low debt obligation of Rs 8-10 crore per annum. Unencumbered cash and equivalent stood at Rs 106 crore as of 31st December 2025. Liquidity is supported by strong gearing and adequate networth, which provide financial cushion in case of any adverse condition or downturn in the business. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes EMS group will continue to benefit from the extensive experience of its promoters and established relationships with clients. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

- Sustained improvement in revenue and sustenance of operating margin of over 22-23% leading to higher-than-expected cash accrual 

- Successful bidding and awarding of new tenders leading to order book to turnover ratio of over 3 times on a sustainable basis. 

- Efficient working capital management leads to lower dependence on external debt and hence sustenance of financial risk profile 

# **Downward factors** 

Significant decline in revenue and/or operating profitability moderating below 20%, thus impacting the net cash accruals Any large debt-funded capex or substantial increase in working capital requirement weakening liquidity and financial risk profile 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Headquartered in Ghaziabad, EMS (erstwhile, EMS Infracon Pvt Ltd) was incorporated in 2010 as a private limited company and was subsequently reconstituted as a public limited company in 2022. It is promoted by Mr Ramveer Singh (Chairman) and Mr Ashish Tomar (Chief Financial Officer and Managing Director). The company, along with its subsidiaries and JVs, provides engineering, procurement and construction services for the infrastructure sector on turnkey basis. It specialises in providing turnkey services in water and wastewater collection, treatment and disposal solutions (including operation and maintenance) and HAM projects. It also constructs roads and buildings and undertakes electrical work. EMS is listed on BSE Ltd and National Stock Exchange of India Ltd.
