### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ratings on the bank facilities and debt instruments of Manappuram Finance Limited at ‘Crisil AA/Stable/Crisil A1+’. 

Crisil Ratings has also **withdrawn** its rating on non-convertible debentures amounting to Rs 20.47 crore as Crisil Ratings has received independent confirmation that these instruments have been redeemed (See 'Annexure - Details of Rating Withdrawn'). The withdrawal is in line with Crisil Ratings' withdrawal policy. 

The ratings continue to factor in Manappuram Finance’s established market position in the gold finance business while maintaining strong financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by high, though improving, operating costs, geographical concentration in operations and the associated risks, and potential challenges associated with the non-gold product segments. 

The assets under management (AUM) of Manappuram Finance (on consolidated basis) grew by around 48.3% (YTD) to 63,798 crore as on March 31, 2026, from Rs 43,034 crore as on March 31, 2025. Loan against gold jewellery continued to account for maximum proportion (about 80%) and grew at healthy pace of around 99% year-on-year thereby taking AUM in this segment to Rs 50,953 crore as on March 31, 2026. As far non-gold segments are concerned, due to prolonged industry vide stress, the company maintained cautious stance. As a result, AUM within microfinance (Asirvad Microfinance Ltd) and vehicle finance segment de-grew in fiscal 2026. However, the company has also been increasing its presence in gold loan segment through its subsidiaries; the gold loan portfolio within Asirvad stood at Rs 2,139 crore with y-o-y growth of around 130%. Nevertheless, the ability of the company to have sustained profitable growth across its non-gold segments remains key rating sensitivity factor. 

On asset quality front, it remained controlled on account of larger exposure towards liquid asset segment i.e. loan against gold jewellery. On a standalone basis, the gross non-performing assets (NPAs) stood at 1.8% as on March 31, 2026, as 

against 2.8% as on March 31, 2025. Despite NPAs, the ultimate credit costs during the fiscal 2026 increased to 1.0% from 0.7% during fiscal 2025. The company has been focusing on streamlining the loan-to-value (LTV) monitoring, renewal of loans after due dates and end use of gold loans in line with latest regulatory directions. This is expected to keep asset quality in control going ahead. As far as asset quality within non-gold loan segments are concerned, NPAs within microfinance stood elevated although improving, post tightening of underwriting and implementation of guardrails after prolonged sectoral issues faced by sector for last 4-6 quarters. As on March 31, 2026, NPAs within this segment stood at 4.8% as against 8.5% as on March 31, 2025. This reduction was mainly on account of write-offs during the period (inclusive of write-offs, the NPAs will remain higher than reported in fiscal 2025 level). Within vehicle finance segments, the gross NPAs elevated to 10.4% as on March 31, 2026, from 6.7% as on March 31, 2025. The AUM in this segment also degrew 37% which also led to increase in NPA level (on account of base effect). Asset quality within housing finance segment remained relatively better with NPAs increasing marginally at around 2.6% as on March 31, 2026, as against 2.4% as on March 31, 2025. Crisil Ratings, overall, believes that the consolidated credit risk profile has the ability to absorb asset quality and earnings risks in the microfinance, vehicle or housing finance businesses over medium term. 

On February 14, 2026, the company announced in stock exchanges that Reserve Bank of India's granted approval for proposed acquisition of control and shareholding of up to 41.66% by Bain Capital. However, in the same announcement, the company also clarified that regulatory approval is pending for the proposed equity investment in subsidiaries i.e. Asirvad Microfinance and Manappuram Home Finance. Post receipt of this approval, the transaction will be taken ahead. 

Subsequently, on March 27, 2026, the first tranche amounting to Rs 2,740 crore (being 9% preferential allotment plus 25% of the aggregate warrant subscription of 9%), has already been received from Bain Capital. The balance portion will be paid upon conversion of the subscription warrants within 4- 18 months from the initial infusion. The proposed equity infusion of around Rs 4,385 crore by Bain Capital should enhance the overall capital position of Manappuram Finance and shall also reduce the gearing levels. The end use of these funds will be towards onward lending and also towards investment in subsidiaries. Subsequent to the investment from Bain Capital, they have nominated two nominee directors and also other key managerial personnel for Manappuram Finance. Further, Bain capital will also have a right to nominate certain members on the board of its group companies as well. Bain Capital will therefore have joint control along- with existing promoters on the entire operations of the Manappuram Finance group. 

# **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of Manappuram Finance and its subsidiaries, Asirvad, Manappuram Home Finance Ltd (MAHOFIN) and Manappuram Insurance Brokers Pvt Ltd. This is because all the companies, collectively referred to as the Manappuram group, have significant financial, managerial and operational linkages, whilst factoring in benefits accruing to its financial flexibility and improved managerial control on account of its ownership by Bain Capital.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Established market position in the gold finance business** 

The family of the promoter, Mr V P Nandakumar, has been in the gold-loan business for more than six decades. Industry expertise led to the company designing an appropriate assessment and underwriting methodology. Assessing the purity of gold, fixing the sum that can be lent against a gram of gold and determining appropriate LTV ratios are critical aspects in the assessment process. The company has a strong brand and reputation in south India (particularly Kerala and Tamil Nadu). Reputation and trust play significant roles in this segment as these assure the customer of getting back personal gold ornaments once the loan is repaid. After shifting towards shorter tenure gold loans of three months in 2015 to de-risk the portfolio from sharp fluctuations in gold prices, the company witnessed stability in business with an increase in customer base and gold holdings. Despite moderate volume growth and increased competition, the company’s gold loan AUM grew by around 99% (y-o-y) as of March 2026 and stood at Rs 50,953 crore. Historically, the company’s operating efficiency – indicated by average gold loan AUM per branch – has been improving over the past few years. Manappuram Finance’s extensive branch network and client base, which are relatively more diverse in terms of geographies and are gradually improving further, should continue to boost its competitive position over the medium term. While the company had started to diversify into non-gold segments, its primary focus would remain on gold loans over the medium term in light of the challenges being faced by other asset classes. 

# **Sound capitalisation** 

The company has maintained a strong capital position while ramping up operations over the years. The consolidated networth and on-book gearing were Rs 16,058 crore and 3.6 times, respectively, as on March 31, 2026. Large accretion to networth in the past several years resulted in healthy capital adequacy ratio of 21% as on March 31, 2026. Lower asset-side risk (security of gold, which is liquid and in the lender's possession) also supports capitalisation. AUM in the gold loan segment is expected to grow at a steady rate and will remain the major asset class over the medium term. Further, Microfinance segment is also expected to grow post tightening of underwriting and implementation of guardrails at a steady rate, while there maybe degrowth in vehicle segment. Over the past six fiscals, gearing (consolidated and standalone) remained below 4 times whereas standalone tier I capital adequacy ratio remained above 20%. 

With regard to capital raising plans, the company has announced that as part of the stake sale transaction, there will be mandatory requirement of open offer for the purchase of an additional 26% stake in the company on an expanded capital basis (excluding warrants). Further, the first tranche amounting to Rs 2,740 crore (being 9% preferential allotment plus 25% of the aggregate warrant subscription of 9%), has already been received from Bain Capital. The balance portion will be paid upon conversion of the subscription warrants within 4- 18 months from the initial infusion. The end use of these funds from Bain Capital will be for onward lending and the investment into subsidiaries, based on the agreement. With additional ~1600 crore equity capital, the group’s capital position will be strengthened along with enhancing presence of professional personnel at board as well as key management level of the Manappuram group as whole. 

While the current management continues to grow the business, Bain Capital has appointed Mr. Rishi Mandavat and Mr. Ashish Arvind as nominee directors on the board of Manappuram Finance. Bain Capital has demonstrated its willingness and capability to support investee companies via fresh capital call outside the investment period, recycling exit realizations and raising of successor funds, which can invest in existing investments. These aspects of Bain Capital’s investment philosophy, and the expectation on continuity of holding majority stake are likely to enable Manappuram Finance to remain well capitalised for its business needs and ensure debt obligations and other commitments are met in compliance with applicable contractual and statutory requirements. 

# **Stable profitability** 

Profitability has remained strong with a standalone return on managed assets (RoMA) being over 4.0% for the six fiscals since 2019 driven by the large profit generated by the gold loan segment.  However, the standalone net profit for the company stood at Rs 1,525 crore during fiscal 2026 as against Rs 1,783 crore in fiscal 2025. During fiscal 2026, the company reported RoMA at 1.8% owing to reduced yields in the gold loan segment and increased credit costs in the microfinance and vehicle segments. The microfinance segment reported losses of Rs 580 crore during fiscal 2026 as against Rs 639 crore during fiscal 2025. Asirvad’ s profitability has been affected by the degrowth of the microfinance book and decline in collections from certain geographies, resulting in its higher credit costs, however the same is expected to improve, in line with industry trends, visible by the reduction in credit costs to average managed assets to 10.2% in fiscal 2026 as against 14.8% in fiscal 2025. The collection has improved with overall collection efficiency in March 2026, which stood at ~92%, which had been declining since September 2024 and was ~75% a year ago. Crisil Ratings, therefore, will continue to closely monitor the microfinance business, its ability to control the credit costs and improve its collections, thereby keeping its earnings profile healthy will remain monitorable. The home finance segment reported net profit of Rs 28.3 crore during fiscal 2026, as compared to Rs 22.8 crore in fiscal 2025. Ability of the company to maintain its yields and limit operating costs will be critical for stability in profitability. Besides, given its diversification into other segments, asset quality and profitability of the non-gold businesses will also remain monitorable. 

# **Stable funding profile** 

During fiscal 2026, the company raised fresh borrowing of around Rs 23,440 crores through term loans and ECBs as against Rs 10,938 crore raised during fiscal 2025. The incremental cost of borrowing was around 8.6% during fiscal 2026 as compared to 9.2% in fiscal 2025. Because of its legacy and highly secured asset class, Manappuram Finance is able to roll over existing bank lines/commercial paper and continue to raise fresh funds from diversified sources. In terms of standalone funding, while a larger proportion of the borrowings comprised funding lines from banks and financial institutions (62%), the company’s resource profile was diversified across avenues such as ECBs (25%), non-convertible debentures (6%), and commercial paper (7%) as on March 31, 2026. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Geographical concentration in operations and the associated risks** 

Operations have significant regional concentration compared to large asset-financing non-banking finance companies (NBFCs); South India accounted for 63% of total branch level presence as on March 31, 2026. Moreover, there is susceptibility to regulatory risks related to revenue concentration in a single asset class (gold-loan financing), which accounts for 78% of revenue. The non-gold loan segments, such as vehicle finance, affordable housing finance and microfinance segments, accounted for 20% of the total portfolio and around 22% of revenue as on March 31, 2026. In view of the large gold loan book (80% of the total portfolio) and the presence of the gold loan business mainly in South India, revenue is likely to remain concentrated in terms of geography and asset class over the medium term. 

# **Potential challenges associated with non-gold loan segments** 

The non-gold segments accounted for 20% of the overall portfolio as on March 31, 2026, which was 47% as on March 31, 2024. The company has shown de-growth in these businesses and has resulted in decrease in the segmental share over the past fiscal, potential challenges linked to seasoning of the loan book and asset quality remain. Within the housing finance segment, MAHOFIN operates in the affordable housing finance segment, catering to self-employed customers engaged in small business activities and thus, have a relatively weak credit risk profile because of the volatile nature of their income and employment in unorganised segments. Similarly, microfinance loans (under Asirvad), through which the company intends to cater to weaker sections of society, are unsecured in nature and are rendered to borrowers with weak credit risk profiles. This segment also exhibits high subjectivity to local sociopolitical issues. The vehicle finance business (under Manappuram Finance) deals with lending against commercial vehicles and equipment – majority of which are used/pre-owned vehicles, which resulted in increased provision costs and subdued disbursements. The non-gold businesses is facing asset quality challenges, leading to a spurt in reported NPAs requiring additional provisioning. The inherent weaknesses of the non-gold segments in which Manappuram Finance operates, the standalone earnings profile of 

non-gold businesses may remain susceptible. From a longer-term perspective, as the growth within these segments has remained limited as yet, the asset quality and profitability in these businesses will be closely monitored. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Liquidity remains strong, with liquid balance of Rs 3142 crore as on May 31, 2026 (including cash and liquid investments of Rs 2,442 crore and unutilised CC/WCDL limit of Rs 700 crore). The company has sufficient liquidity cover to meet its debt obligations arising over next three months, considering 75% collections and excluding the CC/WCDL limits, which are due for rollover during this period, continues to be adequate at over 2.1 times. The company has also been able to roll over existing capital lines and raise incremental funds at competitive rates over the past few quarters. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Manappuram Finance's capitalisation and asset quality will remain strongly supported by its gold loan business. The strong earnings will also provide support as the company diversifies into other asset classes and scales up its non-gold business. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

- Continued strong market position in the gold finance business with increasing diversity in AUM into non-gold business without affecting asset quality 

- Sustenance of profitability with RoMA above 4% on a steady-state basis, while improving asset quality (mainly within non-gold loan segments) 

# **Downward factors** 

Consolidated gearing increasing over 5 times 

- Steep decline in interest collection in the gold loan business or deterioration in asset quality or profitability in the nongold loan segments 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Incorporated in July 1992 and promoted by Mr V P Nandakumar, Manappuram Finance is the flagship company of the Manappuram group. It is a non-deposit-taking NBFC that provides finance against personal gold ornaments. It had ~5,351 branches across India as on December 31, 2025. The company went public in August 1995, with shares listed on the stock exchanges of Chennai, Kochi and Mumbai (Bombay Stock Exchange and National Stock Exchange). Over the past three years, the Manappuram group has diversified into other businesses such as microfinance, vehicle finance, loans against property and affordable housing finance. It also entered the insurance broking business. Overall group’s AUM of Rs 63,798 crore as on March 31, 2026, includes gold loan (80%), microfinance (7%), commercial vehicle finance (5%), MSME (5%), Housing (2%), and lending to other NBFCs (1%). The gold loan portfolio is diversified across 28 states and Union Territories, while the microfinance, commercial vehicle and housing finance portfolios are diversified across 24 states, 22 states and nine states, respectively. 

# **<u>About Bain Capital</u>** 

Bain Capital is a private investment firm with AUM of around $185 billion. It was founded in 1984, and is into private equity, public equity, fixed income, credit, venture capital and real estate investments, across multiple sectors, industries and geographies. It has experience in investing in a diversified set of financial services businesses in India and across the globe, including Axis Bank, 360One (previously known as IIFL Wealth), Judo Bank, L&T Finance Holdings, Legacy Corporate Lending and more. Further, Bain Capital has publicly acknowledged the attractiveness of the Indian market and that it is on track to invest up to $10 billion in the next 3-5 years after successfully deploying $2 billion over the past 12-18 months.
