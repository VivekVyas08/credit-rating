### **<u>Detailed rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil BBB/Stable’ rating on the long-term bank loan facilities and fixed deposits of Synergy Green Industries Ltd (Synergy). 

The rating continues to reflect the extensive experience of the promoters of Synergy in manufacturing heavy iron castings, its established clientele and comfortable financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by working capital-intensive operations and susceptibility of operating profitability to volatility in raw material prices. 

### **<u>Analytical approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has considered the standalone business and financial risk profiles of Synergy. 

Crisil Ratings has treated preference shares worth Rs 10.71 crore as on March 31, 2025, as debt because the shares are expected to be repaid over the medium term. 

Unsecured loan of Rs 6.38 crore, as on March 31, 2025, from the promoters has been treated as debt since it will be repaid over the medium term. 

### **<u>Key rating drivers - Strengths</u>** 

**Extensive experience of the promoters:** The promoters’ experience of more than a decade in the industrial machinery and consumables industry and healthy relationships with customers should continue to support the business. Synergy was established as a greenfield project, and the promoters quickly ramped up operations. They have also extended funding support in the form of preference shares and deposits. 

**Established clientele leading to healthy revenue growth:** Revenue rose ~11% on-year to Rs 362 crore in fiscal 2025 on account of increased demand from established clients. Till September 2025, the company reported operating income of Rs 157 crore which remained slightly lower as compared to the corresponding period of the previous fiscal, on account of rescheduling of customers in domestic wind as well as original equipment manufacturer (OEM) exports. The company had already built up inventory and schedules in pipeline, which is expected to be dispatched in the second half of fiscal 2026, resulting in 5-10% on-year growth. The company focuses on products for high-capacity wind energy generators of more than 4 megawatt (MW) and capacity expansion is expected to boost revenue and profitability over the medium term. 

**Comfortable financial risk profile:** The financial risk profile remained comfortable with networth of Rs 104 crore as on March 31, 2025. The gearing and total outside liabilities to adjusted networth (TOLANW) ratio remained at 1.5 times and 2.25 times, respectively, as on March 31, 2025, which is expected to be around 2 times and 2.6 times, respectively, for fiscal 2026 amid capacity expansion. Debt protection metrics remain comfortable with interest coverage ratio of 3.44 times in fiscal 2025 and expected to be 2.6-2.7 times for fiscal 2026, lower due to higher finance cost from debt availed for undergoing capital expenditure (capex). The capacity addition, which was earlier expected to be completed by September 2025, is now expected to be completed by the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026, delayed on account of higher rainfall in the first half of fiscal 2026. Despite ongoing debt-funded capex to augment capacity, the financial risk profile is expected to remain comfortable with improving profitability and better accretion to reserves. 

# **<u>Key rating drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Susceptibility to fluctuations in raw material prices** : The operating margin remains susceptible to fluctuations in input prices and ranged from 9.11% to 14.56% in the three fiscals through 2025. Raw materials account for 50-60% of sales, and the prices of key raw materials, such as cold-rolled close annealed scrap, mild steel scrap, pig iron and resins, are volatile. The company has been able to pass on the price rise to customers only after a lag of 1-2 quarters. Though the operating margin improved in fiscal 2025 and was 14.12% in the first half of fiscal 2026, sustenance remains critical and will be monitored over the medium term. The margin is not expected to be affected due to the impact of US tariffs since products are sold free on board (FOB) basis. 

**Large working capital requirement** : Gross current assets (GCAs) were sizeable at 178 days as on March 31, 2025, driven by receivables and inventory of 58 days and 63 days, respectively. A large part of the working capital requirement is funded through credit from suppliers. The working capital parameters remained higher as of September 2025 due to higher finished goods inventory, resulting in skewed working capital parameters and expected to stabilize in upcoming quarters. Efficient working capital management will be monitorable. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Adequate 

Bank limit utilisation was 83% on average for the 12 months through November 2025. Annual cash accrual is expected to be over Rs 29 crore against term debt obligation of Rs 10.5-13.55 crore per year over the medium term. The current ratio and cash and bank balance stood at 1.03 times and Rs 54 crore, respectively, as on March 31, 2025. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes Synergy will continue to benefit from the extensive experience of its promoters and established clientele. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors:** 

Higher revenue growth and stable operating margin, leading to net cash accrual of over Rs 35 crore Improvement in the financial risk profile and liquidity 

# **Downward factors:** 

Lower-than-expected revenue growth or drop in the operating margin resulting in accrual less than Rs 22 crore Large working capital requirement or sizeable debt-funded capex weakening the capital structure and liquidity 

# **<u>About the company</u>** 

Incorporated in October 2010 and based in Kolhapur, Maharashtra, Synergy primarily manufactures iron castings for wind turbines. Mr Sachin Shirgaokar and Mr V Srinivas Reddy manage the operations. The company is listed on both the National Stock Exchange of India (NSE) and the Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE).
