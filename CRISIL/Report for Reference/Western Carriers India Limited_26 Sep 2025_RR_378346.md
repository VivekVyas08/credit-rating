### **<u>Detailed rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has revised its outlook on the long-term bank facilities of Western Carriers India Ltd (WCIL) to ‘ **Stable** ’ from ‘Positive’ while reaffirming the rating at **‘Crisil A-’** and has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A2+’ rating on the short-term facilities. 

The revision in outlook takes into account WCIL's lower-than-expected operating profitability, exacerbated by its lengthening working capital cycle. The earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortisation (Ebitda) margin averaged ~5% over the three quarters through the first quarter of fiscal 2026, which is significantly lower than the historical average of 7-8%. The operating efficiency is further constrained by a substantial increase in the working capital cycle, with receivables rising to 131 days as on March 31, 2025, from 114 days a year earlier — significantly higher than the 80-90 days in prior years. Sustained revival in profitability, while ensuring efficient working capital management, will remain monitorable. 

The ratings continue to reflect the extensive experience of the promoter in the transport and logistics business, the company’s established market position, adequate infrastructure, healthy scale of operations, reputed clientele, and healthy financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by large working capital requirement and susceptibility to intense competition, economic downturns, and volatility in EXIM (exports and imports) trade. 

### **<u>Analytical approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has evaluated the standalone business and financial risk profiles of WCIL. 

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

- **Established market position and adequate infrastructure:** The promoter, Mr Rajendra Sethia, has close to four decades of experience in the transport and logistics industry. His strong understanding of the market dynamics and healthy relations with customers and suppliers have helped the company establish a strong market position and set up adequate infrastructure and network, with over 70 branches and multiple warehouses (rented and owned). Though the company primarily operates on an asset-light model, it has significantly strengthened infrastructure over the years by acquiring adequate fleets and containers, complementing the rail-focused model. The company launched its Gati Shakti Multi-Modal Cargo Terminal at Devaliya Station (WCGD) near Morbi in Gujarat. This is an advanced rail terminal and service station spanning over 30 acres, and is equipped to handle multimodal logistics via container and wagon rake systems, serving key industries in the region. 

**Healthy scale of operations and reputed and stable clientele:** WCIL has established relationships with multiple bluechip customers such as Hindustan Unilever Ltd ('Crisil AAA/Stable'), Vedanta Ltd ('Crisil AA/Watch Developing/Crisil A1+/Watch Developing'), and Hindalco Industries Ltd (‘Crisil A1+’). This has helped sustain healthy scale, as reflected in revenue of around Rs 1,727.30 crore for fiscal 2025. 

- **Healthy financial risk profile:** With the initial public offer (IPO) proceeds coming into the business in September 2024, the networth was strong at around Rs 797 crore as on March 31, 2025, against reduced debt of around Rs 170 crore. This led to healthy gearing of around 0.22 time. Reduction in debt by about Rs 100 crore on-year also led to healthy debt protection metrics, as reflected in net cash accrual to adjusted debt ratio and interest coverage of around 52% and around 6 times, respectively, for fiscal 2025. 

# **Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses** 

**Stretched working capital cycle:** The working capital cycle has lengthened over the years due to stretched receivables. The gross current assets were at 176 days as on March 31, 2025. The increased working capital cycle may continue to constrain operating flexibility over the medium term. Improvement in the receivables collection leading to a prudently managed working capital cycle will be critical. 

**Susceptibility to intense competition, economic downturns, and volatility in EXIM trade:** The business risk profile remains susceptible to economic downturns, though this risk is mitigated by the company’s diverse customer base across different sectors. Nevertheless, a subdued economic scenario can adversely affect scale and working capital cycle. Moreover, the domestic road freight transport industry is highly fragmented because of low entry barriers. Although WCIL is one of the market leaders in the FTL (full truckload) segment, its share in the domestic road freight transport industry is low. Competition has intensified in recent times in the container shipping business with the entry of large groups such as Adani, which enjoys robust financial backing, as well as other established players. 

The international supply chain business is directly linked to global EXIM trade and any sluggishness in EXIM trade, in case of a steep fall in global trade, could impact freight volume, freight rates and the profitability of WCIL. Crisil Ratings expects industry conditions to remain subdued over the medium term given the overall muted demand scenario, especially from key markets in Europe, before meaningful improvement begins. 

# **<u>Liquidity: Strong</u>** 

Liquidity remains strong with long-term working capital of around Rs 163 crore coming into the business from IPO funds. Despite the large working capital cycle, the cumulative bank limit of Rs 131 crore was utilised moderately, at 65% on average over the 12 months through July 2025. Bank limits were reduced from Rs 209 crore to Rs 131 crore post the IPO. Annual accrual of around Rs 100 crore should sufficiently cover yearly debt obligation of around Rs 25 crore over the medium term. 

# **<u>Outlook: Stable</u>** 

WCIL will continue to benefit from the extensive experience of its promoter and established relationships with reputed clients. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors:** 

Sustained growth in revenue while maintaining steady operating margin of over 7% Healthy financial risk profile 

# **Downward factors:** 

Slowdown in revenue and dip in profitability leading to net cash accrual below Rs 50 crore Further stretch in the working capital cycle impacting liquidity 

# **<u>About the company</u>** 

Incorporated in 2011, WCIL provides multimodal logistics (road, rail, water, custom house agency, storage and warehousing) services across India. The operations are managed by Mr Rajendra Sethia and his son Mr Kanishka Sethia. Mr Rajendra Sethia had established Western Carriers as a sole proprietorship firm in 1976, which was subsequently taken over by WCIL in June 2013. 

WCIL is one of the largest private, multi-modal, asset-light, fourth-party logistics company in India. It continues to have a rail-focused model and is one of the largest associate partners of Container Corporation of India Ltd (CONCOR).
