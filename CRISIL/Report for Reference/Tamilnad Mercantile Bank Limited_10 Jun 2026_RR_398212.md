### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A+/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the fixed deposits (FD) programmes, short-term fixed deposits and certificates of deposits of Tamilnad Mercantile Bank Limited (TMB). 

The ratings on FD programmes of banks continue to factor in better access to systemic liquidity, in the form of access to call money markets and the liquidity adjustment facility (LAF) of the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). Such external liquidity support, the RBI’s oversight over banks and the deposit guarantees up to a certain quantum provided by the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC) instill confidence among depositors. They mitigate the probability of a run-on deposit in case of any downward rating action on the long-term rating. Moreover, banks enjoy systemic support as indicated by the demonstrated track record of the regulator stepping in to safeguard interests of depositors. 

TMB’s FD base is granular in nature, with 72.3% of the FDs under Rs 1 crore ticket size as on March 31, 2026. The top 30 depositors formed only 12% of total deposits as on March 31, 2026. The bank has maintained depositor profile with well spread maturity of deposits and high granularity. Moreover, the FD renewal rate was around 54% in fiscal 2026. 

The ratings continue to reflect adequate capitalisation of the bank and its improving asset quality and comfortable profitability. These strengths are partially offset by the small scale of operations, high geographic concentration, average resource profile and exposure to risks arising from an ownership dispute. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has considered the standalone business and financial risk profiles of TMB. 

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

### **Adequate capitalisation** 

The bank has a comfortable capital profile with Tier I and overall capital adequacy ratio (CAR) at 32.3% and 33.7%, respectively, as on March 31, 2026. Networth rose to Rs 10,110 crore as on March 31, 2026 as against Rs 9,009 crore as on March 31, 2025, supported by steady internal accrual. Networth coverage for net non-performing assets (NPAs) was healthy at 103.8 times as on March 31, 2026 as against 56.3 times as on March 31, 2025. 

### **Improving asset quality** 

Reported gross non-performing assets (GNPAs) have improved consistently over the past few years and stood at 0.73% (lowest in the last decade) as on March 31, 2026 as against 1.25%  as on March 31, 2025. The improvement in GNPA was mainly attributed to controlled slippages, better recoveries and write-offs. This showcases the bank’s ability to leverage its relationship with customers and recover the dues. GNPAs may remain at similar levels in the near term. However, the bank plans  to increase its exposure towards MSME  loans, and the performance will be monitorable. 

Overall restructured advances formed 0.44% of total advances as on March 31, 2026, and 0.85% as on March 31, 2025. The bank continues to focus on MSME, retail and agriculture loan advances. The overall loan book comprised agriculture loans (38%), followed by MSME loans (29%) and retail loans (28%) as on March 31, 2026. These loans are backed by good quality collateral. While GNPA for corporate advances (8% of overall advances as on March 31, 2026) stood elevated, that for other asset classes has improved significantly over the past few years. 

Nevertheless, asset quality will remain exposed to inherent political, social, economic and environmental risk, amidst exposure to small borrowers. Hence, sustained improvement in asset quality remains a key monitorable. 

# **Comfortable profitability** 

Overall profitability remains comfortable with profit after tax (PAT) of Rs 1,338 crore and RoA of 1.93% in fiscal 2026 as against Rs 1,183 crore and return on asset (RoA) of 1.88% in fiscal 2025. Profitability is supported by decrease in credit cost with a stable net interest margin (NIM) and operating cost. Credit cost as a percentage of average total assets decreased by nearly 20 basis points to 0.1% in fiscal 2026. 

NIM as well as operating costs, as a percentage of average total assets, have been stable at around 2.2% and 3.6%, respectively, over the last three fiscals. Other income, however, inched down marginally to 1.3% in fiscal 2026, as against 1.4% in fiscal 2025. 

The bank’s provision coverage ratio (without Technical write-off) has increased to 74.9% in fiscal 2026 as against 71.2% in fiscal 2025. This should be adequate for the agriculture, MSME and retail asset classes, wherein a significant amount of exposure is backed by collateral and can be recovered by selling the underlying secured assets if the loans become substandard/doubtful assets. The bank’s ability to sustain profitability at comfortable levels will remain a key monitorable. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Average resource profile** 

The bank’s deposit base has grown by 14.9% to Rs 61,712 crore as on March 31, 2026 as against 8.4% to Rs 53,689 crore as on March 31, 2025.  current account and savings account (CASA) ratio has also grown  to 28.1% as on March 31, 2026 as against 26.4% as on March 31, 2025 due  to various initiatives, including the impact of the TBG(Transaction Banking Group) structure brought in earlier. 

Term deposits stood at Rs 44,347 crore as on March 31, 2026 as against Rs 39,496 crore as on March 31, 2025. The bank has maintained a depositor profile with well spread maturities and high granularity. The FD base is granular in nature with ~72.3% of FDs under Rs 1 crore ticket size as on March 31, 2026, and the renewal rate being ~54% in fiscal 2026. Crisil Ratings understands that TMB will continue to focus on improving the renewal rate and increasing retail term deposits. 

The bank also plans to open branches outside of South India and increase its presence in other states. Ability to garner pace in deposit growth commensurate with growth in advances remains a key imperative. Sustainability of overall CASA percentage in a growing business will be an overall indicator of an improved resource profile and will be a key monitorable. 

# **Small scale of operations, driven by regional and product concentration** 

TMB’s overall deposits and advances stood at Rs 61,712 crore and Rs 53,379 crore respectively, as on March 31, 2026. Operations are concentrated in Tamil Nadu with 73% of the branches, 81% of advances and 80% of deposits based in the state as on the same date. However, the bank plans to gradually diversify its operations by opening new branches outside Tamil Nadu gradually. While the bank benefits from its established track record in its core geography, ability to diversify its presence is a key monitorable. Moreover, within the overall advances mix, the bank has a high concentration of agriculture loans (38%), MSME loans (29%) followed by retail loans (28%) and corporate loans (8%) as of March 31, 2026. 

# **Exposure to risks arising from the dispute over ownership** 

The ownership dispute has been carrying on for the past several years. While the bank has been holding its annual general meeting, prolonged adjudication of cases related to this dispute becomes a constraint. Hence, issues relating to shareholding and ownership of the bank will continue to be  monitored. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

The bank has a strong liquidity position with liquidity coverage ratio (LCR) of 134% and excess statutory liquidity ratio (SLR) of 4.31%, as on March 31, 2026. The bank's liquidity also benefits from access to systemic sources of funds such as the liquidity adjustment facility from the RBI, access to the call money market, and refinance limits from development finance institutions. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

TMB should maintain adequate capitalisation to meet its business growth and manage its asset-quality related risk. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Sustained scale-up of operations with overall GNPA maintained below 3% on a steady-state basis Sustenance of profitability at comfortable levels 

# **Downward factors** 

Significant weakening in asset quality, with GNPAs exceeding 5% and translating into pressure on earnings and capitalisation metrics 

Any pressure on deposit profile via outflows 

Weakening of capital position with significant deterioration in the common equity tier 1 ratio 

# **<u>About the Bank</u>** 

TMB is a small private sector bank, with an asset base of Rs 75,300 crore as on March 31, 2026. The bank was set up in 1921 by 10 visionaries of the Nadar Community. Headquartered in Thoothukudi, TMB is present mainly in south India, with 622 branches as on March 31, 2026. Gross advances and deposits stood at Rs 53,379 crore and Rs 61,712 crore, respectively, as on March 31, 2026.
