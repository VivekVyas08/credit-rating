### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has revised its outlook on the long-term bank loan facilities of Sarda Energy and Minerals Ltd (SEML; part of the Sarda group) to **‘Positive’** from ‘Stable’ while reaffirming the rating at **‘Crisil AA-’** . Further, Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A1+’ rating on the short-term bank loan facilities. 

The revision in outlook reflects the significant improvement in business risk profile largely driven by higher contribution from energy segment, post-acquisition of SKS Power thermal plant in August 2024. Crisil Ratings expects this improvement to sustain considering the integrated nature of operations which benefits both the energy and the steel segments. 

In FY25, sales increased to Rs 4,643 crore (FY24: Rs 3,868 crore) with an EBITDA of Rs 1,237 crore (FY24: Rs 819 crore), with energy business contributing ~52% of the EBITDA. EBITDA margin stood at 27.1% in FY25 compared to 21% in FY24. The adjusted ROCE continues to remain steady at 12-14% levels. The thermal power plant had robust plant load factors (PLFs) of 70.5% in FY25, which further increased to ~90% in Q1 FY26. Sustenance of PLFs above 80% will be monitorable. The run-rate for this fiscal remains strong with revenue of Rs 1,633 crore with EBITDA of Rs 690 crore in Q1FY26. SEML is expected to generate EBITDA of over Rs 1600 crore in FY26, largely driven by strong PLFs, healthy merchant tariffs, and favourable cost curves in thermal power segment. Further, The optimization of captive coal reserves has lowered the variable costs. 

The ratings continue to reflect the established market position of the Sarda group in the steel segment. Revenue from steel and ferro segment at SEML was Rs 2,341 crore (FY24: Rs 2,663 crore) with an EBITDA of Rs 528 crore (FY24: Rs 512 crore). EBITDA margin stood at 23% in FY25 compared to 19% in FY24. The performance is likely to remain healthy during fiscal 2026. Further, the operating performance of the subsidiary of SEML, Sarda Metals and Alloys Ltd (SMAL; Crisil A/Positive/Crisil A1), remained stable during fiscal 2025 and is expected to remain healthy over the medium term, as seen in the performance for June 2026 quarter. 

The financial risk profile of the company continues to be strong with adjusted interest coverage ratio at 5.62 times in fiscal 2025 (6.2 times in fiscal 2024), adjusted gearing at 0.46 times in fiscal 2025 (0.41 time in fiscal 2024) along with strong liquidity (cash and cash equivalent stood at around ~Rs 1,602 crore as on July 31, 2025). This will be supported by healthy operating earnings of around Rs 1,000-1,100 crore from energy segment (FY25: Rs 691 crore) and over Rs 500 cr from the steel business (FY25: Rs 528 crore; and FY24: Rs 512 crore). The planned capital expenditure (capex) of Rs 400-500 crore per annum for commissioning and expansion of coal mines is expected to be done through internal accruals with no reliance on external debt. Any debt-funded capex over the medium term will be monitorable. 

These strengths are partially offset by limited long-term offtake contracts, susceptibility to volatility in merchant prices, and exposure to cyclicality in the steel and ferro alloy industries. 

At present, SEML’s thermal plant has tied up 100 MW capacity with strong counterparties. Additionally, SEML has received the Letter of Award (LoA) from Karnataka 100 MW with Karnataka discom supported by healthy tariff of Rs 5.78 per unit under the long-term arrangement for 25 years. Further, SEML has also emerged as L1 bidder with Kerela discoms for 200 

MW capacity backed by healthy tariffs. The successful tie-up of the incremental capacity over the next twelve months will be crucial for any upward rating action and remains a key monitorable. 

# **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of SEML and SMAL, together referred to as the Sarda group. SMAL is a wholly owned subsidiary of SEML and both are engaged in the same line of business. Furthermore, SEML has guaranteed Rs 52 crore of the debt of SMAL (outstanding debt against this guarantee was Rs 9.01 crore as on March 31, 2025) and provided support to the latter through unsecured loans in the past. Crisil Ratings has also fully consolidated the subsidiaries, Madhya Bharat Power Corporation Ltd (MBPCL) and Chhattisgarh Hydro Power LLP (CHP LLP). The remaining subsidiaries, joint ventures and controlled entities are treated as financial investments.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

- **Strong business risk profile:** The company's thermal portfolio is anchored by a 600 MW operational power plant, which was recently acquired through the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC). Notably, the power plant's performance has been improving, with PLFs levels reaching 90% during Q1FY26 (70.5% in FY25) ensuring optimal performance. SEML has a strong fuel security arrangement, with ~75% of its coal requirements met through fuel supply agreements (FSAs) and captive coal mine in Chhattisgarh, which has an operational capacity of 1.68 MTPA, expected to increase to 1.8 MTPA and further expand to 5.2 MTPA in phases. The company is also a major player in the steel and steel intermediates industry in eastern and Central India, with a diversified revenue stream from the sale of intermediate and finished steel products. Furthermore, the company generates revenue from the sale of ferro alloys, and has a presence in the hydro sector with approximately 168 MW operational capacity. With over four decades of experience in the steel industry, the promoters have strategically diversified the company's portfolio to create a robust and integrated business. 

- **Diversified operations with strong backward integration** : The company continues to have varied sources of revenue with operations across mining, steel, ferro alloys and power business (hydro power and thermal power plant). The company's existing coal mining operations, strategically located in close proximity to the power plant, are anticipated to create a synergistic operational linkage, thereby enhancing sales diversification and driving business growth. The company has strong backward integration with a captive iron ore mine that meets 40-50% of the total iron ore requirement while its captive coal mine in Chhattisgarh, with an operational capacity of 1.68 MTPA (expected to increase to 1.8 MTPA), largely meets its coal requirements at a low variable cost ensuring high operational efficiency. Procuring raw material in bulk helps save cost. Similarly, proximity of SMAL to the Vishakhapatnam port in Andhra Pradesh saves logistics cost in the ferro alloys business and helps target global markets. 

- **Strong financial risk profile** : Debt protection metrics were robust, with adjusted interest coverage and net cash accrual to total debt ratios of 5.62 times and 0.34 times, respectively, in fiscal 2025 (6.21 times and 0.38 time, respectively, during fiscal 2024). The capital structure is comfortable, with gearing of 0.46 time as on March 31, 2025 (0.41 time as on March 31, 2024). The financial risk profile is expected to remain healthy due to high cash accrual and limited capex in existing steel and ferro alloys business. Crisil Ratings expects improvement in financial risk profile during fiscal 2026 due to higher accruals with adjusted interest coverage ratio around 10-12 times and gearing to be below 0.40 time for fiscals 2026 and 2027, respectively. The overall debt protection metrics continues to remain healthy. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

- **Limited Long-Term Offtake Arrangements:** The company's thermal plant contributes significantly to its overall business and is exposed to market price volatility and offtake risks due to limited long-term offtake arrangements. Because of inherent variability in merchant prices, the company is exposed to the risk that power may be sold at a rate exceeding the variable cost of generation or may have to significantly reduce its PLF. However, strong power demand over the medium term and high merchant tariffs should support the execution of the PPAs. 

Around 21% of the capacity is currently tied up with long-term contracts which is further expected to increase to ~38% by end of fiscal 2027. Further, SEML has plans to have long term arrangements for ~70-80% of its capacity, with the remaining allocated to short-term contracts or merchant market sales in near term. The successful implementation of this strategy will be key for any upward rating action. 

- **Exposure to cyclicality in the steel and ferro alloy industries** : These are closely linked to the domestic and global economies as growth depends on the level of construction and infrastructure activities. Any downturn in the economic cycle adversely impacts demand. Furthermore, any change in government policies on imports or exports affect the industries. Currently, exports account for approximately 15-20% of the group's revenue. In addition to demand risk, the industries remain exposed to volatility in raw material prices and finished product realisations, which can impact operating margin. The prices are largely subject to global commodity prices. However, this is partially offset by the integrated nature of operations, flexibility in changing revenue mix between steel and steel intermediates, ferro alloys and power, and the ability to pass through any change in raw material prices to customers. Any significant variation in demand and pricing scenario will remain a monitorable. 

**<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Liquidity stood at Rs 1601.5 crore (cash and cash equivalent standalone) while unutilised fund-based bank limit was Rs 191 crore, as on July 31, 2025. Net cash accrual is expected to be over Rs 1,200 crore in fiscal 2026 which, along with existing liquidity, should be adequate to meet debt obligation and capex. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Positive 

The scale of the group is expected to improve driven by healthy generation in the thermal power segment. The financial risk profile is expected to remain strong over the medium term. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Significantly higher-than-expected operating cash accrual from SKS Power on sustained basis, driven by rise in tied-up long-term PPAs at remunerative tariffs and low cost of generation, supporting PLFs of more than 80% 

- Healthy operating performance with continued volume growth in metals business supported by high-capacity utilisation and operating margin of more than 30% due to increased integration, leading to significant and sustained improvement in scale of operations as well as net cash accrual

# **Downward factors**

Any significant increase in exposure to group entities weakening liquidity 

Large, debt-funded capex or acquisition adversely affecting the financial risk profile 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Based in Chhattisgarh, the Sarda group is promoted by Mr Kamal Kishore Sarda, who manages operations with his son, Mr Pankaj Sarda, and a team of professionals. The group manufactures iron pellets, sponge iron, billets, wire rods and wires, along with ferro alloys and eco-friendly fly ash brick. The group has thermal power plants and a waste-heat recovery boiler to generate power that is largely used for captive consumption. The company has expanded its portfolio by acquiring a 600 MW operational power plant, SKS Power Generation Ltd, under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) framework. 

SEML, incorporated in 1973, is the flagship company of the group. It is a vertically integrated producer of steel with captive iron ore and commercial coal mines. It also manufactures and exports niche-grade manganese-based ferro alloys, with selfsufficient captive power from waste heat and coal. Furthermore, the company has successfully diversified its portfolio through the strategic acquisition of SKS Power, marking a significant foray into the energy sector 

SMAL, incorporated in October 2008, is a wholly owned subsidiary of SEML and operates a 2x33-MVA plus 36 MVA ferro alloys plant, backed by an 80-MW captive thermal power plant. 

The group also has interests in hydropower projects through special project vehicles, MBPCL, CHP LLP and Parvatiya Power Ltd with total operational hydropower capacity of ~168 MW.
