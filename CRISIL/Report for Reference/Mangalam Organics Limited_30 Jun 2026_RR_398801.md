### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has removed its ratings on the bank facilities of Mangalam Organics Limited (MOL; part of Mangalam Organics Group) from ‘Rating Watch with Developing Implications’ and has reaffirmed its ratings at ‘ **Crisil BBB+/Crisil A2** ’ while assigned a ‘ **Stable** ’ outlook to the long-term rating. 

The ratings were placed on watch following a fire at one of the camphor manufacturing divisions in July 2025. The watch has now been resolved as the company has fully absorbed the financial impact of the incident in its fiscal 2026 financials by writing off the insurance receivable which group had booked during Q1 fiscal 2026, with no further uncertainty regarding claim realisation. The impact of write-off was partially offset by strong operational performance in Q4 fiscal 2026, supported by robust festive demand, improved product mix, and sharp increase in camphor prices, which aided recovery in profitability and cash flows. 

The ratings continue to reflect the diversified product portfolio and comfortable  financial risk profile, supported by improvement in scale and profitability. These strengths are partially offset by susceptibility to raw material price volatility and large working capital requirement, inherent to the business. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has consolidated the business and financial risk profiles of MOL and Mangalam Brands Pvt Ltd (formerly Campure Pvt Ltd [CPL]), which are strategically important to and have significant degree of operational integration with MOL.

### **–** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

### **Diversified product portfolio** 

The group’s diversified presence across industrial camphor and fast-moving consumer goods (FMCG) continues to support revenue visibility and margin profile. Revenue increased to Rs 622 crore in fiscal 2026 from Rs 530 crore in fiscal 2025, driven by growth in camphor volumes, particularly in higher-margin products such as bhimsen camphor and kapoordani. 

The retail-focused subsidiary is expected to maintain strong growth momentum (~18–20%) supported by product diversification and expanding distribution network, while the parent entity is expected to grow at a steady pace (~10–11%). The increasing contribution from value-added and retail products continues to impart margin stability and reduce reliance on commoditised industrial camphor. 

### **Comfortable financial risk profile** 

The financial risk profile remains supported by healthy networth of Rs 318 crore as against debt of 656.17Crores as on March 31, 2026. Despite elevated debt of Rs 356 crore, gearing and TOLTNW remained moderate at 1.12 times and 1.32 times, respectively, and are expected to improve to below 1 time over the medium term in absence of large debt-funded capex. Debt levels have increased from Rs 271.21Crores as on March 2025 to around Rs 356.17 Crores as on March 2026, driven by higher working capital requirements amidst rising prices of camphor in past 6-9 months due to supply chain constrains. Debt protection metrics strengthened in fiscal 2026, with interest coverage improving to ~3.28 times from 2.74 times in fiscal 2025, supported by improved operating performance. Net cash accrual to debt also improved from 0.12 times in fiscal 2025 to 0.15 times in fiscal 

2026 and is expected to strengthen further in fiscal 2027, reflecting healthy earnings and accrual generation. The strong operational recovery post the fire incident underpins the resilience of the financial profile. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Susceptibility to raw material and camphor prices** 

Profitability remains exposed to volatility in raw material prices and camphor realisations. Key inputs such as alpha-pinene and gum turpentine are largely imported and subject to global supply dynamics. During fiscal 2026, constrained supply of turpentine in major producing regions such as Brazil and the USA, along with forestry restrictions in China, led to a sharp rise in camphor prices in Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27. While this supported margin expansion in fiscal 2026 (operating margin improved to ~14.1% from ~11.1% in fiscal 2025), the inherently volatile nature of the value chain continues to pose risks to margins. Sustained ability to pass on raw material cost fluctuations and maintain product mix will remain key monitorables. 

# **Large working capital requirement** 

The business continues to be working capital intensive, driven by high inventory requirements and fluctuating input prices. Bank limit utilisation increased to ~88% for the 12 months ended May 2026, partly due to higher inventory funding needs amid sharp increase in camphor prices and temporary reduction in sanctioned limits post the fire incident. 

Liquidity pressure arising from working capital intensity is partially mitigated by recovery in inventory levels and expected enhancement of working capital lines. However, maintaining efficient working capital cycle and managing inventory levels during periods of price volatility will remain critical. Group is in process of enhancing its working capital limits from the bank to meet incremental requirement. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Adequate 

Liquidity remains supported by robust accruals and comfortable debt servicing capability. Net cash accruals were around Rs 53 crore in fiscal 2026 against repayment obligations of ~Rs 22 crore, and are expected to improve further to ~Rs 70–72 crore in fiscal 2027 against repayments of ~Rs 33 crore. Despite high bank limit utilisation, liquidity is supported by unencumbered cash of ~Rs 20 crore as of May 2026 and expected additional working capital lines. The improvement in operating performance, especially post Q4 fiscal 2026, has strengthened liquidity position. 

Overall liquidity is expected to remain adequate, backed by healthy accrual generation and absence of significant near-term debtfunded capex. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believe MOL will continue to benefit from the established market position and established relationships with clients 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>**

Growth in revenue and profitability, resulting in operational net cash accrual above Rs 60 crore Improved working capital cycle and financial risk profile 

Improvement in bank limit utilization driven by prudent working capital management 

# **Downward factors:** 

Decline in revenue or operating margin, leading to net cash accrual below Rs 35 crore 

Stretch in the working capital cycle or higher-than-expected debt-funded capex or delay in receipt of enhancement in working capital limits from bank, weakening the financial risk profile 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

MOL was incorporated in 1981, MOL (formerly, Allied Collides Pvt Ltd), is based in Mumbai, and manufactures and trades in fine specialty chemicals, including camphor, resins, and dipentene. The company also has retail operations which includes homecare and personal care products under the brand name of CamPure and Mangalam. Operations are managed by Mr. Kamal Kumar Dujodwala and Mr. Pannkaj Dujodwala.
