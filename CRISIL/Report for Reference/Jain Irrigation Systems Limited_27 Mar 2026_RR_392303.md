## **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has revised its outlook on the long-term bank facilities and non-convertible debentures of Jain Irrigation Systems Ltd (JISL) to **‘Negative’** from ‘Stable’ while reaffirming the rating at **‘Crisil BBB-’** . The rating on the short-term bank facilities has been reaffirmed at ‘Crisil A3’. 

The revision in outlook is primarily due to elevated 0.01% NCD refinancing risk emanating from sizeable repayment obligations of almost Rs. 689 crore due in fiscal 2027 (Rs 241 crore at the end of September 2026 and Rs 449 crore at end of March 2027) towards the 0.01% Non-Convertible Debentures (NCD) and External Commercial Borrowings (ECB2), as the expected standalone operating cashflows of Rs ~200-220 crore (net of capex) will be insufficient to meet these obligations. The timely servicing of the 0.01% NCDs is contingent on receipt of identified overdue receivables (IOR), project receivables, proceeds of non-core asset monetisation as well as incentives from Government of Maharashtra, where progress has remained slow. Crisil Ratings will continue to monitor the progress towards these avenues over the next few months, and this remains critical. 

Owing to slow progress on these avenues, JISL is also exploring refinancing option for the 0.01% NCDs and is in discussion with potential lenders for the same. JISL’s management expects closure of the refinancing of NCDs in next 3 months. As receipts from other identified avenues remain contingent and timing remain uncertain, Crisil Ratings believes timely closure of refinancing of NCDs over the next few months will be a key rating sensitivity factor. 

The recovery of IOR has been slow with outstanding balance of Rs 196 crore as on December 31, 2025 as against Rs 199 crore on March 31, 2025 and Rs 211 crore on March 31, 2024. The project receivables remain high at Rs 923 crore as on December 31, 2025 as against Rs 887 crore as on March 31, 2025. While, JISL expects significant realisation in fiscal 2027 as most projects are near completion stage and are expected to be complete over the next 9-12 months, timely realization of these receivables remains key for 0.01% NCDs servicing and will be a monitorable. 

The financial risk profile of the company also remains constrained due to elevated debt levels with interest coverage ratio of around 2.5-3.0 times estimated for fiscal 2026 as against 2.1 time for fiscal 2025 and the net cash accrual to adjusted debt around 0.10-0.15 time for fiscal 2026 as against 0.10 time for fiscal 2025. Also, liquidity remains stretched with continued full utilisation of bank lines due to working capital intensive nature of operations and elevated receivables. Although the company expects substantial recovery of these receivables in fiscal 2027 as well as enhancement of bank limits in next 6 months, the same remains monitorable. 

Standalone revenues saw recovery in fiscal 2026 with a growth of 13% in nine months ended December 2025 after seeing degrowth in fiscal 2025, led by strong demand in the hi-tech agri segment, which saw 27% on-year growth, owing to strong traction in hi-tech agri, exports and solar pump segments. While domestic plastics segment de-grew in the first half of fiscal 2026, owing to early and extended monsoons, the segment saw recovery in third quarter with pick-up in demand.  In the 

medium term, the sales growth shall also be supported by higher central government budgetary allocation towards irrigation and allied projects, however, actual release of funds under various schemes remain monitorable (e.g. JJM in Maharashtra). 

Standalone operating margin slightly improved to 13.9% for nine months ended December 31, 2025 as against 13.6% for fiscal 2025 owing to increased cost absorption, low raw material prices, better mix and higher share of exports in the MIS segment. While the margins remain susceptible to volatility in raw material prices especially crude linked, it is expected to sustain at ~13-14% over the medium term due to increased scale and demonstrated ability to pass on of prices rises immediately. 

The ratings continue to factor in established business risk profile supported by its leading position in the Micro Irrigation Systems (MIS) segment, long track record of operations and extensive experience of promoters and a wide dealer network. 

# **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of JISL (standalone) and its subsidiaries and step-down subsidiaries engaged in the international plastics business. The entities, collectively referred to as JISL, have same management, financial linkages and similar businesses. Crisil Ratings has not consolidated the agro-processing business under Jain Farm Fresh Foods Ltd (JFFFL) given the minimal business linkages and restrictions on cashflow between JISL and JFFFL, following debt restructuring undertaken at JISL.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Established market position and diversified revenue stream** 

JISL (standalone) has a diversified revenue profile with presence across multiple business segments, hi-tech agri inputs (contributing to 59% of revenue in fiscal 2025), consisting of MIS; tissue culture and plastics (41%), consisting of plastic pipes and sheets. The company has a strong market position in its business segments. In the MIS segment, it is the leader in the domestic market supported by a strong distribution network and in-house research and development (R&D) capabilities. It has an established position in the domestic pipes segment. It benefits from synergies among business segments, which are largely focused on the agricultural community. 

# **Revamped business model with focus on leveraging the extensive dealer network** 

JISL has over 4,000 dealers and sales to dealers and institutional customers contribute to over 65% of revenue, as against less than 60% a few years ago. This is a focus segment for the company especially with no new EPC orders being taken up. The order book of JISL (including overseas plastic business) was Rs 734 crore as on December 31, 2025, vis a vis Rs 683 crore as on March 31, 2025 and Rs 1280 crore as on March 31, 2024. It shrunk from March 2025 as it only includes orders for ongoing EPC projects, export sales and sales to institutions, and does not factor in orders from dealer network (which are received on daily basis). Of the outstanding order book, ongoing EPC projects contribute around Rs ~110 crore, which will be executed by fiscal 2027. JISL has focused on its retail channel and also entered solar pump business to compensate for the loss of revenue from EPC projects. 

# **Extensive experience of the promoters** 

JISL was founded by the Late Mr Bhavarlal Jain who was the pioneer of micro irrigation in India. Currently, his sons Mr Ashok Jain (Chairman), Mr Anil Jain (Managing Director), Mr Ajit Jain (Joint Managing Director) and Mr Atul Jain (Joint Managing Director) are managing overall operations of the company and are assisted by professionals, agricultural scientists, engineers and technicians managing various business segments. Next generation of Jain family members are also actively involved in day-to-day operations of the business. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Elevated debt refinancing risk due to slow progress on identified avenues for repayment of NCD and ECB2** 

Out of the total term debt of Rs 889 crore as on December 31, 2025, the NCD and ECB2 outstanding is Rs 829 crore (includes ECB2 outstanding of Rs 43 crore). Majority of the repayment of NCDs and ECB2 debt is scheduled in fiscal 2027, with the first repayment tranche of Rs 241 crore in September 2026 and Rs 449 crore subsequently in March 2027, and balance Rs 140 crore in fiscal 2028 comprises of each in September 2027 and March 2028. As standalone operating cash flows (net of capex) of around Rs 200-225 crore will be insufficient to meet the 0.01% NCDs obligations falling due in fiscal 2027, the timely servicing of 0.01% NCDs will remain contingent on recovery of project receivables (outstanding of Rs ~923 crore) and IOR, sale of identified land parcel (~Rs 125 crore), and receipt of Government of Maharashtra incentives (~Rs 150 crore), where progress has been slow in the past 12 months. JISL’s management is also exploring complete refinancing of these outstanding 0.01% NCDs and expects closure of the same within 3 months, which will remain critical as the timing for other avenues remain uncertain and will be a key rating monitorable. The company has also obtained shareholder approval to raise up to Rs. 500 crore through the Qualified Institutional Placement route, which can provide additional financial flexibility, however, the timely closure of the same remains monitorable. 

# **Working capital intensive operations led by high project receivables** 

Operations have remained working capital intensive with significant receivables outstanding. Because of focus on EPC projects till fiscal 2019, the company’s working capital requirement was large. The EPC projects segment had issues related to design approval, water availability for testing and change in government. Also, slow and delayed realisation of project receivables from state governments contributed to sizeable stretch in project receivables, which resulted in liquidity 

mismatch and subsequent default on debt obligations. While, JISL has stopped taking up new EPC projects since September 2019. the receivables for EPC projects were significantly high at Rs 923 crore as on December 31, 2025, as against Rs 887 crore as on March 31, 2025, and Rs 880 crore as on March 31, 2024, indicating slower recovery of project receivables keeping liquidity constrained. It increased as on December 2025 due to higher execution of pending projects. While, the completion of the pending EPC projects expected over the next 12 months, and the recovery of the respective receivables will remain monitorable. 

Additionally, in the last two years, there have been lower-than-expected pace of recovery of identified overdue receivables (IOR) notified at the time of restructuring, which has remained largely similar at Rs 196 crore as of December 31, 2025, compared to Rs 199 crore as of March 31, 2025, and Rs 211 crore as of March 31, 2024. 

# **Modest financial risk profile** 

The company had modest financial risk profile due to average debt protection metrices with expected interest coverage ratio of around 2.5-3.0 times and the net cash accrual to adjusted debt around 0.10-0.15 time in fiscal 2026 on account of high debt of Rs. 2706 crore (including international plastic business) as on December 31, 2025, which includes 0.01% NCD and ECB2 of Rs 829 crore. Additionally, net cash accruals will remain insufficient for meeting debt obligations due in fiscal 2027 and hence recovery of substantial portion of receivables and/or timely refinancing of the NCDs, amongst other avenues will remain a key monitorable for financial risk profile. 

# **Susceptibility to volatility in raw material prices and risk related to foreign exchange fluctuations** 

JISL is exposed to fluctuations in the prices of polyethylene, polyvinyl chloride (PVC), polymer resins, which are used as raw materials. Prices of these commodities are determined by their demand-supply scenario and by the price of petroleum which is crude oil linked. While JISL is able to pass on the price volatility to customers for a large portion of sales, profitability remains exposed to raw material price volatility for fixed price contracts. Also, operations involve sizeable export sales as well as import of raw materials and are exposed to adverse fluctuations in foreign exchange (forex) rates. While there is a natural hedge from exports for part of the forex exposure, the company also hedges its forex risk using derivatives. That said, the company is able to pass on fluctuations in raw material prices to the end customer and along with inventory management, it has maintained operating margins of 13%-14%. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Stretched 

JISL’s sanctioned fund-based bank limit of Rs 1,540 crore (increased from Rs 1,505 crore) remains almost fully utilised and liquid surplus continues to be modest. The working capital-intensive nature of operations has also constrained liquidity. While JISL has stopped taking up new EPC projects since September 2019, the recovery of receivables from EPC projects was slower than anticipated keeping liquidity constrained. 

A Trust and Retention Account (TRA) is maintained by lenders, wherein cash flow from operations is parked in advance against forthcoming obligation, and the balance is permitted to be utilised by the company. Continuation of the ASM (Agency for Specialized Monitoring) led monitoring of the TRA provides comfort around timely debt servicing. 

While, annual cash accrual from JISL (standalone) and the international plastics business is expected at Rs 350-450 crore over fiscals 2026-2028, will be sufficient to cover term debt (excluding 0.01% NCDs and ECB2) obligation  of Rs 60 crore due in March 2026 and capex requirements, it would not be sufficient for fully to meet 0.01% NCD and ECB2 debt obligation of Rs 689 crore due in fiscal 2027. The Company is looking at various avenues for financing 0.01% NCD obligation, while priority remains recovery of project receivables of over Rs 900 crore and IOR of Rs 165 crore, incentives receivable of Rs ~150 crore from the Government of Maharashtra in next 6-9 months, Rs ~125 crore realisation from sale of land expected in two tranches, with first tranche expected by June 2026 of Rs 80 crore and balance by December 2026. 

JISL has recently received a working capital sanction of Rs 40 crore from UCO Bank that was availed in January 2026, comprising Rs 35 crore of fund-based and Rs 5 crore of non-fund-based limits which helped the company to tie up the balance untied portion out of the total assessment limit of Rs 1,540 crore (fund-based) and Rs 744 crore (non-fund-based limit). Furthermore, it has received incremental sanction of Rs 100 crore of fund-based facilities (as assessed by lead bank i.e. State Bank of India), expected to be tied up in next 2-3 months. Also, it has received in principle sanction of Rs 100 crore fund-based facilities and Rs ~72 crore of non-fund based facilities from a new lender; however, timely release of the facilities for working capital purposes remains monitorable. 

Previously, the company has received facilities outside consortium of Rs 307 crore, of which Rs 257 crore is available, and the balance is linked to sanction conditions. These include Rs 100 crore e-vendor financing scheme (e-VFS) facility, of which Rs 50 crore is available; dealer financing limits of Rs 150 crore without any guarantee or recourse to the company; and e-treds limits of Rs 57 crore. Banks have also extended an LCBD facility of Rs 160 crore, which helps the company with LC discounting. The conversion of warrants issued in November 2023 resulted in a fresh fund infusion of around Rs 150 crore in May 2025, which was utilised for working capital purposes. The timely enhancement of limits can provide liquidity cushion, working capital flexibility, and will remain monitorable. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Negative 

Crisil Ratings believes the financial risk profile may further weaken and liquidity may get stretched with cash accruals insufficient for servicing significant obligations and slow progress of other avenues for servicing NCD obligations. Firm steps 

towards timely refinancing of 0.01% NCD and ECB2 repayment remain monitorable. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

- Timely closure of the refinancing of 0.01% NCDs in next 3 months, mitigating the debt refinancing risk to significant extent and significantly improving the liquidity profile 

- Better-than-expected operating profits resulting in improvement in debt protection metrics with interest coverage to above 2.80-3.00 times 

- Continued improvement in the working capital cycle with correction in debtor days owing to substantial realisation of project receivables resulting in prepayment of 0.01% NCD and ECB2 

# **Downward factors** 

Delay in refinancing of NCDs and ECB beyond next 3-4 months 

Slow progress on the avenues identified over next 3 months for servicing upcoming 0.01% NCD obligations 

- Any further stretch in the working capital cycle on account of delays in recovery of receivables or any adverse or unexpected conditions impacting liquidity enhancement plans 

- Lower-than-expected operating profits or any increase in debt resulting in the total debt/OPBITDA at over 4.5-5.0 times Any sizeable advances to JFFFL or any large debt-funded capex or acquisitions impacting liquidity and debt metrics 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

JISL was incorporated in 1986 by the Late Mr Bhavarlal H Jain. The company started operations by trading in agricultural inputs and equipment. In 1980, it began manufacturing PVC pipes and commenced MIS operations in 1987. JISL has diversified its presence across multiple segments throughout the agricultural value chain and operates across three broad business segments: hi-tech agri inputs, plastics and agro processing. The company underwent restructuring owing to liquidity issues and the resolution plan was implemented on March 25, 2022. As on December 31, 2025, on fully diluted basis considering conversion of outstanding warrants, the promoters held 26.68% stake in JISL, of which 40.77% is pledged.
