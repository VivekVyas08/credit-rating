## **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil BBB/Stable’ rating on the non-convertible debentures and long-term bank facilities of Moneyboxx Finance Limited (MFL). 

The rating reflects MFL’s comfortable capital position and the extensive experience of its promoters in the financial services industry. However, these strengths are partially offset by the small scale of operations, modest asset quality, and subdued earnings profile. 

Overall assets under management (AUM) declined to Rs 893.0 crore as on March 31, 2026, from Rs 927.2 crore as on March 31, 2025, and Rs 729.6 crore a year earlier, primarily due to subdued business growth and the transfer of assets to an asset reconstruction company (ARC). The present business model is structured to essentially cater to micro enterprises. The company follows a branch-based business model and currently has 156 branches in Tier 2 cities and below, across 12 states. The company initially started operations in Rajasthan, and then gradually expanded its footprint to other states such as Madhya Pradesh, Haryana, Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Bihar, and Gujarat. In fiscal 2025, it expanded to four southern states-Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Telangana. 

The asset quality has been impacted in line with the stress seen across the unsecured segment. The 90+ days past due (dpd) was 6.1% as on March 31, 2026. It witnessed a steep increase during fiscal 2025, from 1.5% as on March 31, 2024, to 6.9% as on March 31, 2025. The increase in delinquencies was due to socio-political issues and the resultant stress across the customer profile. Although the 90+ dpd ratio has come down slightly during fiscal 2026, this improvement is largely driven by the ARC transaction undertaken in the second and third quarters of fiscal 2026, involving the transfer of non-performing assets (NPAs) and written-off assets. The collection efficiency under the ‘X’ bucket (non-overdue buckets) averaged 98.1% for the past six months. Additionally, the company has maintained adequate provisions for its stressed accounts, with a provision cover of 51.1% as on March 31, 2026. Nevertheless, Crisil Ratings believes that despite these early signs of improvement in collections, the company’s ability to show substantial improvement in portfolio quality will be closely monitored, given the inherent borrower profile and high exposure to livestock (53.1% of the portfolio), which is mitigated by the company’s focus on essential sectors and increasing focus on secured lending. 

## **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has assessed the business and financial risk profiles of MFL on a standalone basis. 

## **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Comfortable capital position:** Capitalisation metrics are supported by regular capital infusion. The promoters have demonstrated their ability to raise capital from investors by raising an equity of Rs 304.4 crore since inception, including Rs 91.9 crore in fiscal 2025 (of which, promoter and promoter group made an equity investment of Rs 11.54 crore). In January 2026, the company disclosed stock-exchange approvals for a proposal to raise Rs 43.3 crore through a preferential issue to promoters, promoter group entities, and select non-promoter investors, subject to shareholder and regulatory approvals. Of this, the company has raised Rs 33.4 crore in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 (of Rs 33.4 crore, Rs 26.6 crore was infused by the promoters). Raising capital at regular intervals has resulted in networth increasing to Rs 296.2 crore as on March 31, 2026, from Rs 260.7 crore as on March 31, 2025. Reported gearing also remained comfortable at 2.2 times as on March 31, 

2026, and 2.4 times as on March 31, 2025 (2.6 times as on March 31, 2024). The company has indicated that steady state gearing will be maintained at 4 times in accordance with portfolio growth. Nevertheless, given its growth plans and the asset segment in which it operates, the ability of the company to keep raising sufficient capital while maintaining gearing at comfortable levels will be a key rating sensitivity factor. 

**Extensive experience of the promoters and management in the financial services industry:** The leadership team at MFL is experienced and its expertise has not only allowed the company to establish a diligent in-house risk management framework but also set up an efficient post disbursal follow-up process. The company was founded by Mayur Modi and Deepak Aggarwal, who have extensive experience in the financial services industry. They have more than two decades of experience in companies such as HSBC, JP Morgan, Bank of America, Deutsche Bank and KPMG. Other members of the leadership team have an average experience of over 20 years in their respective fields. The company, through its lending practices, is largely retail focused and has been enabling financing to new-to-credit customers in rural and semi-urban areas. It strives to provide sustainable livelihoods through financing products for its customers. 

The experience and understanding of the promoters have enabled the company to put in place comfortable risk management systems. MFL has adopted a branch-based model with most of its operations from origination to disbursements happening digitally. It has a proprietary credit risk model used to evaluate credit decisions. The company leverages its on-ground presence for loan monitoring. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Subdued earnings profile, constrained by elevated credit costs and operating expenses:** Profitability remained subdued, with the company reporting a profit after tax (PAT) of Rs 1.3 crore with return on managed assets (RoMA) of 0.1% in fiscal 2026, the same as in fiscal 2025. Total income as a percentage of average managed assets increased from 19.1% in fiscal 2025 to 19.5% in fiscal 2026. However, the cost of borrowings also went up with interest expense to average borrowings rising to 12.9% in fiscal 2026 from 11.7% in fiscal 2025, partially offsetting the improvement in income yields. Operating expenses fell marginally to 9.7% in fiscal 2026 compared to 10.1% of average managed assets in fiscal 2025. The decline in the same was limited due to senior leadership additions (including key hires in credit and collections), continued investments to strengthen on ground collections and lower-than-expected growth in AUM amidst shift towards secured lending. The credit costs also remained elevated at 2.6% in fiscal 2026; at 2.7% in fiscal 2025. The provision cover for gross NPAs stood at 51.1% as on March 31, 2026. 

The company’s ability to improve its earnings profile, driven by improvement in portfolio quality and kicking in of operational efficiencies, will remain monitorable. 

**Modest asset quality:** The asset quality has been impacted in line with the stress seen across the unsecured segment. The 90+ dpd was at 6.1% as on March 31, 2026. It witnessed a steep increase during fiscal 2025, due to socio-political issues and the resultant stress across the customer profile. While the 90+ dpd has come down slightly during fiscal 2026, it is largely driven by the ARC transaction undertaken in the second and third quarters of fiscal 2026 involving the transfer of NPAs and written-off assets. The stress on the asset quality aligns with industry-wide stress in the microfinance institution (MFI) sector, driven by subdued rural economic growth, general elections, erratic weather, and segmental overindebtedness. 

To address asset quality concerns, the company has taken steps to improve collections by creating a dedicated collection infrastructure with a team of 300+ on-ground collection personnel, comprising both soft bucket (0–30 dpd) and hard bucket (30+ / 90+ dpd) teams. Approximately 100 personnel focus on hard bucket, while ~150 handle early-stage delinquencies. The collections team is divided to have focused efforts on collections. This is a shift from reliance on the sales team for collections earlier. Additionally, a collection head has been hired to lead this vertical, which is supported by an outsourced tele-calling team and digital tools, such as in-house collection app for dynamic case allocation and tracking, SMS, IVR, and bots, to target early delinquency, as well as intensified efforts on legal recourse for non-performing asset (NPA) cases. The collection efficiency under the ‘X’ bucket (non-overdue buckets) has remained at 98.1% over the past six months. 

The company has also taken steps to clean up the stressed portfolio by transferring NPAs and write offs, together totaling Rs 93.2 crore to an ARC trust. It continued to hold security receipts of Rs 39.6 crore as on March 31, 2026. The 90+ dpd including security receipts and write offs stood at 10.9% as on March 31, 2026. 

Crisil Ratings believes that while collections have shown an improving trend in recent times, their sustenance remains monitorable. Further, the secured portfolio of the company has grown over the last few quarters and is largely unseasoned. The ability of the company to show substantial improvement in portfolio quality as the loan book grows and newer asset classes season remains monitorable. 

**Small scale of operations:** AUM decreased by 3.7% from Rs 927.2 crore as on March 31, 2025 (Rs 729.6 crore as on March 31, 2024) to Rs 893.0 crore as on March 31, 2026. This decline is  partially due to sales to ARC, along with muted business growth and lending partnerships. Nevertheless, AUM remains well-diversified across 12 states, with a focus on essential sectors. The company continues to strengthen geographic diversification through expansion into newer states with an increasing proportion of higher-ticket-size secured loans. The current loan portfolio mix comprises 53.1% towards livestock, 19.7% toward trading, 10.7% toward services, 8.3% toward kirana and 8.2% toward manufacturing. 

Apart from geographical diversification, MFL has also diversified its product mix between the secured and unsecured segments. The company began offering secured business loans against property (LAP) from fiscal 2023, and its share in total AUM has increased from 6.0% share as on March 31, 2023, to 68% as on March 31, 2026. The share of secured loans is expected to further increase to 80.0% (including unsecured loans disbursed under guarantee programmes) of total AUM by fiscal 2027. 

Furthermore, to increase the share of secured loans, the company has commenced commercial solar loan origination through original equipment manufacturer (OEM) partnerships, which is expected to support portfolio growth and improve operating efficiency. The company also plans to expand unsecured personal loans through technology-enabled partnership models, which are expected to support portfolio diversification while maintaining operating efficiency, credit costs and yields. 

Crisil Ratings expects the pace of growth to remain healthy over the medium term, driven by the company’s enhanced focus on geographical expansion. However, the ability to scale up operations, while showcasing improvement in asset quality and profitability, remains monitorable. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Adequate 

Liquidity position is adequate with Rs 68.4 crore of buffer as on April 30, 2026, including cash and bank balance of Rs 21.4 crore, investments in liquid mutual funds, fixed deposits or corporate bonds of Rs 45.0 crore and undrawn working capital lines of Rs 2.0 crore. This covers one month of debt obligation with a cover of 1.3 times. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes MFL will continue to benefit from its experienced promoters and management and will maintain its comfortable capital position over the medium term. However, sustainable improvement in asset quality metrics and earnings profile remain monitorable. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors:** 

Ability to scale up the book while improving asset quality 

Stable earnings profile with return on managed assets (ROMA) of over 2% on steady-state basis 

- Strengthening of the capital position through raising equity capital and gearing remaining below 4 times on steady state basis 

# **Downward factors:** 

- Lack of sustained improvement in asset quality (with NPAs remaining over 5%), leading to subdued profitability with negligible profits 

Moderation in capitalisation metrics with gearing beyond 4 times 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

MFL is a non-deposit taking, base layer non-banking financial company (NBFC-BL) registered with the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and listed on the Bombay Stock Exchange and National Stock Exchange. It is promoted by Moneyboxx Capital Pvt Ltd and provides small-ticket business loans to micro and small enterprises. MFL commenced commercial operations in February 2019. As on March 31, 2026, the company had 156 branches across 12 states. All branches cater to customers present in Tier 2 and below cities.
