# **Update on Apollo Hospitals Enterprise Limited** 

_Note: None of the Directors on Crisil Ratings Limited’s Board are members of rating committee and thus do not participate in discussion or assignment of any ratings. The Board of Directors also does not discuss any ratings at its meetings._ 

Crisil Ratings has taken note of the recent announcement made by Apollo Hospital Enterprises Limited (AHEL; 'Crisil AA+/Positive/Crisil A1+') on May 20, 2026. As per the announcement, AHEL's board of directors has approved a transaction between its wholly-owned subsidiary, Apollo Health and Lifestyle Limited (AHLL; 'Crisil AA-/Positive'), and Kids Clinic India Ltd (KCIL), which operates a chain of mother and child and fertility clinics under the brand 'Cloudnine' across India. As part of the transaction, AHLL will divest its entire stake in its wholly owned subsidiaries - Apollo Specialty Hospitals Pvt Ltd (ASHPL; 'Crisil A+/Watch Developing'), which operates Apollo Cradle mother and child hospitals, and Apollo Fertility Centre Pvt Ltd (AFCPL; 'Crisil A+/Watch Developing') - to KCIL at an enterprise value of Rs.1550 crores. This will include a cash consideration of Rs.765 crores, and an equity stake of 9.9% in the merged KCIL for a consideration of Rs.785 crores. 

With 9.9% equity stake in KCIL, AHLL will be the largest non-financial shareholder of the platform, which is majorly owned by marquee private equity players besides founder promoters. The transaction is subject to approval from the Competition Commission of India (CCI), which is expected to be obtained within the next couple of months. The deal is expected to be completed in a phased manner by the third quarter of fiscal 2026. Upon receipt of CCI approval, a portion of the proceeds will be disbursed to AHLL which will be utilized to repay the entire bank debt in ASHPL and AFCPL prior to the final completion of the transaction. The divestment of the Cradle and Fertility businesses is expected to simplify AHLL's overall business structure. 

Crisil Ratings has also noted that AHEL has obtained board approval on May 20, 2026, for the merger of its wholly-owned subsidiary, Apollo Hospitals North Limited (AHNL; 'Crisil AA/Watch Positive/Crisil A1+'), into AHEL. The proposed consolidation aims to enhance operational efficiency and unify the brand's presence across the region. The merger is expected to be implemented through the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) route, with a projected timeline of 10-12 months for completion. The effective date of the transaction has been set as April 1, 2026. 

Crisil Ratings also notes the  completion of the acquisition of  AHLL’s remaining 30.6% stake held by International Finance Corporation, (IFC) and IFC EAF Apollo Investment Company (IFC EAF) by AHEL for a total purchase consideration of Rs 1254 crores during March 2026 and 0.4% held by ESOP holders for a consideration of Rs.18.69 crores during April 2026. The stake acquisition has been funded from AHEL's healthy cash surpluses which stood at over Rs.2,000 crore at March 31, 2026. Post the acquisition, AHEL’s shareholding in AHLL  increased from 68.8% to 100%. Earlier, on September 12, 2025, the board of directors of AHEL approved the acquisition of remaining 30.6% stake held by IFC in AHLL. AHEL and IFC mutually agreed on an exit arrangement, bypassing IFC's right to exercise its put option as per the shareholder’s agreement, after negotiations between the two parties. 

Crisil Ratings has also noted that AHEL has obtained board approval on May 20, 2026, for the merger of its wholly-owned subsidiary, AHNL, into AHEL. The proposed consolidation aims to enhance operational efficiency and unify the brand's presence across the region. The merger is expected to be implemented through the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) route, with a projected timeline of 10-12 months for completion. The effective date of the transaction has been set as April 1, 2026. 

The ratings continue to reflect the strong market position of AHEL as the largest private healthcare provider in the domestic market, pan India geographical presence, solid operating efficiencies and the company’s healthy financial risk profile, as well as robust liquidity position. These strengths are partially offset by reducing but continuing losses at the Apollo 24*7 platform, and exposure to regulatory risks.
