### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A+/Stable/Crisil A1’ ratings on the bank facilities of Allcargo Terminals Ltd (ATL). 

The rating reaffirmation follows the continued established market position of the company in the container freight station (CFS) business, wherein the company operates seven facilities (including the inland container depot [ICD] in Dadri, Uttar Pradesh, through a joint venture) demonstrating healthy addressable market share in key ports. The ratings also reflect the parentage of the Allcargo group, and a comfortable financial risk profile, supported by steady cash generation, modest external debt and comfortable cash reserves. 

In fiscal 2025, ATL registered a lower year-on-year volume growth of ~1% (~6.20 lakh Twenty-foot Equivalent Units (TEUs) versus 6.13 lakh TEUs handled in the previous fiscal) which, with modest improvement in realisation, resulted in overall revenue growth of ~3% (Rs 755 crore in fiscal 2025 as against Rs 733 crore in the previous fiscal). During the first half of fiscal 2026, the company achieved modest revenue growth of 3%, with volume growth remaining flat at 1%. Crisil Ratings notes the pick-up in volume showcased in the third quarter of fiscal 2026 (with an 18% on-year growth reported) accruing largely on the back of expanded capacities of 2,20,000 TEUs at JNPT and Mundra undertaken in the first half of fiscal 2026. The company also has plans to undertake further capacity expansions, wherein, its ability to monetise these expansions amid uncertainty in export-import (EXIM) trade volume and intense competition in the CFS business remains monitorable. 

Operating profitability (operating profit before depreciation, amortisation, interest and tax) in fiscal 2025 remained in line with the previous fiscal at 10.6%, due to higher operating costs and lower absorption of fixed overheads amid lower volume at the subsidiary, Speedy Multimodes Ltd (SML). Profitability was also constrained by limited available capacity at the main yards at JNPT and Mundra, where the existing facilities are nearly fully utilised, limiting further volume-led growth. Crisil Ratings expects operating profitability to gradually improve to 10.3-10.5% in fiscal 2026 and further increase to maintain in the range of 11–12% over fiscals 2027–2028 in line with ramp-up in operations. 

The company has outlined a defined growth plan to add 5.15 lakh TEUs over fiscals 2026–2030 through a combination of capacity expansions at existing locations and new facilities, of which, 2.2 lakh TEUs have already been commissioned in the first half of fiscal 2026. The total planned capital expenditure (capex) is estimated at over Rs 400 crore over the years and is expected to be funded through a mix of debt and equity or internal accrual. 

The financial risk profile remains comfortable, supported by steady cash generation despite debt-funded investments in Haryana Orbital Rail Corporation Ltd (HORCL), given ATL’s asset-light operating model. Annual net cash accrual of Rs 70– 80 crore provide sufficient cushion for yearly debt obligations of current outstanding as well as to partially support the annual capex plan of Rs 120–150 crore, with the balance to be debt-funded. The gearing is expected to remain moderate at 0.50– 0.60 time. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of ATL and its operating subsidiary, SML. These entities are collectively referred to as ATL and are under common management and engaged in related businesses. Crisil Ratings has not included lease liabilities as recognised under Ind AS 116 in debt and thereby has adjusted Ebitda (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation) by excluding lease rental components in depreciation, finance costs and profit.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Established market position in CFS operations with plans to diversify in multi-modal logistic solutions** 

ATL operates six CFS and an ICD facility in India with consolidated TEU capacity of around 10,50,000 per annum. The company operates at all major ports in India, namely, Jawaharlal Nehru Port Authority (JNPA, rated ‘Crisil AAA/Stable’; Maharashtra), Mundra (Gujarat), Kolkata, and Chennai. JNPA is the largest Indian port handling over 50% of the overall container traffic, and ATL has around 13-14% non-Direct-Port-Delivery (DPD) addressable market share. Furthermore, its operating subsidiary SML is the sole player in ‘Cluster 1’ which is closest to the port, thereby having locational advantage. 

The company has a defined expansion plan in place with an aim to expand capacities and consolidate volume at the existing facilities of JNPT, Chennai and Mundra where the company is operating at near optimum utilisation and to diversify revenue streams through ICD operations where the company has planned to commercialise its first rail-linked ICD in Farukhnagar, Haryana over fiscals 2027 and 2028. ICD operations with multi-modal logistical solutions have relatively higher margin due to lower competitive intensity, thereby resulting in higher pricing power. 

# **Parentage of the Allcargo group** 

ATL was formed out of a business restructuring process carried out at Allcargo Logistics Ltd (Allcargo), announced in fiscal 2022. Despite the demerger from Allcargo, ATL continues to enjoy the benefits of being associated with the Allcargo group, which has presence across the entire logistics value chain, such as international supply chain, express logistics, contract logistics, among others. 

Common parentage supports the sustenance of the asset-light business model of ATL, which entails capital allocation flexibility. For instance, ATL’s standalone CFS facilities at JNPA and Chennai are leased from its group company Transindia Real Estate Ltd (TREL). The initial land acquisition investment for the ICD project at Jhajjar has also been made by TREL. 

# **Comfortable financial risk profile, despite upcoming capex expansion plans** 

The company has followed an asset-light model to conduct its operations so far, and hence, has lower actual capex spending, resulting in minimal dependance on long-term borrowings. Despite its debt-funded investment in HORCL, the gearing moderated to ~0.5 time as on March 31, 2025 as compared with ~0.2 time a year ago. In fiscal 2025, ATL had an outstanding debt of Rs 113 crore which reduced significantly in the first half of fiscal 2026 to Rs 68 crore, driven by prepayments. 

With the total capex exceeding Rs 400 crore over a span of fiscals 2026–2030, driving the expansion from erstwhile capacity of 8,30,000 TEUs to 13,45,000 TEUs and factoring the management’s guidance, Crisil Ratings expects ~60% of the planned capex to be debt-funded. The gearing is expected to nevertheless remain moderate at 0.50–0.60 time. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Modest scale of operations and exposure to intense competition in the CFS business** 

ATL was initially a part of Allcargo contributing to around 3% of revenue and about 10% of operating profit. The company was demerged from Allcargo in fiscal 2022 and began to operate as a separate entity in fiscal 2023. Furthermore, during fiscal 2022, ATL acquired 85% stake in SML for Rs 102 crore, which enhanced its TEU capacity from 5,30,000 TEUs to 8,30,000 TEUs. ATL acquired the balance 15% stake in SML in fiscal 2025 making SML a wholly owned subsidiary of the company. 

Despite an established market position followed by capacity augmentation through the acquisition of SML, the scale of operations is modest, as reflected in the revenue of Rs 755 crore in fiscal 2025. The scale of operations is expected to remain modest, constrained by exposure to high competition (especially in JNPA) and low pricing power. Low entry barrier has led to an increase in CFS facilities near ports, resulting in intense competition and margin pressure, as evidenced by the contraction in operating margin from 15% in fiscal 2023 to around 10.6% in fiscal 2025. That said, the company plans to de-risk its operations by entering into multi-modal logistics solutions such as rail-linked ICDs, which offer a stable revenue profile, with higher profitability. The company had also purchased a land parcel admeasuring 59.48 acre in Mundra in March 2024 besides firming up expansion plans for Chennai and JNPT markets, which is expected to aid volume over the medium term. 

# **Susceptibility to regulatory changes and volatility in EXIM trade volume:** 

The CFS business is directly linked to Indian trade and hence, remains exposed to risks arising from fluctuations in shipping rates, trade volume, EXIM and custom policies. Furthermore, regulations such as the introduction of Direct-Port-Delivery (DPD) and centralised parking plaza at JNPA have further impacted profitability. DPD was introduced in 2016 and at JNPA, port DPD volume increased from 30% in August 2017 to 41% in July 2018, to 58% in October 2020, to 72% in December 2023 and further to 80–85% in fiscal 2025. 

As 51% of ATL’s volume handled accrues from JNPA, the increase in DPD volume resulted in contraction in the company’s realisation to around Rs 11,882 per TEU in fiscal 2024 (Rs 12,450 per TEU in fiscal 2023) from Rs 14,965 per TEU in fiscal 2016. While some recovery has been noted in fiscal 2025 where the realisation/TEU was higher at Rs 12,157, the sustenance and growth in such realisation remains monitorable for Crisil Ratings. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Adequate 

Expected annual net cash accrual of Rs 70–80 crore will sufficiently cover the yearly debt obligations of the current outstanding as well as any planned capex-driven debt over the medium-term factoring that the expansion capex will be funded through a combination of debt and equity or internal accrual. The company had healthy cash reserve of around Rs 119 crore as on March 31, 2025 (Rs 83 crore as on March 31, 2024). 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes that ATL will continue to benefit from its established market position in the logistics industry, especially at key ports where it conducts its CFS operations. In addition, plateauing of realisation pressure, resulting in stable gross profitability per TEU, followed by steady volume growth, shall result in the sustenance of healthy operating efficiency. Furthermore, the financial risk profile shall also remain comfortable, driven by limited external debt despite the planned capex, steady cash generation and comfortable cash reserves. 

# **<u>Rating Sensitivity Factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors:** 

Sustained double-digit revenue growth over the medium term followed by diversification in revenue, while operating margin increasing to 13–15% 

Sustenance of strong financial risk profile and build-up of cash surplus 

# **Downward factors:** 

Weakening of business performance or sharp decline in profitability, resulting in operating margin contracting to 8–9% on a sustained basis 

Weakening of the financial risk profile due to any large debt-funded acquisitions, or change in business plant or strategy, or higher-than-anticipated increase in exposure to subsidiary companies constraining liquidity. 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

ATL operates CFS and ICD across the country. The company operates facilities in Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata, Mundra and Dadri, having total installed capacity of 10,20,000 TEUs. Currently, the company operates six CFS and an ICD facility and will be opening an ICD in Jhajjar, Haryana. It is predominantly a CFS player, and operations include import or export cargo stuffing and de-stuffing, customs clearance and other ancillary services. 

ATL was formed post demerger from Allcargo. The National Company Law Tribunal, vide order dated January 5, 2023, approved the scheme of demerger, which became effective from April 1, 2023. As on December 31, 2025, the promoters, Mr Shashi Kiran Shetty and his family members, as well as trusts controlled by the promoter family held 69.2% stake in ATL, foreign portfolio investors held 5.33%, bodies corporate owned 1.6%, while the balance was held by public and others. 

For the first half of fiscal 2026, the company reported revenue of Rs 394 crore and profit after tax (PAT) of Rs 20 crore (Rs 385 crore and Rs 21 crore, respectively, in the corresponding period of the previous fiscal).
