### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A-/Positive’ rating on the long-term bank facilities of Shriram Properties Ltd (SPL; part pf SPL group). 

The rating continues to reflect the strong market position of SPL, backed by an established brand, strong execution record, healthy saleability and robust financial flexibility. The rating is also supported by SPL’s adequate financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by exposure to refinancing risk and susceptibility to cyclicality inherent in the real estate sector. 

The outlook continues to factor the sustained financial risk profile of the company with healthy debt metrics despite muted operating performance caused by market headwinds due to inclusion of new approval system in the Bengaluru region and state election in Maharashtra. That said,  operating performance is expected to improve with expected sales volume of ~5 million square feet (msf), sales value of over Rs 2,800 crore and collection of ~Rs 1,800 crore in fiscal 2026, as against 4.3 msf, Rs 2,282 crore and Rs 1,484 crore (including Development Management (DM) collection), respectively, in fiscal 2025 backed strong launch pipeline of estimated 5 – 7 msf in the current fiscal. 

Despite moderate operating performance in fiscal 2025, financial risk profile was comfortable and leverage low. Debt for residential projects remained around Rs 749 crore as on March 31, 2025 (including SPL share of debt from joint ventures [JVs]), compared to Rs 724 crore a year earlier due to lower-than-anticipated costs as planned launches were delayed by a quarter. 

Debt protection metrics as characterised by gross debt to cash flow from operations (CFO) and CFO to interest remain healthy. Debt to CFO ratio is expected to improve to below 2 times and CFO to interest ratio to remain above 4 times over the medium term. The cost of debt was high at 11.35% in fiscal 2025 though the company demonstrated its ability to bring it down from 13.7% in fiscal 2021, further reduction in cost of borrowings will be monitorable. 

Overall bank debt is expected at Rs 850-950 crore (including JVs) over the medium term. Financial flexibility is supported by the company’s refinancing ability and steady construction progress in ongoing projects. The rating duly factors the established development track record of the SPL group, supporting healthy saleability of its projects in Bengaluru, Pune, Kolkata and Chennai, along with comfortable cash flow and adequate financial flexibility. 

These strengths are partially offset by susceptibility to cyclicality and risks inherent in the real estate sector and exposure to intense competition. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of SPL and its subsidiaries, associates (based on consolidated financials of SPL) and JVs. This is because these entities, collectively referred to as the SPL group, have common promoters and are in the same business.

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

### **Established brand and market position in the real estate markets of Bengaluru, Chennai and Kolkata** 

With a track record of over two decades, the group has an established position in the real estate markets of Bengaluru, Chennai and Kolkata. It has developed over 28 msf of residential area (built-up), mainly focused on mid-market and midmarket premium housing categories, and has around 35 msf in the pipeline, of which 5 - 7 msf is expected to  be launched in the next three - four quarters  Projects are geographically diversified with less than 50% in Bengaluru, below 20% in Chennai and around 30% in Kolkata. Asset-light JV model, joint development agreements (JDA) and development management (DM) will account for more than 60% of the portfolio. The group aims to leverage its leadership position to become a partner of choice for landowners through JV/JDA and DM projects. 

Steady demand for affordable and middle-income projects will likely ensure healthy collection and sales over the medium term. The business risk profile will continue to be supported by the company’s long track record of execution and delivery. Its ongoing projects had healthy sales, collection and construction progress of 80%, 67% and 53%, respectively, as on March 31, 2025. In fiscal 2025, sales and collection remained range bound at Rs 2,288 crore and Rs 1,484 crore (including DM collection), respectively (Rs 2,362 crore and Rs 1,391 crore in fiscal 2024). Regional market challenges in select states led to a deceleration in sales and collections, and delayed launches, resulting in range-bound performance for both metrics 

# **Comfortable financial risk profile** 

The financial risk profile is comfortable driven by strong operating performance and sustained leverage. Gross debt in the residential projects remained range-bound at Rs 749 crore as on March 31, 2025 (including SPL share of debt from JVs), as against Rs 724 crore as on March 31, 2024. Gross debt to CFO ratio is expected to improve to below 2 times and CFO to interest ratio to above 4 times in fiscal 2026, supported by sustained healthy collection. The ratios will likely remain healthy at these levels over the medium term. 

The cost of debt was high at 11.35% in fiscal 2025, despite declining from 13.7% in fiscal 2021, and is expected to reduce further, which will be credit monitorable. Networth was sizeable at Rs 1,355 crore as on March 31, 2025. SPL has issued corporate guarantees for debt raised by its subsidiaries. The financial risk profile will likely be strong over the medium term, supported by steady improvement in collection and no major debt planned for construction or land acquisition. 

# **Improving cash flow visibility** 

Cash flow visibility is healthy supported by improving operating performance of the core business as well as cash inflow from sale of land parcels and asset monetisation. The cash flow is expected to improve over the medium term, supported by healthy launch pipeline and a good mix of projects in various stages of development at the group level. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Susceptibility to cyclicality in the real estate industry** : Cyclicality causes fluctuations in cash inflow. Against this, cash outflow towards projects and debt obligation is relatively fixed, resulting in substantial cash flow mismatch. Decline in sales may lead to lower collection over the medium term. 

**Exposure to geographic and project concentration risks:** The company's revenue remains heavily concentrated in Bengaluru for fiscal 2025, but it has made significant strides in expanding its footprint into Chennai, Pune, and Kolkata. With a substantial pipeline of 5 - 7 msf of projects, both ongoing and under approval, set to be launched over the next three to four quarters, the company expects to reduce its concentration risk in one micro market and achieve a more balanced revenue profile. As the entire revenue comes from the real estate development business, operations will remain highly susceptible to economic cycles. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Liquidity will remain strong over the medium term, supported by healthy saleability and collection in ongoing projects as well as in new launches. Financial flexibility is supported by strong refinancing ability and healthy relationships with investors. The group had unsold inventory of over Rs 1,472 crore as on March 31, 2025, and land bank of 314 acre in Kolkata and Chennai against which additional debt can be raised, if required. Furthermore, cash and equivalent of Rs 346 crore as on June 30, 2025, support liquidity. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Positive 

Crisil Ratings believes SPL will continue to benefit from its established position in the real estate markets of Bengaluru, Chennai and Kolkata, and expected improvement in performance. The financial risk profile will likely strengthen, aided by continued focus on deleveraging. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Steady improvement in revenue leading to increase in collection and CFO Strengthening of the financial risk profile with debt to CFO ratio below 2.5 times on a sustained basis 

# **Downward factors** 

Sharp decline in operating cash flow, triggered by slackened project saleability or delays in project execution Weakening of the financial risk profile with debt to CFO ratio remaining above 3 times on a sustained basis
