### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has upgraded its rating on the long-term bank loan facilities of Man Industries India Limited (MIIL) to ' **Crisil A+/Stable** ' from 'Crisil A/Stable'. The short-term rating has been reaffirmed at 'Crisil A1'. 

The upgrade reflects a significant improvement in MIIL's business risk profile following the strategic acquisition of National Pipe Company (NPC), Saudi Arabia, which was completed on May 21, 2026. This acquisition is expected to significantly improve MIIL's scale of operations by over 50-60% and operating profit before depreciation, interest, and tax (OPBDIT) margins to around 14-15% (12.3% in fiscal 2026). It will also enhance geographic diversification and add 430,000 metric tonnes (MT) of LSAW/HSAW capacity in Saudi Arabia. NPC, which was acquired at an attractive valuation of 1.5x EV/Ebitda, is expected to be revenue and earnings accretive from day one, given its fully operational status. The acquisition will also provide MIIL with an immediate entry into the growing Saudi Arabian market. 

The upgrade also factors in the improvement in MIIL’s operating performance, supported by OPBDIT margins improving to 12.3% in fiscal 2026, from 8-10% in the past, driven by better product mix focused on exports and value-added products. 

The NPC acquisition, valued at Rs 1,000 crores (USD 102 million), was funded through a mix of debt (USD 70 million) and equity (USD 32 million). Despite the incremental debt, MIIL's financial risk profile is expected to remain comfortable, with gearing of around 0.6-0.7 times and a Total Outstanding Loans to Tangible Net Worth (TOL/TNW) ratio of 2-2.2 times in fiscal 2027, as against 0.25 times and 1.45 times, respectively, in fiscal 2026. Liquidity is expected to remain strong, with net cash accruals of over Rs 450 crores against repayment obligations of Rs 240-250 crores. 

The ratings continue to reflect MIIL's established market position in the submerged arc welding (SAW) pipes industry, along with a healthy financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by the integration risks associated with the NPC acquisition, the working capital-intensive nature of operations, and the company's susceptibility to cyclicality in end-user industries, along with volatility in raw material prices and foreign exchange (forex) rates. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

For arriving at its ratings, Crisil Ratings has consolidated MIIL and its wholly owned subsidiaries - Man USA INC, and Man Overseas Invest FZCO, Merino Shelters Private Limited, Man Offshore and Drilling Limited, Man International Steel Industries Company (MISIC) and Man Stainless Steel Tubes Limited as there are financial fungibilities between the companies. The entities are collectively referred to as the Man group. The subsidiaries are strategic to MIIL in view of their strong integration with its operations.

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

### **Improved business risk profile following acquisition of NPC:** 

On 21st May 2026, MIIL acquired 100% stake in National Pipe Company (NPC), a Saudi Arabia based, API certified large pipe manufacturer, through its wholly owned subsidiary Man International Steel Industries Company (MISIC) for a total consideration of ~Rs 1000 crores (USD 102 million). NPC is a debt free company and has recorded revenues of Rs 2000 crores (USD 211 million) and OPBDIT margins of 25% in CY25. 

NPC with its installed manufacturing capacity of 4,30,000 MTPA of HSAW and LSAW pipes, will increase MIIL’s combined group capacity to ~ 16,00,000 MTPA and will position MIIL’s positioning as one of the larger pipe manufacturing groups in India and the Middle East. 

The acquisition will enable MIIL to directly participate in Saudi Arabia’s growing demand for uncoated pipelines across oil and gas, water transmission, desalination, and industrial projects. This is significant given the scale of infrastructure investment under Saudi Vision 2030 and the Saudi’s expanding energy and water infrastructure program **.** MIIL is also in process of setting up a coating plant in Saudi Arabia (expected to be completed by March – 2027) thereby making MIIL a fully integrated player in Saudi Arabia and enhancing its product offering and margins going forward. 

NPC has maintained a longstanding relationship of over two decades with Saudi Aramco, one of the world’s largest oil producers and is on the approved vendor list of Saudi Aramco and its EPC contractors, Saudi Water Authority (SWA), Saudi Water Partnership Company (SWPC), Water Transmission & Technologies Co. (WTTCO), Kuwait Oil Company (KOC), Qatar Petroleum etc. The breadth of this clientele provides significant revenue stability and reduces counterparty concentration risks 

# **Established position in the SAW pipes industry:** 

The domestic pipes industry is consolidated, with the top four players accounting for approximately 80% of capacity. Limited competition is due to the large capital requirements and the necessity to have critical accreditations and customer approvals. MIIL is one of the largest SAW pipe players in India, with over 30 years of operations, and produces both helically submerged arc welded (HSAW) and longitudinal submerged arc welded (LSAW) pipes. It has been manufacturing SAW pipes, branch pipes, and coatings since 1995 

MIIL had also set up a 175,000 MT capacity ERW plant in Fiscal 2024, which enabled it to cater to the small diameter pipes space. With its upcoming 20,000 MT stainless steel tubes plant in Jammu (expected to be completed by September 2027), MIIL's customer base and revenue profile are expected to broaden further. However, the commissioning and ramp-up of this plant remain monitorable. 

MIIL's operating performance continues to remain healthy, with revenue of Rs 3,564 crores and margins of 12.3% in Fiscal 2026, compared to Rs 3,507 crores and 9.6% in the previous year. OPBDIT margins improved by approximately 275 basis points in Fiscal 2026, driven by a better product mix focused on exports and value-added products. The revenue growth prospects for the India business continue to remain strong, supported by a healthy order book of approximately Rs 3,000 crores (execution timeline of 6-8 months) and the expected ramp-up of the upcoming plants." 

# **Healthy financial risk profile:** 

The financial risk profile is expected to remain healthy, despite debt funded acquisition of Rs 1000 crores and upcoming capex of ~ Rs 600 crores, supported by a sizeable adjusted networth of Rs 2017 crores in fiscal 2026. Gearing is expected to increase temporarily but nonetheless remain comfortable at 0.6-0.7 times in fiscal 2027 (0.25 times as on March 31, 2026). 

TOL/TNW is expected to peak to 2-2.2 times respectively in fiscal 2027 (1.45 times in fiscal 2026), while interest coverage which stood at ~3.1 times in fiscal 2026 is expected to improve to ~3.3-4 times over the medium term supported by higher profitability. 

Liquidity is expected to remain healthy over the medium term, as expected cash accruals of over Rs 450 crores is expected to comfortably meet the yearly debt obligation of Rs 230-240 crores. The company’s ability to manage the incremental working capital requirement while maintaining comfortable debt coverage ratios remains crucial, given the moderate-to-high utilisation of the fund-based limit. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Susceptibility to cyclicality in end-user industries and to volatility in raw material prices and forex rates:** 

The Man group has historically derived majority of the revenue from the oil and gas segment and the remaining from the water and irrigation segment. Slowdown in the oil and gas industry because of a significant decline in crude prices impacted operations in the past. Strong demand from new projects in the oil and gas segment in key markets of India and the Middle East is critical for improvement in overall operations. Any major and continued slowdown in end-user industries will weaken demand for line pipes and impact performance. 

Furthermore, operations remain exposed to government policies and preferences with respect to factors such as local supply and trade duties. Consequently, the group’s order inflow and operating performance are highly linked to the demand prospects from these sectors. Moreover, there is a lead time of 2-4 months between the application and the final award of a tender. Because these contracts are of a fixed-priced nature, the Man group cannot pass on increases in input costs to customers after applying for the tender. 

The company is also exposed to forex functions as reflected by the forex losses incurred by the company during fiscal 2023 and fiscal 2026. The company hedges its forex exposures, thereby mitigating the risk. 

# **Working capital-intensive operations:** 

Gross current assets have been around 200 days in the past, driven by debtors of over 100 days. However, in the past two years, inventory and debtor days have remained stretched. In Fiscal 2025, working capital was impacted due to several factors, including a delay in the execution of a Taiwan order, which led to inventory buildup. The execution of this order was completed in Q1 of Fiscal 2026. In Fiscal 2026, inventory and debtor days increased to approximately 180 and 128 days, respectively, due to the Middle East conflict, which hindered the delivery of ships to the United Arab Emirates. However, working capital levels are expected to normalize in the current fiscal year, as MIIL has received approval to deliver the shipment to the eastern port of UAE - Fujairah port. 

Also, concentration in receivables renders MIIL susceptible to steady increase in the working capital requirement, and hence, remains a key monitorable. As of March 31, 2026, out of the total debtors of Rs 1,250 crores, Rs 109 crores remained under arbitration. According to the company, this amount has a high chance of recoverability 

# **Integration risks associated with large acquisition:** 

The acquisition of a  NPC, a large overseas manufacturing company, presents integration challenges, including operational alignment, management bandwidth, and timely ramp-up of the combined platform. While MIIL's management has articulated a clear strategy for integrating NPC's operations, the company's ability to realize the anticipated synergies remains a key monitorable. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Liquidity is aided by moderate fund-based working capital lines utilization and internal cash accruals; the company also avails need-based project-specific limits. MIIL is expected to earn NCA of Rs 450 crores to Rs 580 crores against repayments of Rs 230-250 crores each year in the next 3 fiscals. The working capital limit of over Rs 2180 crore (fund based Rs 310 and non-fund-based limits of Rs  1870 crores) were utilized ~77% on average over the 11 months through April 2026. Cash and cash equivalents were around Rs 657 crores (unencumbered of ~Rs 300 crores) as on March 31, 2026. Crisil Ratings expects internal accrual, cash & cash equivalents, and unutilized bank lines to be sufficient to meet repayment obligations as well as incremental capex requirements. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes that MIIL's credit profile will sustain in the medium term driven by healthy order book position and operating margins. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Improved operating performance leading to operating margin of above 13-14%. Improvement in business risk profile aided by diversification of product profile. 

Considerable improvement in liquidity on account of realization of non-current assets / non-core investments. 

# **Downward factors** 

Weak operating performance resulting in a fall in operating margins below 10%. 

- Weakening of scale owing to reduction in scale of operations and substantial reduction in outstanding order book Deterioration in financial risk profile due higher than expected debt funded capex or any support provided to nonbusiness-related entities. 

Any liability due to SEBI audit observations impacting the business or financial risk profile of the company. 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

MIIL is one of the largest SAW pipe players in India with combined capacity of 11.75 lakh tonne per annum, distributed equally between HSAW and LSAW and ERW. Incorporated in 1988, it commenced operations with the production of aluminum extrusion products. In 1995, it diversified into manufacturing SAW pipes. In March 2008, MIIL bought 55% stake in Man Infraprojects Pvt Ltd (Man Infraprojects), which undertook real estate development and increased its stake to 100% after acquiring the promoter shareholding in September 2011. In September 2013, MIIL announced a restructuring of its businesses. As part of the arrangement, with effect from April 1, 2014, Man Infraprojects was hived off from MIIL.
