### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the bank facilities of Nestle India Limited (Nestle India). 

The ratings continue to reflect the leadership position of Nestlé India in several product categories, well-established brands and diversified revenue profile, ably supported by an extensive distribution network. The company also enjoys technical and research and development (R&D) support from its parent, Nestlé SA (rated ‘AA-/Stable/A-1+’ by S&P Global), which is the world’s largest player in the branded and packaged foods industry. The financial risk profile of Nestle India is healthy, aided by strong cash accrual, minimal debt and superior liquidity. These strengths are partially offset by exposure to intense competition in the fast-moving consumer goods (FMCG) sector and volatility in raw material prices. 

Revenue grew by ~15% yoy for FY26 vis a vis FY25 to Rs 23,072 crore. The performance was driven by high double-digit growth in terms of both volume and value in confectionery (KITKAT, MUNCH & MILKYBAR), powdered & liquid beverages (coffee & ready to drink) and prepared dishes (MAGGI & pet care business). Milk products and nutrition has seen a slow growth value wise with marginal decline in volume, rising milk prices, high packaging costs/transportation costs, intense competition and company’s focus on premium and high growth segments likely accounts to this shift. The revenue is likely to grow by ~6-8% over medium term supported by the sustained growth in penetration led volume expansion across the segments and moderated by the pricing (price hikes remain the last resort after exploring operational efficiency measures). 

EBITDA margin saw a slight dip to ~23% in FY26 from 23.8% in FY25 on account of commodity headwinds. Edible oil prices and milk prices remained elevated, while wheat quality and quantity were adversely affected by unseasonal rains. The margin is expected to remain healthy driven by push into premium segments and high margin products slightly offset by inflationary pressures. Also, constant focus on operating cost efficiency will help cushion the impact of rising commodity prices, logistic costs and packaging costs. 

Nestle India plans to invest Rs ~5,000 - Rs 6,000 crore over the medium term as part of its ongoing capital expenditure (capex) to ramp up existing and new capacities pan-India across its product portfolio. The capex is to be funded by internal cash accrual and liquidity available with the company and may take external debt if required, the financial risk profile should remain strong supported by healthy cash accrual of Rs 1,884 crore** as of March 31, 2026, and adequate liquidity maintained by the company.

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

### **Strong business risk profile, supported by leading position in several product categories, well-established brands and diversified revenue profile** 

Nestlé India is a leading player in the Indian FMCG industry, with established market position in most product categories that it is operates in. The company is a pioneer in the culinary segment, with a range of products under the Maggi brand. It is among the top-two players in most of its product categories, including milk products and nutrition, beverages, prepared 

dishes and cooking aids and chocolate and confectionery. In these segments, Nestle India benefits from its well-established brands. 

Revenue is diversified as of March 2026, with 33.4% generated from milk and nutrition products (dairy products and weaning foods), 15.8% from powdered and liquid beverages (instant coffee, iced tea and other beverage vending mixes), 31.5% from prepared dishes and cooking aids (the Maggi range) and 19.3% from chocolates and confectionery (including Kit Kat and Munch). Nestle India remains strong supported by its well diversified revenue profile, dominant position in several product categories and leading market share across 85% of the product portfolio. 

# **Continued technical support from Nestlé SA** 

Most of Nestlé India’s brands are part of the parent’s international portfolio. The company enjoys access to its parent’s proprietary technology and strong R&D capabilities **.** Switzerland-based Nestlé SA holds 62.76% stake in Nestle India and is the world’s largest player in the food and beverages sector. 

# **Healthy financial risk profile** 

The financial risk profile is robust, supported by strong operating cash flow and comfortable capital structure with negligible debt. Despite volatility in raw material prices, changes in product mix and prudent cost management have ensured a stable operating margin of ~23% in fiscal 2026, resulting in sustained strong cash flow. Despite its intent to undertake capex of Rs ~6,000 crore to further expand its product portfolio, set up new plants, the financial risk profile is expected to remain strong over the medium term, with healthy cash accrual and low gearing. The capex incurred is to met through internal cash accrual majorly with no new debt addition. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Susceptibility to intense competition** 

The domestic FMCG segment is highly competitive with the entry of new players, including multinationals, in various divisions such as instant noodles, packaged foods, beverages, chocolates and confectionary. Competition keeps increasing owing to aggressive product launches, evolving consumer preferences and strong marketing strategies adopted by players. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Superior 

It is supported by adequate liquidity of over Rs. 1350 crores as on March 31, 2026 and unutilized bank lines, liquidity will continue to improve, with strong net cash accrual expected over the medium term. Liquidity is also supported by minimal utilization of the fund-based limit and nil external term borrowing. Net cash accrual remains healthy ~Rs 1884 crore** even after the dividend payout as of March 31, 2026. 

# **<u>ESG Profile</u>** 

Crisil Ratings believes Nestle India’s ESG profile supports its strong credit risk profile. 

# **Key ESG Highlights** 

- Nestle India has undertaken several initiatives, including adoption of energy-efficient technologies and purchase of 

- renewable energy certificates (RECs). As a result, the share of renewable energy in total energy consumption increased to ~60% in fiscal 2025 from ~47% in the previous fiscal. 

- On environmental stewardship, the company reported a decline in Scope 1 and 2 emissions intensity, which was ~17% 

- lower than FY 2024-25. 

- On water stewardship, the company continues to focus on improving operational water efficiency, which translated into 

- a ~11% CAGR (between FY 2023-24 & 2025-26) decline in water withdrawal intensity, remaining broadly in line with the peer average. 

- In fiscal 2025, the company reported that 7 out of 9 factories operate under a Zero Liquid Discharge (ZLD) framework, 

- wherein treated effluent is fully reused for process and land maintenance. Additionally, ~100% of total waste generated was recovered through reuse, recycling, or other recovery methods 

- Nestle India has maintained its plastic-neutral status since 2020, while progressively reducing virgin plastic, increasing 

- the use of recycled content where feasible and strengthening waste recovery systems through the Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) mechanism. 

- On the social front, attrition rate among the permanent workforce was at a relatively low rate (~6%) 

- With respect to input sourcing, Nestle India had higher-than-peer-average share of inputs sourced from within India 

- (~86% of total inputs purchased) and MSMEs (~25% of total inputs purchased) 

- With respect to workplace safety, the company reported nil fatalities among its workforce during the year, thus 

- extending its zero-fatality track record to four consecutive fiscal years 

- Nestle India’s governance structure is characterized by ~55% of its Board comprising of independent directors, ~33% 

- woman directors, a ~100% investor complaint redressal rate, and extensive financial disclosures 

There is growing importance of ESG among investors and lenders. Nestle India’s continued commitment to ESG principles will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Nestlé India will continue to benefit from its leading market position in key FMCG segments, healthy operating efficiency and strong parent support. The financial risk profile is expected to remain healthy over the medium term, supported by adequate cash flow and healthy capital structure. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Downward factors** 

- Erosion in market share in key business segments, constraining cash generation 

- Large, debt-funded capex or acquisition, weakening the financial risk profile with gearing increasing significantly above 

- 0.5 time on sustained basis 

- Substantial decline in liquidity, leading to moderation in the financial risk profile 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Nestlé India (62.76% owned by Nestlé SA) began trading operations in India in 1912, as Nestlé Anglo Swiss Condensed Milk Company (Exports) Ltd. Nestlé India commenced manufacturing operations in 1961 by setting up a plant in Moga, Punjab. The company manufactures products under four categories: milk products and nutrition, powdered and liquid beverages, prepared dishes and cooking aids and chocolates and confectionery. It has nine manufacturing facilities in India - at Moga; Samalkha, Haryana; Nanjangud, Karnataka; Ponda and Bicholim, Goa; Choladi, Tamil Nadu; Pantnagar, Uttarakhand; Tahliwal, Himachal Pradesh; and Sanand, Gujarat. 

In April 2024, Nestle India entered an agreement to form a JV with Dr Reddy’s to expand its nutritional offerings. The JV named Dr Reddy's and Nestle Health Science Ltd has been formed with Dr Reddy’s holding 51% and Nestlé India 49%, with a call option to increase shareholding up to 60% for Nestle India after six years at a fair market value. Nestle India has made investments of Rs ~706 crore towards this JV.
