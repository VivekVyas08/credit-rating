### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AA/Stable’ rating on the long-term bank facilities of Leela Palaces Hotels & Resorts Limited (LPHRL; formerly known as Schloss Bangalore Limited (SBL) part of Schloss group). Further, Crisil Ratings has **withdrawn** its ratings on Rs.1362.96 crore bank facilities basis client request as well as receipt of no dues certificate from lenders. The withdrawal is in line with Crisil withdrawal policy. 

The rating continues to factor in the diverse portfolio of luxury hotels operating under the well-known The Leela luxury brand. The Schloss group’s properties enjoy strong market positioning with healthy operating metrics (industry-leading average room rates [ARR], revenue per available room [RevPAR] and earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation [Ebitda] margin) and benefit from a geographically diversified presence across major metro cities and key leisure destinations in India. The ARR for the first nine months of current fiscal increased by 12.7% y-o-y to ~Rs. 23,000/night for Schloss group’s owned domestic portfolio. Furthermore, the occupancy levels have also increased by 310 bps to 67.8% for the first nine months of current fiscal (64.7% in the first nine months of fiscal 2025,; 67.9% in fiscal 2025; 66.9% in fiscal 2024). This led to an increase in adjusted operating revenue of 16% to Rs. 1043 crore in first nine months of fiscal 2026 (Rs. 898 crore in the first nine months of fiscal 2025). The operating margin also improved to 46% for the first nine months of fiscal 2026 (43% corresponding period of previous fiscal). 

The rating also reflects the significant improvement in Schloss Group’s financial risk profile following its initial public offering and debt reduction and expectations of healthy net cash accruals which will support the company’s business growth while maintaining low leverage over the medium term. 

Gross debt (including lease liabilities) has reduced to ~Rs. 1665 crore as on December 31, 2026 from ~Rs. 4,141 crore as on March 31, 2025 post IPO in June 2025. With the deleveraging undertaken, gearing is expected to remain below 0.5 time in the medium term, from 1.34 times as on March 31, 2025. Likewise, net debt to Ebitda is expected to sustain below 2.5 times in the medium term, compared with above 4 times in fiscal 2025, aided by deleveraging and Ebitda growth. This is despite the company having sizeable capital expenditure (capex) plans of ~Rs. 700–800 crore per annum over fiscal 20272029. 

The Schloss group has expanded internationally acquiring 25% stake in 546 key operational property in Dubai for consideration of Rs. 421 crore ($47.7 million). The property is expected to undergo rebranding to The Leela in next 2 years and the Schloss group will contribute another ~Rs. 200 crore ($21 million) for the same. The company is expected to receive HMA fees post rebranding as well as minority share in profits once the operations stabilise. The company also signed a management contract with an 80-key luxury desert resort in Jaisalmer in fiscal 2026. Further, the Schloss group has acquired a 71-knley luxury resort in Coorg for consideration of Rs. 560 crore (excluding stamp duty) in March 2026. The group plans to incur capex towards rebranding and refurbishing these properties in sync with The Leela brand. With this the portfolio of Schloss group has expanded to 15 fully operational hotels (seven owned, seven managed, and one franchised), with a total key count of 4161 as of March 2026. Additionally, ~725+ keys are expected to be added through upcoming properties in Mumbai, Agra, Ayodhya, Ranthambore, Bandhavgarh, and Srinagar over the next four fiscals alongside capexdriven refurbishments and upgrades at existing flagship hotels. 

Maintaining prudent financial discipline and timely execution of capex without cost overruns will be important. Given the substantial capex outlay in the medium term, any delay in project execution or cost overruns could impact the company’s 

financial position. Therefore, timely project execution, cost management as well as successful ramp up and stabilization of new hotels in the next 2-3 fiscals will be monitorable. Further, any large debt-funded acquisitions impacting the financial risk profile of the company will remain monitorable. 

The rating continues to benefit from the Brookfield group’s (deriving strength from Brookfield Asset Management’s [BAM]) ability to exercise control on the financial and liquidity management of the company as well as from its intention to extend support to the entity, if and when needed. The Brookfield group, through BSREP III India Holding (DIFC) Ltd continues to hold majority ~75.91% shareholding in the Schloss group post IPO. Crisil Ratings also understand that the Brookfield group will continue to provide strategic guidance and support to the Schloss group both on an ongoing basis and in the event of any exigency. These strengths are partially offset by the susceptibility of revenue and profitability to cyclicality in the hospitality industry and risks related to sizeable capex being incurred over the medium term. 

# **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of LPHRL (erstwhile Schloss Bangalore Limited) and its subsidiaries Schloss Chanakya Pvt Ltd (SCHPL), Schloss Chennai Pvt Ltd (SCPL), Schloss Udaipur Pvt Ltd (SUPL), Schloss HMA Pvt Ltd (SHPL), Schloss Gandhinagar Private Limited (SGPL) , Leela Palace and Resorts Ltd (LPRL), Tulsi Palace Resort Private Limited (TPRPL), , Schloss Tadoba Private Limited, Transition Cleantech Services Five Private Limited, Inside India Resorts Private Limited, Anasvish Tiger camp Private Limited, Buildminds Real Estate Private Limited, Aries Holdings (DIFC) Limited and Leela Luxe Hotels & Resorts Private Limited and its joint ventures, (referred as Schloss group) because of their strong business and financial linkages, common management and fungibility of funds. All the companies are collectively referred to as Schloss group. 

Crisil Ratings has also notched up the rating assigned to the group to factor in strong managerial, business, and financial benefits of the parent Brookfield group, deriving strength from BAM.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Healthy operating performance and reputed brand in the luxury hospitality segment and diversified portfolio of assets** 

Leela is an established brand in the luxury hotel space and operates in key Indian markets, which have high entry barrier and are strategically important. Presence in prime locations helped these properties attract both leisure as well as business demand. 

Given the strong brand position, these properties have maintained healthy occupancy and ARR. Consolidated revenue stood at ~Rs 1,345 crore and Ebitda margin at 47.1% in fiscal 2025, as against ~Rs 1,206 crore and 48.9%, respectively, in fiscal 2024. For the first nine months of fiscal 2026, the operating revenue further increased by 16% to Rs. 1043 crore (Rs. 898 crore in corresponding period of previous fiscal) while the operating margin increased to 46% (43%) for the same period The operating performance is expected to continue improving driven by the robust domestic leisure and business travel owing to strong economic activity and traction in international travel. 

The group owns and operates seven hotels (including recently acquired resort in Coorg and Dubai) and manages another seven entities and one franchise hotel, which provides healthy assets as well as geographic diversification. However, the group is exposed to revenue concentration as more than ~70% of revenue comes from three properties - Leela Palace Bengaluru and The Leela Palace New Delhi and The Leela Palace Chennai. 

# **<mark>Expectation of support from the Brookfield Group</mark>** 

The Schloss group benefits from Brookfield group’s ability to exercise control on its financial and liquidity management as well as from its intention to extend support to the entity, if and when needed. The Brookfield group, through BSREP III India Holding (DIFC) Ltd continues to holds majority ~75.91% shareholding in Schloss group post-IPO. 

Brookfield has demonstrated its financial support towards the Schloss group in the past. Further, BSREP III has given a Right of First Offer (ROFO) for all hospitality assets being acquired by BSREP III in India to Schloss group highlighting it’s strategic importance to the parent. Recently, Brookfield and Schloss group have won an award letter from MMRDA for a plot in BKC, Mumbai under a 50:50 joint venture for development of a hospitality asset which also demonstrates the importance of Schloss group to the Brookfield group.  Overall, Crisil Ratings expects that the Schloss group will continue to benefit from Brookfield’s global experience in the hospitality segment, its advanced systems and processes, and also derive synergies from Brookfield’s ecosystem. 

# **Significantly improved financial risk profile that is likely to sustain over medium term** 

The Schloss group‘s financial risk profile strengthened significantly in fiscal 2026 post IPO in June 2025. As a result, gearing is expected to improve to below 0.5 time in the medium term from 1.34 times as on March 31, 2025. Net debt to Ebitda is expected to sustain below 2.5 times in the medium term, compared with above 4 times in fiscal 2025, aided by significant deleveraging and Ebitda growth. This is despite the company having sizeable capex plans of Rs 700–800 crore per annum over fiscals 2027–2029. Maintaining prudent financial discipline and timely execution of capex without cost overruns will remain monitorable. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Exposure to cyclicality in the hospitality industry** 

The hospitality sector is susceptible to downturns in the domestic and international economies. Growth in revenue per available room (RevPAR) in business destinations is more sensitive to macroeconomic indicators, such as nominal growth in gross domestic product. On the other hand, leisure destinations are more sensitive to non-economic factors such as terror attacks and health-related travel warnings, as seen during the pandemic. Besides, the RevPAR of premium hotels declines more sharply during downturns in comparison to mid-sized or economy hotels, but operating cost remains high. Thus, cash flow from these properties may remain vulnerable to economic downturns. 

# **Execution risk associated with sizeable capex** 

The Schloss group has plans to add ~725 keys over next four years with capex outlay of Rs 700-800 crore per annum over fiscals 2027–2029, including equity infusion towards Leela Dubai. Given the substantial capex outlay in the medium term, any delay in project execution or cost overruns could impact the company’s financial risk profile, making timely project execution and cost management monitorable. Further, any large-debt funded acquisitions impacting the financial profile of the company will remain monitorable. However, the group’s extensive experience in the field and support from the Brookfield group will provide support. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Liquidity remained strong, with unencumbered cash and cash equivalents of Rs. 593 crore as on December 31, 2025. The liquidity is expected to moderate for March 2026 with acquisition of a resort in Coorg funded through a mix of debt and owned funds. Additionally, the group has working capital limit of Rs. 98 crore, with moderate utilisation, and expects to generate cash accruals of Rs. 500–600 crore annually over fiscal 2027-2028. The company has reduced its gross borrowings post-IPO and refinanced its existing debt for a tenure of 15 years (till fiscal 2041) such that there are minimal repayments in fiscal 2027-2031. The company, however, plans to incur sizeable capex of Rs. 700-800 crore annually over the next 3-4 fiscals towards under-construction hotels as well as expansion of keys in owned portfolio. The same is expected to be met comfortably through a mix of debt and internal accruals. Crisil Ratings also understands that Brookfield will ensure that the Schloss group maintains adequate liquidity and will support cashflow requirements. Brookfield group support will be a key monitorable. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

The business risk profile of the Schloss group will continue to benefit from sustained improvement in occupancy and ARR, along with premium positioning of the respective hotel in the geographies. The financial risk profile is expected to moderate over the medium term, driven by the planned capex in under-construction portfolio. However, the same is expected to remain comfortable with healthy accruals from operating hotels and HMA fees.

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

LPHRL (erstwhile Schloss Bangalore Ltd) was incorporated in 2019 to acquire the Leela Palace Bengaluru, a luxury hotel operating with 356 keys in Bengaluru. The company owns and operates The Leela group of hotels, a leading luxury hospitality chain in India. 

# **<u>About the Group</u>** 

LPHRL, part of the Brookfield group, owns and operates The Leela group of hotels, a leading luxury hospitality chain in India. The company owns, manages and operates a portfolio of high-end hotels and resorts across key business and leisure destinations such as Delhi, Bengaluru, Udaipur, Chennai, Jaipur, Gurugram, Hyderabad, Gandhinagar and Kerala. On May 31, 2024, Brookfield’s holding fund (BSREP III) transferred ownership of five hotel-owning subsidiaries– Schloss Chanakya Pvt Ltd, SCPL, SUPL, Schloss HMA Pvt Ltd and Tulsi Palace Resort Pvt Ltd (Jaipur hotel)–to the Schloss group, making them wholly-owned subsidiaries of LPHRL. 

# **<u>About the sponsor fund</u>** 

BSREP III fund which holds 75.91% in LPHRL is 100% managed by BAM: Brookfield Asset Management which is also the principal investor in the fund. The fund through which the investment is being done, has a fixed tenure of 10 years (with an option for two one-year extensions) with investments typically happening in the first four years.
