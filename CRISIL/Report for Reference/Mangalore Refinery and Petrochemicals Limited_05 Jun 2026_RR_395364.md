### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+' ratings on the bank facilities, debt instruments and corporate credit rating of Mangalore Refinery and Petrochemicals Limited (MRPL). 

The ratings continue to take comfort from the strong operational, financial, and managerial support the company receives from its parent, Oil and Natural Gas Corporation Limited (ONGC). MRPL continues to remain strategically important to its parent’s strategy of being an integrated oil and gas entity with presence established across the entire oil and gas value chain. 

Strong operating performance with healthy capacity utilisation supported the improvement in refining economics in fiscal 2026, translating into a sharp recovery in profitability. The company reported throughput of ~17 MMT at ~113% utilisation, while GRM (including inventory gains/ losses) improved significantly to $9.22 per barrel from $4.45 per barrel in the previous fiscal, driven by healthy product crack spreads across key categories. Over the medium term, the company’s GRM is expected to be healthy and in-line with product crack spread in the international markets. 

Amidst the volatile crude prices and forex movements caused by the ongoing West Asia conflict affecting only the month of March, MRPL’s operating performance improved materially in fiscal 2026 - supported by healthy refining margins and stable throughput. While the operating income (net of excise duty) waned by ~6% YoY to Rs. 88,667 crore amidst softer crude prices, the Operating Profit Before Depreciation, Interest and Tax (OPBDIT) increased sharply to ~Rs 6,235 crore in fiscal 2026 from ~Rs 2,380 crore in fiscal 2025, driven by recovery in GRMs, improved operating efficiencies and inventory gains. Resultantly, debt protection metrics strengthened with adjusted interest coverage improving to ~7.11 times in fiscal 2026 from ~2.42 times in the previous fiscal. However, gearing remained stable around unity at ~1.04 times as on March 31, 2026 (vs ~1.03 times as on March 31, 2025), with overall debt increasing to ~Rs 14,300 crore, largely on account of higher working capital borrowings and forex impact on external commercial borrowings. Over the medium term, healthy operating performance and cash accrual should be sufficient to fund capex needs, upcoming debt as well as planned investments; with stable debt levels keeping financial risk profile healthy in fiscal 2027. The company continues to derive financial flexibility from being a part of the ONGC group and its ability to access capital markets, which enables it to raise funds on a short notice at favorable rates. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has partially consolidated Shell MRPL Aviation Fuels and Services Limited, a joint venture of MRPL. The ratings also centrally factor in the strategic importance of the company to, and strong support from, its parent, ONGC.

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Strong support from parent:** MRPL benefits significantly from the operational, financial, and managerial support of ONGC, which owns an effective 88.58% stake in the company, 71.63% held directly and the remaining indirectly through its shareholding in Hindustan Petroleum Corporation Ltd (rated 'Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+'). The company is critical to the parent’s strategy of being an integrated oil and gas entity with presence across exploration and production, refining, and marketing. Business integration of the wholly owned petrochemical entity with MRPL has increased the strategic importance of the latter to ONGC, which aims to be the leader in the oil, gas and petrochemicals value chain. 

**Comfortable financial risk profile:** The company’s financial risk profile improved in fiscal 2026, supported by a sharp recovery in its operating performance and GRMs. Despite an increase in total debt to ~Rs 14,300 crore as on March 31, 2026 (from ~Rs 12,867 crore as on March 31, 2025), driven by higher working capital requirements and forex impact on external borrowings, the overall credit metrics remained comfortable. Gearing was largely stable at ~1.04 times as on March 31, 2026 (vs ~1.03 times as on March 31, 2025), while adjusted interest coverage improved significantly to ~7.11 times from ~2.42 times in the previous fiscal. Over the medium term, healthy operating performance and steady cash accruals are expected to adequately support capex requirements and planned investments, while keeping leverage at moderate levels. However, exposure to crude price volatility, forex movements, and working capital intensity will remain key monitorables. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Susceptibility to volatility in crude oil prices and product cracks:** Crude oil prices have been volatile over the past few years, especially in the last month leading up to the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 driven by the unrest in the Middle East. While the average prices for the Indian basket in fiscal 2026 were stable at $71/bbl in comparison to the average of $79/bbl in fiscal 2025, the war-affected month of March 2026 saw a sharp spike with the prices averaging ~$113/bbl. Such volatility has translated into volatile GRMs. Further, as MRPL imports around 80% of its crude oil requirement, any volatility in oil prices impacts operating performance while also exposing it to fluctuations in foreign exchange rates. 

**Moderate business risk profile** : Being a standalone refinery, operating performance has high earnings sensitivity to GRM. Overall GRM was impacted in fiscal 2020 and fell to $2.5 per barrel which had recovered strongly since and remained healthy at $8-11 per barrel till fiscal 2024 on the back of healthy crack spreads and support from inventory gains. However, with global economic slowdown, moderation in product crack spreads and volatility in global oil prices leading to inventory losses led to reported GRM’s having moderated to $4.45/bbl in fiscal 2025. In fiscal 2026, healthy crack spreads resulted in recovery of GRMs to maintain ~$9.22/bbl also factoring in high inventory gains from crude price volatility amidst the ongoing West Asia conflict. Over the medium term, GRM is expected to be healthy, albeit moderated from the fiscal 2023-2024 levels. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Superior 

The support received from ONGC strongly benefits the company’s financial flexibility, enabling it to access funding sources at attractive rates. MRPL also has access to secured sanctioned fund-based limit of Rs 900 crore of which, ~Rs 430 crores has been allocated to various banks, with nominal utilisation. Further, the company also has sufficient available non-fundbased limits. Combined long-term debt obligation of around Rs 300-400 crore in fiscal 2027 is expected to be sufficiently covered by MRPL’s internal accruals. 

# **<u>ESG Profile</u>** 

Crisil Ratings believes the environment, social, and governance (ESG) profile of MRPL supports its strong credit risk profile. 

The oil and gas sector has a moderate environmental and social impact, primarily driven by its raw material sourcing strategies, waste intensive process, and its direct impact on the health of the environment. The company has continuously focused on mitigating its environmental and social risks. 

# **<u>Key ESG highlights:</u>** 

# **Environmental** 

- MRPL remains committed to embedding sustainability into every aspect of its operations and has set a Net Zero target (Scope-1,2) by the year 2038. 

- During the financial year 2024-25, MRPL generated solar energy totaling 8,838 MWh and total solar energy consumed was 56,729 MWh (including the solar energy purchased). Several other energy efficiency improvement measures led to total fuel savings of 38,725 MT of Oil equivalent 

- 78.54% of total hazardous and non-hazardous waste was recycled & re-used and remaining was co-processed in the cement industry, thereby achieving minimum quantity of hazardous waste disposal to Treatment, Storage and Disposal Facility (TSDF) & Incineration. 56,77,318 m<sup>3</sup> (Cubic Meter) of desalinated water from plant was utilized during financial Year 2024- 25, as a result of many water conservation measures 

- A 50-acre greenbelt has been developed at Pilikula Biodiversity Park with approximately 4,000 native plant species of Western Ghats. Additionally, a 25-acre afforestation project at Bengre was executed in coordination with the Karnataka Forest Department 

# **Social** 

MRPL achieved 111 days for refinery and 3442 days for aromatic complex without reportable lost time injuries. Also, 100% of all vessels hired for crude and vessels accepted for export necessarily meet environmental compliance terms covered under MARPOL. 

- During FY 2024–25, MRPL had provided training on skills and health & safety practices to more than 80% of the employees. Besides, a high degree of unionization was recorded among the workforce (~89% for employees & ~95% for workers) 

- MRPL takes up the CSR programmes under five focus areas, namely Shiksha Samrakshan, Arogya Samrakshan, Bahujan Samrakshan, Prakruti Samrakshan and Sanskrithi Samrakshan, it also promotes sustainable and equitable development in aspirational districts. 

# **Governance** 

- MRPL’s governance structure is characterized by one-third board being independent with nominee directors from promoter company. 

- Audit & Nomination and Remuneration committees are dominated by independent members with an independent director serving as the chairperson. Furthermore, decision making on sustainability related issues is overseen by Corporate Social Responsibility and Sustainability Development Committee (CSR&SD). 

- Nearly 100% of the Investor complaints and references were resolved (43262 of 43269 complaints resolved) and there were no whistleblower complaints during FY 25. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** <u>Stable</u> 

Crisil Ratings believes the company will remain strategically important to, and will continue to receive the required operational, managerial, and financial support from its parent, ONGC.

# **Downward Factors:**

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

A standalone refinery, MRPL is ~88.58% held by ONGC, 71.63% held directly and the remaining indirectly through its shareholding in Hindustan Petroleum Corporation Ltd. It is located near Mangaluru port in Karnataka, and has a nameplate capacity of 15 million tonne per annum. 

Its aromatic complex, commissioned in fiscal 2015, has capacity to produce around 920 kilo tonne per annum (KTPA) of paraxylene and around 280 KTPA of benzene, along with other by-products. The plant has one of the largest paraxylene manufacturing capacities in India. It utilises feedstock (naphtha and aromatic streams) from the company’s refinery adjacent to the plant. 

MRPL’s refinery configuration has a Nelson Complexity Index of 11.67, and Petchem intensity of ~9.5%.
