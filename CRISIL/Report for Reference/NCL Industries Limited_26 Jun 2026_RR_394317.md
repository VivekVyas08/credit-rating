### **<u>Detailed rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its 'Crisil A/Stable/Crisil A1' ratings on the bank facilities and fixed deposit programme of NCL Industries Limited (NCL). 

The ratings continue to reflect NCL's strong market presence in south India, improved business performance, and healthy financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by the company's vulnerability to fluctuations in input costs, risks associated with significant upcoming capital expenditure (capex), the cyclical nature of the cement industry, and the commoditised nature of its products. 

### **<u>Analytical approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of NCL and its subsidiary, Tern Distilleries Pvt Ltd (TDPL). Also, NCL has a joint venture (JV), NCL Buildtek & NCL Industries JV, with its group company, NCL Buildtek Ltd. However, the JV has no major business activities. In April 2023, the company acquired Vishwamber Cements Ltd (VCL), which became a wholly owned subsidiary of NCL in fiscal 2024.

### **<u>Key rating drivers - Strengths</u>** 

**Established market position in south India:** NCL is a well-established player in the cement industry with over four decades of experience. The company has a significant production capacity, with 2.6 million tonne per annum (MTPA) of clinker and 4 MTPA of cement. Its operations are supported by an abundance of limestone from its captive mines and a reliable captive power generation capacity of 23.5 MW, which meets approximately 30–35% of its power requirements. The company's strategic location also contributes to its moderate operating efficiency. 

In addition to its core cement business, NCL has diversified its product portfolio by entering into other building materials segments, including ready-mix concrete and cement-bonded particle boards. The company has discontinued its doors division, effective fiscal 2026. Its products are marketed under several brands, including Nagarjuna Cement, Nagarjuna RMC, Bison Panels and NCL doors. 

**Business performance to remain strong:** NCL reported a strong business performance, driven by a recovery in profitability, which was fuelled by improved sales volumes and high realisation. Following a sharp decline in fiscal 2025, the company saw an improvement in price realisation in fiscal 2026. Revenue reached Rs 1,420 crore, driven by growth in cement revenue, despite a moderation in other segment revenue. The company's decision to discontinue its loss-making doors division in fiscal 2026 is expected to further enhance its bottom line. 

In the medium term, realisations are expected to remain stable, while the company is poised for volume growth in fiscal 2027, driven by a full year of operations at its recently commissioned Vizag Unit. NCL's margin is expected to remain above 11% in fiscal 2027. 

**Healthy financial risk profile; however, debt-funded capex in pipeline:** As of March 31, 2026, NCL had healthy networth of Rs 940 crore and gearing of 0.25 time. However, the company's debt is expected to increase over the next three fiscals due to planned capex of Rs 910–920 crore to set up a hybrid power facility in Tamil Nadu. The capex will be undertaken in phases, with Phase 1 expected to be funded by debt of Rs 292 crore. 

While this will lead to an increase in debt, the company's strong networth is expected to keep the peak gearing below 0.9 time. Additionally, the debt protection metrics are expected to remain healthy, as the interest during construction is being capitalised as part of the project cost, and the debt is proposed to have a moratorium of 2 years. 

# **<u>Key rating drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Exposure to intense competition and volatility in raw material prices:** As a cement player, NCL is exposed to volatility in the prices of key inputs such as coal, petcoke, raw materials (excluding limestone, which is sourced from captive mines), packing materials, and diesel. The cement industry is characterised by intense competition and limited product differentiation, which constrains the company's pricing power and flexibility. 

**Susceptibility to risks related to the commoditised nature of products and cyclicality in the cement industry:** The cement industry is prone to capacity additions that occur in fits and starts, due to the lengthy gestation period required to set up new facilities and the tendency of multiple players to add capacities during peak market cycles. This has historically led to unfavourable price cycles. During cyclical downturns, the industry experiences slow demand, which limits capacity utilisation and hinders the ability of players to pass on increases in input costs. 

**Project risks associated with the upcoming capex:** The project undertaken by the company to set up a hybrid power facility entails various risks. The demand risk is assessed as moderate, as the primary intention is to utilise the power produced for internal consumption. The implementation risk is considered moderate to high, given that the project is still in its nascent stage, despite having a timeline of 20 months for Phase 1, which includes land acquisition, and a comfortable residual timeline till March 2028. The company has already acquired around 410 acre of the required 509 acre of land. Finally, funding risk is moderate, as although debt tie-up is pending, the company is in advanced discussions with lenders, and its strong track record and healthy balance sheet are expected to facilitate funding, with cash flow generated annually sufficient to meet the equity portion of the capex. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

The company's bank limit utilisation has been relatively low, averaging less than 20% over the past 12 months through April 2026, indicating a comfortable liquidity position. With expected cash accrual of over Rs 120 crore per fiscal year, the company is well-equipped to meet its yearly term debt obligations of Rs 32 crore over the medium term, leaving a surplus to provide a liquidity cushion. Furthermore, the proposed debt for capex is expected to have a two-year moratorium, providing additional flexibility to manage its debt obligations. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes NCL will continue to benefit from its established market position and the extensive experience of its promoters. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors:** 

Higher realisation, with healthy utilisation of 90%, resulting in improved business performance Sustenance of the financial risk profile and liquidity at healthy levels. 

# **Downward factors:** 

Sharp decline in realisation or utilisation, leading to low revenue and operating margin below 7% Weakening of the financial risk profile or liquidity on account of crystallisation of contingent liabilities 

# **<u>About the group</u>** 

NCL, incorporated in 1979 by the late K Ramachandra Raju, manufactures different varieties of cement, cement-bonded particle boards, ready-mix concrete and doors. K Gautam (Managing Director) currently manages the operations. NCL acquired TDPL in fiscal 2021 and intends to set up a greenfield cement unit in the land owned by the latter in Visakhapatnam. Currently, there are no operations in the subsidiary. In April 2023, NCL acquired VCL for Rs 16.23 crore. VCL became a wholly owned subsidiary of NCL in fiscal 2024. VCL is not operational and has not established any cement plants. It was acquired to convert the limestone mines of 322 acre under its lease into captive mines of NCL.
