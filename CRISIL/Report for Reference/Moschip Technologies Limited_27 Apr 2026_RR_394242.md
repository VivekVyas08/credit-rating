## **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has placed its rating on the long-term bank facilities of Moschip Technologies Limited (Moschip) on ‘ **Rating Watch with Positive Implications** ’. 

The rating action follows an expected strengthening of Moschip’s business risk profile with the company announcing its plans of acquiring a majority stake in Vayavya Labs Private Limited (VLPL), a Belgavi based semiconductor company specializing in the embedded systems segment; subject to receipt of necessary statutory approvals. 

Moschip plans to acquire a 73% stake from the existing shareholders of VLPL, for consideration of Rs.245.49 crore, to be funded through a mix of cash (Rs.148.52 crore) and issuance of equity shares. VLPL has expertise in embedded software, digital twin platforms and verification technologies, primarily catering to the automotive industry. The proposed acquisition is expected to strengthen Moschip’s business risk profile, with VLPL’s experience in embedded systems complementing Moschip’s capabilities in the semiconductor design space. The acquisition will also provide Moschip with access to new markets, such as Japan, Europe, Israel, and Australia, and boost its cross-selling opportunities. 

This acquisition, subject to receipt of necessary approvals is expected to conclude by end of May 2026 post which the rating watch will be resolved. Further, Moschip intends to acquire the balance 27% equity stake in fiscal 2029 wherein the valuation would be linked to VLPL’s operating performance. While this acquisition is sizeable, Moschip’s financial risk profile is expected to remain comfortable on account of a healthy capital structure, no major capex plans given the company is a fabless entity, and accruals sufficient to support the operations of the combined entity. 

The ratings continue to draw comfort from Moschip’s own operations, which are improving steadily primarily on account of market share absorption benefits arising from a moderate scale of operations presently, supported by its experienced management team and in-house training capabilities, a key strength in a technically complex industry such as semiconductors, and past track record of growth through inorganic routes. Revenues have grown by 30% on-year to Rs.432 crores during the first nine months of fiscal 2026. Operating profitability continued to remain rangebound at 11.2% during 9MFY26 (FY25:12%) on account of high employee costs, as the company continues to prioritise top-line growth. 

Medium term revenues are expected to grow at double-digit rates, driven by Moschip’s analog design capabilities and turnkey semiconductor projects, which differentiate the company from peers largely reliant on staff augmentation. Growth will be further supported by acquisition-led synergies with expected improvement in the embedded systems segment, continued scaling of staff deployment and offshore delivery centres, and established engagements with global design partners. This will be supported by additional revenues arising from Moschip’s collaboration with the Government of India for developing a high-performance computing (HPC) chip, where receipt of milestone-linked payments has been timely. Operating margins are likely to remain stable, aided by gradual improvement in workforce utilisation, and a higher share of fixed-price contracts under VLPL, which offer superior profitability. 

These strengths are partially offset by Moschip’s moderate scale of operations with high geographic and customer concentration risks as well as the inherent industry risk of cyclicality in operations that the company is exposed to. 

The financial risk profile has remained stable, driven by a healthy operating performance and low reliance on external sources of funding. Debt protection metrics should remain stable over the medium term considering no capex plans due to Moschip’s and VLPL’s fabless operations, timely payments received from counterparties who are established global players, and expected low reliance of debt over the medium term. That said, any significant exposure to external debt that could alter its debt protection metrics would be monitorable.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Established management team with semiconductor expertise, expected to drive integration and scale-up postacquisition** 

For close to a decade, Moschip Technologies has been led by an executive leadership team with over 30 years in the semiconductor industry, which has essentially driven the turnaround in the company’s operations. The semiconductor industry is highly complex in nature requiring technical knowledge, especially considering the emergence of new technologies and evolving growth segments. 

The management’s capabilities have enabled Moschip’s expansion across the value chain, reflected in a 40% CAGR in revenues over fiscals 2019-25. Post the acquisition of VLPL, Moschip’s leadership is expected to drive operational improvements, enhance execution, and support scaling of VLPL’s business, while also driving diversification of overall revenues. 

# **Established set-up to tap into a readily available talent pool** 

Employee costs are the largest cost head for semiconductor design services companies and usually account for around ~75% of cost of sales. However, the semiconductor industry faces a challenge in attracting and retaining skilled workers. Additionally, as Moschip scales up, retaining a stable workforce is essential to support its growth trajectory. To address the industry-wide shortage of skilled talent, Moschip has its own training institute in Hyderabad. The program enrolls engineering graduates, is conducted twice a year, and trains students thereby providing a talent pipeline relevant to projects undertaken by the company. The intake is aligned with Moschip’s manpower requirements based on ongoing and upcoming projects. This, coupled with employee-forward initiatives, has helped the company register a significantly lower attrition rate compared to the industry average. 

Similarly, VLPL operates a training institute in Belgavi, further strengthening talent availability and supporting employee engagement going forward. 

# **Strong financial risk profile** 

The financial risk profile is driven by Moschip’s healthy capital structure and debt protection metrics. The acquisition of VLPL, though material with respect to the scale of Moschip’s operations, is not expected to deteriorate Moschip’s financial risk profile on account of it being prudently funded with cash generated through internal accruals and equity. The financial risk profile is expected to be stable, supported by cash accruals sufficient to manage the combined entity’s operations, and no major capex plans due to operations as a fabless entity. 

Working capital intensity remains low, characterised by favourable payment terms of around 60-100 days in the semiconductor industry and further benefitting from reliable blue-chip clientele. As the company operates in engineering services, it has no inventory. Continued reliance on internal accruals for running operations is expected to restrict dependence on external sources of funding. Any significant exposure to external debt that could alter its debt protection metrics would be monitorable. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Exposure to inherent risks and cyclicality in the semiconductor industry** 

The semiconductor industry is inherently cyclical in nature due to its dependence on end-user demand as demand from the consumer electronics and automotive industries mimic broader economic cycles. Additionally, the semiconductor industry is marked by high capital intensity resulting in capacity expansions during peak periods and consequent oversupply and pricing pressures during downturns. This is reflected in annual renewals of contracts by clients, though Moschip’s established client relations and track record of project executions partially mitigates this. However, Moschip has rolled out initiatives around new product launches catering to emerging trends such as IoT chips, higher adoption of AI  as well as foresees growth opportunities from data centres, automotives and wireless applications. This, coupled with the company's current moderate scale that enables it to grow faster than the overall industry could mitigate the industry led risks to an extent. 

# **Modest scale of operations compared to global counterparts, with high geographic and client concentration** 

Despite registering strong growth in revenues, the company’s revenue size is currently modest when compared to global semiconductor design peers. This is partially offset by the company deploying its own workforce on projects for large 

multinational clients. However, the semiconductor industry is inherently concentrated with a few dominant players, and therefore Moschip faces significant client concentration risk, with top five customers accounting for a majority of the revenue. Continued reliance on limited number of clients could lead to cash flow volatility in the event of delays or cancellations. Most of its clients continue to be based in the US, which accounts for a majority of the semiconductor industry, underscoring its geographic concentration, though the recent collaboration with the Central Government offers revenue visibility over the medium term. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Adequate 

The company has access to fund-based working capital lines of Rs. 78 crores and non-fund-based limits of Rs.28 crore for the purpose of execution of Government tenders. The reliance on these limits is estimated to be low as the company receives timely payments for the HPC chip project. Cash and liquid surplus are expected to build up gradually, given low debt obligation in the near term. The company is unlikely to have capex plans as it outsources manufacturing to the Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC). That said, acquisition of the remaining stake of VLPL, contingent on VLPL’s valuation as of FY28, may warrant reliance on debt funding. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Strong double-digit growth in revenues while maintaining healthy operating margins of 13-14% Efficient working capital cycle and low reliance on external debt leading to maintenance of financial risk profile 

# **Downward factors** 

Sustained deterioration of the business risk profile due to decline in revenues on account of  customer loss/weak orderbook levels or operating margin falling to below 9-10% 

Significant deterioration of the financial risk profile due to weakened operating performance, stretched working capital or larger-than-expected external debt raise to fund inorganic growth plans 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Moschip is a Hyderabad-based company engaged in semiconductor and electronic system design services, with over two decades of operational history. Originally established in 1999 as a fabless semiconductor company, the company operates along the semiconductor value chain through project executions and tie-ups with global semiconductor players. 

For the nine months ended 31<sup>st</sup> December 2025, the company registered revenues of Rs.431.9 crore and profit after tax of Rs.27.4 crore as against Rs.332 crore and Rs.25 crore, respectively, during the corresponding nine months of fiscal 2025. 

As of 31<sup>st</sup> December 2025, promoters held 40.97% of the equity shares of the company, of which a majority is ultimately held by IQuest Enterprises Limited, and the remaining is held by the public.
