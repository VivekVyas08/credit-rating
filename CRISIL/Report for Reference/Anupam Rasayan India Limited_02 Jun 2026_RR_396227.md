### **<u>Detailed rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has placed its ratings on the bank facilities of Anupam Rasayan India Limited (ARIL; part of the Anupam Rasayan group) on **‘Rating Watch with Developing Implications** '. Crisil Ratings has assigned its ‘ **Crisil A+** ’ rating to the non-convertible debentures and placed on **‘Rating Watch with Developing Implications** '. 

The ratings have been placed on watch following announcement by ARIL of acquisition of 43.3–48.2% equity stake in Bliss GVS Pharma Ltd, a pharmaceutical (pharma) formulations company based in India, which may be funded through debt, equity or internal accrual. Capital allocation to fund this acquisition will be critical; ARIL is likely to fund the transaction through debt and equity funding. Crisil Ratings will continue to engage with the company’s management and resolve the watch once it obtains clarity on the funding and approvals. Crisil Ratings notes that ARIL will undertake debt of Rs 300 crore and the balance will be funded by global investment management fund in the form of non-controlling non-voting equity investment, and hence, expects the rating to not move more than a notch. That said, Crisil Ratings will continue to monitor developments regarding the acquisition. 

The business risk profile is stable, with improvement in the agrochemicals segment and ramp-up of pharma and polymer divisions in fiscal 2026, which led to increase in revenue to Rs 2,365 crore from Rs 1,439 crore in fiscal 2025. Operating margin declined to 22.19% in fiscal 2026 from 27.67% in fiscal 2025 owing to limited ability to pass on higher cost. The working capital cycle improved, as reflected in decline in inventory from 510 days as on March 31, 2025, to 324 days as on March 31, 2026, and fall in receivables from 190 days to 148 days, respectively. The working capital cycle is expected to improve with the full consolidation of Jayhawk Fine Chemicals LLC (JFCL) in fiscal 2027. 

The ratings continue to reflect the group’s strong business risk profile, backed by established market position and diversified revenue streams, and healthy financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by large working capital requirement, modest operating efficiency and susceptibility of operating margin to volatility in raw material prices and foreign exchange (forex) rates. 

### **<u>Analytical approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of ARIL and its wholly owned subsidiaries―JFCL, Jainam Intermediates Pvt Ltd, ARIL Transmodal Logistic Pvt Ltd, ARIL Fluorospeciality Pvt Ltd, Anupam Japan GK, Anupam Europe AG and Anupam USA, LLC; and subsidiary, Tanfac Industries Ltd (Tanfac). This is because these entities, collectively referred to as the Anupam Rasayan group, are in similar businesses and have operational linkages.

**<u>Key rating drivers - Strengths</u>** 

**Established market position and healthy operating efficiency:** The group has a strong market position in custom synthesis and specialty chemicals because of integrated operations and robust clientele. Backed by presence of around four decades, it has expertise in complex multi-step synthesis and innovative manufacturing processes. It caters to chemical and agrochemical companies, including 31 multinationals and 75 clients. Customer-centric solutions have positioned ARIL as a preferred partner for clients across various industries, including agrochemicals, personal care, pharmaceuticals and other specialty segments (polymers and electronic chemicals). Expansion into high-value fluorination chemistry products and polymer applications is likely to strengthen the market position. 

**Diversified revenue streams and backward integration:** The pharma and polymer segments are estimated to contribute more than 30% of revenue in fiscal 2026. These segments are positioned as growth drivers, reducing dependence on agrochemicals. The company is commercialising new molecules launched in fiscal 2024 and in the first half of fiscal 2025, and is focusing on growing contribution from Japanese customers in the fluoropolymer segment, new product introductions in the US market and applications in engineering fluids and polymers in the polymer segment. 

ARIL has acquired Tanfac, which is a leading producer of hydrogen fluoride and potassium fluoride in India. These materials are critical inputs for fluorinated molecules used in pharma, polymers and agrochemicals. By acquiring Tanfac, ARIL has secured an uninterrupted supply of these raw materials. 

**Strong financial risk profile:** Capital structure will be supported by reduction in debt and absence of large, debt-funded capital expenditure (capex). Networth and total outside liabilities to adjusted networth (TOLANW) ratio were healthy at Rs 3,334 crore and 0.78 time, respectively, as on March 31, 2026. Over the medium term, the TOLANW ratio is expected at less than 0.80 time. 

Debt protection metrics were robust on account of strong operating profitability. The financial risk profile will likely remain healthy over the medium term with steady accretion to reserve and no debt-funded capex. 

# **<u>Key rating drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Working capital-intensive operations:** Gross current assets (GCAs) were large at around 504 days as on March 31, 2026 (646 days a year earlier), owing to inventory and  receivables of more than 300 days and 140 days, respectively. Inventory reduced to 310 days as on March 31, 2026, from 500 days a year earlier. The working capital cycle is likely to improve with commercialisation of new products and completion of the acquisition of JFCL in fiscal 2027. 

**Susceptibility to volatility in forex rates, economic downturns and intense competition from global players:** The company derives over 50% revenue from exports to Europe, North America, and other regions, and imports 15–20% of its raw materials, thus benefiting from a partial hedge. Though open positions are hedged through forward contracts, operations remain susceptible to sharp changes in forex rates. Any economic downturn impacting demand poses additional challenge. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Liquidity will be supported by expected net cash accrual of Rs 280–350 crore against debt obligation of Rs 40 crore in fiscal 2027. Bank limit utilisation averaged 88% for the 12 months through April 2026. Cash and equivalent stood at more than Rs 378 crore as on March 31, 2026. 

Tanfac has been debt-free in the past three fiscals and has no working capital limit. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors:** 

Prudent working capital management with GCAs below 250 days, and improvement in liquidity 

Steady increase in volume growth with significant diversification in the revenue profile; increase in contribution of specialty chemical/pharma/polymer divisions, and maintenance of operating margin above 25% 

# **Downward factors:** 

Steady decline in revenue and fall in operating margin below 18% 

Large, debt-funded capex or further stretch in the working capital cycle (GCAs above 450 days), with gearing above 1 time 

# **<u>About the group</u>** 

ARIL, incorporated in 1977, is promoted by Anand Desai. The company manufactures specialty chemicals used in crop protection, pharma, polymer, pigment and biocide industries. Its manufacturing units are at Sachin and Jhagadia in Gujarat. The company has ISO 9001-2015, ISO 45001-2018 and ISO 14001-2015 certifications for quality, occupational health and safety and environmental management systems, respectively. ARIL is listed on the Bombay Stock Exchange and the National Stock Exchange. It acquired 25.79% in Tanfac on May 20, 2022. In fiscal 2025, Tanfac achieved revenue of Rs 557 crore and Ebitda (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation) of Rs 131 crore.
