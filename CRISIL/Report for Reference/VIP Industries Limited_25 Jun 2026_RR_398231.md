### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has downgraded its rating on the long-term bank facility of VIP Industries Limited (VIP) to **‘Crisil A-/Negative’** from ‘Crisil A/Negative’ and has reaffirmed its rating on the short-term bank facilities and commercial paper programme at ‘Crisil A2+’. 

The rating action factors in higher-than-anticipated decline in the company’s operating performance in fiscal 2026 and the expectation that it will remain below earlier estimates over the near term. VIP is likely to continue reporting net losses in fiscal 2027, which will constrain the financial risk profile and impact key debt metrics. The company is undergoing a transition with Multiples Alternate Asset Management (Multiples) acquiring 31.9% stake and assuming management control in December 2025. 

Revenue declined 15% year-on-year to Rs 1,858 crore in fiscal 2026 amid intense competition across channels. The company reported operating (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation [Ebitda]) losses for the third consecutive time in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 partly due to one-time costs of Rs 23 crore and inventory liquidation support expenses of Rs 30 crore. Consequently, operating losses in fiscal 2026 widened to Rs 241 crore compared with Ebitda of Rs 88 crore in fiscal 2025. Gross margin contracted sharply by ~930 basis points (bps) to ~36% in fiscal 2026 (fiscal 2025: ~46%), driven by low realisation and provisioning of Rs 122 crore towards slow-moving inventory. The entire provision was recognised in the first three quarters of fiscal 2026 with no further provisioning expected in fiscal 2027. Liquidity remained supported by monetisation of non-core assets and inventory liquidation. Two assets were monetised in the third quarter of fiscal 2026 with a third asset identified for sale in the first half of fiscal 2027; its timely execution remains monitorable. 

Revenue growth is expected to remain modest in fiscal 2027 (mid-single digits) before improving to 12–13% compound annual growth rate (CAGR) over the medium term, supported by new product launches and initiatives to strengthen channel presence. Operating profitability is expected to recover gradually, aided by cost rationalisation measures, including supply chain optimisation, value engineering, scale-based sourcing and improved retail network efficiency. While these measures should support margin recovery and reduce net losses, the pace of profitability improvement remains a key rating sensitivity. Debt protection metrics are expected to stay constrained in the near to medium term due to modest profitability. 

Crisil Ratings expects debt to rise over the medium term to support working capital needs and capital expenditure (capex) for expansion and maintenance. Networth declined to Rs 288 crore as on March 31, 2026 from Rs 614 crore a year earlier due to losses, though planned asset monetisation partly offset the impact on cash flow. Liquidity remains adequate, supported by unencumbered cash of Rs 116 crore and absence of long-term debt obligation. 

Multiples, along with co-investors, acquired 31.89% stake in VIP through a two-tranche transaction at Rs 388 per share, completed between September and December 2025. Crisil Ratings will monitor the company’s strategy and financial policy under the new management with expectations of timely promoter support, if required. 

The ratings continue to reflect VIP’s established brand in the luggage segment, supported by a diversified product and revenue profile, and potential support from new promoters. These strengths are partly offset by weak profitability due to intense competition from organised and unorganised players, modest financial risk profile and high working capital intensity.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Established brand in the luggage industry, supported by diversified product range and strong distribution network** 

VIP holds a dominant position in the Indian luggage industry and caters to mass and premium segments. The company has a diversified range of products, priced across a wide range. It derived around 60% of revenue in fiscal 2025 from upright hard luggage, followed by upright soft luggage (16%), backpacks (13%), duffel bags (8%) and handbags for women (3%). It has products across premium (Caprese and earlier also Carlton), mass premium (VIP, Sky bags) and value (Aristocrat, Alfa) categories, and thus caters to a larger spectrum of the luggage industry. Furthermore, it has a strong distribution network with nearly 15,000 points of sale globally, of which almost 14,000 are in India. VIP supplies its products to over 40 countries globally. The company had a market share of 29% as on December 31, 2025, which declined from over 40% earlier. All channels, including general trade, modern trade, retail trade and e-commerce were impacted. Improvement across sales channels with recovery in market share will remain monitorable. 

# **Expected support from the new promoters** 

Multiples, along with their co-partners, are expected to extend timely support to VIP should the need arise. Multiples has invested sizeable funds across a plethora of sectors and has access to funds. While turnaround of VIP’s business may take some time, Multiples remains committed which provides some comfort, given VIP’s moderating financial position. Multiples has access to industry experts and has roped in several senior level executives, including the chief marketing officer, who brings expertise from various sectors to revive the subdued performance. Various strategies are being implemented towards reduction in inventory, cleaning up warehouses, reworking forecasting module and improvement in processes. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Weak profitability due to intense competition** 

Rising competition from existing players and new entrants across both organsied and unorgainsed markets has impacted volume and realisation in the past two fiscals. Tough market conditions resulted in decline in market share to 29%. Intense competition and heavy discounting, impacting realisation, have moderated Ebitda in the past two fiscals. This, along with provisioning slow-moving inventory and additional discounts, has resulted in operating loss of Rs 241 crore in fiscal 2026 as against operating profit of Rs 88 crore a year earlier. Volatile polymer prices, exacerbated by the West Asia conflict, may continue impacting profitability in the interim. That said, with recovery in sales and timely liquidation of old inventory, discounts should decline over the medium term, thereby benefiting the operating margin. 

# **Modest financial risk profile** 

The financial risk profile has moderated from comfortable levels maintained in earlier years, owing to weakening profitability for the past two fiscals. Networth will continue to see erosion due to net losses that are likely to continue over the medium term. Despite operational losses in fiscal 2026, the company’s debt (including lease debt) remained flat at Rs 760 crore owing to monetisation of non-core assets and reduction of inventory. However, with lower profitability over the medium term, debt will increase to cover the working capital requirement and modest annual capex, resulting in sub-par debt protection metrics. The absence of material debt obligation provides some support to the financial risk profile. Material equity infusion, if any, used for debt reduction can help resurrect the financial risk profile. 

# **Large working capital requirement** 

The luggage industry is working capital-intensive on account of large inventory maintained through several stock-keeping units. As a result, gross current assets (net of cash) have been historically high above 150 days. However, the company has been able to prudently align its inventory with payables to limit incremental working capital requirement. As an exception, it saw sizeable inventory build-up in fiscal 2024. The company has been consistently making efforts to reduce the inventory, which stood lower at Rs 472 crore as on March 31, 2026, compared with Rs 698 crore a year earlier and Rs 916 crore as on March 31, 2024. Owing to ageing inventory, VIP provided provisioning of Rs 122 crore in fiscal 2026, which weakened profitability. The company is focusing on further bringing down its slow-moving inventory. That said, reduction in working capital debt, timely liquidation of slow-moving inventory and recovery in profitability will remain monitorable. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Adequate 

The overall working capital facilities were utilised ~65% on average for the six months through April 2026. However, utilisation of fund-based and non-fund-based bank limit against available drawing power is almost 90% in recent months. The company had no long-term debt as on March 31, 2026, which obviates pressure on debt term obligation. Capex is expected to remain modest, largely for routine maintenance. 

Liquidity in the near term will be supported by unencumbered cash surplus of Rs 116 crore as on March 31, 2026, and proceeds expected from sale of land (Rs 50–53 crore) in the near term. With limited unutilised working capital limit, the company is arranging for additional banking limit to ensure operations are not interrupted; material increase in limits will be 

critical for meeting incremental working capital needs with net losses expected to continue. The new promoters are expected to extend timely support to VIP, should the need arise. 

# **<u>ESG Profile</u>** 

The environment, social and governance (ESG) profile of VIP supports its already strong credit risk profile. 

This sector can have a significant impact on the environment owing to high water consumption, waste generation and greenhouse gas emissions. The sector’s social impact is characterised by health hazards, leading to higher focus on employee safety and wellbeing and impact on the local community, given the nature of operations. 

VIP has continuously focused on mitigating its environmental and social risks. 

# **Key ESG highlights:** 

In fiscal 2025, the company reported lower-than-peer average intensities of energy consumption (~7.6 megawatt-hour per crore of revenue), scope 1 and 2 emissions (~5t CO2e per crore of revenue) and hazardous and non-hazardous waste generation, aided by several measures taken to improve process efficiency 

- On the social front, gender diversity is higher than peers (with ~12% female employees and ~46% female workers). Its attrition rate was ~17% in fiscal 2025 in line with peers 

- Regarding workplace safety, the company reported nil lost-time injury frequency rate among employees and workers Its governance structure is characterised by a board of eight members, comprising ~50% independent directors and ~38% women directors, split in chairman and CEO positions along with a high investor complaint redressal rate (~100%). It also has extensive disclosures. 

There is growing importance of ESG among investors and lenders. The commitment of VIP to ESG principles will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence and access to capital markets. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Negative 

The negative outlook reflects subdued profitability, constrained by low realisation and volume owing to intense competition. Furthermore, the financial risk profile has weakened owing to continued losses and recovery is expected to be slower-thananticipated. Timely receipt of proceeds from sale of balance non-core assets, along with a gradual recovery of operating margin, and possible equity infusion will remain monitorable. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Significant and sustained growth in revenue, driven by increase in market share and operating margin recovering to 7–9% on sustained basis, supported by ramp up in volume and cost-control initiatives 

- Significant equity infusion by the new promoters, leading to improvement in the financial risk profile and key debt protection metrics 

# **Downward factors** 

Continued business underperformance, leading to operational and net losses 

- Debt levels remaining high due to continuing stretch in the working capital cycle or large debt-funded capex, further weakening sub-par debt protection metrics 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

VIP was incorporated under the Dilip Piramal group as a wholly owned subsidiary of Blow Plast Ltd (BPL) in January 1968. In fiscal 2007, BPL was merged with VIP, following restructuring within the group. The company manufactures hard luggage in India and markets hard and soft luggage sourced from India, China and its Bangladesh subsidiaries. VIP is one of largest players in the luggage industry in India. 

In September 2025, Multiples, along with co-investors—Samvibhag Securities Pvt Ltd, Profitex Ventures LLP and Siddhartha Sacheti—took control of the company and was classified as promoters with 5.89% stake as on September 30, 2025. In the third quarter of fiscal 2026, Multiples completed the acquisition of the balance 26% stake from the Dilip Piramal group/public shareholders.
