### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has upgraded its rating on the long-term bank facilities of Bharat Heavy Electricals Limited (BHEL) to **‘Crisil AA/Stable’** from ‘Crisil AA-/Stable’ and reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A1+’ rating on the short-term bank facilities of the company. 

The upgrade in the rating reflects the strengthening of BHEL’s business and financial risk profiles over the past two years, supported by strong revenue visibility and sustained execution-led growth driven by robust order book and significant improvement in profitability. Outstanding order book stood at an all-time high of Rs 2.4 lakh crore as on March 31, 2026, led by revival in domestic thermal power capital expenditure (capex), providing revenue visibility for a longer term. Order book quality has improved, with increasing share of central public sector units (CPSUs) and private players along with increasing orders from diversified segments such as railways, defence, transmission and nuclear power. With most power sector orders scheduled for completion by fiscals 2029–2030, execution intensity is expected to remain strong, supporting sustained growth in scale. 

Revenue grew 19% year-on-year in fiscal 2026, driven by improving execution. The power segment contributed ~75% of the total revenue for fiscal 2026 (74% in fiscal 2025) while the industry segment (including exports) generated the rest. The operating margin also rose to 7%, from 5.1%, owing to higher execution of recently awarded orders with price variation clauses, milestone-based billing structures and improved procurement strategies. The margin is expected to reach 8–10% over the medium term, supported by completion of low-margin legacy orders, improving operating leverage, better pricing terms and higher share of better-priced contracts. 

BHEL continues to operate with high working capital intensity, inherent to long-gestation projects. However, the company has structurally improved its working capital cycle. Gross current asset (net of cash) moderated to 415–420 days as on March 31, 2026, from above 440 days a year ago, driven by focused monitoring and liquidation of receivables, reduction in realisation of aged debtors and stabilisation in contract assets amid shift from back-ended billing to milestone-based payments in new contracts. The working capital cycle should further improve over the medium term, with reduction in contract assets days. The shift from back-ended billing to milestone-based payment structures will keep contract assets stable despite higher execution levels. However, working capital intensity remains a key monitorable, given the large order sizes and execution cycle. 

The financial risk profile has strengthened significantly, supported by improved profitability and working capital management. 

Total debt reduced to Rs 7,950 crore as on March 31, 2026, from Rs 8,795 crore a year ago. Healthy, non-interest-bearing customer advances supported incremental working capital requirement and limited dependence on debt. Interest coverage improved to 4.24 times in fiscal 2026, from 2.36 times in fiscal 2025, and should further strengthen to over 5 times in the near term with higher operating profitability and a more efficient working capital cycle. 

The capital structure is marked by comfortable gearing of 0.31 time and total outside liabilities to tangible networth (TOL/TNW) ratio of 1.74 times as on March 31, 2026 (0.36 time and 1.60 times respectively a year ago). 

Liquidity remains strong too, marked by sufficient net cash accrual and unencumbered cash and bank balance of Rs 11,867 crore as on March 31, 2026. The annual capex of ~Rs 600 crore will be largely funded through internal cash accrual, which is expected to remain above Rs 1,500 crore per annum over the medium term. Additionally, presence of customer advances clause in the projects and available commercial paper limit of Rs 5,000 crore provide financial flexibility. BHEL will also be investing Rs 

2,500–3,000 crore in the JV (Bharat Coal Gasification and Chemicals Ltd) over the next four years. This investment will be largely funded through internal resources, with no long-term debt envisaged. 

The ratings continue to reflect the leading market position of BHEL in the boiler-turbine-generator (BTG) segment and its strong financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by structural issues in the power sector, longer execution cycle for the power projects and large working capital requirement.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Leading position in the BTG and heavy electrical equipment markets** 

BHEL, a Maharatna PSU, is the leading player in India's BTG and heavy electrical equipment markets, accounting for over 50% of the country’s installed capacity of conventional power projects. The company’s superior execution capabilities support its dominant market presence. BHEL has a market share of above 80% in the 800-megawatt segment in all the tenders floated in the thermal power segment for the past 18–24 months. The vast majority of new thermal power projects in India are executed by the company. To ensure long-term growth, BHEL has diversified into other areas such as hydropower, nuclear power, power transmission, defence, rail transportation, syngas etc. Despite this diversification, the power segment continues to constitute over 80% of the total order book worth Rs 2.4 lakh crore. 

# **Strong and growing order book ensuring revenue visibility** 

BHEL’s business risk profile remains robust, supported by a strong, record-high executable order book of Rs 2.40 lakh crore as on March 31, 2026 (against Rs 1.96 lakh crore a year ago)—translating into order book to revenue ratio of ~7 times and providing strong revenue visibility in the long term. Order inflows remained robust at over Rs 75,000 crore in fiscal 2026, driven by revival in domestic thermal capacity addition. Furthermore, order book quality improved with a higher share of projects from CPSUs and strong private entities, increasing contribution from diversified segments such as railways, defence, nuclear and transmission. BHEL is also working on the coal gasification project with Coal India Ltd (CIL) and has already formed a JV (Bharat Coal Gasification and Chemical Ltd) with CIL for production of ammonium nitrate; this positions the company for substantial long-term orders. The growing order book along with better pricing flexibility and payment terms in the newer contracts, will support BHEL’s operating margin and working capital cycle over the medium term. 

# **Sustained execution-led growth with strong medium-term outlook** 

Execution momentum has strengthened materially, with revenue growing 19% to reach Rs 33,782 crore in fiscal 2026. Revenue is expected to report a compound annual growth rate of 19–20% over the medium term, supported by robust order book in thermal and diversified industrial segments. Sustained scale expansion has enabled BHEL to cross critical thresholds for improved operating leverage. This along with execution of better-priced contracts with price escalation clauses for raw material and completion of legacy low-margin orders led to improvement in the operating margin to 7.0% in fiscal 2026, from 5.1% in fiscal 2025. The margin is expected to rise further to 8–10% over the medium term, led by increasing share of new, high-margin contracts, favourable payment structures and continued benefits from operating leverage. 

# **Healthy financial risk profile with improving coverage metrics** 

The financial risk profile has strengthened, with total debt declining to Rs 7,950 crore as on March 31, 2026, from Rs 8,795 crore a year ago. This reduction was achieved despite growing revenue and working capital needs. Although operations remain working capital intensive, the incremental requirement was largely funded through internal cash accrual and higher customer advances. The company has transitioned from net debt in fiscal 2025 to a net cash position in fiscal 2026. The capital structure remained comfortable, with gearing of 0.31 time as on March 31, 2026 (0.36 time a year ago) and TOL/TNW ratio of 1.74 times (1.60 times). Debt protection metrics also improved; the interest coverage ratio increased to 4.24 times in fiscal 2026, from 2.36 times in fiscal 2025, and is expected to exceed 5 times in the near term and furthermore to ~7 times over the medium term. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Exposure to structural risks in the power sector** 

The company remains significantly exposed to the power sector, which previously faced structural challenges due to slowed execution and lower orders. However, the current order book quality has improved with revival in coal-based thermal power projects with stronger counterparties. Timely, cost-efficient execution will remain monitorable; any delays in execution could impact revenue growth, profitability and the working capital cycle. 

# **High working capital intensity inherent to business model** 

BHEL’s operations remain structurally working capital intensive due to long execution cycle of the projects and leading to high level of receivables and contract assets. However, the risk of doubtful receivables is largely mitigated as 75–80% of debtors are either to central or state PSUs. Gross current assets (comprising debtors, contract assets and inventory) dropped to 418 days as on March 31, 2026, from 437 days a year ago, owing to the realisation of legacy debtors and contract assets. Debtors outstanding for more than two years improved to less than 17% of the overall debtors in fiscal 2026, from 47% in fiscal 2023. Going forward, efficient management of the incremental working capital requirement amid scaling operations will be a key monitorable. Inventory increased to 155 days as on March 31, 2026, as BHEL ramped up its execution and is expected to remain at similar levels over the medium term. These incremental working capital needs will be supported by growing cash accrual and higher customer advances. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Liquidity is driven by free cash and equivalent of around Rs 11,867 crore as on March 31, 2026, and a largely unutilised commercial paper limit of Rs 5,000 crore. The company has fund-based working capital limit of Rs 9,500 crore, which was utilised at about 84% during the six months through May 2026. In the absence of any yearly maturing debt over the medium term, the annual capex of ~Rs 600 crore and incremental working capital requirement will be funded through internal cash accrual and customer advances. 

# **<u>ESG Profile</u>** 

The environment, social and governance (ESG) profile of BHEL supports its already strong credit risk profile. The sector has a moderate environmental and social impact, driven by high water consumption and direct impact on the health and wellbeing of stakeholders. 

BHEL’s focus on addressing these ESG risks supports its already strong credit risk profile. 

# **<u>Key ESG highlights</u>** 

- BHEL has set up nearly 41.8 megawatt-peak capacity of solar power plants, which has helped the company reduce electricity consumption. This reduced the company’s carbon footprint by 28,700 MTCO2-equivalent during fiscal 2025 BHEL has installed 140 rainwater harvesting systems, 21 effluent treatment plants and 19 sewage treatment plants to manage grey water sustainably 

- The governance structure is characterised by independent directors comprising 30% of its board independent as of June 2026. BHEL has a committee at the board level to address investor grievances. 

There is growing importance of ESG among investors and lenders. The commitment of BHEL to ESG principles will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence, given the high share of market borrowing in the company’s overall debt. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

BHEL will continue to benefit from its efficient order execution and project delivery, leading to continuous growth in revenue and profitability, and strong financial risk profile. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

- Sustained improvement in scale of operations, with earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortisation margin sustaining above 9% 

Improvement in the working capital cycle and moderation in GCAs, driven by reduction in outstanding contract assets 

# **Downward factors** 

- Weakening of the business risk profile through low order intake or delay in execution of orders, resulting in reduced scale of business 

Operating profit margin sustaining below 5%, thereby weakening debt protection metrics and overall financial risk profile 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

BHEL is an integrated power plant equipment manufacturer. This Maharatna PSU is one of the largest engineering and manufacturing companies in India. The Government of India holds 58.17% equity stake in the company. 

BHEL operates in the power and industry segments. The power division supplies power plant equipment—such as turbo generators, boilers, turbines and accessories—and erects all types of plants based on gas, coal, hydro, nuclear and solar power. The industry division caters to diverse sectors such as process industries, transportation, power transmission and distribution and defence. BHEL designs, engineers, manufactures, constructs, tests, commissions and services a wide range of products. It has 15 manufacturing units and four active JVs. The company has a widespread overseas footprint, with references in 95 countries.
