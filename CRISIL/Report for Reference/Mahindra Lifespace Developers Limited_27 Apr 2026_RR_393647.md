### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AA/Stable' rating on the long-term bank facilities of Mahindra Lifespace Developers Limited (MLDL). Crisil Ratings has also **withdrawn** its rating on the commercial paper programme of Rs 300 crore at the company's request. There is no outstanding commercial paper, as confirmed by the issuer. The rating action is in line with the withdrawal policy of Crisil Ratings. 

The rating continues to reflect the strong brand name, established track record, scaling up of the residential development and significant improvement in the financial risk profile led by deleveraging on account of equity raised in fiscal 2026. The rating also draws comfort from the strong parentage and expectation of need-based support from Mahindra & Mahindra Ltd (M&M; 'Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+'). These strengths are partially offset by exposure to cyclicality inherent in the real estate business. 

The business risk profile is supported by the company’s continued scale-up in launches and development with residential sales value expected to increase to an all-time high of Rs 3,000-3,200 crore in fiscal 2026 and above Rs 4,200 crore over the medium term (up from Rs 2,804 crore in fiscal 2025). The healthy operating performance of MLDL continued in the first nine months of fiscal 2026, as the company reported strong sales of Rs 1,773 crore, steady collections of Rs 1,472 crore. The increase in sales will be backed by both ongoing projects and new launches. The sales booking and construction progress in the ongoing residential projects also continue to be healthy. The pipeline is primarily for premium/mid-premium segment, in line with the demand trends. 

The residential collections are expected to reach ~Rs 1,700-1,900 crore in fiscal 2026 and over Rs 2,500 crore in the next two fiscals, driven by committed receivables of ~Rs 3,900 crore from ongoing projects and current unsold inventories of Rs 3,960 crore as on December 31, 2025, and upcoming launches. The integrated cities and industrial clusters (IC&IC) segment is expected to continue contributing Rs 450-500 crore to the overall collections in the near term. 

The financial risk profile has improved substantially post equity raise via rights issued (also subscribed by the parent - M&M in relation to its shareholding) of Rs 1,500 crore in the first quarter of fiscal 2026, leading to significant deleveraging from debt levels of Rs 1,400 crore to ~Rs 406 crore as on December 31, 2025. It will further be supported by strong collections and hence healthy cash flow from operations (CFO).  Debt protection metrics are expected to remain healthy, with debt to CFO of below 1.1 times over the medium term (Crisil estimates). CFO to interest may remain healthy, at above 9 times over the medium term (Crisil estimates). 

Liquidity remains comfortable, with cash and cash equivalents of ~Rs 700 crore (of which ~15% is unencumbered) as on December 31, 2025. Adequacy of committed receivables remains high at ~80%. 

In line with its growth strategy, MLDL plans to incur capital expenditure (capex) towards land acquisition and construction over the medium term. The capex will be funded through a mix of cash accrual, external debt and external equity partnerships. The finalisation of such deals and their impact on the financial risk profile are monitorable. 

That said, MLDL continues to be a key entity in the Mahindra group and M&M has subscribed to the rights issue, for its part of the shareholding, done in the first quarter of fiscal 2026.  MLDL is one of the ‘growth gems’ identified by M&M and hence, will remain core to the growth strategy of M&M group. 

# **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of MLDL with its subsidiaries and joint ventures. This is because all these entities operate in the real estate sector and related segments, with significant operational and financial linkages with MLDL, and share a common management with the parent. 

Crisil Ratings has also applied its parent notch-up framework to factor in expected distress support from M&M, given the strategic importance of MLDL to the parent. Further, Crisil Ratings has treated ICDs as debt.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Established market position and growing scale of operations** 

MLDL has an established track record, backed by a strong brand, focusing on timely execution and high saleability of projects. The Company’s development footprint spans 53.65 msf (saleable area) of completed (23.75 msf in area), ongoing and forthcoming residential projects and over 5000 acres of ongoing and forthcoming projects under development / management in its IC&IC business as on December 31, 2025. The core markets for the company include Mumbai Metropolitan Region, Pune & Bengaluru, focusing on premium /mid premium segments (sunsetting affordable segment). It has total estimated gross development value of ~Rs 46,770 crore, including unlaunched projects of Rs 40,210 crore, future phases of the current project worth Rs 2,600 crore and current inventory of Rs 3,960 crore as on December 31, 2025. 

With the company’s continued scale-up in launches and development, the residential sales value is expected to reach all time high of Rs 3,000- 3,200 crore in fiscal 2026 and over Rs 4,200 crore over the medium term (up from Rs 2,805 crore in fiscal 2025). The healthy operating performance of MLDL continued in the first nine months of fiscal 2026, with strong sales of Rs 1,773 crore and steady collections of Rs 1,472 crore. The sales booking and construction progress in the ongoing residential projects also continue to be healthy. The pipeline is primarily for premium/mid-premium segment, in line with the demand trends. 

The residential collections are expected to reach ~Rs 1,700-1,900 crore in fiscal 2026 and over Rs 2,500 crore in the next two fiscals, driven by committed receivables of Rs ~3,900 crore from ongoing projects and current unsold inventories of Rs 3,960 crore as on December 31, 2025, and upcoming launches. 

# **Healthy financial risk profile** 

The financial risk profile has improved substantially post equity raise via rights issue of Rs 1,500 crore in the first quarter of fiscal 2026, leading to significant deleveraging from debt levels of Rs 1,400 crore to ~Rs 406 crore as on December 31, 2025. It will further be supported by strong collections and hence healthy cash flow from operations (CFO). Debt protection metrics are expected to remain healthy, with debt to CFO of below 1.1 times over the medium term (Crisil estimates). CFO to interest may remain healthy, at above 9 times over the medium term (Crisil estimates). 

# **Strong support from the parent, M&M** 

The company represents the Mahindra group’s interest in real estate, and hence, remains strategically important to the parent, given its visibility and branding as a Mahindra venture. The group has also identified MLDL as one of its ‘growth gems’ and will continue to support the company’s growth aspirations. The M&M had subscribed to the rights issue done in the first quarter of fiscal 2026. The rating factors in the financial flexibility arising from the ability to raise funds in the capital market and the operational oversight from the parent. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers</u> -** **<u>Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Exposure to risks and cyclicality inherent in the residential and integrated cities segments of the real estate sector** 

The company operates across various segments including its core premium/mid-premium segment) and across several cities including its core markets (MMR, Pune, Bengaluru) in the residential space. In the IC&IC segment, the company is developing integrated city and Industrial cluster projects in Chennai, Jaipur and Ahmedabad (Gujarat). In the commercial IC&IC segment, the total sale of area on long-term lease depends on the local demand and hence, the level of industrial activity. Exposure to risks and cyclicality inherent in both the residential and IC&IC segments of the real estate sector may cause volatility in saleability and realisation. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Liquidity remains comfortable, with cash and cash equivalents of ~Rs 700 crore (of which ~15% is unencumbered) as on December 31, 2025. Adequacy of committed receivables remains high at ~80% and should suffice to cover the debt obligation and incremental construction cost. The company has a proven track record and ability to raise funds with financial investors/strategic partners; or via capital markets. 

# **<u>ESG Profile</u>** 

The environment, social and governance (ESG) profile of the company supports its already strong credit risk profile. 

The real estate sector has a significant impact on the environment owing to high emissions, waste generation and impact on land and biodiversity. The impact on social factors consists of labour-intensive operations and safety issues on account of construction-related activities. 

MLDL has an ongoing focus on strengthening the various aspects of its ESG profile. 

# **Key ESG highlights** 

MLDL has made a public commitment to develop only net-zero buildings by 2030 and has set a broader climate goal to achieve carbon neutrality (Scope 1+2) across its operations and portfolio by 2040, driven by science-based greenhouse gas (GHG) emission reduction targets approved under the Science Based Targets initiative. 

- In fiscal 2025, the company reported a GHG emissions intensity of 9.2 kilogramme of carbon dioxide equivalent per square foot (kgCO2e/Sqft) for its residential projects and 0.0092 kgCO2e/Sqft for IC&IC projects. 

- MLDL maintains a 100% certified green building portfolio across its residential and mixed-use developments, demonstrating its commitment to sustainable development. 

- The company's workforce has a gender diversity ratio of 24% as of fiscal 2025, higher than the sector average. However, the company's safety record declined in fiscal 2025, with three fatalities reported and a lost time injury frequency rate of 0.466, which was higher than the previous fiscal. 

- MLDL's governance structure features a strong presence of women on its board (around 38%), a dedicated grievance redressal mechanism and comprehensive financial and non-financial disclosures. 

There is growing importance of ESG among investors and lenders. MLDL’s commitment to ESG principles will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence, given its high foreign portfolio investor shareholding and access to capital markets. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

MLDL is expected to maintain its healthy business and financial risk profiles, backed by strong brand name, execution capabilities and expansion in the land bank over the medium term. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

- Significant and sustained increase in collections, driven by high sales in residential projects Strengthening of the financial risk metrics, led by sustained improvement in cash flow Increase in shareholding by M&M or higher strategic importance of MLDL 

# **Downward factors** 

- Sharp decline in revenue and operating profitability or higher-than-expected, debt-funded land acquisition, leading to debt to CFO sustaining above 2.5 times 

Downgrade in the rating of M&M or change in its support philosophy towards MLDL 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Incorporated as Gesco Corporation Ltd in 1999, the company was renamed as Mahindra Gesco Developers Ltd in fiscal 2003 and got its current name in fiscal 2008. MLDL develops residential real estate projects and integrated business cities. 

MLDL focuses on a diverse residential portfolio, ranging from premium and mid-premium projects to affordable housing under the Mahindra Happinest brand. It also has a commercial portfolio focusing on IC&IC projects through brands-Mahindra World City and Origins by Mahindra. The company has several strategic partnerships in the IC&IC category. 

MLDL is listed on the Bombay Stock Exchange and the National Stock Exchange. M&M held ~52% stake in the company as on December 31, 2025.
