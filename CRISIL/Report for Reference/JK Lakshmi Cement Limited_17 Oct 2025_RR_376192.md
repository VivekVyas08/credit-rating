### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AA/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the bank facilities, commercial paper programme and fixed deposits of JK Lakshmi Cement Ltd (JKLC). Further, Crisil Ratings has also migrated its rating on the bank loan facilities from Udaipur Cement Works Ltd (UCWL) to JKLC, following UCWL’s amalgamation with JKLC and on receipt of the letter of novation.. Also, Crisil Ratings has **withdrawn** its rating on the bank facilities of Rs 241.06 crore at the request of the company and on receipt of requisite documents. The rating action is in line with the Crisil Ratings policy on withdrawal of ratings. 

The ratings continue to reflect the company’s healthy business risk profile, marked by its established market position in the northern region, highly cost-efficient operations, and strong financial risk profile driven by robust liquidity. These strengths are partially offset by exposure to project risks and to volatility in input cost, realisation and cyclicality in the cement industry. 

In fiscal 2025, operating income decreased ~9% on-year owing to ~10% decline in realisation due to industry-wide reduction in cement prices amid modest increase in volume. As a result, profitability, as measured by earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation (Ebitda) per tonne, stood at Rs 713 in fiscal 2025, compared with Rs 878 in fiscal 2024. 

In the first quarter of fiscal 2026, operating income increased 11% on-year owing to revival in volume and recovery in realisation. This led to improvement in Ebitda per tonne to Rs 936 (Rs 732 in the first quarter of fiscal 2025). With expected sustenance of recovery in realisation, Ebitda per tonne is expected to increase to Rs 800–900 per tonne from fiscal 2026 onwards. 

JKLC is implementing a brownfield project with 2.3 million tonne per annum (MTPA) clinker capacity in Durg (Chattisgarh) and 4.6 MTPA split grinding capacity spread over Durg (Chhattisgarh), Prayagraj (Uttar Pradesh), Madhubani (Bihar) and Patratu (Jharkhand), with commissioning expected in phases by fiscal 2028. Furthermore, in line with its target of increasing the grinding capacity to 30 MTPA by fiscal 2030, the company is expected to enter the northeastern region by setting up a greenfield project besides increasing the capacity in the northern region. While the company has already secured limestone mines of 250 MT in the northeast, granular details regarding project size and timelines are yet to be finalised. The company is expected to undertake total capital expenditure (capex) of Rs 4,500–5,000 crore during fiscals 2026–2029 to increase its capacity besides undertaking efficiency improvement projects and maintenance capex. Crisil Ratings understands that these projects will be funded 70% through debt and the remaining through internal accrual. Though the company will remain exposed to project execution risk, past track record of successfully completing large capacity addition projects (most recent being an integrated 2.5 MTPA grinding and a 1.5 MTPA clinker facility in Rajasthan during fiscal 2024) will support the credit risk profile. With sizeable capex, the net debt to Ebitda ratio is expected to increase over fiscals 2026–2029 (from 1.6 times as on March 31, 2025). However, the ratio is expected to sustain below 2.5 times at all times. 

While the company is open to inorganic growth opportunities, Crisil Ratings derives comfort from the capex prudence exhibited by the company in the past and understands that the company has flexibility to defer some of the organic 

expansion plans in case any opportunity materialises to ensure credit metrics remain adequate. Any significant and sustained deterioration in the credit metrics will remain monitorable.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Healthy market position in the northern region** 

On consolidated basis, JKLC has installed cement grinding capacity of 18 MTPA, of which 10.75 MTPA is in the northern region as on September 30, 2025, making it among the top five players in the region. The cement grinding capacity is supported by clinker capacity of around 10 MTPA, largely concentrated in the northern region. The company also has diversified presence in the western and eastern regions, with grinding capacity of 3.6 MTPA and 3.5 MTPA, respectively. The cement from its northern and western region plants (Sirohi, Jhajjar, Kalol and Surat) is primarily sold in Rajasthan, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra, which contribute to more than 75% of the sales, while Chhattisgarh and Odisha contribute to 70% of cement from its eastern plant (Durg and Cuttack). 

# **Cost-efficient operations** 

The plants are highly cost-efficient and JKLC is one of the lowest cost producers in the cement industry. Proximity between plants and captive limestone mines assure supply of key raw material at low rates. Furthermore, the rising share of captive power sourcing with total capacity of 235 megawatt (MW) at consolidated level, including captive thermal power plants of 74 MW, waste heat recovery system of 45 MW and solar power plant of 112 MW, along with power purchase agreement for wind power capacity of 4 MW, makes the operations self-sufficient and met 62% of the power requirement in fiscal 2025. The strategic locations of these plants ensure competitive freight cost compared with other industry players. Proximity to raw materials, rise in captive sourcing of power and competitive freight costs will ensure high-cost efficiency over the medium term. 

# **Healthy financial risk profile** 

The financial risk profile has remained healthy with financial leverage, as measured by net debt to Ebitda ratio, stood at 1.6 times in fiscal 2025 (against 1.3 times in fiscal 2024). Adjusted interest coverage ratio declined during fiscal 2025 to 5 times from 7.5 times in fiscal 2024, owing to increase in debt and decline in profitability. The net debt to Ebitda ratio is expected to inch up over the medium term with the company undertaking large capex towards brownfield expansion at Durg and other planned capacity additions besides a railway siding in Durg. However, the net debt to Ebitda ratio will sustain below 2.5 times at all times. Furthermore, given the improvement in profitability, with ramping up of newly commissioned facilities, the financial risk profile is expected to remain healthy over the medium term. Also, while the company is open to inorganic growth opportunities, Crisil Ratings derives comfort from the capex prudence exhibited by the company in the past and understands that the company has flexibility to defer some of the organic expansion plans in case any opportunity materialises to ensure credit metrics remain adequate. Any significant and sustained deterioration in the credit metrics will remain monitorable. 

Furthermore, being part of the JK group (eastern zone), with extensive experience of the promoter and strong liquidity, lends adequate financial flexibility to the company. Liquidity has remained strong at over Rs 500 crore consistently over the past decade and stood at Rs 1,140 crore as on June 30, 2025. Liquidity is likely to remain healthy at more than Rs 500 crore over the medium term. This helps the company to tide over any unforeseen adversities, considering the cyclical nature of the cement industry. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Exposure to project risks** 

The company is expected to undertake few large projects over the medium term to increase capacity to 30 MTPA by fiscal 2030. The  brownfield project cost of 2.3 million tonne per annum (MTPA) clinker capacity in Durg (Chattisgarh) and 4.6 MTPA split grinding capacity spread over Durg (Chhattisgarh), Prayagraj (Uttar Pradesh), Madhubani (Bihar) and Patratu (Jharkhand), is expected to be around Rs 3,000 crore and will be funded through debt of Rs 2,100 crore and the remaining through internal accrual. Furthermore, the company is expected to set up integrated greenfield project in the northeast region besides expansion in the northern region. Thus, the company will remain exposed to risks related to project execution and ability to ramp-up new capacities. 

# **Susceptibility to volatility in input cost, realisation and cyclicality in the cement industry** 

Capacity addition in the cement industry tends to be sporadic because of the long gestation period for setting up a facility and numerous players adding capacity during the peak of a cycle. This has led to unfavourable price cycles for the sector in the past. Decline in cement prices across the industry during fiscal 2025 impacted the profitability of cement players. Realisation and profitability are also affected by demand, supply, offtake and regional factors. Moreover, profitability remains susceptible to volatility in input prices, including raw material, power, fuel and freight. 

# **<u>Liquidity: Strong</u>** 

JKLC has maintained healthy liquidity above Rs 500 crore consistently over the past decade and is expected to continue to do so over the medium term. The liquidity stood at Rs 1,140 crore as on June 30, 2025. The fund-based bank limit of Rs 450 crore at consolidated level was utilised at 57% on average during the six months through May 2025. Cash accrual and liquidity will be more than adequate to repay debt of around Rs 182 crore and Rs 193 crore in fiscals 2026 and 2027, respectively, and partly fund the capex. 

# **<u>ESG profile</u>** 

Crisil Ratings believes the environment, social and governance (ESG) profile of JKLC supports its already strong credit risk profile. 

The cement sector has a significant impact on the environment owing to higher emissions, waste generation and water consumption. This is because of energy-intensive cement manufacturing process and its high dependence on natural resources, such as limestone and coal, as key raw materials. The sector has a social impact as its operations affect local community and involve health hazards. However, JKLC has continuously focused on mitigating its environmental and social risks. 

# **Key ESG highlights:** 

- JKLC aims to achieve net-zero emissions by 2047 and has committed to meet 100% electrical energy consumption through renewables by 2040 (as part of RE100 membership). 

- As part of the near-term targets, the company aims to achieve 7 times water positivity, 60% renewable energy consumption and 20% thermal substitution rate (TSR) by 2030. Against it, the company has achieved 4.49 times water positivity, 48.57% renewable energy consumption and 10.39% TSR till fiscal 2025. 

- The company’s Scope 1 and 2 emissions and energy consumption intensities at ~0.61 tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent and ~0.7 MWh per tonne of cementitious material produced, respectively, is lower compared with its peers. The company’s gender diversity of workers at ~4% is higher compared with its peers. Furthermore, its lost time injury frequency rate (LTIFR) for employees and workers was nil in fiscal 2025. 

- Its governance structure is characterised by 50% of the board comprising independent directors, three-woman directors, dedicated investor grievance redressal system and extensive financial disclosures. 

# **<u>Outlook: Stable</u>** 

Crisil Ratings believes JKLC will continue to benefit from its improving market position, healthy operating efficiency and healthy financial risk profile over the medium term. 

# **<u>Rating Sensitivity Factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Improved business risk profile on the back of significant improvement in market share on a sustained basis Sustained improvement in Ebitda per tonne, above Rs 1,200 

# **Downward factors** 

- Weakening of the business risk profile due to loss of market share and Ebitda per tonne declining sharply on a sustained basis 

- Any large, debt-funded capex or acquisition or decline in profitability, resulting in net debt to Ebitda ratio of more than 2.5 times on a sustained basis 

Substantial delay in capex leading to cost and time overruns. 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

JKLC is part of the JK group (eastern zone) and was promoted by the late Mr Lala Lakshmipat Singhania and late Mr Hari Shankar Singhania. The company is now headed by Mr Bharat Hari Singhania (Chairman Emeritus) and Smt Vinita Singhania (Chairperson and Managing Director). 

The company set up its first cement plant in 1982 with 0.5 MTPA capacity, which has now increased to 18 MTPA and clinker capacity of 10 MTPA as on September 30, 2025. It has integrated units in Sirohi, Udaipur and Durg, and grinding units in Jhajjar, Cuttack, Kalol and Surat. It has a total of 235 MW of captive power capacity at the consolidated level, which includes thermal power plant of 74 MW, waste heat recovery plant of 45 MW, solar power plant of 112 MW and power purchase agreement for wind power capacity of 4 MW. 

The National Company Law Tribunal, Jaipur Bench sanctioned the composite scheme of amalgamation and arrangement between UCWL, Hansdeep Industries & Trading Company Ltd and Hidrive Developers and Industries Ltd, with JKLC on June 12, 2025. The scheme became effective on July 31, 2025, with appointed date of April 1, 2024. 

For fiscal 2025, consolidated operating income of JKLC stood at Rs 6,200 crore against Rs 6,794 crore during the previous fiscal. Profit after tax (PAT) stood at Rs 276 crore in fiscal 2025 as compared with Rs 488 crore in fiscal 2024.
