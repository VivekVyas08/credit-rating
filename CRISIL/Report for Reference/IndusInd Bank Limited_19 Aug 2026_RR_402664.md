Detailed Rationale
Crisil Ratings has revised its outlook on the long-term debt instruments of IndusInd Bank Ltd (IndusInd Bank) to ‘Stable’ from
‘Negative’ while reaffirming its rating at ‘Crisil AA+’. Further, Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A1+’ rating on the short-term
debt instruments of the bank.
 
The revision in outlook factors in the gradual improvement in the earnings profile, stabilisation in the deposit franchise with
increasing share of granular deposits, and the strategic shift in lending to relatively stable asset segments.
 
The bank reported profit of Rs 1,037 crore and return on assets (RoA) of 0.8% for the first quarter of fiscal 2027 (Rs 889 crore and
0.2%, respectively for fiscal 2026), driven by lower credit cost and better operating profit. Credit cost declined to around 1% for
this period, from 1.5% for fiscal 2026. Pre-provisioning profit (PPoP), as a percentage of average assets, has improved to 2.0% for
the first quarter of fiscal 2027, as against 1.7% in fiscal 2026. Sustained improvement in profitability remains key monitorable.
 
With respect to the liability franchise, the bank has been reducing its reliance on high-cost deposits and focusing on more granular
deposits. As a result, share of average retail deposits [as per liquidity coverage ratio (LCR) definition] stood at 49.5% for the
quarter ended June 30, 2026, as against 47.9% in the previous quarter. This has also led to a drop in cost of deposits to 5.95%,
from 6.07% for the previous quarter and 6.44% for the corresponding period of the previous fiscal. Sustained growth in retail
deposits and improvement in the cost of deposits vis-à-vis peers remains monitorable for the bank. X
 
On the assets side, the bank has adopted a balanced strategy and is focused on building a diversified loan portfolio across retail,
SME and corporate loan segments. While it will leverage its strong presence in the vehicle finance and microfinance segments, it
will also scale up secured asset classes, including home loans. Further, the SME portfolio is likely to gradually build up and
contribute 10–15% of the overall loan book over the medium term.
 
The ratings continue  to  reflect healthy capitalisation of the bank, with a high core equity ratio and  adequate  pre-provisioning
profitability. However, these strengths are partially offset by the bank’s average asset quality and modest resource profile vis-à-vis
peers.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths
Healthy capitalization
Capitalisation metrics remained strong, as reflected in common equity tier 1 (CET1) ratio and overall capital adequacy ratio (CAR)
of 16.1% and 17.2% respectively as on June 30, 2026, as against 16.2% and 17.5%, respectively, as on March 31, 2026, and
15.1% and 16.2%, respectively, as on March 31, 2025. Networth was healthy at Rs 64,798 crore as on June 30, 2026, as against
62,867 crore as on March 31, 2026, and Rs 62,532 as on March 31, 2025. Networth coverage for net non-performing assets
(NPAs) was also comfortable at ~17 times as on June 30, 2026. The bank is likely to remain well-capitalised, supported by steady
accrual and commitment from the promoters, to infuse support via equity, if required.

at 2.0% during the  first quarter of fiscal 2027  as against 1.7% in fiscal 2026 (2.1% in fiscal 2025), despite muted growth in

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses
Asset quality - a key monitorable
Overall asset quality of IndusInd Bank remains average, despite some improvement seen recently - gross GNPA narrowed to
3.25% as on June 30, 2026, from a high of 3.64% as on June 30, 2025 (1.92% as on March 31, 2024).
 
GNPAs have increased since fiscal 2025, owing to slippage in the microfinance business, amidst industrywide stress. Reported
GNPA for microfinance loans increased to 19.9% as on March 31, 2026, compared to 14.6% as on March 31, 2025 (4.5% as on
March 31, 2024), owing to the base effect. Size of the microfinance book reduced to ~5% of advances as on June 30, 2026,
driven by significant run down and write-off of stressed legacy loans. Arrest in deterioration of the book has also helped the bank’s
overall asset quality metrics improve. The management now expects the portfolio to grow in a calibrated manner, with all fresh
loan originations are being covered under the Government of India’s Credit Guarantee Fund for Micro Units (CGFMU) scheme.
This should avert any potential credit loss in the segment.
 
The GNPA metrics across most of the other retail segments have remained stable, despite low growth in the advances base.
Consumer Banking reported a GNPA of 1.6% as on 30 June 2026, compared with 1.7% as on 31 March 2026 and 1.9% as on 31
March 2025. Rural banking, other than micro finance, saw reduction to 6.4% as on 31 June 2026 from 7.0% as on 31 March 2025.
 Meanwhile, vehicle loan book saw rise in GNPA to 3.0% as on 30 June 2026 compared to 2.7% as on 31 March 2026 and 1.7%
as on 31 March 2025, however, slippages trend have been stable. Corporate, including SME and wholesale, saw some increase
in GNPA to 2.2% as on 30 June 2026 compared to 1.7% as on 31 March 2025.
The strategy to scale up the share of secured retail segments, including home loans and gold loans, should strengthen overall
asset quality of the portfolio. Nevertheless, ability to do so will remain monitorable.
 
Moderate resource profile
Total deposits stood at Rs 4.14 lakh crore as on June 30, 2026, compared with Rs 3.99 lakh crore as on March 31, 2026, and Rs
4.10 lakh crore as on March 31, 2025. The bank continues to strengthen the granularity of its deposit franchise by reducing
reliance on high-cost deposits and increasing the share of retail deposits; share of average retail deposits increased to 49.5% as
on June 30, 2026, from 47.9% in the preceding quarter. The growing share of granular retail deposits has also brought down the
cost of deposits to 5.95% in the first quarter of fiscal 2027, from 6.07% in the last quarter of fiscal 2026 and 6.44% in the first
quarter of fiscal 2026. While the CASA ratio was stable at 30% as on June 30, 2026, it remains below peer levels and the industry
average.
 
Despite improving granularity of the liability franchise, dependence on bulk deposits remains relatively high, with the top 20
depositors accounting for 15% of total deposits as on June 30, 2026, albeit exhibiting a declining trend over the years.
Accordingly, the bank's ability to further strengthen its retail deposit franchise, reduce depositor concentration, and sustain
improvement in its cost of deposits relative to peers will remain monitorable from a credit perspective.
Liquidity Strong
Liquidity remains comfortable with an average LCR of 127% for the quarter ended June 30, 2026. Furthermore, liquidity also
benefits from access to systemic sources of funds such as the liquidity adjustment facility from the Reserve Bank of India, and
access to the call money market. However, any huge outflow of deposits will be a key monitorable
Outlook Stable
Crisil Ratings believes IndusInd Bank will maintain its healthy capitalisation and adequate pre-provisioning profitability.

Upward factors
Improvement in resource profile with higher share of retail deposits and lower cost of deposits vis-à-vis peers
Continued growth momentum marked by comfortable asset quality metrics and strong capital position with CET1 ratio
(including capital conservation buffer or CCB) reported to be above 13%, on a sustained basis
Sustained improvement in earnings profile
 
Downward factors
Deterioration in asset quality and/ or earnings profile
Decline in capital adequacy ratios (including CCB) with CET I remaining below 11% on a sustained basis.
About the Bank
IndusInd Bank is a new-generation private-sector bank; it commenced operations in 1994. The bank has a pan-India presence,
with around 6560 branches (including 3348 branches of Bharat Financial Inclusion Ltd) and 2853 automated teller machines
(ATMs) as on June 30, 2026. It also has representative offices in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and London. The bank has multilateral ties
with other banks, ensuring access to more than 95,000 ATMs for its customers. It has four divisions: corporate and commercial
banking, consumer banking, global markets group, and transaction banking.
