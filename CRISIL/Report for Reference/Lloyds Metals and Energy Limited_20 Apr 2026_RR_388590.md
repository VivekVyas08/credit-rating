### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has assigned its **‘Crisil A1+’** rating to the short-term bank facilities of Lloyds Metals and Energy Ltd (LMEL) and has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AA/Stable’ rating on the long-term bank facilities and non-convertible debentures (NCDs). 

The ratings continue to reflect LMEL’s strong and improving business risk profile, supported by its leading position in the iron ore mining industry, significant cost advantages due to its allocation-based mine resulting in lower royalty payments, and forward integration into downstream products. The rating also factors in the company’s healthy, albeit moderating, financial risk profile, aided by prudent capital structure and healthy cash accrual. These strengths are partially offset by concentration of mining operations in a single region, exposure to project risks associated with large capital expenditure (capex) and susceptibility to cyclicality inherent in the steel industry. 

In the first nine months of fiscal 2026, LMEL reported on-year revenue expansion of 101% to Rs 11,093 crore, driven by volume ramp-up across mining operations aided by improved evacuation through the slurry pipeline and clearance for expanded mining operations. Commencement of pellet operations and the consolidation of Thriveni’s mine developer and operator (MDO) operations since the second quarter of fiscal 2026 also aided the revenue growth. Consequently, consolidated earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (Ebitda) margin improved by ~180 basis points onyear to 32.4%. Margins benefited from operating leverage via higher mining volume, better fixed cost absorption and improvement in the value-added mix, with pellets delivering superior margins compared with merchant iron ore. Furthermore, optimisation in logistics cost through the commissioning of the 85-kilometre (km) slurry pipeline has lowered per-tonne freight costs. While initial ramp-up inefficiencies in new downstream units weighed on the full margin potential, LMEL is likely to sustain operating margin through stabilisation of assets and cost efficiencies from captive integration. In fiscal 2026, the company produced 21.96 million tonne (MT) of iron ore, against 10 MT in fiscal 2025. In fiscal 2025, it registered revenue of Rs 6,721 crore and Ebitda margin of 29.1%. 

Over the medium term, LMEL is likely to witness strong revenue growth, supported by monetization of its capex of Rs 42,000 crore (fiscals 2024-2030; ~Rs 9,600 crore completed till December 2025), to be undertaken in phases, toward building an integrated steel plant. This includes investment across direct reduced iron (DRI), sinter, coke ovens, hot metal and flat steel products. Of the total outlay, Rs 22,000 crore is earmarked for the integrated steel plant and second phase of the slurry pipeline, which will be undertaken only after successful commercialisation of the preceding phases. The capex plan includes Rs 8,000 crore to expand the slurry pipeline network, enhancing the logistics capability. 

The company's global growth initiatives include a 50% equity stake in Nexus Holdco FZCO, which holds copper mining concessions and a processing plant in the Democratic Republic of Congo. The total investment for this project is estimated at $120 million. This acquisition diversifies LMEL's commodity portfolio beyond iron ore, providing exposure to copper. 

LMEL has also acquired 49% in CHEMAF Group via $30 million cash consideration through a joint venture (JV) with USbased Virtus Minerals, enhancing its presence in copper and cobalt minerals. While the asset is distressed with one plant 

operational at present, it involves a $250 million capex programme to finalise two nearly complete processing plants. Postcompletion, the entity expects to leverage its low-cost, captive reserves to generate revenue. While jurisdictional and execution risks are inherent, the involvement of Thriveni as the MDO provider and the heavily discounted acquisition price support the strategic rationale. Furthermore, LMEL is pursuing expansion initiatives in Singapore, South Africa and Bougainville, focused on metals and mineral exploration. While these strategic investments are intended to enhance growth prospects and diversify revenue streams, they expose the company to heightened execution and geographical vulnerabilities. Given LMEL’s limited operational track record outside India, any delay in the timely commissioning of these international facilities, capex overrun, or sluggish operational ramp-up represent risks and will be monitorable. 

As a core philosophy, 60-70% of internal cash accrual will be consistently earmarked for capex, which will limit incremental debt requirement for funding capex. Timely commissioning of the pellet capacity of 4 million tonne per annum (MTPA) and slurry pipeline of 85 km reflects the project execution capabilities of the company. Crisil Ratings believes LMEL’s phased execution strategy, moderate debt funding, available cost efficiency and state incentives provide visibility for long-term profitability and return metrics. 

LMEL’s financial risk profile is robust at present, supported by healthy networth of Rs 7,000 crore as of September 30, 2025, and strong cash generation capabilities. However, leverage metrics witnessed moderation compared with March 31, 2025, driven by the consolidation of debt at the MDO level following the stake acquisition in Thriveni Earthmovers Infra Pvt Ltd (TEIPL) and the redeemable preference shares raised as part of the acquisition transaction. Total debt (inclusive of lease liabilities) stood at Rs 8,163 crore as of September 30, 2025. Consequently, gearing stood at 1.17 times and annualised net debt to Ebitda ratio ~2.0 times. While the reliance on incremental debt is projected to have pushed the net debt to Ebitda ratio above 1.0 time in fiscal 2026, the management’s articulated policy to maintain steady-state net debt to Ebitda ratio below 1.0 time provides credit comfort. A potential initial public offering at TEIPL is also expected to control overall debt levels. While Crisil Ratings expects the expansion to be calibrated to ensure metrics remain within acceptable thresholds, adherence to this financial discipline remains monitorable. 

Debt protection metrics are expected to remain healthy, with interest coverage above 8 times and net cash accrual to adjusted debt ratio above 0.4 time over the medium term. Gearing is expected to remain under 1 time. Expected net cash accruals should l comfortably cover annual debt obligation of Rs 2,000–3,000 crore and part of the capex requirement. 

In July 2025, LMEL completed acquisition of ~80% stake in TEIPL, which has the MDO business demerged from Thriveni Earthmovers Pvt Ltd (TEMPL). TEMPL is one of India’s leading MDO companies with a proven track record across India and Indonesia. Founded and promoted by B Prabhakaran, the group has a strong track record in scaling operations across 4–5 projects (including LMEL, wherein they have achieved full or near-to-full ramp up of capacities from under utilisation). The MDO operations will add revenue of Rs 6,000–7,000 crore annually to LMEL’s consolidated financials over the medium term, at an Ebitda margin of ~25%, in addition to cost savings (Rs 400–500 per tonne) stemming from direct control over MDO operations at Surjagarh mines. The MDO business was acquired at an enterprise value of ~Rs 4,952 crore, comprising equity investment of Rs 70 crore by LMEL, with the rest as debt and preference shares in the acquired entity. 

LMEL operates under a dual-promoter structure involving B Prabhakaran of the Thriveni group and the Gupta family. While the group under the Gupta family has experienced financial distress in the past - particularly in its steel and finance entities - there are no liabilities outstanding. The company has transitioned to a more conservative approach, with clear strategic focus and defined functional responsibilities. Nonetheless, legacy promoter-related litigations and concerns arising therefrom remain monitorable, given the active legal cases involving the company or its promoters. However, no significant liabilities are expected to arise from these proceedings.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Largest private iron ore mine with rapid ramp-up and cost efficiency** 

LMEL has materially strengthened its mining profile following the appointment of TEMPL as the MDO in fiscal 2022. Leveraging TEMPL’s operational expertise, mechanised mining practices, and focused community engagement, the Surjagarh iron ore mine in Gadchiroli, Maharashtra, achieved rapid ramp-up to its rated capacity of 10 MTPA by fiscal 2025, successfully stabilising operations in a region historically affected by sociopolitical unrest. 

The mine’s longevity is underpinned by 863 MT of JORC-compliant reserves, comprising 157 MT of direct shipping ore (DSO) and 706 MT of Banded Hematite Quartzite (BHQ). This provides strong long-term resource visibility and supports sustained production at elevated capacity levels. 

In June 2025, LMEL received environmental clearance to expand mining capacity to 55 MTPA (including 45 MTPA of BHQ). This positions Surjagarh as the largest single-location iron ore mine in India by permitted capacity, materially enhancing 

LMEL’s scale and earnings potential. Crucially, Surjagarh is an allocation-based mine, exempt from auction premiums. Consequently, LMEL’s royalty outgo is ~20% of the average selling price (ASP) - substantially lower than the ~40% for public sector mines and >150% for auctioned captive mines. This structural advantage underpins LMEL’s cost competitiveness and resilience across price cycles. Additionally, operational control over the MDO arrangement is projected to lower consolidated mining cost by Rs 400–500 per tonne. 

# **First-mover in BHQ beneficiation** 

India faces increasing scarcity of high-grade hematite ores, and LMEL is pioneering large-scale beneficiation of BHQ, a historically underutilised resource. With ~706 MT of BHQ reserves and strong pilot outcomes (iron grade >66%, yield 3840%), the company is establishing 45 MTPA of beneficiation capacity through a strategic JV with Sinosteel Equipment and Engineering Co Ltd. 

This initiative will ensure feedstock continuity for future steelmaking facilities while maximising ore utilisation. The successful commissioning of the beneficiation capacity will enable monetisation of lower-grade reserves, strengthening the company's resource efficiency and long-term earnings profile. 

# **Strategic acquisition of Thriveni’s MDO business (TEIPL)** 

LMEL has completed acquisition of ~80% stake in TEIPL from TEMPL. TEMPL was one of India’s leading MDO companies with a proven track record across India and Indonesia. Founded and promoted by Mr. B Prabhakaran, a veteran of the Indian mining industry, the group has a strong track-record in scaling operations across 4–5 projects (including LMEL, wherein they have achieved full or near-to-full ramp up of capacities from under utilisation). TEMPL manages operations for 15 mines, covering iron ore, coal and barytes. 

The acquisition of TEIPL has augmented LEML’s revenue by Rs 6,000–7,000 crore (annualised basis) besides diversifying revenue and providing scale benefits. It also gives LMEL operational control, margin uplift and cost efficiency (Rs 400–500 per tonne reduction in consolidated mining cost). Further, it facilitates backward integration, mitigates related-party concerns and opens avenues for future MDO projects, especially for captive power and steel plants. 

With the overall enterprise value of the transaction is ~Rs 4,952 crore, it comprises ~80% equity stake for Rs 70 crore, while the balance is a mix of existing debt in books of TEIPL and redeemable preference shares (RPS; for which LMEL has provided irrevocable and unconditional guarantee up to Rs 2,500 crore) raised in TEIPL. Both TEIPL and the RPS holders retain early redemption rights. As part of the overall guarantee of Rs 2,500 crore, LMEL has extended its guarantee to NCDs of Rs 1,745 crore raised at Mahaprabhu Projects Pvt Ltd (Thriveni group company) to ensure timely completion of the transaction. This guarantee shall be released only post full repayment of the debt facility. 

Post the National Company Law Tribunal approval, which was received in May, 2025, the demerged MDO business, including all assets and liabilities, stand transferred under the demerger scheme effective April 1, 2025. 

# **Healthy, albeit moderating, financial risk profile; likely to sustain despite sizeable capex** 

LMEL maintains a healthy financial risk profile, though recent strategic consolidations have led to a temporary moderation. As on September 30, 2025, consolidated networth was ~Rs 7,000 crore, while total debt remained moderate at Rs 8,163 crore (including lease liabilities). The consolidation of MDO-level debt and redeemable preference shares resulted in a gearing of 1.17 times and annualised net debt to Ebitda ratio of ~2.0 times as on September 30, 2025. This compares to gearing of 0.16 times and net debt to Ebitda ratio of 0.48 time as on March 31, 2025. 

LMEL is executing a sizeable capex programme of Rs 42,000 crore (fiscals 2024-2030) alongside international resource diversification ($150 million investment in African copper and cobalt assets in addition to other geographies). These initiatives are expected to be funded through a mix of robust annual cash accrual, equity warrant proceeds and projectspecific debt. 

The company raised Rs 1,218 crore through a qualified institutional placement in July 2024 and Rs 2,723 crore through a preferential issue, reflecting a healthy equity buffer. Pre-IPO equity raise of Rs 1,600 crore in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 at TEIPL also provides comfort. 

With the debt-funded acquisition of TEIPL, LMEL’s net debt to Ebitda ratio is estimated above 1.0 time in fiscal 2026, though the management has committed to a financial policy framework capping steady-state net debt to Ebitda ratio below 1.0 time. While any sharp increase in debt-funded capex or acquisition spend could impact leverage, the same is expected to be judiciously planned and executed. Nevertheless, material deviation from the guided financial discipline will remain monitorable. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Susceptibility to geographic concentration, regulatory and legal risks** 

Mining and plant operations are concentrated in the Gadchiroli and Chandrapur districts of Maharashtra, which saw sociopolitical unrest in the past. With full-scale operations since 2021, post TEMPL’s entry as MDO operator, the region has seen improvement due to extensive CSR, law enforcement coordination and community support. Any disruptions stemming from sociopolitical issues or otherwise may adversely impact LMEL’s operations. Furthermore, any change in the iron ore 

royalty structure will be monitorable, given its potential impact on cost dynamics and profitability. Recent entry into international assets also exposes the company to associated execution and geographical risks. 

Furthermore, LMEL and its promoters are party to various litigations covering environmental, land and other issues. While no material financial liability is expected, regulatory compliance and any adverse outcome involving these proceedings, which may have a bearing on LMEL’s business or financial risk profile, shall be monitorable. 

# **High capex intensity and execution risk** 

LMEL is undertaking large capex of Rs 42,000 crore till fiscal 2030 (~Rs 9,600 crore completed till December 2025), which exposes it to execution and stabilisation risks over the medium term. The capex is planned across product segments, including beneficiation, pelletisation, DRI, wire rods, hot metals, blast furnace. 

In addition, LMEL has initiated selective non-domestic investments (~$150 million) to diversify its resource base, including a strategic foray into copper assets in Africa through a joint ownership structure in Nexus Holdco FZCO and a stake in CheMAF group. LMEL is also expanding its presence in Singapore, South Africa and Bougainville, with focus on metals and mineral exploration. 

Though the capex will improve the product profile while sustaining cost efficiency, its timely completion without material cost overrun will be monitorable. While LMEL plans to maintain prudent leverage through phased investments and Ebitda-linked funding discipline, execution delays, cost overruns, or adverse commodity cycles may impact returns and cash flow and will be monitorable. 

# **Susceptibility to steel price cycles** 

With its transition into an integrated steel player, LMEL will face increased exposure to cyclicality in the steel sector, given the close linkage between demand for steel products and the domestic and global economies. End-user segments such as real estate, civil construction and engineering are also cyclical. Any significant reduction in demand and prices adversely impacting operating margin and cash accrual will remain monitorable. Nonetheless, the company’s cost-efficient structure, captive ore and downstream focus help cushion operating margin. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

LMEL is likely to generate annual net cash accrual of Rs 7,000–8,000 crore in fiscals 2027 and 2028, against which the company has debt obligation of Rs 2,000–3,000 crore annually, including the debt at TEIPL. LMEL plans capex of Rs 7,500– 8,500 crore annually over the same period towards its downstream projects as well as investments towards global expansion. While the company would avail of debt for funding part of the capex, it has flexibility to adjust the pace of capex in line with cash accrual generated. Liquidity is also supported by cash and bank balances of ~Rs 1,200 crore and unutilised fund-based working capital and overdraft lines as of December 2025. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

LMEL’s credit risk profile will remain stable over the medium term, supported by the successful ramp-up of its mining operations with environmental clearance up to 55 MTPA, cost-efficient backward-integrated business model and strong cash accrual. While the company is undertaking large capex, prudent financial planning, along with phased investments and expected ramp-up of high-margin downstream operations, will support its financial risk profile. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Considerable ramp-up in revenue with compound annual growth rate of more than 20% over the medium term, along with diversification of the revenue profile with successful ramp up of projects, while benefitting from strong operating margin of over 35% on a sustained basis 

Sustenance of healthy financial risk profile and debt protection metrics while pursuing growth opportunities 

# **Downward factors** 

Sluggish revenue growth and operating margin declining to below 20% due to slower-than-expected in ramp up of projects, leading to lower-than-expected cash accrual 

Any change in the capex funding philosophy, moderating the debt protection metrics on sustained basis, with net debt/Adjusted Ebitda exceeding 1.0-1.2 times 

Any adverse regulatory events or legal challenges, adversely impacting the business or financial risk profiles 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Incorporated in 1977, LMEL is a Maharashtra-based integrated metal and mining company and India’s largest private merchant miner of iron ore in terms of current production run rate. The company operates the Surjagarh iron ore mine, with a permitted environmental clearance capacity of 55 MTPA, including beneficiation of low-grade BHQ ore. 

The company also operates DRI plants with capacity of 340,000 TPA, a 34-megawatt captive waste heat recovery boiler power plant and an 85-km slurry pipeline from Hedri to Konsari (both in Maharashtra). It has commissioned a 4-MTPA pellet plant at Konsari, marking the first step towards becoming a fully integrated iron ore player. With over 863 MT of JORCcompliant iron ore reserve, LMEL is now transitioning into an integrated steel producer with an estimated Rs 32,700 crore capex rollout through fiscal 2029. 

In July 2025, LMEL completed acquisition of 79.82% stake in TEIPL, the MDO business arm of TEMPL, strengthening its mining integration and diversifying its revenue base. 

For the first nine months of fiscal 2026, LMEL reported consolidated operating income of Rs 11,093 crore and profit after tax of Rs 2,299 crore (Rs 5,528 crore and Rs 1,248 crore in the corresponding period of the previous fiscal).
