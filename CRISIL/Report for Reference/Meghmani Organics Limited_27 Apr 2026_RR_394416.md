### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has revised its outlook on the long-term bank facilities of Meghmani Organics Limited (MOL) to **'Negative'** from 'Stable' while reaffirming the rating at **'Crisil A** ', The short-term rating has been reaffirmed at 'Crisil A1'. 

The revision in outlook reflects slower-than-anticipated recovery in revenue and profitability in fiscal 2026, impacting cash generation, with performance over medium term also expected to remain below prior estimates. Consequently, key metric, such as adjusted interest coverage and debt-to-earnings before interest, depreciation, tax, and amortization (EBITDA), are now estimated to be lower for fiscal 2026 and the medium term. The sustenance of even these revised estimates will remain a key monitorable. 

During the first nine months of fiscal 2026, MOL reported consolidated revenue of Rs.1,700 crore, reflecting 11% y-o-y growth. The crop protection segment (75% of revenue) drove this, growing 19% to Rs. 1,283 crore supported by recovery in exports market, though partially offset by subdued US demand following an increase in effective tariffs from 25% to 50% in August 2025. The pigment segment (20% of revenue), declined 16% to Rs. 353 crore owing to US tariff impact, muted demand in Europe and North America and elevated channel inventories. Revenue from subsidiaries improved but remained modest – Kilburn Chemicals Ltd (KCL) at Rs 50 crore (vs Rs 14 crore last fiscal) and Meghmani Crop Nutrition Limited (MCNL) at Rs 25 crore (vs Rs 6 crore) in nine months of fiscal 2026. KCL’s TiO2 plant has been shut in December 2025, following the withdrawal of antidumping duty (ADD) and a sharp increase in a key raw material prices, and is expected to remain shut until ADD is re-imposed and conditions stabilize. For full fiscal 2026, consolidated revenues are estimated to see marginal growth over fiscal 2025, but below previous estimates. Going forward, revenue growth is expected to be modest due to ongoing geopolitical uncertainties and evolving global trade dynamics, particularly in the pigments segment. 

Standalone operating margins improved to 12.4% in the first nine months of fiscal 2026 (vs 7.7%), driven by agrochemical segment margin expanding to 16.5% (from 10.7%), partly offset by pigments division margin contracting to 3.4% (from 4.5%). Consolidated operating margins, however, remained constrained at 9.2% (vs 5.1%) due to continued losses of Rs 45 crore at KCL. With the TiO2 plant shutdown, KCL’s annual losses are expected to reduce to Rs. 10-15 crore. Operating margins in the second half of fiscal 2026 are expected to moderate due to partial absorption of higher US tariffs and lower operating leverage, with full year estimated at 8-9%, flat versus fiscal 2025 but below previous expectations. While margins are expected to improve going forward, they are likely to remain modest amid ongoing geopolitical uncertainties and rising input prices. The company’s ability to pass on cost increases will remain a key monitorable. 

MOL's financial risk profile remains at average levels. The adjusted gearing ratio stood at 0.55 times as of September 30, 2025, unchanged from March 31, 2025. The same is expected to sustain below 0.5 times over the medium term, aided by debt repayment and profit accretion. The Total Outside Liabilities/Tangible Net Worth (TOL/TNW) ratio is also estimated to remain below 1 time. The adjusted interest coverage ratio remained subdued albeit improving to 3.02 times during nine months of fiscal 2026 (vs 2.16 times), and is expected to sustain at 3-5 times over the medium term. Gross debt/EBITDA declined to 3.5 times (from 4.6 times as of March 2025) and is expected to sustain below 3 times going forward. However, both metrics are expected to remain below earlier estimates, as highlighted previously. 

For fiscal 2026, debt repayment of Rs. 155-160 crore and capital expenditure (capex) are estimated to be funded through internal accruals of Rs. 145-150 crore, with the balance met from cash and cash equivalents. As of December 31, 2025, the company had an unencumbered liquid surplus of Rs. 12 crore and unutilized fund-based working capital lines of over Rs. 125 crore, providing additional liquidity cushion. Over the next two fiscals, annual net cash accruals of Rs. 150-180 crore is expected to be sufficient to meet its debt obligations of Rs. 125-130 crore in fiscal 2027 and Rs. 75-80 crore in fiscal 2028. The company does not plan to incur any major capex over the medium term. 

On April 4, 2026, MOL approved a scheme of amalgamation for the merger of its wholly-owned subsidiaries, KCL and MCNL, into the parent entity. The proposal is now subject to approval from shareholders, lenders, and the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT). The proposed restructuring aims to simplify the group structure and consolidate operations into a single listed entity, enabling better resource utilization, cost efficiencies, and operational synergies. Upon obtaining the necessary approvals, the proposed merger is likely to provide liquidity support by facilitating faster utilization of input credits under GST and quicker absorption of accumulated losses in these subsidiaries. 

The ratings continue reflect MOL’s established market position in the agrochemicals and pigments segments, diversified revenue in terms of products and end-user industries, and adequate operating efficiency due to integrated nature of operations. The ratings are also supported by healthy financial flexibility and extensive experience of the promoters in the agrochemicals and pigment segments. These strengths are partially offset by average financial risk profile and modest debt protection metrics, large working capital requirement and exposure to risks inherent in the agrochemicals sector.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Established market position in the pigments and agrochemical industries** 

The Meghmani group has an established market position in its core business segments: pigments and agrochemicals. Specifically, it is the largest producer of copper phthalocyanine (CPC) blue and ranks among the top three global players in pigment blue, with long-standing relationships with key customers. In the agrochemicals sector, the group is one of the largest manufacturers of pesticides in India, with a presence across the value chain in both technical and formulation segments. Additionally, the group has a portfolio of over 30 brands of various pesticide formulations in India 

# **Diversified revenue profile** 

In the first nine months of fiscal 2026, approximately 75% of the group's revenue was generated from the agrochemicals division, with the remaining 25% coming from the pigments, TiO2 and Nano urea segments. The company's revenue diversity is further enhanced by its presence in both domestic (~15%) and international markets (~85%). As the multipurpose plant ramps up production, the product portfolio is expected to expand further. Additionally, the company has ventured into the crop nutrition segment, introducing products such as nano urea, bio stimulants, and micro nutrients. This segment has shown significant growth, with revenue increasing to Rs. 25 crore in the first nine months of fiscal 2026, compared to Rs. 6 crore in the same period of fiscal 2025. Notably, MOL has become the first private player to manufacture nano liquid urea. Going forward, this segment is expected to scale up gradually, driven by growing demand, particularly in exports. At an overall level, revenue growth is expected to be modest due to ongoing geopolitical uncertainties and evolving global trade dynamics, which are likely to impact off-take, mainly for the pigments segment. 

# **Integrated operations leading to cost advantages** 

The group has successfully integrated backwards into the manufacturing of copper phthalocyanine (CPC) blue, resulting in significant cost savings. In its agrochemicals business, the group has established facilities for producing key intermediates, including cypermethric acid chloride, meta phenoxy benzaldehyde, and meta phenoxy benzyl alcohol, which are essential components in crop protection products. This strategic move has reduced the group's reliance on imports and enhanced its self-sufficiency. Furthermore, with the commercialization of the multi-purpose plant, the group has expanded its capabilities to manufacture a broader range of technicals. The healthy integration of its production facilities is expected to support the business risk profile over the medium term, providing a stable foundation for future growth 

# **Strong financial flexibility and extensive experience of the promoters** 

The group's financial flexibility is supported by a robust capital structure, with a gearing ratio of 0.5 times as of September 30, 2025. Additionally, despite significant working capital requirements, the utilization of working capital debt (with enhanced limits of Rs. 700 crore) remained moderate at approximately 77% for the 12 months ending February 2026. This provides sufficient headroom to access additional loans, enabling the group to navigate challenging situations effectively. As a leading player in the industry, the Meghmani group is one of the largest pigment manufacturers globally and a major player in the agrochemicals space. The promoters of the group bring extensive experience spanning over five decades, having played a pivotal role in scaling up both business segments. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u> Large working capital requirement** 

The group's operations are working capital-intensive due to the seasonal nature of its key businesses. A significant proportion of agrochemical sales in the domestic market and pigment sales in the overseas market are concentrated in the second and fourth quarters, respectively. While exports help mitigate dependence on the seasonal domestic agrochemicals market, they also put pressure on the working capital cycle, as the group is required to offer credit terms of 3-4 months to overseas clients, leading to extended receivables. Given the inherent characteristics of its business, the group expects its working capital requirements to remain substantial. 

# **Average financial risk profile and modest debt protection metrics** 

The group's financial risk profile is average, characterized by elevated debt levels and lower-than-anticipated cash accruals, resulting in modest debt protection metrics. Despite high debt levels of Rs. 840 crores as of September 30, 2025 (compared to Rs. 829 crore as of March 31, 2025), the capital structure remains healthy, driven by a strong tangible net worth of Rs. 1,521 crore as of September 30, 2025 (compared to Rs. 1,498 crore as of March 31, 2025), which translates to a gearing ratio of 0.55 times (March 31, 2025: 0.55 times). Gearing is expected to sustain below 0.5 times over medium term. The adjusted interest coverage ratio remained subdued, despite improving to 3.02 times in the first nine months of fiscal 2026, compared to 2.16 times in the same period of fiscal 2025, primarily due to higher interest costs resulting from elevated debt levels. However, with expected improvements in profitability and debt repayment, the adjusted interest coverage ratio is likely to improve to 3-5 times over the medium term. The gross debt-to-EBITDA ratio, although high, decreased to 3.5 times as of September 30, 2025 (from 4.6 times as of March 31, 2025), and is expected to sustain below 3 times going forward, driven by debt repayment and improved profitability. Despite improvement, debt protection metrics are likely to remain modest and below prior expectations 

# **Exposure to risks inherent in the agrochemicals sector** 

The demand for agrochemicals is closely tied to agricultural production, which is heavily influenced by monsoon patterns. In India, a significant portion of the cultivated land remains unirrigrated, relying on monsoon rainfall to meet its water requirements. Consequently, surplus or inadequate rainfall can have a direct impact on the group's domestic revenue and profitability. Additionally, the agrochemicals industry is subject to stringent regulatory requirements, with separate and specific registration processes in place across different countries. As a result, changes in export and import policies in these countries can affect Indian agrochemical exporters, such as the Meghmani group. Furthermore, any bans on key molecules will also require close monitoring 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Adequate 

The company's liquidity is expected to be supported by its strong financial flexibility and healthy capital structure, providing sufficient headroom to raise additional funds in case of emergencies. Meghmani Organics Limited (MOL) is estimated to generate net cash accruals (NCA) of Rs. 145-150 crore in fiscal 2026, which, combined with a liquid surplus of Rs. 12 crore as of December 31, 2025, was largely sufficient to meet its repayment obligations of Rs. 155-160 crore for fiscal 2026. Balance residual surplus supported by incremental working capital borrowings was sufficient to meet capex and working capital requirements. Over the next two fiscals, MOL is expected to generate NCA of Rs. 150-180 crore, which will be largely sufficient to meet its repayment obligations of Rs. 125-130 crore and Rs. 75-80 crore in fiscal 2027 and fiscal 2028, respectively, as well as capex requirements of Rs. 45-50 crore. Additionally, despite significant working capital requirements, the utilization of working capital debt (on enhanced limits of Rs. 700 crore) remained moderate at approximately 77% for the 12 months ending February 2026. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** <u>Negative</u> 

Crisil Ratings believes that MOL's negative outlook is driven by its operating performance which has been lower than expected and is likely to persist over the medium term, thereby impacting cash generation and key debt protection metrics. Nevertheless, the company's ability to raise funds and its unutilized bank lines will continue to provide support to its liquidity profile in the event of financial exigencies 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Sustenance of healthy performance marked by double-digit revenue growth, while maintaining operating margins at over 11-13%. 

- Strong cash generation and prudent funding of capex and working capital leading to sustained healthy debt protection metrics. 

# **Downward factors** 

- Significant moderation in cash generation due to sluggish demand conditions and lower-than-expected operating margin. 

Slower-than-expected recovery in operating performance leading to debt/Ebitda sustaining at 4-4.25 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

The Meghmani group was established in 1986 by Mr Jayanti Patel, Mr Ashish Soparkar, Mr Natwarlal Patel, Mr Ramesh Patel and Mr Anand Patel. The group manufactures green and blue pigment products, which are used in printing ink, plastic, paints, textiles, leather and rubber. It also manufactures a wide variety of commonly used pesticides for crop and non-crop applications. The latter includes insect control in wood preservation and food grain storage. 

During nine months of fiscal 2026, MOL reported revenue of Rs 1,700 crore (from Rs 1,526 crore in the first nine months of fiscal 2025); the company generated net profit of Rs.21 crore during the first nine months of fiscal 2026 as against net loss of Rs.30.4 crore in the corresponding period of the previous fiscal.
