### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its 'Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+' ratings on the bank facilities of Maruti Suzuki India Ltd (MSIL). 

The reaffirmation reflects the strong business risk profile of MSIL, backed by its market leadership position in the domestic passenger vehicles (PV) industry. MSIL held 38.78% share in the domestic market and 46.15% share in the export segment during the first half of fiscal 2026. The company’s domestic sale volumes declined by 6% in the first half of fiscal 2026 over the comparative period of fiscal 2025, due to muted domestic demand, high channel inventory requiring production rationalization, and postponement of purchase in anticipation of Goods and Services Tax (GST) reforms. However, export volumes grew by almost 40% in the first half of fiscal 2026, over corresponding period of fiscal 2025, taking share of exports in MSILs overall volumes to ~21%, from ~15% earlier. Healthy export growth also enabled overall 1% increase in volumes in the first half. 

In the domestic market, Utility Vehicle (UV) contributed 39.9% of sales for MSIL in the first half of fiscal 2026 while the remaining 60.1% of sales was contributed by passenger car & Vans segment. However, in the overall domestic market, UVs contributed ~66% during first half of fiscal 2026, thus indicating MSIL’s scope to further improve its product mix in line with the industry levels. 

Revenue has grown by 10.5% in first half of fiscal 2026, primarily supported by healthy realizations on account of continued shift in product mix in favour of UVs in domestic segment and strong export volume growth. For fiscal 2026, Crisil Ratings expects MSIL to register revenue growth of 15-17%, primarily driven by 8-9% volume growth, led by revival in domestic demand in the second half of fiscal 2026, and continuing strong export growth. Earlier, revenue grew by 7.8% in fiscal 2025, driven by modest volume and realization growth of 4%, enabled by improved product mix in favour of UVs. 

Operating margin declined to 11.9% in the first half of fiscal 2026 from 13.8% in the first half of fiscal 2025, on account of moderate increase in raw material prices, unfavorable forex movement, higher advertisement expenses and limited price correction across models, given subdued volume growth in the domestic market. Crisil Ratings expects MSIL’s operating margin will sustain at 12-13% over the near to medium term supported by improving operating leverage due to surge in offtake of small cars on the back of recent GST reforms, expected rise in share of exports, anticipation of stable raw material prices and forex movements. While the company is operating its plants at nearly ~90% capacity at present, commissioning of fresh capacity, currently under implementation, will bolster MSIL’s market leadership position. 

MSIL’s financial risk profile continued to remain robust, supported by strong cash generation, sizeable networth and only sporadic short term debt raise. Interest coverage and debt/earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (Ebitda) ratios were healthy at above 100 times and 0.01 time, respectively, in fiscal 2025 (vis-à-vis 99 times and 0.01 time, respectively, in fiscal 2024). Liquidity was superior with cash and cash equivalents of around Rs 61,505 crore available as on September 30, 2025. 

MSIL is expanding its production capacity by 1 million units at its greenfield plant at Kharkhoda, Haryana. Construction of the new plant is spread over four phases, phase-1 of 250,000 cars per annum was completed in fourth quarter of fiscal 2025, and production capacity enhanced to 2.6 million units per annum. Going forward, MSIL will continue to augment its production capacity by 250,000 units annually in the remaining 3 phases at Kharkhoda. Completion of the Kharkhoda plant 

will take MSIL’s total capacity, including Suzuki Motors Gujarat Pvt Ltd (SMG), and 100,000 units planned at Manesar plant, to 3.35 million units per annum by end fiscal 2028. Besides, MSIL also has plans to further enhance its capacity to produce 4 million units per annum by fiscal 2030-31. The company is expected to incur capex of Rs.12000-15000 crore per annum in the next few years to fund its expansion and modernization, and fund the same largely through accruals, aiding sustenance of strong debt metrics. 

The reaffirmation of ratings also reflects the strong business risk profile of MSIL in the domestic PV segment, driven by its market leadership position, the largest distribution network and improving operating efficiency. Besides, the ratings are also supported by the robust financial risk profile and superior liquidity position. These strengths are partially offset by susceptibility of operating margin to volatility in raw material prices, intense competition in the domestic market, where MSIL remains the market leader.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Dominant market position in the domestic PV segment:** MSIL dominates the PV segment, with a domestic market share of around 38.78% in the first half of fiscal 2026, despite facing intense competition from peers such as Hyundai Motors India Ltd (HMIL, rated ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’), Kia Motors Ltd, Mahindra & Mahindra Ltd (M&M, rated ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’) and Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles Pvt Ltd (TMPVL, rated ‘Crisil AA+/Stable/Crisil A1+’) which have sizeable presence in the fast-growing UV segment market in India. Through offerings such as Brezza, Grand Vitara, Jimny and Fronx, the company held a market share in the UV segment of 23.5% in the first half of fiscal 2026. The company also plans to launch 1-2 UV model every year, with around, ten new models before 2031, include battery electric vehicle (BEV) variants, which will expand the product portfolio for MSIL. Besides, the entry level segment where MSIL has a dominant position, is seeing increased demand due to reduction in prices of small cars, owing to the new GST reforms. In September 2025, MSIL launched ‘Victoris’, a new compact SUV positioned as a new flagship model for Maruti's Arena dealership network and available with several engine options, a 5-star Bharat NCAP safety rating, and advanced features like a Level 2 ADAS suite. 

‑ In addition to the model launches, MSIL was one of the first movers to adopt factory fitted CNG powertrain technology in India which has been witnessing strong growth since last 3-4 fiscals, primarily supported by the increase in CNG infrastructure. During fiscal 2025, around 35% share of domestic volumes sold by MSIL are CNG variants, which is expected to increase further.The success of existing models, combined with the expanding product portfolio primarily CNG variants, has solidified its dominant market position. For instance, seven of the top 10 models sold during the year belonged to MSIL. Over the medium term, the company will continue to launch new models especially in the mid-to-premium segment. MSIL also commenced the production first Battey Electric Vehicle (BEV), the eVITARA, from the Gujarat factory and over 7,000 units of eVITARA were exported to 12 European countries until September 2025.  The eVITARA is expected to be launched in the domestic market in January 2026. 

MSIL has effectively leveraged its association with its parent, Suzuki Motor Corp (SMC), which has extended product development support, shared technological expertise, and provided access to a broad product range and support in procuring rare earth magnets given the strategic importance of MSIL. During fourth quarter of fiscal 2025, company has commissioned Phase 1 of its new manufacturing facility at Kharkhoda, Haryana with an annual capacity of 250,000 units. Further MSIL will continue to add 250,000 units every year to reach 1 million capacity at Kharkhoda facility. Company currently has 4 manufacturing facilities viz. Gurgaon, Manesar, Kharkhoda in Haryana and Gujarat facility which is housed in SMG, wholly owned subsidiary of MSIL since fiscal 2024. Besides, MSIL has also outlined capex plans to commission higher capacity by fiscal 2030-31. 

**High operating efficiency, aiding cost competitiveness:** Operating efficiency of MSIL is among the best in the industry, with continuous process enhancement, high indigenisation, established linkages with component suppliers and flexible manufacturing processes, resulting in effective cost control. However, the operating margin moderated to 11.9% in first half of fiscal 2026, as compared to 13.1% and 13.2% in fiscals 2024 and 2025, respectively, on account of moderate increase in raw material prices, unfavorable forex movement, higher advertisement expenses and limited price correction across its models. Crisil Ratings expects MSIL’s operating margin to sustain at 12-13% over the medium term with largely supported by improving operating leverage due to surge in offtake from small cars on the back of recent GST reforms, expected rise in share of exports and anticipation of stable raw material prices and forex movements. 

**Robust financial risk profile:** MSIL reported a large networth of ~Rs 95,348 crore as on March 31, 2025, and liquidity of around ~Rs 61,000 crore as on September 30, 2025. Strong cash generating ability and modest working capital debt have translated into healthy debt protection metrics. Crisil Ratings expects key debt metrics to remain comfortable, as net cash accrual of over ~Rs 15,000 crore, expected per fiscal, along with cash surplus, should help MSIL fund its sizeable capex 

plans, including the plant at Kharkhoda and new manufacturing plant in Gujarat. Hence debt metrics are expected to remain comfortable. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Susceptibility of profitability and market share to intense competition and business cycles:** Operating margin of MSIL has fluctuated sharply in the past, amid steep rise in raw material cost and cost of compliance with regulations. Considering the price-sensitive nature of certain segments like entry-level PVs, the company either absorbs any hike in cost partially or passes on the same with a lag, which impacts the margin adversely. Besides, continuous spends incurred towards complying with regulatory requirements impact the return on investment. 

The Indian PV market is highly competitive, with existing and new players launching new models regularly, especially in the compact and mid-sized UV segments. The number of players in this segment increased to 19 in fiscal 2021, from 7 in fiscal 2008. With more players and models vying for a share of the growing pie, competition is intensifying. The industry has also experienced multiple headwinds in the past decade, with fiscals 2020 and 2021 witnessing a steep fall in volume. Also, with peers launching a higher number of UV models in the past, and having access to semi-conductor chips, MSIL lost market share till fiscal 2021 and for a part of fiscal 2022. However, new product launches in the UV segment, has helped the company regain its lost share. Crisil Ratings believes that MSIL’s ability to sustain and grow its market share will be driven by its ability to launch new variants and models successfully in the domestic market. 

**Declining sales of small cars in the domestic market:** MSIL continues to dominate the mini cars segment, with around ~94% market share in the first half of fiscal 2026, through Alto and S-Presso models. Besides this, MSIL held around 67% market share in the compact car segment, resulting in overall market share of ~66% in the passenger car segment. The company has maintained a steady market share as most peers have exited the market in the past few fiscals and share of small cars in the overall domestic PV market has been reducing since 2020. The total domestic PV volumes has risen to around 43 lakh units in fiscal 2025 (up from around 33.7 lakh units in fiscal 2019), and to 20.5 lakh units in first half of fiscal 2026. However, the share of small passenger cars has fallen to 31% in fiscal 2025 from 66% in fiscal 2019 and around 30% in the first half of fiscal 2026. 

However, post implementation of new GST reforms had a significant positive impact on small car segment, leading to a surge in sales and enquiries driven by lower prices. In addition, MSIL has provided additional discounts in this segment to further boost demand, largely arising from two-wheeler owners. MSIL is expected to align its production schedule in line with changing consumer preferences, which may result in share of small cars rising in the current fiscal. Nevertheless, sustenance of growth momentum in the small car segment remains a monitorable, as the revival was largely on account of reduced GST rates and additional discounts. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Superior 

MSIL has superior liquidity, on account of healthy cash accrual and liquid surplus of over Rs 61,505 crore as on September 30, 2025. Cash accrual generated would suffice to cover the working capital, and incremental capex towards capacity expansion and maintenance. As on September 30, 2025, company has sanctioned bank limits of Rs 10,000 crore which has been sparsely utilized. 

# **<u>ESG Profile</u>** 

Crisil Ratings believes the ESG profile of MSIL supports its already strong credit risk profile. The company’s commitment to ESG principles will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence. 

The automobile industry has a moderate impact on the environment given the extent of emissions, water consumption and waste generation. Effect of operational activities on employees does have an impact on society. The company is focusing on mitigating environmental and social risks. 

# **<u>Key highlights:</u>** 

The company’s scope 1 and 2 emissions intensity stood at ~3 tCO2e per crore of revenue in fiscal 2025, lower compared with scope 1 and 2 emissions intensity of ~4 tCO2e in fiscal 2023. 

- Further, the company has steady share of green vehicles (vehicle running on CNG/biofuel or hybrid engine technologies), which has also resulted in a lower than peer average Scope 3 emission intensity (from the material category use of sold products) 

- MSIL has seen a healthy safety track record with low lost time injury frequency rate (0.01 for employees, and NIL for workers) and nil fatalities reported during fiscal 2025. Also, customer complaints were lower than the peer average. As of March 31, 2025, MSIL governance structure is characterized by 36% of its board comprising of independent directors, 27% woman board directors, dedicated investor grievance redressal system, 100% investor complaint redressal rate and extensive financial disclosures. 

There is growing importance of ESG among investors and lenders. MSIL’s commitment to these principles will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence, given the high shareholding with financial institutions and access to domestic and foreign capital markets. 

**<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes that MSIL will maintain its dominant position in the domestic PV segment, supported by a large and successful product portfolio, new launches, strong distribution network, and access to SMC's technology. MSIL is also likely to maintain its robust financial risk profile and superior liquidity position.

# **Downward factors** 

- Significant MSIL’s market position (market share reduces below 30%) due to increasing competitive intensity. Material increases in external debt, in the event of a large capex, denting key debt metrics. 

- Sizeable increase in pay-out by way of buy-back, dividend and royalty to SMC, resulting in significant decline in cash surplus. 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Incorporated in 1981, MSIL is the market leader in the domestic passenger car industry. In 1982, the Government of India and SMC entered into a joint venture agreement, whereby SMC acquired a 26% stake in MSIL. By September 2007, the government had offloaded its equity to Indian financial institutions, including banks and mutual funds. The company has manufacturing facilities in Gurgaon and Manesar. In July 2023, MSIL announced acquisition of 100% stake in Suzuki Motor Gujarat Pvt Ltd (SMG) from the parent Suzuki Motor Corporation (SMC) and as on date SMC holds 58.19% stake in MSIL. Along with SMG's unit in Gujarat, total installed capacity is ~2.6 million units per annum.
