### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ratings on the bank facilities of Hinduja Global Solutions Limited (HGS) at ‘Crisil A/Stable/Crisil A1’ 

The ratings continue to reflect the strong financial risk profile, characterised by low gearing, adequate financial flexibility and liquidity, along with benefits from diversification in geographic and segmental reach in its operations. Free cash, loans and investment balances provide high financial flexibility. These strengths are partially offset by underperformance and vulnerability arising from exposure to intense competition in the business process management (BPM) segment within the broader information technology-enabled services (ITeS) industry, as well as in the highly competitive broadband and digital TV media market, which continues to exert pressure on growth and margins. .The company’s significant exposure to group entities also remains monitorable. 

HGS’s consolidated revenue for the first nine months of fiscal 2026 stood at Rs 3,223 crore, reflecting a marginal year-onyear (y-o-y) degrowth of 0.6%, following a full-year fiscal 2025 revenue of Rs 4,404 crore (Rs.4616 crore in fiscal 2024). The BPM business is currently navigating a moderation phase characterised by ongoing restructuring with AI-led technology disruptions and loss of some customers. With a pivot toward higher margin artificial intelligence (AI) solutions such as AgentX and AMLens, the sales pipeline remains healthy, supported by new client additions. Successful ramp up of these initiatives remains a key monitorable. The media division, forming ~30% of consolidated revenue, exhibited a muted performance in fiscal 2026. The traditional video segment faces competition from free-to-air government offerings, such as DD Free Dish. Meanwhile the broadband vertical faced degrowth due to the curtailment of unprofitable businesses. Overall with initiatives being undertaken and new customer additions, revenue is expected to register single-digit growth over the medium term. 

The company’s operating performance has moderated in fiscal 2026; however, same is expected to improve from fiscal 2027, with HGS continuing to invest in emerging Generative AI (Gen AI) and other AI technologies. While the media business faces structural stagnation, improvement will depend on a geographic pivot into underpenetrated broadband markets and new use cases for existing customers. 

Operating margins or earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (EBITDA) margin faced compression, falling to 2.2% in the first nine months of fiscal 2026 from 3.5% the corresponding period, previous fiscal (8.2% in the corresponding period of fiscal 2024). This decline resulted from customer losses in traditional BPM lines and the curtailment of low-profit relationships in the media segment, amidst one-time costs related to headcount rationalisation. As per Ind AS 116, the company incurs lease expenses for media-related assets, with total lease outflows expected to exceed Rs 400 crore in fiscal 2026. Adjusting for these lease-related payments, operating cash flows remain negative. However, overall annual cash flows are supported by treasury income of Rs.450-500 crore, generated on surplus funds of around Rs 6,500 crore, largely invested in Hinduja group companies. Crisil Ratings expects a gradual recovery in operating margins over the near to medium term to mid to high single digits, driven by a higher mix of AI-integrated services and the results of various cost-optimisation measures undertaken over the last 12 months. The company’s ability to successfully scale its proprietary technology solutions and achieve better fixed-cost absorption remains a key monitorable to achieve this. 

Gross debt—comprising bank and other borrowings of Rs 1,202 crore and leases of ~Rs 700 crore—stood at a moderate Rs 1,900 crore as on December 31, 2025, compared to Rs 1,943 crore as on March 31, 2025, with gearing around 0.3 time. Over the medium term, the group plans to incur capital expenditure (capex) of Rs 100-140 crore per fiscal (excluding lease related additions) for both media and BPM businesses. The group intends to fund these expenditures through a mix of cash accrual, interest earned on funds, and existing liquidity, ensuring limited reliance on fresh debt. In the first quarter of fiscal 2027, HGS brought back ~US 80 million of surpluses from overseas group companies, which may be used to retire debt or shore up domestic liquidity. The company’s strong networth and liquid surplus will continue to support the overall financial and credit risk profiles. 

# **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of HGS and all its subsidiaries, including those operating in the media business. All the entities share a common management, and are engaged in a similar line of business, with significant operational and financial linkages. Crisil Ratings considers these entities as being strategic to HGS, in view of their strong integration with the parent’s operations. Debt includes lease liabilities, following the adoption of Ind AS 116.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Benefits from diversification in geographic and segmental reach:** The company operates across diverse verticals, such as technology, media and telecom, consumer and retail, banking and financial services, public sector entities, and others. The company has been gradually increasing its share of deliveries to low-cost locations in India and Philippines against key customer locations (the US, UK and Canada). The company is also shifting work offshore to its low-cost centres in South Africa and Colombia, to serve high-potential international markets. While new customer additions are likely to be slow in the legacy BPM space, client additions in the new age analytics and digital segment could be bolstered by mid-sized acquisitions, which in turn could enhance the margins. 

The inclusion of NXTDIGITAL Ltd (NDL), the flagship media business of the Hinduja group, has diversified the business risk profile and expanded its geographic reach. The media vertical contributed approximately 28% to revenue in fiscal 2025, supported by its three-decade-long track record in the media and communication vertical.  In fiscal 2024, India accounted for around 44% of revenue, compared to 10% in fiscal 2022. The company has a pan-India presence, with an increased reach in lower tier cities, where it provides TV services through a dual delivery platform comprising the terrestrial fiber route and the country's only Headend-In-The-Sky (HITS) satellite platform, under the brands INDigital and NXTDIGITAL, respectively.  In addition to TV services, the subsidiary ONEOTT Entertainment Ltd offers broadband and internet services in 40 cities. The company's higher focus on customer acquisition, driven by the broadband business, is seen as a key growth driver going forward. The company also plans to leverage the HITS platform to serve rural and underserved markets. 

**Healthy financial risk profile with strong capital structure and liquidity:** The financial risk profile will continue to benefit from high net worth and low gearing (estimated at 0.30 times at March 31, 2026. Gross debt — comprising bank and other borrowings of Rs 1,202 crore and leases of around Rs 700 crore, totalled ~Rs 1,900 crore as on December 31, 2025. Healthy cash accruals, bolstered by treasury income, should suffice to fund annual capex of Rs 100-140 crore (excluding lease-related additions), ensuring lower reliance on additional debt. Gearing is therefore expected to remain at comfortable levels. Albeit, interest coverage ratio has moderated to ~3.0 times and will show gradual improvement over the medium term, compared with 9-10 times historically. 

Aggregate surplus funds of Rs 6,429 crore as on December 31, 2025, included cash and bank balances of around Rs 706 crore; the rest were parked as loans, deposits and investments in various Hinduja group entities. The actual deployment of funds post acquisitions, investments in group companies, expansion by NDL and other developments, and the ability to recall these funds at short notice, remain key credit monitorables. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Exposure to intense competition impacts price flexibility and cost management** : Both the ITES and broadband and digital video segments are highly competitive. Key factors that determine the operating margin include quality, pricing, reliability, range of services and data security technology. HGS competes with established players, such as Genpact, WNS (Holding) Ltd and Firstsource Solutions Ltd (‘Crisil A+/Positive/Crisil A1’) in the ITES space. It also competes with Reliance Jio Infocomm Ltd ('Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+') and Bharti Airtel Ltd (‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’) in the broadband and digital video business. Furthermore, increase in wages and costs associated with hiring, training and retention of talent pose challenges for adequate staffing and seat utilisation. Profitability will remain susceptible to competition, rising employee costs and the ability to transfer any hike in cost to customers. 

In the BPM segment, restructuring of legacy costs related to the healthcare business, and cost rationalisation efforts had earlier helped improve margins. However, in fiscals 2025 and 2026, the margin remained muted due to revenue degrowth, amidst geopolitical and macro-economic challenges in key global markets. As such, recovery in margins remains a key monitorable. Additionally, the highly competitive media business has yielded low margins, yet it requires significant capital to sustain growth in the subscriber base. Sustained growth in the subscriber base, coupled with improvements in cost structure, is expected to enhance margins over the medium term and remains a key monitorable. 

**High exposure to group companies:** HGS has invested around Rs 600 crore across various group companies in the form of intercorporate deposits. These investments generate a higher return of 8-9%, compared to fixed deposits. The loans are returnable on call. HGS has partially recalled these loans to fund its share buy-back in fiscal 2024. Additionally, it has invested around Rs 3,700 crore in debt instruments of overseas group companies and around Rs. 1,800 crore as loans to overseas group entities, earning a healthy interest, albeit subject to volatility in foreign exchange rates. These investments are also available on call and could be used to fund acquisitions outside India. Excluding these investments, liquid cash and bank balances were ~Rs 600 crore as of May , 2026. Substantial exposure to group companies remains a key monitorable. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Aggregate surplus funds of Rs 6,429 crore as on December 31, 2025, comprised cash and bank balances of around Rs 706 crore, with remainder invested as loans, deposits and investments in various group entities. This surplus was bolstered by proceeds from the sale of the healthcare business. Utilisation of the working capital limit of Rs 504 crore, against drawing power, averaged around 75% over the 12 months through March 2026. Annual term debt obligations are low at Rs 50-70 crore over fiscals 2027-2028. The combined capex—excluding lease additions—of Rs 100-140 crore per fiscal could lead to a moderate increase in debt, or be funded from cash surpluses. In the first quarter of fiscal 2027, HGS brought back ~US 80 million of surpluses from overseas group companies, which may be used to retire debt or shore up domestic liquidity. Crisil Ratings expects cash surpluses, including those in India, will be maintained at a healthy level, and funds will be repatriated back to HGS from group companies, for any inorganic growth opportunities or to support operations. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes the business risk profile of HGS will continue to be supported by its established client relationships and presence in diversified businesses and geographies. Financial risk profile should also remain strong, backed by large networth, low gearing and adequate financial flexibility. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward Factors:** 

- Sustained healthy revenue growth for existing business leading to EBITDA margin (post Ind-AS 116) above 13-14% on a sustained basis. 

Maintenance of strong financial risk profile and debt metrics while pursuing organic and inorganic growth opportunities 

# **Downward Factors:** 

- Sluggish performance leading to revenue de-growth or operational losses on a steady-state basis impacting cash generation. 

- Large, debt-funded acquisitions impacting financial risk profile and key debt metrics, with adjusted interest coverage below 3-3.5 times on sustained basis. 

Sizeable reduction in liquid surplus to fund growth opportunities/share buyback/dividend payout over the medium term. Substantial further increase in exposure to group companies 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

HGS is a part of the Hinduja group that includes Ashok Leyland Ltd, Hinduja Leyland Finance Ltd (‘Crisil AA+/Crisil AA/Stable/Crisil A1+’),, Hinduja Housing Finance Ltd (‘Crisil AA+/Crisil AA/Stable/Crisil A1+’), Hinduja Renewables Energy Pvt Ltd (‘Crisil AA-/Stable/Crisil A1+’), Hinduja Renewables One PVt Ltd (‘Crisil AA-/Stable/Crisil A1+’), Hinduja Renewables Two Pvt Ltd (‘Crisil AA-/Stable’) and Hinduja Rooftop Private Ltd (‘Crisil A+/Stable’). The group holds a stake in IndusInd Bank Ltd ('Crisil AA+/Negative/Crisil A1+') as well. 

HGS provides BPM services, primarily back-office processing and contact centres, to domestic and international clients. As on December 31, 2025, it had 30 delivery centres in 10 countries and 18,000+ employees. 

On November 12, 2022, HGS received approval from the National Company Law Tribunal to acquire NDL. Incorporated in 1985, NDL is the flagship media business of the global Hinduja group, with a track record spanning over three decades in the media and communications segment. 

During the first nine months of fiscal 2026, HGS reported a consolidated profit after tax (PAT) of Rs 19 crore on an operating income of Rs 3,223 crore, against Rs 102 crore on Rs 3,243 crore, respectively, during the corresponding period in the previous fiscal. PAT in fiscal 2026 includes profit of Rs 148 crore from discontinued operations (Rs 219 crore in the corresponding period of the previous fiscal).

Crisil Ratings pioneered the concept of credit rating in India in 1987. With a tradition of independence, analytical rigour and innovation, we set the standards in the credit rating business. We rate the entire range of debt instruments, such as bank loans, certificates of deposit, commercial paper, non-convertible/convertible/partially convertible bonds and debentures, perpetual bonds, bank hybrid capital instruments, asset-backed and mortgage-backed securities, partial guarantees and other structured debt instruments. We have rated over 33,000 large and mid-scale corporates and financial institutions. We have also instituted several innovations in India in the rating business, including ratings for municipal bonds, partially guaranteed instruments and infrastructure investment trusts (InvITs). 

Crisil Ratings Limited ('Crisil Ratings') is a wholly-owned subsidiary of Crisil Limited ('Crisil'). Crisil Ratings Limited is registered in India as a credit rating agency with the Securities and Exchange Board of India ("SEBI"). 

For more information, visit www.crisilratings.com
