### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil BBB/Stable/Crisil A3+’ ratings on the bank loan facilities of Suyog Telematics Limited (STL). 

The ratings continue to reflect STL’s established market position and above average financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by STL’s large capital requirement expected over the medium term and moderate scale of operations. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has consolidated the business and financial risk profiles of STL with its wholly owned subsidiaries (Lotus Tele Infra Private Limited]  Together these are referred to as Suyog Group. 

_Please refer Annexure - List of Entities Consolidated, which captures the list of entities considered and their analytical treatment of consolidation._ 

### **<u>Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description</u>** 

### **Strengths:** 

- **Established regional market position:** STL, established in 1995 by Mr. Shivshankar Lature, has built a strong regional market position as a provider of passive telecommunication infrastructure provider, including poles, towers, optical fiber cables, and related assets. Registered as an Infrastructure Provider Category-I (IP-I) with the Department of Telecommunications, STL has leveraged its strong relationships with various telecom operators and efficient operations to expand its presence beyond its traditional stronghold in the Mumbai circle. The company has further diversified its geographic reach through the acquisition of Lotus Tele Infra Private Limited, which operates in the Delhi circle, and is expected to achieve revenues of Rs. 250-260 crores in FY26, following a strong start with Rs. 55 crores in Q1FY26. 

**Above-average financial risk profile:** Despite planned capex of Rs 800-900 crore over a medium term, financial risk profile is expected to remain healthy. Networth was Rs 388 crores as on March 31, 2025, and leverage level is moderate as reflected in total outside liability to adjusted networth (TOLANW) ratio of 0.73 times as on March 31, 2025. While the capex is expected to be partially funded by debt; TOLANW is likely to remain at less than 1.5 times over the medium term owing to moderate accretion to reserves. 

### **Weaknesses:** 

**Large capital requirement:** The telecom tower industry is capital-intensive, with significant capex requirements, although most investments are made on a build-to-order basis. To support its growth plans, the company is expected to expand its tower portfolio in the near term, having already added new towers to diversify its geographic presence. The estimated capex outlay for the medium term is around Rs 800-900 crore. However, the industry's oligopolistic nature, with a concentrated customer base, poses a risk to receivables and potential cash flow mismatches. Furthermore, delays in commencing new operations or generating revenue from new towers could impact the company's ability to service its debt and maintain adequate liquidity. Therefore, close monitoring of the ramp-up of operations and the debt profile will be crucial to ensure the company's financial stability. 

- **Moderate scale of operations and geographical presence:** The company's scale of operations remains moderate, with an operating income of Rs 193 crore in fiscal 2025, although it has shown an improving trend. While the company 

has made progress in diversifying its geographical presence, with orders now spanning 28 circles compared to 12 previously, the benefits of this expansion are expected to materialize over the medium term, and the extent of their impact remains to be seen. Notably, the company's revenue concentration remains high, with approximately 60-65% of its revenues generated from Mumbai and Maharashtra, leaving it exposed to potential risks such as revisions to rental agreements with customers or changes to long-term lease agreements with government agencies. 

# **<u>Liquidity: Adequate</u>** 

NCA is likely to provide 1.5-3 times cushion to repayment obligations over the medium term. Net cash accrual, expected to be above Rs.100 crore per fiscal over the medium term and should comfortably cover the annual term against debt obligation of Rs 30-70 crore. 

Any delay in payment from the counter parties can put pressure on the liquidity profile. The absence of any working capital limit constrains the liquidity profile. The lease rentals come in a designated escrow account from where the lenders take their dues, and the balance is available for the company’s use 

# **<u>Outlook: Stable</u>** 

STL should benefit from its promoter's experience in the telecom tower industry and established relationships with customers and agencies. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors:** 

Growth of 20-25% in revenue with improved operating margin, leading to significantly stronger net cash accrual. Improvement in working capital cycle, especially receivables cycle across clients and sustained financial risk profile with TOLANW remaining below 1 times and strong debt protection metrics. 

# **Downward factors:** 

Moderation in revenue growth and operating margin falling below 35% leading to lower-than-expected cash accruals. Larger-than-expected debt funded capex, large dividends or significant reduction in accruals weakening the financial risk profile, especially liquidity 

# **<u>About the Group</u>** 

STL, is a Mumbai based company, is a passive telecommunication infrastructure provider in India, engaged primarily in the business of installing and commissioning of Poles, Towers and Optical Fibre Cable (OFC) Systems in India. 

STL also acquired 95% stake in Lotus Tele Infra Private Limited in FY 25
