

## **Rating Rationale** 

October 18, 2025 | Mumbai 

# **NMDC Steel Limited** 

_Rating removed from ‘Watch Developing’; Rating Reaffirmed_ 

### Rating Action 

**Total Bank Loan Facilities Rated Rs.4500 Crore Crisil BBB+/Stable (Removed from ‘Rating Watch with Long Term Rating Developing Implications’; Rating Reaffirmed)** 

_Note: None of the Directors on Crisil Ratings Limited’s Board are members of rating committee and thus do not participate in discussion or assignment of any ratings. ### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has removed its rating on the long term bank facilities of NMDC Steel Limited (NSL) from **‘Rating Watch with Developing Implications’** and has reaffirmed the rating at ‘Crisil BBB+’ while assigning a **‘Stable’** outlook. 

The rating had been placed on watch on June 14, 2023, considering the Government of India's (GoI) plans to divest its stake<sup>[1]</sup> . Crisil Ratings notes the slow progress of the divestment process. Thus, ratings have been removed from watch. However, Crisil Ratings will continue to monitor any new developments on the process, as and when they emerge, and this will be a rating sensitivity factor. Until the divestment is completed, Crisil Ratings expects GoI’s support to NSL will continue. 

The rating reflects the ramp-up in operational performance that NSL has demonstrated since Q4FY25, after a slower-thanexpected start post commissioning. NSL had healthy capacity utilization of 78% in Q2FY26 (Q1FY26: 82%; FY25: 50%). This performance is expected to sustain, driven by the company's efforts to optimize production costs, thereby supporting expected improvement in operational efficiency over the medium term. 

The profitability of NSL has shown an uptrend as well with expected H1FY26 EBITDA ranging between Rs 700-750 crore (H1FY25: Rs -1335 crore). NSL is further expected to benefit from increase in scale of production, favourable raw material costs and realisations now almost at par with market prices. The key monitorable will be NSL's ability to maintain sustainable operational efficiencies and achieve healthy Ebitda, which will be crucial for its long-term viability. 

Crisil Ratings notes NSL has successfully repaid the scheduled Rs 523.80 crore NCD due in August 2025, utilizing its existing liquidity and internal accruals. As on September 30, 2025, NSL had a debt obligation of Rs 918 crore over the next four quarters against the total cash and cash equivalents of Rs 814.8 crores. Further, NSL also has access to sanctioned working capital limit of Rs 2,600 crore of which Rs 275 crore was unutilized on September 30, 2025. Generating consistent and sustainable operating profits will also be crucial for supporting debt servicing in fiscal 2026. Any deviation from this will be a rating sensitivity factor. 

The rating continues to factor in the support NSL is receiving as a GoI entity, through NMDC Ltd (NMDC) Crisil AAA/Crisil A1+. Furthermore, Crisil Ratings understands that the Ministry of Steel has mandated NMDC to provide necessary support to NSL till it is divested by the government. Any change in the support philosophy will be a key rating sensitivity factor. 

The rating also factors in the susceptibility of NSL to cyclicality in the steel industry, and to risks related to availability of key raw materials and stabilisation of operations. 

_[1] According to the current divestment plan, GoI will divest majority of its stake (50.79% out of 60.79%) in NSL while the remaining 10% stake will be transferred to NMDC. The expression of interest (EoI) for the divestment was floated on December 1, 2022, and the government had received multiple bids for the same. However, official bidding is yet to commence._ 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has considered the standalone business and financial risk profiles of NSL. 

Crisil Ratings has also applied its criteria to notch up the standalone ratings of entities, factoring in access to government support. NSL will receive support from the government (through NMDC), considering its strategic importance and majority 

ownership (60.79%). 

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Business risk profile of NSL expected to sustain and sequentially improve, supported by better operational performance and raw material security** 

NSL’s operating performance has sustainably improved since FY25. NSL is likely to report ~Rs 1340 crore operating profit for the full fiscal 2026, driven by better utilization rates and market realization, marking a notable turnaround from the previous year, where it posted operating loss of Rs -1788 crore. The cash accruals generated by NSL are supported by the nine-year contract with NMDC for iron ore procurement (transactions done on arm’s length basis). The iron-ore comprises 15-20% of the overall cost of production for NSL. Crisil Ratings understands that the payments to NMDC are made on adhoc basis, basis the strength of the performance of NSL. Any surplus generated will be utilized to repay NMDC payables, however, these will be subordinate to external debt obligations. Improvement in operating performance along with liquidity levels will be a critical monitorable going forward. 

# **Government support to continue till the divestment is completed** 

The government plans to divest its stake in NSL, wherein 50.79% stake will be sold to a private sector entity and the remaining 10% will be held by NMDC. Crisil Ratings understands that currently only EoI has been received and financial bids are yet to commence However, Crisil Ratings understands that till the time divestment is completed, GoI, through NMDC, will continue to provide operational/administrative and need-based support in case of any exigency. Accordingly, even after the transfer of assets and liabilities of the steel plant to the new entity -- NSL, NMDC has been providing operational and administrative support to it. Any developments in the process of divestment will be monitorable. Additionally, any change in the support philosophy of GoI towards NSL will be a key rating sensitivity factor. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Moderation in financial risk profile due to continued operating losses** 

NSL has faced significant delay in ramp-up and stabilisation of operations resulting in operational losses upto fiscal 2025. Further, during fiscal 2025, the industry realisations had been under pressure due to the high quantum of cheaper imports. However, prices of steel have improved in the domestic market since March 2025, especially post the implementation of the safeguard duty, resulting in improvement in realizations. Going forward, the company expects to continue witness improvement in utilisation rates and improved integration supporting the overall operating performance, cash accrual and debt servicing. 

The slower-than-expected ramp up and operational hindrances during fiscal 2025 contributed to operating losses resulting in weakening of NSL’s financial risk profile. However, most of the capex of the steel plant has been funded through equity, with low existing external project debt (total debt of ~ Rs 5,000 crore against total project cost of Rs 24,000-25,000 crore), thereby resulting in low finance cost and supportive capital structure over the medium term. While interest servicing has been monthly, quarterly principal repayments for NSL’s outstanding debt has commenced from March 2024 and has been serviced in a timely manner. Further, the company have also successfully repaid the scheduled Rs 523.80 crore NCD due in August 2025 through its internal accruals. 

# **Susceptibility to cyclicality associated with the steel industry and availability of key raw materials** 

The inherent cyclicality in the steel industry exposes steelmakers to a high degree of volatility in operating margin and, in turn, to debt protection metrics. Demand for steel is sensitive to trends in key end-user industries, such as automobiles, infrastructure, construction and consumer durables. However, raw material security, by way of long-term contract with NMDC for procurement of iron ore, mitigates the risk to an extent. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Adequate 

As of September 2025, the company had total cash and cash equivalents of ~Rs 815 crore, of which unencumbered cash balances of Rs 186 crore. Further, NSL also has access to sanctioned working capital limit of Rs 2,600 crore of which unutilised working capital limits are of Rs 275 crore as on September 30, 2025. Against this, there are total debt obligations (principal and interest repayments) of Rs 918 crore due over the next 4 quarters. 

Liquidity will also be supported by the company getting extended credit from NMDC Ltd for iron ore purchases. Further, NSL is expected to receive government support in case of any exigency. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes NSL will continue to benefit from improving operating performance and majority ownership of the GoI. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Sustaining plant utilization rates at more than 80% 

Healthy operating profitability and robust free cash generation while maintaining strong capital structure 

# **Downward factors** 

Plant utilization rates significantly lower than 50% on sustained basis resulting in lower-than-expected profitability and cash accruals, leading to stretched liquidity 

Change in support philosophy by the parent towards NSL 

Any material progress in GoI’s divestment plan 

## **<u>About the Company</u>** 

NMDC has set up a 3 million tonne per annum steel plant at Nagarnar — Nagarnar Iron & Steel Plant (NISP) - under greenfield expansion. During most of the development and construction phase, the plant was constructed under NMDC, rather than in a special purpose vehicle. Subsequently, it was decided to demerge NISP into a separate entity, NSL.
