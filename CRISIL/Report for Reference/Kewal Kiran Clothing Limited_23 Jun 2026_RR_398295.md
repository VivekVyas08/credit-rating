### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its rating on the long-term bank facilities of Kewal Kiran Clothing Limited (KKCL) at ‘Crisil AA-/Stable’. 

The rating continues to reflect KKCL’s established position in the domestic menswear segment, with recognized brands, diversified geographic, channel presence and healthy financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by revenue concentration with high contribution from ‘Killer’ brand, vulnerability of performance to changing fashion trends and exposure to intense competition in the apparel industry. 

During fiscal 2026, consolidated revenue grew by ~18% year-on-year to Rs. 1,305 crore on the back healthy like-for-like (LFL) growth of 9.4%. Volumes grew by 7% to 180.1 lakh units in fiscal 2026 as against 167.8 lakh units during the corresponding period in previous year. The flagship brand ‘Killer’ which contributes around 50% of the overall revenue witnessed double digit growth in fiscal 2026. Additionally, fiscal 2026 marked the first full year of consolidation of Kraus postacquisition, which further supported revenue growth. Going forward, revenue growth is expected to be driven by healthy LFL growth as well as demand from new store additions. 

The gross margins improved to ~48.3% in fiscal 2026 from ~47.9% in fiscal 2025, primarily due to a favorable change in product mix and softening of raw material cost. Despite partial offset from higher employee costs, EBITDA margins expanded to 18.2% in fiscal 2026 from 17.1% in the previous fiscal. However, in the current fiscal, profitability may witness some moderation due to an expected rise in key input costs such as cotton, along with increased freight, fuel, and gas expenses, with margins likely to moderate to 17-18%. Over the medium term, margins are expected to sustain at healthy levels of 18–19%, supported by operating leverage benefits from higher volumes and continued cost rationalization. 

The financial risk profile continues to remain strong with a healthy networth of Rs. 891 crore as on March 31, 2026, a net debt free balance sheet and strong debt protection metrics. External borrowing stood lower at Rs. 48 crores as on March 31, 2026 in comparison to Rs. 108 crore as on March 31, 2025 with prudent working capital management. Overall debt including lease liabilities declined to Rs. 128 crore as of March 2026 from Rs. 164 crore as of March 2025. Company is expanding its own stores (mainly on lease-based model) as well as franchise stores with plans to add total 60-70 stores per annum. As a result, total debt (including lease liabilities) is expected to rise moderately over the medium term while external borrowing remaining rangebound. Gearing is expected to remain comfortable below 0.25 times over near to medium term. Interest coverage remains strong and stood at 18 times during fiscal 26. KKCL’s liquidity position also remained strong with unencumbered liquid surplus of over Rs 316 crores as on March 31, 2026. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of KKCL and its subsidiaries. This is because all these companies are in the same business and have strong financial and operational linkages.

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

### **Established position in the domestic menswear segment, with recognized brands and diversified geographic and channel presence** 

Well diversified apparel product base with 6 brands across different segments like casual wear, formal wear, clubwear, semiformal and ethnic wear. KKCL has pan India diversified presence with the highest proportion derived from the eastern region. Furthermore, the company sells its products through different channels like EBOs, e-commerce, MBOs, national chain stores and LFS stores. The brand portfolio of KKCL includes flagship brand Killer and other brands namely Lawman, Integriti, Easies, Desi Belle and Kraus. Lawman and Integriti which cater to mid-range Men’s formals and clubwear bring in about 10-15% revenue and Kraus Casuals acquired in June 2024 contributes to around 20 -25% of the revenue combined. 

# **Healthy financial risk profile** 

The financial risk profile is expected to remain healthy, with strong debt protection metrics and healthy liquidity over the medium term. The company has minimal short-term debt on its books and networth of over Rs. 891 crore as on March 31, 2026, supporting strong debt metrics; gearing stood at 0.14 time on March 31, 2026 and is expected to remain comfortable below 0.25 times over the medium term despite increase in lease liabilities expected in the coming fiscals. Interest coverage is expected to remain comfortable over 15 times over the medium term. Liquidity is strong, supported by unencumbered cash and marketable securities of around Rs 316 crore as on March 31, 2026. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Revenue concentration, with high contribution from the Killer brand** 

The revenue of KKCL is concentrated in terms of brand as well as product, with Killer contributing more than 50% to the revenue; consequently, the share of jeans remained over 50%. The company’s sustained focus on the Killer brand has helped strengthen its positioning, enhance marketing effectiveness, and improve customer recall. While other brands, such as Integriti and Éasies, have also grown over the years and the company is focusing on increasing contributions from these brands. The company has also forayed into new brands namely Junior Killer, Desi Belle and Kraus. However, Killer will remain a significant revenue contributor over the medium term due to its strong brand recall. With the acquisition of KCPL, while revenue proportion of Killer has come down, it continues to remain the major revenue contributor. 

# **Exposure to intense competition in the apparel segment and vulnerability to changes in fashion trends in the domestic market.** 

The Indian market, with its burgeoning youth segment, has attracted prominent global brands in the apparel segment. These range from mass-appeal to premium brands, across age groups, and pose significant competition to KKCL's brands. Furthermore, the emergence of e-commerce has intensified competition. Business is driven by fashion trends, and the target segment's aspirations are significantly influenced by peers, role models and the media. Therefore, their association with brands may change based on their perception of the value offered by the brands. Thus, manufacturers of branded apparel need to constantly innovate and adapt to the changing preferences of the target segment. KKCL, with its team of inhouse designers who work on the upcoming season's collections, is likely to have the ability to adapt to changing trends. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

The company had liquid investments (including cash and cash equivalents) of Rs 316 crore as on March 31, 2026. Liquidity position is expected to remain strong over the medium term owing to healthy accruals which will be sufficient to meet annual capex plans of Rs. 30-60 crore (excluding right-of-use Asset for new leases). Furthermore, bank lines of Rs 145 crore are utilized at an average of ~56% for the past 12 months ending March 2026 which coupled with annual accruals of over Rs. 140-190 crore per annum will sufficiently cover capex and incremental working capital requirement. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings expects KKCL to sustain its credit risk profile, supported by its established brands and distribution network in the domestic apparel business, and its prudent financial risk profile. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward Factors** 

Significant increase in revenue with sustenance of healthy operating margins. 

Maintenance of strong financial risk profile, through prudent funding of capex, and efficient working capital management. 

# **Downward Factors** 

Sluggish revenue growth impacting operating profitability (below 10-12%) and cash generation on sustained basis. Moderation in the financial risk profile because of large, debt-funded capex, higher-than-expected store expansion or stretched working capital cycle impacting debt metrics 

Substantial reduction in cash surpluses, due to material dividend payout, share buy-back or capital reduction 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

KKCL was established in 1980 as a partnership firm named Kewal Kiran and Co by Mr Kewalchand P Jain and Mr Hemant P Jain and was reconstituted as a public limited company with the present name in fiscal 2006. The company designs, manufactures and markets branded jeans, and a wide range of apparel products for men and women. Key brands include “Killer”, “Integritti”, “Lawman” and “Easies” for men and “Desi Belle” and “Kraus” for women. 

KKCL has 3 manufacturing units located across 3 states with a total area of ~2.93 lakh sq. ft., it has garment stitching units in Mumbai, a washing unit at Vapi (Gujarat), and a finishing and packaging facility at Daman (Union Territory of Daman and 

Diu). As on March 31, 2026, the company has 666 retail stores, of which 15-20% are owned and balance operated by franchisees.
