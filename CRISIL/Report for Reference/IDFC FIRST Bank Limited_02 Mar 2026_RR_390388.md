# **Update on IDFC First Bank Limited** 

_Note: None of the Directors on Crisil Ratings Limited’s Board are members of rating committee and thus do not participate in discussion or assignment of any ratings. The Board of Directors also does not discuss any ratings at its meetings._ 

Crisil Ratings has taken note of the disclosure made by IDFC First Bank Limited (IDFC First, the Bank) on February 22, 2026, reporting an incident involving unauthorized and fraudulent activities by certain employees at a particular branch in Chandigarh and potentially involving other individuals / entities/ counterparties. 

From February 18, 2026 onwards, certain Haryana Government entities engaged with the Bank regarding their respective accounts with the Bank. During this process, differences were observed between the actual balances in these accounts and the balances mentioned by the said Haryana Government entities holding accounts with the Bank. 

Based on the preliminary internal review conducted by the bank, the matter seems confined to a specific group of Haryana government-linked accounts operated through the said branch in Chandigarh and does not extend to other customers of the Chandigarh Branch. The aggregate amount under reconciliation due to this issue, across the identified accounts, was initially estimated at Rs 590 crore and, any further impact will be determined based on receipt of further information, validation of claims, recoveries of any nature including those made through the process of marking lien on fraudulent beneficiary accounts maintained with other Banks, liabilities of other entities involved in the fraudulent transactions, and the legal recovery process. 

After the discovery of this incident, the bank has: 

- i.) Placed four suspected officials under suspension pending investigation and plans to pursue strict disciplinary, civil and criminal action against the employees and other external individuals responsible, in accordance with applicable law. 

- ii.) Filed a complaint with the Police authorities and intends to extend full cooperation to the investigating agencies. iii.) Sent recall requests to certain beneficiary banks to lien mark balance in suspicious accounts held in these banks. 

- iv.) Appointed KPMG as an independent external agency to conduct a forensic audit of the matter and this exercise is likely to take 4-6 weeks to complete. 

On February 24, 2026, the bank paid Rs 583 crore (including interest) to Haryana Government against their deposits. 

On December 31, 2025, the bank reported a networth (consolidated) of Rs 47,088 crore  against a tier I and overall capital adequacy ratio (consolidated) of 13.8% and 15.8%, respectively. The aggregate amount under reconciliation due to this event was ~0.9% (net of tax) of the bank’s reported consolidated networth on December 31, 2025. Crisil Ratings estimates a one-time impact of this alleged fraud on the bank’s overall profitability, however – at this juncture, the bank's preprovisioning operating profitability and capital adequacy remain healthy to absorb this impact. 

The final extent of discrepancies, however, will be contingent on findings of the forensic audit. 

As on December 31, 2025, the bank had deposits of Rs 2.91 lakh crore and a CASA (current account and saving account) ratio of 51.6%. While Crisil Ratings understands from the management that there have not been any major premature deposit outflows thus far since the disclosure of the said developments, the same will be closely monitored on an on-going basis. Furthermore, the Bank continues to maintain adequate liquidity buffers in LCR over and above the regulatory requirement. 

Crisil Ratings will continue to monitor the developments pertaining to this event and, any further provisioning requirement from this specific issue or discovery of similar instances at other branches of the bank, any adverse regulatory action stemming from this and potential increase in run-off from deposits due to dilution in depositor confidence - will be key rating sensitivity factors. 

To arrive at the ratings, Crisil Ratings combines the business and financial risk profiles of IDFC First, and its subsidiaries based on majority shareholding of the bank in the subsidiaries, business and financial linkages and shared brand for accessing the credit rating of the company. 

The outstanding ratings remain driven by healthy capitalization and steady scale up of business, backed by strengthening of both retail asset base and liability franchise. These strengths are partly offset by modest profitability of the bank, constrained by elevation in credit costs particularly due to increase in delinquencies within the microfinance portfolio.
