### **<u>Detailed rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has upgraded its ratings on the bank loan facilities of Rishabh Instruments Ltd (RIL; part of the Rishabh group) to **‘Crisil BBB+/Stable/Crisil A2’** from ‘Crisil BBB/Stable/Crisil A3+’. 

The rating action reflects the improvement in the business risk profile with continued strong financial profile.  The group reported operating income of Rs 570 crore with operating margin of 16.3% in the first nine months in fiscal 2026 as against Rs 533 crore and 6.06%, respectively, reported in same period in fiscal 2025. Operating margin improved with continuous focus on cost optimisation, and improved profitability in die casting segment on account of price escalations and reduction in low margin automotive business. This led to increase in cash accruals and strengthening the liquidity profile of the group, which is also supported by unencumbered cash and equivalents of Rs 188 crore as on September 2025. 

The ratings reflect the Rishabh group's established position in the industrial control products (ICPs) and testing and measuring instruments (TMI) businesses, diversified customer profile, increasing revenue and healthy financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by volatility in operating margin and large working capital requirement.

### **<u>Key rating drivers - Strengths</u>** 

**Established market position:** The Rishabh group is a leading player in the ICP and TMI business. It is one of the world’s largest manufacturers of panel meters. The company also manufactures various other ICPs and TMIs including controllers, transducers, multifunction meters and current transformers. The Rishabh group has strong in-house product development experience. The group has technical collaborations with many international instrument manufacturers. Besides, the group benefits from the extensive experience of its promoter in the electronics equipment industry and its qualified management team. 

**Strong and diversified clientele:** The group has a wide distribution network, with 11 offices across the country. It also exports to more than nine countries including the Middle East, Latin America, Germany, Poland, the UK, the US, Argentina, African countries and Australia. It has a healthy client base which continues to aid the business risk profile of the company. 

**Increasing revenue:** The operating income increased steadily over the years, from Rs 397 crore in fiscal 2021 to Rs 723 crore in fiscal 2025, backed by increasing product portfolio, geographical diversification of clientele and acquisitions. Strong in-house research and development (R&D) established customer relationships and professional management should help the Rishabh group maintain steady topline growth and a stable market position over the medium term. 

**Strong financial risk profile:** Owing to steady accretion to reserve, the networth rose to Rs 569 crore as on March 31, 2025, from Rs 533 crore a year ago and is expected to remain healthy for fiscal 2026 in absence of any major debt-funded capex plans. The capital structure was adequate, with the gearing and total outside liabilities to adjusted networth (TOLANW) ratio of 0.17 time and 0.46 time, respectively, as on March 31, 2025. These ratios are expected to sustain at similar levels as on March 31, 2026. The debt protection metrics were strong driven by improved profitability and limited debt, as reflected in interest coverage and net cash accrual to total debt ratios of 10.54 times and 0.5 time, respectively, for fiscal 2025 and expected to further improve for fiscal 2026 with improvement in profitability. 

# **<u>Key rating drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Large working capital requirement:** The operations are working capital intensive, as reflected in the gross current assets of 249 days as on March 31, 2025, driven by receivables of 69 days and inventory of 88 days. To customers, the group provides open credit of 60–90 days. It maintains large raw material inventory since certain raw materials required for production need to be imported due to high lead time. With increasing revenue, the working capital requirement is expected to further increase, and its management will remain monitorable. 

**Volatile operating margin:** The operating margin was volatile at 7.66–14.3% over the three fiscals through March 2025, which was on account of higher employee cost, elevated subcontracting expenses driven by higher inflation in Poland and increase in employee stock ownership plan (ESOP) expenses in fiscal 2024. Furthermore, the operating margin was impacted by the aluminium die casting business, which reported operating loss in fiscal 2025. However, the margin rose to 16.3% in the first nine months in fiscal 2026 due to continuous cost optimisation, maintaining optimal product mix and improved profitability in die casting segment on account of price escalations and reduction in lower margin automotive business. Sustenance of the operating margin will remain monitorable. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** <u>Adequate</u> 

Bank limit utilisation was nil for the 12 months through December 2025 in Rishabh Indian entity and the subsidiary limits were moderately utilised. Annual cash accrual is expected at Rs 97–109 crore and will sufficiently cover the term debt obligation of Rs 17–20 over the medium term. The current ratio was healthy at 3 times as on March 31, 2025, and is expected to be similar over the medium term. Liquidity is supported by unencumbered cash and equivalents of Rs 188 crore as on September 30, 2025. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** <u>Stable</u> 

Crisil Ratings believes the Rishabh group will sustain its market position over the medium term, backed by its large product basket, healthy relationships with customers, diversified markets and competent management. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors:** 

- Sustenance in revenue growth and maintenance of operating profitability above 14.5% resulting in better-thanexpected cash accrual. 

Improvement in the working capital cycle and sustenance of the financial risk profile and liquidity 

# **Downward factors:** 

Decline in revenue and operating profitability below 11% resulting in lower-than-expected cash accrual Leveraged capital structure or weakened liquidity owing to large, debt funded capex or acquisition, or stretch in working capital cycle 

# **<u>About the company</u>** 

Incorporated in 1985, RIL manufactures, designs and develops ICPs such as transducers, analogue and digital panel meters, and electronic testing and measurement instruments such as hand-held multimeters, and digital and analogue insulation testers. It also manufactures aluminium high-pressure die-casting through its subsidiary. It has manufacturing facilities in Nashik, Poland and China, with modification centres in the US and the UK. The group is promoted by Mr. Narendra Goliya. It is listed on the National Stock Exchange and Bombay Stock Exchange.
