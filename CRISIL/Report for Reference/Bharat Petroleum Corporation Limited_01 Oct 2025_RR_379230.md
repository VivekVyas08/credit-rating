### **<u>Detailed Rationale0</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has assigned its ‘ **Crisil AAA/Stable** ’ rating to the Rs.1995.2 crore non-convertible debentures of Bharat Petroleum Corporation Ltd (BPCL) while reaffirming its ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the bank loan facilities and existing debt instruments. Also, the rating on Rs 1995.2 crore of NCDs is **withdrawn** (see 'Annexure - Details of Rating Withdrawn') on receipt of redemption confirmation from debenture trustee. The rating withdrawal is in line with the policy of Crisil Ratings. 

The ratings continue to reflect BPCL’s established market position in the domestic oil refining and marketing sector in India, its branding initiatives and strong operating efficiency. The ratings also factor in the company's strategic importance to the Government of India (GoI) and expectation of continued support from the latter. These strengths are partially offset by exposure to project implementation risk and inherent volatility in operating profitability, caused by fluctuations in input cost. 

Operating performance was affected in fiscal 2025, with earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortisation (Ebitda) declining to Rs 25,560 crore. as against Rs 44,806 crore in fiscal 2024. This was due to drop in gross refining margin (GRM) to $6.82/bbl from $14.14/bbl, with moderation in product cracks and tapering of discounts in crude sourced from Russia, from $8/bbl in fiscal 2024 to $3/bbl in fiscal 2025.  Profitability was further affected by under-recoveries in the liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) segment. Despite these challenges, BPCL’s operating income (net off excise duty) was stable at Rs 4.40 lakh crore in fiscal 2025, relative to Rs 4.48 lakh crore in fiscal 2024, supported by strong refinery output and increasing marketing volume. 

In the first quarter of fiscal 2026, operating income stood at Rs 1.12 lakh crore, and was at par with the previous quarter and corresponding period of the previous fiscal. However, GRMs have declined to $4.88/bbl as of June 2025 ($7.86/bbl in the corresponding quarter of the previous fiscal) despite crude prices softening to ~$67/bbl ($85/bbl) due to inventory losses and further tapering of discounts on Russian crude to $1.5-$3/bbl. Overall profitability remains supported by stable retail fuel prices. GRM for fiscal 2026 should sustain between $5-7/bbl and the increase in excise duty on petrol and diesel by Rs 2/litre effective from April 2025, may be offset by reduction in crude oil prices, as compared to $79/bbl in fiscal 2025. 

Under-recoveries in LPG were worth Rs 10,446 crore as on March 31, 2025, and have further increased to Rs 12,523 crore as on June 30,2025. However, in April 2025, the government announced a hike in price of cooking gas cylinders by Rs 50, which should help OMCs to an extent. Further, in August 2025, the government announced a compensation to OMCs for LPG underrecoveries; BPCL’s share is expected to be around 25%. LPG loss per cylinder has declined from Rs 170 as on April 30, 2025, to Rs 150 as on June 30, 2025, and further to Rs 100 and around Rs 50 as of end-July and September 2025, respectively. 

Financial risk profile remains modest, marked by consolidated gearing of 0.77 time as on March 31, 2025 (0.73 time as on March 31, 2024), due to increase in working capital requirement, driven by relatively lower cash accrual with moderation in margin. 

BPCL is making steady progress on its refinery expansion programme across four projects in Mumbai, Kochi, Bina, and Andhra Pradesh. The Mumbai project involves replacing the existing fluid catalytic cracking unit (FCCU) and the catalytic cracking unit (CCU) with an upgraded petro resid FCCU, at a cost of Rs 14,200 crore, with completion likely by May 2029. In Bina, the refinery expansion project, worth Rs 50,000 crore, aims to increase capacity from 7.8 MMTPA to 11 MMTPA, with scheduled completion in 2028. The Kochi project, valued at Rs 5,000 crore, includes installation of a 400 KTPA polypropylene (PP) plant and revamping of the petro resid FCCU, with scheduled completion deadline of October 2027. 

BPCL has also allocated Rs 6,100 crore for pre-project activities towards the greenfield refinery-cum-petrochemical complex in Andhra Pradesh. Further, the Bina project has secured a loan agreement of Rs 31,802 crore with the State Bank of India-led consortium, ensuring funding is in place for the largest ongoing project. While the company has an established track record in the oil refining space, timely implementation of this schedule within the budgeted time and cost, as well as the return on these investments would be monitorable. 

# **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of BPCL and its subsidiaries and joint ventures (JVs) - the subsidiaries have been fully consolidated and the JVs have been proportionately consolidated. Crisil Ratings believes these entities are strategically important to, and have considerable operational linkages with, BPCL. The ratings also factor in support from the GoI.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Strategic importance to, and continued support from, GoI** 

The oil refining and marketing activity is strategic for India's economic development. Oil marketing companies (OMCs) dominate the domestic market for key petroleum products such as motor spirit, high-speed diesel, jet fuel and LPG. Uninterrupted supply of these products is contingent for smooth operations of OMCs, such as BPCL. The company remains strategically important to GoI and plays a key role in implementing the government's socio-economic policies. GoI also holds 52.98% stake in BPCL and exercises management control. Any diminution in the company's strategic importance or in GoI’s shareholding will remain key monitorable. 

# **Established market position in oil refining and marketing and branding initiatives aiding revenue growth** 

BPCL accounts for nearly 14% of the country's refining capacity and holds ~25% share in the domestic petroleum products market. The company commands a higher retail market share in key products such as motor spirit and high-speed diesel. Its market position is underpinned by the entrenched marketing and distribution infrastructure with 24,197 retail outlets as on September 1,2025. BPCL also has an active domestic LPG distributor base of 6,267 crore as of September 1,2025 and has undertaken aggressive branding and marketing exercises. 

# **Strong operating efficiency** 

High capacity utilisation of refineries ensure strong operating efficiency. Furthermore, proximity of refineries to the coast translates into logistic benefits and helps control the transportation cost involved in procuring crude oil. Modernisation and expansion of capacities undertaken at several refineries should support GRMs. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Modest financial risk profile** 

Consolidated gearing has marginally increased to 0.77 time as on March 31, 2025, from 0.73 time as on March 31, 2024. Reduced profitability has led to moderation of debt protection metrics, as reflected in interest coverage ratio of 8.17 times for fiscal 2025, as against 11.67 times in the previous fiscal, and debt/Ebitda ratio of 2 times against 1 time over the same period. 

The company has sizeable capex plans over the medium term, and timely implementation within the budgeted cost and returns earned on added capacities would be closely monitored. Planned annual capex for the projects remains moderate in the range of Rs 16,000-18,000 crore for the next two fiscals.  Major outflow of Rs 33,000-35,000 crore is expected in the later stage of projects over fiscals 2028 and 2029. Despite the capex plans, the management has indicated that peak debt/equity would be maintained at a maximum of one time. 

# **Exposed to project implementation and stabilisation risk** 

BPCL is making steady progress on its refinery expansion programme. The Bina project has achieved 14% overall progress during the first quarter of fiscal 2026, with major part of the implementation being completed, including onboarding of 

technology licensors and consultants, and completion of front-end engineering design. The project is now in the phase of detailed engineering and procurement, with tenders for critical equipment and EPC packages already floated. The Kochi PP project has achieved 12.2% progress, with license selection and basic design engineering completed, and orders placed for six major long-lead items. The EPC tendering process for the PP unit is currently underway. Further, BPCL has completed initial activities for the petro residue FCCU and the associated facilities at the Mumbai refinery. The company is also progressing on the greenfield refinery chemical complex in Andhra Pradesh, with Rs 6,100 crore approved for pre-project activities such as land acquisition and feasibility studies. 

Ongoing projects are overseen by a team of experienced personnel, contractors and external consultants. However, set up of the petrochemical unit does involve certain technical complexities and performance of the petrochemical sector remains volatile. Any significant time and cost overrun could impact leverage metrics of the company. Once commissioned, the ability to ramp-up and achieve optimum capacity utilisation and operating efficiency will be critical. 

# **Susceptibility to volatility in crude oil prices** 

Crude oil prices have been volatile over the past few years. Prices for the Indian basket fell sharply to a low of around $20 per bbl in April 2020 before rising significantly to over $110 per bbl in March 2022; the procurement price averaged around $93 per bbl in fiscal 2023, around $83 per bbl in fiscal 2024, around $79 per bbl in fiscal 2025 and $67 per bbl as on June 30,2025. The ongoing geo-political tensions have led to a sharp decline in crude oil prices. BPCL imports nearly 80% of its requirement and, thus, remains susceptible to volatility in the rupee-dollar exchange rate and a corresponding increase in value of imports. Ability to compensate for these volatilities through the marketing margin remains a key monitorable. 

# **<u>Liquidity: Superior</u>** 

As a Maharatna company, BPCL enjoys strong financial flexibility, driven by support from GoI and access to low-cost funds from the domestic and overseas markets. As of March 2025, the company held cash and equivalents (including oil bonds) of around Rs 14,073 crore, against Rs 10,636 crore as of March 2024. Healthy cash accrual and available liquidity should suffice to meet scheduled debt obligations of Rs 11,000-13,000 crore per fiscal in 2026 and 2027. 

# **<u>Environment, social, and governance (ESG) profile</u>** 

Crisil Ratings believes that the company’s ESG profile backs its already strong credit risk profile, in addition to benefits from the support received from GoI. 

The oil and gas sector has a significant impact on the environment due to the high carbon emissions released from refineries and petrochemical plants. In this context, BPCL has been continuously focusing on mitigating its environmental and social risks to ensure minimal impact. 

# **Environmental** 

- BPCL has outlined a clear roadmap to build 10 GW of renewable energy by 2035, reinforcing their commitment to decarbonization of their operations. At present, the total installed renewable energy capacity stands at 154.86 MW with projects amounting to 171 MW of renewable capacity under-construction. 

- The goal to become Net Zero for Scope 1 and Scope 2 Greenhouse Gas Emissions by 2040, aligns with the national target of Net Zero by 2070. Replacement of conventional Steam Methane Reforming (SMR) methods at the Mumbai and Bina refineries with Green Hydrogen technology is expected to contribute approximately 15% to total emissions abatement. BPCL is investing heavily in biofuels, they plan to develop 200-220 medium-sized Bio-CNG plants with an estimated production capacity of 1.30 MMT per annum, two bio-refineries (1G [First generation] & 2G [Second generation]) are being established at Bargarh, Orissa and a 16.35% ethanol blending in FY 25 has been achieved. 

- 1,17,343 MT of non-hazardous waste was recycled, and 5,551 MT of plastic waste equivalent was reclaimed under EPR. The ZWL certification underscores the company’s resolve towards sustainable operations and environmental stewardship. 

- Over 1.78 lakh trees were planted using techniques such as Miyawaki afforestation, seed bombing, and conventional plantation methods that expanded cumulative green cover to 12,17,784 trees, contributing to the sequestration of approximately 25,571 metric tonnes of CO2 equivalent (MTCO2 eq.) 

# **Social** 

- 2,553 training hours were logged on Health, Safety, Security and Environment (HSSE), covering for 2,361 Participants. In refineries, 125.55 Mn man-hours were recorded without lost time accidents (LTA) 

- BPCL enhanced learning environments via infrastructure upgrades, Science on Wheels, WASH facilities, and distribution of educational bag-cum-desks. Several CSR schemes translated to more than 33.5 lakh+ beneficiaries from vulnerable and marginalized groups. 

- 100% of the employees and non-permanent workers were trained on health, safety measures and skill upgradation. Also, 31.8% of material was procured from MSMEs/MSEs, surpassing the mandated target of 2025. 

- 99.82% of customer complaints were resolved with a closure time of two days and the remaining ones were addressed and closed satisfactorily within 3 days of registration. 

# **Governance** 

- BPCL’s governance structure is characterized by 40% of the board comprising independent directors and dedicated investor grievance redressal mechanism and extensive disclosures. 

- Most of the statutory committees (Audit, NRC, Stakeholder Relationship & Risk Management) were dominated by independent members with an independent director serving as the chairperson. 

Nil complaints were received from Investors (other than shareholders), also there were no whistleblower complaints during FY 25. 

There is growing importance of ESG among investors and lenders. BPCL’s continued commitment to ESG principles will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence and ensure ease of raising capital from markets where ESG compliance is a key factor. 

# **<u>Outlook: Stable</u>** 

Crisil Ratings believes BPCL will continue to benefit from the management control and majority ownership of GoI.

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

BPCL, a government undertaking (52.98% ownership as on June 30, 2025), is an integrated refining and marketing company. It is India's second-largest oil marketing and third-largest refining company, with consolidated refining capacity of 35.3 MTPA, representing 14% of India's total installed capacity. The company operates two refineries along the west coast: a 12-MTPA refinery in Mumbai and a 15.5-MTPA refinery in Kochi. Additionally, Bharat Oman Refineries Ltd (BORL), which operated a 7.8MTPA refinery in Bina, was merged with BPCL in July 2022. Retail operations are supported by a nationwide marketing network, comprising a 2,600 km product pipeline and 22,011 retail outlets. 

For the first quarter ended June 30, 2025, BPCL reported net profit of Rs 6,839 crore on revenue of Rs 1,12,551 crore as against Rs 5,627 crore and Rs 1,13,095 crore, respectively, for the corresponding period of the previous fiscal.
