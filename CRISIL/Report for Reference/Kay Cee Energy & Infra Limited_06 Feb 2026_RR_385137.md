### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ratings on the bank facilities of Kay Cee Energy & Infra Limited (KCEIL) at ‘Crisil BBB-/Stable/Crisil A3’. 

The ratings continue to reflect the company’s established regional presence backed by the experience of the promoters in the heavy electrical equipment and engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) industry and the healthy financial risk profile of the company. These strengths are partially offset by presence in a highly fragmented industry, and large working capital requirement. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

For arriving at the ratings, Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of KCEIL and its joint venture Suryavayu Renewable and Energy Solutions Private Limited. 

Unsecured loan of Rs 3.64 crore as on March 31, 2025, from the promoters has been treated as debt as the loan is likely to be repaid over the medium term.

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Established regional presence backed by extensive experience of the promoters:** The three-decade-long experience of the promoters and their technical expertise in EPC contracts and extra high-voltage (EHV) lines segments have enabled them to develop an understanding of the industry and strong relationships with customers and suppliers. 

Timely execution  of repeat orders from existing clients along with onboarding of new customers resulted in significant rise in revenue to Rs 83 crores in the first half of the current fiscal 2026 as compared to Rs 38 crores in similar period last fiscal. In the full fiscal 2026, the operating income is expected to improve to around Rs 230 crore, through timely execution of its orderbook in hand of Rs. 575 crores as on September 30, 2025, to be executed over the next 18-24 months, providing healthy medium-term revenue visibility. 

**Moderate financial risk profile:** The financial risk profile was comfortable, supported by adjusted net worth of Rs 61.69 crore as on March 31, 2025. Capital structure was healthy, as indicated by gearing and total outside liabilities to tangible networth (TOLTNW) ratio of 0.89 time and 1.81 times, respectively, as on March 31, 2025, supported by limited reliance on external debt and healthy accretion to reserve. Debt protection metrics were strong, as reflected in interest coverage and net cash accrual to adjusted debt (NCAAD) ratios of 5.74 times and 0.31 time, respectively, in fiscal 2025. 

In April 2025, the company raised funds through QIP of Rs 25 crore to support growth and improve cash flow. 

Adjusted networth is expected to improve to over Rs 112 crore as on March 31, 2026, supported by funds raised through QIP and healthy operating profitability over growing scale of operations. In the absence of any major debt-funded capital expenditure (capex), the capital structure will strengthen in fiscal 2026 with gearing and TOLTNW ratio expected at 0.45-0.5 time and 1.10-1.2 times, respectively. The interest coverage and NCAAD ratios are expected to remain robust at 7.5-7.75 times and 0.50-0.55 time, respectively, in fiscal 2026. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Susceptibility to risks inherent in tender-based business amid intense competition:** KCEIL undertakes EPC projects by submitting bids for tenders floated by government or private entities. Hence, revenue and profitability depend on the ability to bid successfully for tenders. Intense competition may continue to constrain scalability, pricing power and profitability. Expenditure of government agencies and public sector undertakings is directly linked to the economy. Any delay or deferment of capex in end-user industries could limit scalability. Though the operating margin sustained at 17.79% in fiscal 2025 (19.9% in fiscal 2024), in the first half of the current fiscal, the company has generated operating margin of 18.07%, which is expected to sustain around 18% in fiscal 2026. Sustenance of the operating margin amid risks related to tender-based operations and intense competition will remain monitorable. 

**Large working capital requirement:** Operations are working capital intensive, as indicated by gross current assets (GCAs) of 359 days as on March 31, 2025, driven by receivables of 96 days, inventory of 99 days and large statutory deposits. As the company is in the initial stages of executing some orders, the working capital requirement may improve once execution is stabilised and realisations are timely. The GCAs are expected around 350-360 days as on March 31, 2026, driven by expected receivables and inventory of 110 days and debtors of 120 days. The working capital cycle is partly supported by payables of around 176 days as on 31<sup>st</sup> March 2025. Similar trend is expected to continue over the medium term. Efficient working capital management amid increasing revenue will remain monitorable. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Adequate 

Liquidity was adequate with fund-based working capital limit utilised around 91%  over the 12 months through Nov 2025. Owing to high execution of orders in the second half of fiscal, average utilisation of fund-based limits is expected to remain high. Cash accrual, expected to be around Rs 27-46 crore per annum, will sufficiently cover yearly term debt obligation of Rs 5.5-2.2 crore over the medium term. In addition, surplus will cushion the liquidity. 

The company raised Rs 25 crore through QIP in April 2025 to fund its working capital requirement. 

Current ratio was healthy at 1.60 times on March 31, 2025. The promoters will likely extend equity and unsecured loans to meet working capital requirement and debt obligation. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes KCEIL will continue to benefit from the extensive experience of the promoters. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Steady increase in revenue and sustenance of operating margin leading to higher cash accrual Prudent utilisation of QIP funds leading to working capital limit utilisation below 80% 

# **Downward factors** 

Decline in revenue and operating margin leading to cash accrual below Rs 10 crore 

Further stretch in the working capital cycle leading to utilisation of fund-based working capital limit over 90%; or large, debt-funded capex weakening the financial risk profile and liquidity 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Incorporated in 2015, KCEIL undertakes EPC projects as well as handling, erection, testing and commissioning of equipment and materials for power transmission and distribution systems, including transmission lines, construction of substations, automation, extension/modification and expansion of existing power systems for various government and private entities. KCEIL is listed on the NSE SME exchange.
