### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AA/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the bank facilities of Raymond Lifestyle Ltd (RLL). 

The ratings continue to reflect the company’s robust business risk profile, supported by its dominant position in the domestic worsted suiting business, established apparel brands, diversified revenue streams and strong retail network. The ratings further factor in the strong financial risk profile of the company, supported by minimal net debt and adequate liquidity. These strengths are partially offset by exposure to risks posed by various factors, including volatility in raw material prices and foreign exchange (forex) rates, intense competition, large addition of stores and macroeconomic factors such as global trade uncertainties stemming from US tariffs, downturns and inflationary pressure. 

RLL reported a muted performance in fiscal 2025, with consolidated operating income of Rs 6,177 crore (Rs. 6,536 crore in fiscal 2024), affected by weak discretionary demand, a material ransomware incident that disrupted operations for 25-26 days, and softer performance in the export segment. In the six months ended September 30, 2025, revenue improved to Rs 3,263 crore (up 11% on-year) as domestic demand recovered, leading to strong growth in the branded textile and apparel businesses, while trade uncertainties continued to strain the export-oriented garmenting division. Crisil Ratings expects RLL to record high single-digit to low double-digit revenue growth over the medium term. Continued expansion of the retail footprint, rise in discretionary demand, and scaling of the garmenting business, amid policy and geopolitical shifts (including opportunities arising from Bangladesh and UK-FTA), may lead to higher order inflow. 

The branded textile segment registered revenue of Rs 3,002 crore and earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (Ebitda) margin of 14.0% (Rs 3,450 crore and 20.9%, respectively, in fiscal 2024). The segment was adversely affected and reported a drop in volume, due to the ransomware event and subdued discretionary demand. However, revenue has picked up in the first half of fiscal 2026, growing 17% on-year to Rs 1,653 crore, and Ebitda margin rose to 17.6%, driven by wedding-season bookings, premium mix (Super 100–140 ranges), and regained shelf space in multi-brand outlets (MBOs). The segment remains well-positioned for further growth via premiumisation, deeper penetration into top luxury MBOs, and targeted engagement programmes. 

The branded apparel segment registered revenue and operating margin of Rs 1,593 crore and 7.4%, respectively, in fiscal 2025 (vis-à-vis Rs 1,587 crore and 11.9%, respectively, in fiscal 2024). Revenue remained flat amid weak mid-premium discretionary spending and disruption in operations during the fourth quarter. For the first half of fiscal 2026, revenue grew 16%, though the margin softened to 5.1% due to elevated advertising spends (30–35% growth on-year), cost of new store ramp-up and longer breakeven cycles. The segment is expected to benefit from continued premiumisation and casualisation, strong traction in Raymond RTW and Ethnix, and deeper penetration in tier II and tier III markets via expansion in asset light segments such as Multibrand Outlets (MBO), The Raymond Shop (TRS) outlets and Large Format Stores (LFS). 

The garmenting segment registered revenue and operating margin of Rs 1,068 crore and 4.7%, respectively, in fiscal 2025 (Rs 1,035 crore and 10.1%, respectively, a year before). The segment saw significant margin pressure due to caution exercised by buyers, adverse mix, training cost incurred for new lines, and geopolitical/logistic disruptions. Revenue declined by 9% in the first half of fiscal 2026 to Rs 466 crore, with operating margin dropping to 1.5%, amid global trade 

uncertainties and order deferrals. Planned capacity ramp-up and broadening of the customer base should aid recovery, with margin expected to normalise with stable utilisation rates. 

The high-value cotton shirting segment registered revenue and operating margin of Rs 800 crore and 14.3% (including onetime subsidy), respectively, in fiscal 2025.  Revenue declined 4% on-year due to lower volume, though the margin was temporarily inflated due to a one-time government subsidy of around Rs 53 crore.  For the first half of fiscal 2026, revenue remained flat at Rs 416 crore, with Ebitda margin improving to 10.7%, driven by B2B (business-to-business) demand for cotton/linen blends, productivity gains, and better product mix. Over the medium term, the segment is expected to grow moderately, supported by premiumisation (Regio Line, silk blends) and expansion in linen shirting. 

Operating profitability for RLL remains inherently supported by its vertically integrated textile operations. However, pre-lease consolidated Ebitda margin fell to ~7.7% in fiscal 2025, and stood at 9.3% (pre-lease) for the first half of fiscal 2026 (4.6% post lease for fiscal 2025 and 5.8% for the first half fiscal 2026), owing to aforesaid challenges. With operating leverage improving in the second half of fiscal 2026 and recovery in the apparel/garmenting segment, profitability should normalise in the medium term. The same however, remains a key monitorable. 

The financial risk profile remains strong, with the company reporting a net debt-free status and net cash of Rs 97 crore as on March 31, 2025. As on September 30, 2025, net debt increased temporarily to Rs 246 crore, driven by seasonal working capital expenses, incurred to meet festive and wedding demand, store investments and marketing expenses. With expected recovery in operational performance and working capital cycle likely to normalise in the second half of the fiscal, RLL may return to a near net-debt-free position by March 2026 and sustain the same over the medium term. Gearing is likely to be less than 0.3 time over the medium term (0.3 time as on March 31, 2025). Debt protection metrics should be comfortable too, supported by healthy operating cash flow, moderate capital expenditure (capex; largely maintenance), and a capitallight retail expansion strategy. Liquidity is further supported by steady banking limit and absence of large-term debt obligations. Crisil Ratings expects the strong capital structure and debt protection metrics to sustain. 

During the last week of September 2025, the Income-Tax Department conducted a survey under Section 133A at multiple Raymond Group premises, including offices and manufacturing units. The company has confirmed full cooperation with the authorities, and the survey concluded on September 30, 2025, with no adverse findings communicated as of date. Any material adverse outcome of the same shall remain key monitorable. 

# **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has consolidated the business and financial risk profiles of RLL and its various subsidiaries. These entities are part of the diversified Raymond group, and some have strong business linkages and financial fungibility and all of them operate under a common management. 

Goodwill arising due to valuation adjustments on demerger from Raymond Ltd has been directly adjusted against networth, as it represents valuation adjustments and has not arisen on payment of any consideration. 

Crisil Ratings has excluded lease liabilities as recognised under IndAS116 in debt and has adjusted the Ebitda, by excluding lease rental components in depreciation and finance cost.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Dominant position in the worsted suiting business:** Strong track record of over ten decades, healthy brand image and large retail network have helped Raymond establish a healthy market position in the worsted suiting business. Raymond is India’s largest manufacturer of worsted fabrics and wool blends and enjoys a dominant market share. It had 1,110 retail outlets branded as The Raymond Shop (TRS) as on September 30, 2025, across India and abroad. It holds a significant market share of 50-55% in the suiting segment. 

**Diversified revenue streams across apparel and textiles:** RLL has a well-diversified revenue profile, with significant presence in branded textiles (46% of revenue in fiscal 2025), branded apparel (25%), garmenting (17%), and high-value cotton shirting (12%).The company owns well-known brands such as Park Avenue, Raymond ready-to-wear, ColorPlus, and Parx, and has introduced the made-to-measure (MTM) store concept to offer custom-fit solutions. The company has also enhanced its focus on ethnic wear under the “Ethnix by Raymond” brand, which sees healthy traction, especially during the wedding season between April and May and October to December. 

**Strong retail network:** Having one of the largest retail store networks across India and overseas, comprising 1,110 TRS outlets, 50 made-to-measure (MTM) stores, and 503 exclusive brand outlets as on September 30, 2025, RLL has been able to reinforce its market position. The company  is expanding its dealership network to tier-3 and 4 cities and towns, and now has 20,000 touch points across the country. It uses the asset-light approach to widen its distribution reach and plans to expand further. 

**Healthy financial risk profile:** Total debt stood at Rs 1,476 crore as on September 30, 2025 (Rs 1,270 crore as on March 31, 2024), while the entity continued have a cash surplus of over Rs 1,200 crore as on the same date. While company was 

net debt positive at September owing to seasonal buildup of working capital, net debt free status is expected to be reachieved by end of the fiscal. Earlier, financial risk profile improved as proceeds from sale of the FMCG business sale were used to repay external debt in fiscal 2024. Debt protection metrics should be healthy with limited borrowings and gearing (on external debt) is expected to remain healthy over the medium term, with sustained healthy operating performance. Cash surplus is likely to remain adequate over the medium term after meeting capital expenditure (capex), term debt repayment and working capital requirement. Any large debt-funded inorganic acquisition is a key monitorable. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Exposure to volatility in raw material prices:** Volatility in cotton and wool prices has led to fluctuation in operating profitability. Raymond imports bulk of its wool requirement from Australia and New Zealand; it maintains a hedge book for a large portion of its forex exposure. For instance, material rise in prices of wool and cotton (owing to increase in minimum support price in India) led to moderation of overall operating profitability in fiscals 2020 and 2019, respectively. The impact was partly offset by the company’s ability to pass-on the hike in cost to its customers. 

**Moderate susceptibility of operating performance to economic downturns, global trade uncertainties stemming from US tariffs, inflationary pressure and large annual addition of stores:** Revenue and profitability remain susceptible to economic downturns, given the discretionary nature of products. In a cautious spending scenario, discretionary segments such as gems, jewellery and apparel are impacted the most, while non-discretionary segments such as food, grocery and pharmacy are less affected. For instance, temporary store closures, restricted mobility and curtailed discretionary spending during the first and second waves of the Covid-19 pandemic restricted growth during fiscal 2021 as well as the first quarter of fiscal 2022. Also, revenue growth slowed down considerably from the fourth quarter of fiscal 2023, due to muted discretionary demand, over a large base of the previous fiscal. Furthermore, large expansion by retailers can exert pressure on their operating margin as earnings from existing stores may not adequately offset losses from high proportion of new stores. 

RLL’s exposure to exports market remains modest at around 15–17% of consolidated revenues, and limited direct dependence on the US market, amid global trade uncertainties. However, heightened geopolitical and trade-related uncertainties, including tariff actions by the US, have resulted in second-order impacts on export performance, reflected in a “wait-and-see” approach by customers, deferment of orders, re-routing of shipments and increased pricing pressure across key export markets. These factors could weigh on near-term volume visibility and margin stability. Sustained volatility in global trade flows and its impact on export growth and profitability remain key monitorables. 

**Intense competition in the domestic apparel business:** The domestic apparel business is highly fragmented, with competition intensifying between the organised players. Brand penetration is likely to increase in the long term, among leading players such as Grasim Industries Ltd (Grasim; ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’; erstwhile Aditya Birla Nuvo Ltd merged with Grasim) and Aditya Birla Fashion & Retail Ltd (‘Crisil AA+/Stable/Crisil A1+’), Aditya Birla Lifestyle Brands Ltd. (‘Crisil AA+/Stable/Crisil A1+’), (Siyaram Silk Mills Ltd (‘Crisil AA-/Stable/Crisil A1+’) and Arvind Ltd (Arrow), operating via various brands such as Louis Philippe, Van Heusen, Allen Solly and Peter England. The apparel retail industry may record steady growth, driven by strong same-store sales, new store launches, improved penetration of organised retail, and higher contribution from online channels. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Liquidity is supported by sizeable, unencumbered surplus of over Rs 1,200 crore, held in mutual funds, fixed deposits and as cash balance as on September 30, 2025. Bank limit utilisation averaged 46% during the six months through September 2025. Cash surplus is likely to remain high, despite annual capex of Rs 200-250 crore and term-debt repayment of Rs 20-90 crore per annum over the next 2-3 years. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings expects RLL to maintain a healthy business and financial risk profile over the medium term. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors:** 

- Substantial and sustained revenue growth of over 15% annually, over the medium term, along with healthy operating profitability 

Sustenance of strong liquid surplus and debt metrics, supported by adequate cash generation 

# **Downward factors:** 

- Sluggish business performance with steep moderation in operating profitability to below 8-9% (Pre IndAS) on a sustained basis 

- Substantial increase in debt or reduction in liquid surplus, due to larger-than-expected capex, dividend payments, sharebuy-back, or acquisitions 

Any material adverse outcome of income tax survey proceedings, impacting the financial profile materially 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Incorporated in 1925, Raymond is one of the leading integrated producers of worsted suiting fabrics globally. On a standalone basis, the company manufactures 38 million metre of fabric per annum. It offers more than 20,000 designs and colours of suiting fabric, and exports to over 40 countries. 

The lifestyle segment includes suiting, garments, apparel and shirting, while the non-lifestyle segment includes the denim, engineering (tools and hardware, and automotive components) under Raymond Ltd and real estate businesses under Raymond Realty Ltd. As on September 30, 2025, the promoters held 57.14% stake and public held the rest (including financial institutions) in RLL. 

Following completion of RLL’s demerger process, the scheme became effective on June 30, 2024. Through its order, the National Company Law Tribunal has approved the scheme of arrangement between Raymond, RLL and Ray Global Consumer Trading Ltd and their respective shareholders, with appointed date of April 1, 2023.
