### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has revised its outlook on the long-term bank facilities of Sheela Foam Limited (SFL) to ‘ **Positive’** from ‘Stable’ while reaffirming the rating at ‘ **Crisil AA-** ’. 

The revision in the outlook factors in the improvement in the business risk profile of SFL as witnessed by successful integration with Kurlon Enterprise Ltd (KEL) and its market leadership position in the mattress and foam segment providing strong impetus for healthy growth in revenues and expansion in profitability. The revision in outlook also factors in strengthening of financial risk profile with continuous deleveraging. 

Consolidated revenues increased by 7% year on year to Rs.2771 crores driven by healthy volume growth in 9m-FY26 despite some moderation in realizations owing soft raw material costs. SFL has opened 600 retail showrooms till Dec 2025 with a target of 700 retail showrooms in fiscal 2026 as a strategy to maintain and expand their market share. With prices of crude linked raw materials on the rise since past couple of months, realizations are also expected to increase as company has taken calibrated price hikes. Consequently, revenue growth is expected to be stronger next fiscal driven by a combination of healthy volume growth and improved realizations. 

Operating margins have improved to 10% in the first nine months of fiscal 2026 compared to 8.4% in same period previous fiscal despite forex and mark to market losses on investments during the period. With realization of 80% of the synergy benefits from KEL acquisition, recent price hikes being undertaken, along with improving share from higher margin mattress division, operating margins are expected to improve further going forward. Working capital cycle to remain comfortable with debtor and inventory days staying at 35-40 days each. 

Financial risk profile has strengthened with substantial reduction in debt resulting in improvement in capital structure and debt protection metrics. As of April 2026, SFL has repaid 3/4<sup>th</sup> for the debt taken in the form of Non-Convertible Debentures to fund the acquisition of KEL. With this residual acquisition debt expected to be completely retired by October 2026, gearing may improve to below 0.15 times and interest coverage at over 10 times going forward. Liquidity also remains healthy supported by cash surplus of Rs.110 crores as of April 2026, low bank limits utilisation, healthy accruals and some core assets which could be liquidated as required. 

The ratings continue to be driven by the established market position of SFL and its diversified geographic presence and revenue profile and the strong financial risk profile of the SFL group. The strengths are partially offset by susceptibility to volatility in input prices and competition from the unorganised segment. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of SFL and its subsidiaries as all the entities, collectively referred to as the SFL group, are in the same business, and SFL has managerial control over all the entities.

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u> Established market position in the modern mattress business** 

SFL is the largest producer of flexible slab stock polyutherane (PU) foam and mattresses in India. Post-acquisition of KEL, the second-largest player in the mattress segment, SFL has a combined share of 29% in the modern mattress market, along with its well-established brands, Sleepwell and Kurlon. The product range includes mattresses, pillows, cushioning material, and specialized foams. A pan-India distribution network facilitates access to retail and industrial customers. Gradual shift from the unorganised to organised sector, backed by growing consumer awareness about health benefits of using quality mattresses, has helped the company gain market share. 

# **Healthy revenue diversity** 

The revenue profile is diversified in terms of product segments and geographical reach. Though mattresses continue to be a dominant revenue contributor, the SFL group also has significant presence in retail and industrial foam products. Apart from furniture cushioning, the group sells flexible slab stock PU foam to manufacturers of automotive seats, footwear and clothing. SFL has also made a strategic investment of 43.05% in the House of Keiraya – Furlenco in the last two years, which primarily rents and also sells furniture; an allied business to SFL. 

Furthermore, the group is present in Australia, through Joyce Foam Pty Ltd (JFPL), the largest local player (40% market share) in the foam business, and the European Union through Interplasp, which operates from Spain. 

The Australian and Spanish businesses accounted for 23% of revenue in fiscal 2025. Healthy geographical diversity in revenue shall benefit operations over the medium term and protect the company from any regional disruptions. 

SFL has a strong presence in northern and western India, whereas KEL has major presence in eastern and southern India. Acquisition of KEL will thus, help SFL widen its overall geographic reach. 

# **Comfortable financial risk profile** 

The financial risk profile is marked by a healthy capital structure, improving debt protection metrics and adequate liquidity. Tangible networth is expected to remain healthy over Rs.2500 crores in the medium term with comfortable accretion to reserves. The debt payment is on track with the debt expected to reduce to Rs.555 crores in fiscal 2026 from Rs.1283 crores in the previous fiscal. The same is expected to reduce further with the balance NCD repayment of Rs.361 crores raised for KEL acquisition in fiscal 2027. 

The gearing is expected to remain healthy at below 0.1 times in the medium term. Interest coverage is likely to improve to over 5 times in fiscal 2026 from 2.7 times in fiscal 2025 and further to over 10 times in the medium term with decreasing interest burden. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Susceptibility to volatility in input prices** 

Raw material cost forms a significant portion of overall manufacturing cost (60-65%). The key raw materials, polyol and toluene diisocyanate (TDI), are manufactured from by-products of crude oil, and their prices are linked to crude oil prices and prevailing demand-supply conditions. Prices have fluctuated sharply in the past few years. However, the company managed to achieve higher gross margin through bulk discounts on purchases, improvement in yields, material substitution and use of innovative technologies such as variable pressure foam (VPF), and other input material rationalisation. Sustenance of the same would lead to greater profitability. 

With heightened geo-political conflict in Middle East, SFL’s ability to pass on the increase in raw material prices to consumers and maintain a healthy margin remains a key monitorable. The raw material price fluctuations are usually readily passed on to the institutional customers in the foam segment, while it is more gradual in the mattress segment as their retail price increases are generally kept calibrated with competitors. 

Operating margin was impacted in FY25 (7.3%) as the lower-margin business of KEL was consolidated with SFL. SFL has adopted various initiatives on both higher sales realisations and cost control to gradually reduce the gap in operating profitability between both the companies which is likely to improve the overall profitability of the company over the medium term. 

# **Exposure to intense competition from unorganized players and new age players and threat of substitutes** 

SFL faces intense competition from unorganized players, which forms more than 50% of the mattress market in India. Furthermore, new-age players on e-commerce platforms add strong competition. The ability to maintain the market share across a range of products amid intense competition will remain a rating sensitivity factor. Further, growing environmental awareness and available sustainable solutions are paving way for bio-based foam alternatives to PU foams such as soybased sustainable foams, polyolefin foams, and latex etc. In the current market scenario with sharp increase in the prices of raw materials, organized players like SFL are better positioned to gain market share from them as unorganized players find it difficult to navigate price increase absorption in the market while simultaneously managing to contain  increase in their working capital requirements. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Apart from adequate cash accrual, liquidity will be also aided by cash & liquid investment balances of Rs.110 crores in April 2026. Bank limit of Rs 1000 crore remains moderately utilised at 6% over the 6 months ended Jan 31, 2026. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Positive 

Crisil Ratings believes SFL will continue to benefit from its strong market position and likely advantages from consolidation of both entities along with the strengthening of financial risk profile with the anticipated debt reduction.

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

SFL, promoted by the late Ms Sheela Gautam, commenced commercial production of PU foam at its factory in Sahibabad, Uttar Pradesh in 1971. It has 12 manufacturing units, which includes 5 units for PU-foam facilities, with combined capacity of 1,39,000 tonne per annum (TPA), across India including 10,000 TPA for Jabalpur plant, which got commenced in December 2023. The company sells foam, coir and spring mattresses under the Sleepwell brand and non-mattress foam products under the Feather Foam brand. The promoters hold ~65.69% shareholding in the SFL. 

JFPL, acquired by SFL in 2005, is the largest player in the PU foam business in Australia. It manufactures polyester, reticulated, viscoelastic and memory foam, while using VPF technology. It caters to furniture and automobile seat manufacturers and the bedding industry. Currently, it has foaming capacity of 17,000 TPA. 

In December 2016, SFL completed its initial public offering, wherein the promoters diluted 14.32% of their stake. Shares on sale were offloaded by Polyflex Marketing Pvt Ltd, a company held by the promoters. 

In October 2019, SFL acquired Interplasp. Established in 1987, Interplasp specialises in manufacturing flexible PU foams using VPF technology and mainly supplies to mattress and furniture manufacturers in Spain. It also markets to mattress manufacturers in Portugal and foam convertors in Morocco. It has a single facility with capacity of 22,000 TPA. 

In October 2023, the company also acquired 35% stake in House of Kieraya (HOK) – Furlenco which was further increased to 43.05% in Dec 2025. Furlenco was founded in 2012 by Mr. Ajith Mohan Karimpana. It is today one of the fastest growing furniture companies in India and is a tech-driven business. The company operates under the brand name Furlenco. 

SFL has an incremental voting right in Furlenco of up to 50.01% even when the company acquired 35% stake in the company. The incremental voting right remains unchanged even though the company has acquired additional stake in Furlenco. The incremental stake of 50.01% will remain till the time SFL acquires up to 50.01% stake in Furlenco.
