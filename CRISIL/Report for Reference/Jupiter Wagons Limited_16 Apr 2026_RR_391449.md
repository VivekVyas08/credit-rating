### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AA-/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the bank facilities of Jupiter Wagons Limited (JWL). 

Operating performance weakened in the first nine months of fiscal 2026, with income falling 27% year-on-year to Rs 2,136 crore. This decline was primarily driven by a 35% drop in wagon dispatches (4,151 units compared to 6,343 previously), stemming from a chronic wheelset shortage from the Rail Wheel Factory. Furthermore, low tendering activity by Indian Railways has reduced the pending order book to Rs 5,041 crore as of December 31, 2025, down from Rs 6,304 crore on March 31, 2025. 

With freight wagons constituting 69% of the current backlog, future growth depends on continued orders from Indian Railways and private clients, alongside timely execution. Therefore, fresh order inflow and the company's ability to manage industry-wide supply chain bottlenecks are vital for a recovery in its scale of operations. 

The ratings continue to reflect the established market position supported by technology tie-ups and partnerships, healthy scale of operations and financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by exposure to risks relating to fluctuation in raw material prices, intense competition, working capital intensive operations and risks associated with ongoing capex. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

For arriving at the ratings, Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of JWL, with its joint ventures JWL Dako Cz India Private Limited, JWL Kovis (India) Private Limited, JWL Talegria (India) Private Limited, its step-down subsidiary Jupiter Tsaw Onedrone India Private Limited and its subsidiaries Stone India Limited, Jupiter Tatravagonka Railwheel Factory Private Limited (rated, 'Crisil A+/Stable/Crisil A1'), Habitation Realestate LLP & Jupiter Electric Mobility Private Limited.

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Established Market Position Supported by Strategic Partnerships and Technology:** JWL is a premier Indian wagon manufacturer with an annual capacity of 10,800 units, boasting a strong reputation and deep-rooted relationships with government and private clients across the iron, steel, power, logistics, mining, and cement sectors. These long-standing relationships, along with alliances with reputable OEMs, ensure a steady order inflow. 

Leveraging technical partnerships with global entities, JWL has diversified beyond traditional wagon manufacturing into a broader product portfolio. A key strategic initiative is the forged rail wheelsets project via JTRFPL, which enhances backward integration and reduces import reliance. Furthermore, the partnership with Slovakia-based Tatravagonka brings European expertise, mitigating technical and execution risks for JTRFPL. 

With revenue streams diversified (approx. 75% from wagons, 10% from wheelsets, 10% from commercial vehicle load bodies, and 5% from containers/components like CMS crossings and bogies), JWL is well-positioned to capitalize on infrastructure spending. The combination of technological expertise, robust demand, and strategic capacity expansion is expected to drive growth in the medium term. 

**Healthy scale of operations:** Revenue was constrained at Rs 2,136 crore during the first nine months of fiscal 2026, primarily due to a shortage of rail wheels supplied by the Rail Wheel Factory for Indian Railways orders. Supply-side constraints eased starting September 2025, with an expected improvement in scale during Q4 of fiscal 2026. The lower-thananticipated scale in fiscal 2026 resulted in operating margin moderation, which fell by 147 bps to 13.1% in the nine months through December 2025, down from 14.6% during the same period in the previous fiscal. 

On a standalone basis, JTRFPL registered revenue of Rs 385 crore and an operating margin of 17% in the first nine months of fiscal 2026, improving from Rs 333 crore and 13%, respectively, in fiscal 2025, driven by increased capacity and utilization. 

Furthermore, investments in the greenfield capex for a forged wheelset manufacturing plant in Odisha are progressing as expected and should be completed by the end of CY 2027. 

Recovery in the scale of operations, healthy order inflows, and the timely completion of ongoing capacity expansions are expected to drive revenue growth over the medium term and will remain key rating sensitivity factors. 

**Healthy financial risk profile:** Strong networth of Rs 2,751 crore as on March 31, 2025, and healthy operating performance in fiscal 2025 supported the financial position, with a gearing of 0.2 time, total outside liabilities to total net worth (TOLTNW) ratio of 0.5 time, and interest coverage of 9.6 times. During fiscal 2026, debt levels remained elevated despite muted operating performance as inventory holding increased to around 120 days (to minimize production interruptions caused by raw material shortages). This, along with rising balance sheet debt to fund the ongoing rail wheelset capex (of ~Rs 2,500 crore between fiscals 2026 and 2028, funded by a debt-to-equity ratio of 65:35), resulted in gearing and interest coverage of 0.3 time and 6.1 times, respectively, as of September 30, 2025. Going forward, a recovery in scale and prudent working capital management will be crucial for sustaining a healthy financial risk profile. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Exposure to risks relating to fluctuation in raw material prices and intense competition:** Key inputs include steel and related products. While IR (Indian Railways) projects generally have a long execution period and are covered by pricevariation clauses to a large extent, private sector orders are typically fixed-price. Hence, to an extent, JWL's operating profitability is susceptible to fluctuations in steel prices during the project execution period. On the other hand, pricing power is restricted as orders are competitive and awarded based on bids submitted by wagon manufacturers. However, bid premiums cover volatility risk, while prudent inventory management and superior integration protect margins. As such, cost pass-through is critical for the sustenance of healthy operating margins. 

**Working capital intensive operations:** Gross Current Asset (GCA) days were in the range of 181-208 days during the three fiscals through March 31, 2025, driven by inventory and debtors (84 days and 76 days, respectively, as on March 31, 2025), as ~50% of work on hand comprises orders from private players. Working capital requirements were partially supported by creditors, ranging from 46-71 days over the same period. Going forward, GCAs are likely to remain elevated at over 200-250 days due to the working capital intensity of operations (driven by inventory holding). However, this is supported by sizeable funds earmarked for capex and general corporate purposes, cushion in working capital limits, back-toback contracts with suppliers, and healthy accruals sufficient to meet incremental working capital requirements. 

**Risks associated with ongoing capex** : JTRFPLs planned capex of ~Rs 2,500 crore between fiscals 2026 and 2028, is being funded in debt-to-equity ratio of 65:35. Though the commissioning is planned in phases, timely receipt of statutory clearances have bearing on completion risk. Almost ~80% of the output is expected to be consumed by JWL and Tatravagonka which mitigates the demand risk. However, the offtake is linked with fortunes of the freight wagon and passenger coach industry, and any time or cost overrun may have an impact on the financial position and flexibility and will be closely monitored. Furthermore, long gestation capex results in low return on capital employed (RoCE), thus necessitating a close watch on the improvement in operating efficiency driven by rise in the scale of operations. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Bank limit utilisation is moderate around 60 percent for the past ten months ended December 2025. Cash accrual are expected to be over Rs 250-370 crore which are sufficient against term debt obligation of less than Rs 15 crore over the medium term through fiscal 2028. The surplus will act as a cushion to the liquidity of the company. Repayment on the term loan for the greenfield capex of JTRFPL is scheduled in 40 equal quarterly instalments starting June 2029. Cash bank balance and mutual fund investment were healthy at Rs 557 crore on September 30, 2025. The current ratio is healthy at 2.2 times on March 31, 2025. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes JWL will continue to benefit from extensive experience of its promoters, its established market position, technology tie-ups and partnerships and continuous backward integration. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors:** 

Sizeable improvement in market position & revenues, driven by stronger execution capabilities, healthy order inflow, with operating margins sustained over 14% resulting in higher net cash accruals. 

Lower project risk and prudent working capital management resulting in sustenance of financial position and flexibility. 

# **Downward factors:** 

- Delay in offtake of orders due to raw material supply constraints or delays in various regulatory clearances or capacity expansion resulting in interest coverage below 4 times. 

- Time or cost overrun in the ongoing capex or increase in share of loss in JV or rise in working capital requirement or sizeable dividend payout exerting pressure on the financial risk profile. 

# **<u>About the Group</u>** 

JWL _(formerly Commercial Engineers and Body Builders Co Ltd [CEBBCO]_ ) was incorporated in 1979. The present management of the company, through the erstwhile JWL, had invested in CEBBCO in 2019 under a debt resolution plan. JWL amalgamated with CEBBCO through a reverse merger and the combined entity has been listed on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE). The company’s name (formerly known as CEBBCO) was changed to Jupiter Wagons Ltd (JWL) with effect from May 25, 2022. 

Mr Murari Lal Lohia is the Chairman Emeritus, Mr Vivek Lohia is the Managing Director and Mr Vikash Lohia is the Deputy Managing Director. 

JWL manufactures railway wagons, wagon components, weldable CMS crossings, load bodies for commercial vehicles and containers. JWL has its manufacturing units located in Kolkata (West Bengal), Jabalpur (Madhya Pradesh), Jamshedpur (Jharkhand), Indore (Madhya Pradesh). It has capacity to manufacture ~10,800 wagons annually and is backward integrated with a foundry shop to manufacture various components of a typical wagon like couplers, bogies, draft gears and CRF section. 

JWL has partnerships with leading global companies via joint ventures (JVs) for manufacturing brake disc, brake systems for rolling stock and weldable CMS crossings through JWL Dako Cz India Pvt Ltd, JWL Kovis (India) Pvt Ltd and JWL Talegria (India) Pvt Ltd. 

It has also entered the electric mobility sector through a subsidiary, Jupiter Electric Mobility Pvt Ltd, concentrating on e-LCV (electric light commercial vehicles) for last mile delivery. Its subsidiary, 

Habitation Realestate LLP holds property, has no business operations. 

JTRFPL (earlier known as Bonatrans India Pvt Ltd) was incorporated in June 2013 and is a subsidiary of JWL. On March 20, 2024, JWL acquired Bonatrans India Pvt Ltd and its name was revised to its present name on September 17, 2024. JTRFPL manufactures wheels, axles and wheelsets primarily used in railway rolling stock, locomotives and metro applications, with an annual production capacity of 12,000 wheelsets. Its manufacturing facility is in Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (Aurangabad), Maharashtra.
