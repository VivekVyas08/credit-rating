### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has revised its outlook on the long-term bank facilities of Savita Oil Technologies Limited (SOTL) to **‘Negative’** from ‘Stable’ while reaffirmed the rating at **‘Crisil AA’** ; The short-term rating has been reaffirmed at ‘Crisil A1+’. Crisil Ratings has also **withdrawn** its rating on the bank facilities of Rs 52.9 crore at the request of the company and after receiving a no dues certificate from the bank. This is in line with the Crisil Ratings withdrawal policy. 

The outlook revision factors in the weaker-than-expected operating performance, which resulted in moderation in business risk profile. The operating margin declined to 4.2% during fiscal 2025 from 7% in fiscal 2024 owing to continuous volatility in base oil prices (key raw material) last fiscal. As the company has to maintain inventory of 2-3 months at any given point of time, declining input prices during fiscal 2025 resulted in carryforward of high-cost inventory. Additionally, increase in advertisement spends for the newly launched lubricating oil brand, Savsol Ester 5, to increase market share coupled with decline in exports due to geopolitical instability and increased freight charges due to red sea issue affecting shipping routes leading led to decline in margins. Ebitda (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortisation) was down to Rs 3,680 per KL in fiscal 2025 from Rs 6,319 per KL in fiscal 2024. While the financial risk profile remains healthy owing to absence of debt, improvement in the operating margin, translating into overall recovery in the cash accrual and return metrics, shall remain monitorable. 

SOTL registered 2% on-year revenue growth during fiscal 2025, driven by increased volumes of 5%; though the gain was partially offset by decline in realisation across all segments due to falling base oil prices. The volume in the transformer oil and lubricating oil segments has increased consistently over the past three fiscals, and a similar trend is expected to continue over the medium term, aided by government focus on infrastructure and demand from the automotive industry. However, demand for white oil has declined due to muted demand from the fast-moving consumer goods sector. Nevertheless, demand for white oil is expected to improve hereon. The overall volume is likely to continue to grow in high single digit over the medium term. 

The ratings continue to reflect the established market position of SOTL, diversified revenue profile across end-user industries and products, and healthy financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by susceptibility of operating margin to sharp volatility in foreign exchange (forex) rates and base oil prices, working capital-intensive operations and exposure to intense competition. 

The financial risk profile will remain healthy, driven by an increase in net cash accrual and sustenance of a strong capital structure following healthy networth. SOTL has nil long-term debt, and its short-term debt is entirely because of trade acceptances for import of base oil. Gearing is expected to remain below ~0.2 time over the medium term. The debt protection metrics should remain robust too, with interest coverage ratio expected at over 15 times over the medium term. Net cash accrual of Rs 150-200 crore will be sufficient to cover maintenance capital expenditure (capex) of Rs 35-40 crore per annum over the medium term. Interest coverage ratio is also expected at over 8 times. 

Additionally, gross current assets (GCAs) improved from ~185 days in fiscal to 160-240 days over the five fiscals ended March 31, 2021, led by inventory of 75-95 days. The company has to maintain base oil inventory of 2-3 months given the transit time of 4-6 weeks. Inventory reduced from 89 days as on March 31, 2024, to 80 days as on March 31, 2025, and is 

expected to further reduce as the company is looking at more prudent inventory management to mitigate itself against losses amidst volatility in crude oil prices. 

SOTL had floated a 100% subsidiary, Savita Greentec Ltd (SGL), in October 2022 to manufacture recycled polythelene terephthalate (rPET) flakes and rPET pallets from PET waste bottle. The overall project cost was estimated at Rs 250 crore, to be funded entirely through internal accrual of SOTL. SOTL infused Rs 125 crore during fiscal 2024 by subscribing to optionally convertible debentures (OCDs) issued on private placement basis. Subsequently, SGL redeemed Rs 40 crore worth OCDs in fiscal 2025. As a result, the net infusion currently stands at Rs 85 crore by way of OCDs. Land was purchased in Bharuch, Gujarat, for Rs 65-70 crore during fiscal 2024. However, capex plans in SGL is on hold as the project is under review and no capex is expected over the medium term. No capex has been incurred, except on land purchase/developments.

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description</u>** 

# **Strengths:** 

**Established market position in the base oil industry:** The company has an established market position with one-third market share each in the domestic transformer oil and white oil segments. In the lubricating oil segment, the market share is relatively lower. SOTL caters to established and reputed clients, including Hindustan Unilever Ltd (‘Crisil AAA/Stable’), Dabur India Ltd (‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’), and Hitachi Energy India Ltd (‘Crisil AAA/Stable’); in addition to various state electricity boards (SEBs). Demand for the company’s major three product segments – transformer oil, liquid paraffin/white oils and lubricating oil – is expected to remain stable over the medium term. 

**Diversified revenue profile across end-user industries and geographies:** Diversity in end-user base and geographical reach lends stability to SOTL’s revenue profile. The transformer oil and white oil segments contributed ~35% each to revenue during fiscal 2025, while lubricating oil contributed 28% . The company’s products cater to three different end-user segments: power and distribution transformers, cosmetics and healthcare, and automotive and industrial lubricants, thus mitigating downturn in any particular industry. Exports, which contribute 15-20% to the revenue, enhance geographical outreach. Furthermore, SOTL’s wind power plants, aggregating 53.80 megawatt (MW), generated revenue of Rs 29 crore in fiscal 2025 from sale to SEBs and other customers. 

**Healthy financial risk profile:** Despite moderation in cash accrual, the financial risk profile is expected to remain stable over the medium term due to healthy networth of  Rs 1661 crore as on 31<sup>st</sup> March, 2025 and nil long-term debt obligation and limited capex. The capital structure remained strong, with total outside liabilities to tangible networth ratio and adjusted gearing of 0.42 time and 0.08 time, respectively, as on March 31, 2025. The ratios are expected to remain comfortable at below 0.2 time and 0.4 time, respectively, over the medium term. The debt protection metrics are expected to remain healthy, with interest coverage and net cash accrual to adjusted debt ratios of above 8 times and above 0.9 time, respectively. Liquidity remains comfortable with free cash and liquid investment of Rs 254 crore as on March 31, 2025 

# **Weaknesses:** 

**Susceptibility to sharp volatility in forex rates and prices of base oil:** Base oil, the key raw material (constituting 8590% of total input cost), is a crude derivative. Hence, its price is susceptible to any sharp volatility in crude prices. Moreover, as the company imports over 80% of its base oil requirement on account of limited availability in the domestic market, it remains exposed to sharp fluctuations in forex rates. Against this, exports comprise 15-20% of total revenue, which act as natural hedge. The remaining forex exposure is hedged through forward contracts and options, as per approved hedging policy. 

**Working capital-intensive operations:** The GCAs have been 160-240 days over the five fiscals ended March 31, 2021, led by inventory of 75-95 days. The company has to maintain base oil inventory of 2-3 months given the transit time of 4-6 weeks. Inventory reduced from 89 days as on March 31, 2024, to 80 days as on March 31, 2025, and this is expected to further reduce as the company is looking at more prudent management of inventory to mitigate itself against inventory losses amidst the volatility in crude oil prices. 

**Exposure to intense competition:** Low entry barrier and limited product differentiation have led to intense competition in the base oil processing industry. Focus on volume leads to competitive pricing and players are unable to fully pass on any increase in base oil prices to customers immediately. However, SOTL can pass on increase in raw material cost with a lag. SOTL also benefits from technical approvals from key customers for the supply of products. This acts as an entry barrier as obtaining such approvals is time consuming for new entrants. 

# **<u>Liquidity: Strong</u>** 

Cash and equivalent stood at Rs 234 crore as on March 31 2025. Cash accrual is expected at Rs 150-200 crore over the medium term and will be sufficient to fund annual capex of Rs 35-40 crore. Fund-based bank limit of Rs 60 crore was unutilised over the 12 months through March 2025, and will provide financial cushion during any exigency. 

# **<u>Outlook: Negative</u>** 

Lower-than-expected operating margin and cash accrual, along with volatility in the base oil prices, may impact the overall credit risk profile of SOTL over the medium term. However, SOTL will continue to benefit from its market leadership position, launch of ester technology, healthy product diversity and risk management practices. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Substantial increase in volumes while maintaining stability in operating margin at 10% on a sustained basis Sustenance of healthy financial risk profile and debt protection metrics 

# **Downward factors** 

- Substantial decline in business performance and cash accrual, with operating margin falling below 7% on a sustained basis 

Significant increase in debt in order to fund any large capex or stretch in the working capital cycle impacting the financial risk profile 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Established in 1961, SOTL is a leading player in the transformer oil, white oil, and industrial and automotive lubricants industries. These products are essentially obtained by refining base oil, and topping with additives to derive the required characteristics. The company’s manufacturing facilities at Turbhe and Mahad in Maharashtra; and Kharadpada and Silli in Silvassa have combined manufacturing capacities of ~550,000 per annum. The company also has wind power capacity of 53.80 MW which it sells to SEBs and other users under long-term agreements.
