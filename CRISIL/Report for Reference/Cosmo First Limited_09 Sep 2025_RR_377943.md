### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its 'Crisil AA-/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the bank facilities of Cosmo First Limited (CFL). 

The reaffirmation factors in the comfortable business risk profile, supported by the diversified product portfolio with majority of the revenue (~71% in fiscal 2025) coming from the specialised packaging films segment. The ratings also factor in the company’s established market position across both commodity and speciality flexible packaging segments in India and abroad, and comfortable financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by vulnerability of operating performance to volatility in raw material prices, demand-supply dynamics, and risks associated with timely commissioning and ramp-up of additional capacities. 

Revenue during Q1 fiscal 2026 improved by 16% y-o-y to Rs 820 crores. In fiscal 2025 improved by 12% y-o-y to Rs 2,900 crore from Rs 2,592 crore in fiscal 2024 supported by improvement in realization, while volumes saw a flattish growth during the year. The growth in realization was supported by an increasing share of value-added products and favorable industry dynamic driving up the prices in the BOPP segment. The operating margin also improved to 13.8% in Q1 fiscal 2026 against 10.7% in fiscal 2025, supported by these factors. Crisil Ratings expects revenue growth of 30-35% in fiscal 2026, driven by the ramp-up of newly commissioned capacities with EBITDA margins ranging between 13-14%. 

The financial risk profile is supported by a comfortable liquidity, with cash and equivalent of over Rs 370 crore as on March 31, 2025. The company has a policy of maintaining a cash balance equivalent to more than 1.5 years of debt obligation. The capital structure remains strong, with gearing below 1 time despite an increase in debt for funding capex. Interest coverage ratio had improved to 3.6 times in fiscal 2025 (against 2.4 times in fiscal 2024). Despite the addition of debt supported capex in fiscal 2026, the financial risk profile is expected to be healthy.

CRISIL Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of CFL and its wholly owned subsidiaries on account of operational and financial linkages among them.

### **<u>Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description</u> Strengths:** 

### **Market leadership in the packaging film industry** 

CFL currently has the largest BOPP capacity in India and accounted for ~30% of the domestic installed BOPP capacity currently in India. CFL commissioned a new BOPP line of 81,000 TPA in Q1 fiscal 2026 increasing its total capacity to ~277,000 tonne per annum (TPA), 

CFL remains a strong performer in the value-added segment of the flexible plastic packaging industry. It commissioned its specialised BOPET line (30,000 TPA) in the second quarter of fiscal 2023. CFL is also in the process of adding capacity of coating manufacturing plant (of 15,000 tonnes) in its wholly owned subsidiary  i.e. Cosmo Global Films Private limited. With the addition of these capacities, the value-added product segment of CFL will strengthen.CFL is the leading  manufacturer of coating packaging products in India. 

# **Increasing focus on speciality products** 

CFL has been gradually moving towards the value-added segment, with speciality products contributing 70% to the revenue (in volume terms) in fiscal 2025 and Q1 fiscal 2026 against 43% in fiscal 2020. The company has also been investing in research and development and capex involving value-added products such as thermal and coated labels, rigid packaging and speciality BOPET; apart from catering to the customised requirements of clients in various end-user industries. The rising share of speciality films should improve realisations, keeping profitability comparatively resilient as speciality margin is relatively less susceptible to cyclicality compared with commodity products. 

# **Comfortable financial risk profile supported by liquidity cushion** 

Networth remained healthy at Rs 1,448 crore as on March 31, 2025. CFL has planned an expansion capex of Rs 270 crore for coating plant and maintenance capex during fiscals 2026, which will be funded through debt and internal accrual in the ratio of 80:20. Improving profitability and gradual reduction in debt will help limit gearing to below 1 time despite addition of debt. Any new, large, debt-funded capex or acquisition could adversely impact on the financial risk profile and will remain monitorable. The company has a policy of maintaining a liquidity cushion equivalent to 1.5 years of debt obligation, which supports it during downturns. 

# **Weaknesses:** 

# **Susceptibility to volatility in raw material costs and demand-supply dynamics** 

The BOPP and BOPET industry, especially the commodity business, is cyclical. Product realisations have fluctuated in the past depending on the demand-supply gap. Also, the industry is highly fragmented, and players tend to add large capacities when prices improve, leading to a fall in product realisations. Profitability is also vulnerable to volatility in raw material prices, which account for 65-70% of sales. With the boost in demand during Covid, players had added capacities (~45% added by BOPET and ~25% by BOPP players between fiscals 2022 and 2023), which led to a fall in realisations and margins of packaging players, with BOPET players seeing a steeper fall compared with BOPP players. 

The profitability of CFL remains susceptible to such fluctuations, though partially shielded by value-added products, the prices of which are linked to raw material prices to some extent. Cosmo Group plans on increasing its share of value share in the medium term. 

# **Timely commissioning and ramp-up of newly added and planned capacities** 

CFL has commissioned its BOPP lines during Q1 fiscal 2026, at a cost of Rs 350 crore and was funded in a debt-to-equity mix of 80:20. 

The company is also adding capacity of coating and is expected to be commissioned in April 2026. The product is an important part of the value-added segment of CFL and timely commissioning and ramp up of the same will add value to the revenue and profitability profile of the group. 

# **<u>Liquidity: Strong</u>** 

The liquidity is supported by expected healthy cash accrual of Rs 350-450 crore in the next three fiscals. The fund-based limit of Rs  545 crore was utilised 25% on average over the 6 months through March 2025. Cash equivalent was sufficient at over Rs 370 crore as on March 31, 2025 (including encumbered cash of Rs 120 crores). Internal accrual, cash and equivalent, and unutilised bank limit will be adequate to meet debt obligation and working capital requirement. However, the company may contract debt to fund additional capex or working capital requirement. 

# **<u>Outlook: Stable</u>** 

The operating performance of CFL is likely to benefit from its increasing share of the speciality products segment and completion of its BOPP capex plant over the medium term. 

# **Environment, social and governance (ESG) profile** 

The ESG profile of CFL supports its strong credit risk profile. 

Flexible packaging manufacturers have a high impact on the environment, driven by high power consumption done during the manufacturing process. The sector also has a significant social impact because of its large workforce across its own operations and value chain partners, and due to its nature of operations affecting the local community and the health hazards involved. CFL has been focusing on mitigating its environmental and social risks. 

# Key ESG highlights: 

ESG disclosures of the company are evolving; and it is further strengthening the disclosures over the medium term. During the year under review, the company has adopted following key measures: 

i) 50%+ usage of renewable power as a source of energy in plants ii) 45% of water treated at Cosmo: planning towards zero liquid discharge iii) recycling of manufacturing waste for further film production (About 95%) 

Along with this, CFL has laid down specific environmental goals such as reduction of carbon emissions and fuel consumption, and was able to achieve 40% reduction in carbon emission. 

The governance structure is characterised by 70% independent director, effectiveness in board functioning and enhancing shareholder wealth, presence of investor grievance redressal mechanism and extensive financial 

disclosures. 

There is growing importance of ESG among investors and lenders. The commitment of CFL to the ESG principle will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence, given shareholding by foreign portfolio investors and access to both domestic and foreign capital markets. 

# **<u>Rating Sensitivity Factors</u>** 

# **Upward Factors** 

Significant increase in the scale of operations while sustaining healthy operating margin, driven by greater market share and product diversity 

Improvement in the financial risk profile with debt to EBIDTA improving to below 1.25x and sustenance of liquid surplus 

# **Downward Factors** 

Significant additional debt-funded capex (including any cost overrun), sharp moderation in profitability, delay in ramp up of capacity and higher-than expected capital outflow for unrelated diversifications, leading to weakening of the financial risk profile; for instance, a sustained increase in the debt to EBITDA  to 3 times Substantial moderation in market share and operating profitability. 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Established in 1981, CFL manufactures BOPP films for the domestic and international markets. Its products are used for packaging, lamination and labelling across various industries. The company entered the specialised BOPET segment in fiscal 2023. 

CFL is also involved in other businesses, such as pet care supplies and specialised chemicals. However, each of these constitutes less than 10% of the total revenue.
