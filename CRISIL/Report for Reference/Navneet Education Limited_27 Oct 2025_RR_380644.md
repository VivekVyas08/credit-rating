### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A1+’ rating on the short-term bank facility and commercial paper programme of Navneet Education Ltd (Navneet). 

The rating continues to reflect the company’s established market position in the educational books segment in Gujarat and Maharashtra, healthy presence in the stationery segment in the domestic and global markets, and comfortable financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by limited geographical diversity in the publishing business, seasonality of business operations and susceptibility to intense competition. 

Marginal growth witnessed in fiscal 2025 at Rs. 1781cr against Rs. 1748cr last year (2% growth) at the back of 3% growth in publication segment with no major curriculum change in the states of Gujarat, Maharashtra and stationery marking flattish growth on account of degrowth in domestic stationery segment nullifying the growth in stationery exports. That said, NEL has registered strong operational growth of 17% on a 3-year CAGR basis, while maintaining an operating margin of over 17%. 

Revenue growth is expected to be driven by change in curriculum which has already started for the states of Gujarat and Maharashtra and healthy growth expected in the domestic stationery business with stabilisation of paper prices and the company’s efforts to expand in the non-paper segment. While there has been no major impact seen so far on exports stationery business due to impact of US tariffs, the same shall remain monitorable. Navneet is exploring various alternatives for addressing the same. Customers prefer to source from multiple locations and vendors and as such no significant impact on demand is expected in the near term for fiscal 2026. The overall impact of tariffs over the medium term will remain a monitorable. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of Navneet and its subsidiaries: Navneet Futuretech Ltd (formerly, eSense Learning Pvt Ltd), Indiannica Learning Pvt Ltd, Navneet (HK) Ltd, Navneet Learning LLP and Navneet Tech Venture Pvt Ltd. The subsidiaries are strategically important to, and have high operational integration with, Navneet.

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Established market position and healthy growth prospects for the publishing business:** Navneet has a leading market position in the supplementary books segment in western India, primarily Maharashtra and Gujarat. Strong brand equity, an extensive distribution network, and superior content creation capability backed by a team of experienced authors and established tie-ups with schools support the market position. Changes in the school curriculum and initiatives to expand into new geographies will support scale of operations in the educational books segment over the medium term. 

**Healthy presence in the global stationery market:** Barring the impact of the pandemic in fiscal 2021, Navneet clocked strong revenue growth in the stationery segment, largely driven by exports. A preferred partner status from retailing giants such as Walmart, supports the stationery segment. The company is a dominant Indian player in the US market with clients 

such as Walmart, Target and Staples, and continues to diversify its presence in Europe and Africa to minimise dependence on any one key customer. 

**Healthy financial risk profile:** The balance sheet is healthy, driven by Crisil Ratings adjusted networth of Rs 1,827 crore as on March 31, 2025, versus Rs 1,238 crore previous fiscal, and working capital debt (Rs 125 crore) resulting in strong debt protection metrics with gearing at 0.07 time in fiscal 2025 against 0.2 times in the previous fiscal. Healthy cash-generating ability and prudent working capital management have led to no dependence on external borrowings. The total outside liabilities to tangible networth ratio was comfortable at 0.26 time as on March 31, 2025, while the interest coverage ratio remained healthy at 16.9 times in fiscal 2025 (14.4 times previous fiscal). The overall financial risk profile is likely to remain comfortable, supported by healthy accrual, nil long-term debt, and strong liquidity (including unutilised bank limit). 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Limited geographical diversity in the publishing business:** The mainstay publishing business (contributed 43% to the revenue in fiscal 2025) provides study materials for the state boards of Gujarat and Maharashtra, leading to revenue concentration and limitation of geographical diversity, which constrains the business risk profile. That said, the stationery segment for Navneet has shown steady growth in the domestic and international markets following pandemic-related disruptions. 

**Exposure to intense competition:** Intense competition from large companies such as ITC Ltd (‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+'), as well as from unorganised players limits pricing flexibility in the domestic market for stationery. In the publishing business, second-hand books have a prominent market share, which constrain sales when there is no change in syllabus or when there is a rise in paper prices. 

**Susceptibility of operating margin to fluctuations in raw material prices:** In the publication and stationery industry, cost of production and profit margin depend heavily on raw material prices, which account for a major portion of the production cost. Prices of key materials (primarily paper) have been volatile in the past few years. Moreover, the company may not be able to fully pass on increase in raw material prices to customers owing to intense competition. Further the imposition of tariff on stationary exports business could also impact the operating margins and will remain a monitorable. 

# **<u>Liquidity: Strong</u>** 

Liquidity is healthy with expected accrual of Rs 230-240 crore (from the usual business, excluding the extraordinary gain from divestment of its stake in K12 in fiscal 2025), which is sufficient to meet expected capex of Rs 120 crore annually over the medium term amid nil term debt obligation. The company continues to maintain healthy liquid surplus. The liquidity is also supported by access to working capital limit of Rs 472 crore, which was utilised 6% on average in the 12 months through August 2025, providing cushion to meet any exigencies. With gearing of 0.07 time as on March 31, 2025, there is sufficient headroom to raise debt for capex or liquidity, if required. 

# **<u>ESG Profile</u>** 

Crisil Ratings believes the ESG profile of Navneet supports its adequate credit risk profile. 

# **Key ESG highlights** 

In its efforts to reduce reliance on fossil fuels, the company has installed 724 kilowatt peak (kWp) and wind power plants with a capacity of 4.8MW, offsetting close to 60% of Scope 2 emissions. 

- Initiatives to reduce the carbon footprint have led to a significant reduction in carbon dioxide emissions, resulting in overall carbon offset rate of 51%. 

- In fiscal 2025, of the total paper procured as raw material, 53% was recycled/reused. 

- To decrease plastic waste, water-based ink carboys are lined with polybags for reuse by the manufacturer, significantly reducing plastic consumption. Moving away from lower-micron plastics (BOPP, PP bags, and shrink film) to highermicron alternatives minimises environmental risks associated with plastic usage. 

- Hazardous materials generated are disposed through government-authorised vendors, ensuring strict compliance with regulatory requirements and guaranteeing the safe and responsible handling of hazardous waste, including e-waste. Zero liquid discharge mechanism is implemented at all the manufacturing sites, mandated by the State Pollution Control Board. Also, water treated at the sewage treatment plants and the effluent treatment plants is reused for gardening. Navneet has implemented the Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) system across all its plants and offices, with its head office and two major sites holding ISO 45001 certification for their adherence to industry-leading safety standards. Navneet has an adequate governance structure with the presence of an investor grievance redressal mechanism, and adequate disclosures but board consists of 4 independent directors out of total 11 board members. 

There is growing importance of ESG among investors and lenders. Navneet’s continued commitment to ESG principles will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence.

- Substantial increase in working capital requirement or any large, debt-funded investments leading to gearing remaining above 1.50 times 

Higher-than-expected investment in group or associate entities impacting Navneet’s financial risk profile 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Navneet was incorporated as Bookwing Publication (India) Ltd in 1959 by Gala family and was renamed Navneet Publications (India) Ltd in 1992 and got its present name in August 2013. Publications are sold under the Navneet, Vikas and Gala brands. The product portfolio additionally includes paper-based and non-paper-based stationery. The company also provides e-learning services in Gujarat and Maharashtra through its wholly owned subsidiary, Navneet Futuretech Ltd. 

Navneet has collaborated with around 1,900 schools to provide digital teaching solutions in classrooms. Products such as e- learning tablets, cloud-based interactive exams and application-based audio visuals have been identified as key growth areas. The company is focusing on business-to-business products such as top class, which target educational institutions. Navneet recently divested its 20.25% stake in K12, reducing it to 14.92%. Thereby, the latter has ceased to be an associate company of Navneet.
