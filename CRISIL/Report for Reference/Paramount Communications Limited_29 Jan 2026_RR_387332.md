### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ratings on the bank facilities of Paramount Communications Ltd (PCL; part of the Paramount group) and subsequently **withdrawn** the ratings at the company’s request and on receiving no-objection certificate from the lender. This is in line with the Crisil Ratings withdrawal policy. 

The rating reflects the group's longstanding presence in the cable and wires industry, supported by extensive experience of its promoters, diversified product profile and end user industry, and its healthy financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by working capital-intensive operations and exposure to intense competition. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

To arrive at its ratings, Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of PCL, along with wholly owned subsidiaries, i.e., Paramount Holdings Ltd (PHL), AEI Power Cables Ltd (APCL) and Valens Technologies Pvt Ltd (VTPL). These entities are together referred to as the Paramount group. However, PHL and APCL are currently non-operational.

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Longstanding market presence in the cable and wire industry, supported by the extensive experience of the promoters:** Established in 1955, the Paramount group has a longstanding presence in the cable and wire manufacturing industry. This has helped the promoters, Mr Sanjay Aggarwal and Mr Sandeep Agarwal, to gain strong understanding of market dynamics and establish healthy relationships with suppliers and customers. The company caters to large players such as Adani, Godrej, Larsen & Toubro Ltd and Ambuja, in the domestic market and derives 25-35% of its revenue from exports. It is present in over 25 countries, with USA being a key market. The pan-India network of 198 distributors, 154 dealers and 808 retailers enable the company to have a vast reach and cater to a diverse customer base across India. With growing demand and customer base, the company has achieved revenue of Rs 1,575 crore in fiscal 2025, as compared to Rs 1071 crore in fiscal 2024, and Rs 879 crore for the first half months of fiscal 2026. 

**Diversified product portfolio and end-user industry base:** The Paramount group manufactures a wide range of cables and wires, including power cables, railway cables, telecom cables, special cables, domestic wires and cables, and others. Power cables accounts for 65-70% of total revenue, followed by railway cables. The product profile includes high- and lowvoltage power cables, optical fiber cables, other telecom cables, railway cables, house wires, specialised cables, instrumentation and data cables, and fire survival cables. The company caters to customers across segments such as power, telecom, railways, and domestic wires, among others. Diversity in product portfolio and customer base should help enhance the scale of operations over the medium term. 

**Healthy financial risk profile:** Networth stood at Rs 717 crore as on March 31, 2025, and is expected to improve further, with steady accretion to reserves. Though the reliance on external debt has increased, given lower cash flows from operations amid the decline in operating margins, the capital structure continues to remain comfortable with total outside liabilities to adjusted networth (TOL/ANW) and gearing ratio expected to remain below 0.5 times over the medium term. Debt protection metrics is expected to deteriorate to decline in operating margins, however, they will continue to remain comfortable due to moderate leverage. Interest coverage ratio has deteriorated to 7.2 times in first half of fiscal 2026 from 

12 times in fiscal 2025 and is further expected to deteriorate however it would continue to remain above 3 times over the medium term. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Exposure to intense competition and volatility in operating margins:** The presence of several organised and unorganised players in the wire and cable industry, has led to intense competition, which restrains the pricing power and operating margins of the players. Profitability also remains vulnerable to any sharp and sudden fluctuations in commodity prices. 

The operating margins of the company were further affected in the first half of fiscal 2026 due to the tariff imposed by US to India, and the company is facing competition with various other countries including Vietnam, Cambodia, South Korea, Turkey, Ecuador etc given the tariff imposition on them is lower at 15%. The operating margins declined to 3.2% in the first half of fiscal 2026 from 8.2% in FY25. The margins are expected to remain under pressure over the medium term amid the uncertainty in regards to the tariff imposition by US and continued intense competition in the industry. 

**Large working capital requirement:** Gross current assets were high at 141 days as on March 31, 2025, driven by large receivables and inventory of 56 and 78 days, respectively. The company needs to extend a long credit period to its customers, which keeps receivables high in the range of 75-95 days. To ensure timely fulfilment of customer requirements, the group may continue to hold large inventory of 70-90 days over the medium term. Overall working capital cycle is supported through creditors of 35-45 days and timely realisation of debtors in the absence of working capital limits. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Stretched 

The company is recently sanctioned with fund based working capital limits and has an average utilization of 85% over the past three months through November 2025, however the utilization was over 95% till October 2025. The utilisation levels eased out in November with enhancement in banking lines, however, sustenance of sufficient cushion amid high working capital requirement remains a monitorable. Cash accruals are expected to be tightly matched against repayment obligations in the second half of fiscal 2026. Current ratio was healthy at 3.2 times as on March 31, 2025. Cash and bank balance of around Rs 76 crore as on September 30th 2025, is majorly lien marked. Low gearing and moderate networth offer financial flexibility to withstand adverse conditions or downturn in the business. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Negative 

Crisil Ratings believe the credit profile of the company would continue to remain under pressure amid the tariff impositions by USA on India leading to pressure on operating margins. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Improvement in the operating margins above 4.5% while maintaining the scale of operations leading to higher-than expected net cash accrual 

Effective management of working capital cycle and sustained financial risk profile, leading to improved liquidity 

# **Downward factors** 

Continued low margins below 2% or decline in revenues leading to lower cash accruals Stretch in the working capital requirement weakening the financial or liquidity profile 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

PCL was incorporated in September 1994. PCL, along with its subsidiaries, manufactures various types of cables and wires such as power cables, railway cables, telecom cables, special cables, domestic wires and cables and markets its products under the ‘Paramount’ brand. Manufacturing facilities are located in Khushkhera (Rajasthan) and Dharuhera (Haryana). PCL is listed on the National Stock Exchange of India (NSE) and Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE). Operations are managed by Mr Sanjay Aggarwal (Chairman & CEO) and Mr Sandeep Aggarwal (Managing Director).
