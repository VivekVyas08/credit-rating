### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AAA/Stable’ rating on the long-term bank facilities of Avenue Supermarts Ltd (ASL). 

The rating continues to reflect the company’s strong presence in the organised food and grocery (F&G) retail segment, driven by its expanding geographical footprint, efficient operations and robust financial risk profile. The company's growth initiatives, including its plans to add 10-15% new stores annually are expected to further enhance its market presence. However, timely ramp-up of those stores will remain monitorable. Additionally, the company's diversified revenue streams, especially the food and grocery segment, will help navigate any potential downturns in discretionary spending during times of high inflation. While the company is not immune to external factors such as regulatory changes, competition and inflationary pressures, its strong operating efficiency and calibrated approach to expansion mitigate these risks. The company's e-commerce business, operating in 24 cities, is currently incurring losses and the management's prudent expansion plan will help minimise the risk. 

On a consolidated basis, revenue registered on-year growth of 17% to Rs 59,363 crore in fiscal 2025 from Rs 50,793 crore, driven by scale up of stores opened over the past couple of years, new store additions and healthy like to like growth of 8.4%. The company has been consistently increasing its geographical footprint and, as on June 30, 2025, it had 424 stores spread across 14 states and union territories as against 371 stores as on June 30, 2024. It is expected to add 45-55 stores per annum, thereby deepening its penetration in the organised F&G retail segment. During the first quarter of fiscal 2026, the company achieved revenue of Rs 16,360 crore witnessing 16% growth on year with the operating margin at 7.9% as against 8.7% during the corresponding period of previous fiscal attributable to change in product mix. 

In fiscal 2025, earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation (Ebitda) stood at Rs 4,511 crore (7.6% margin) against Rs 4,118 crore (8.1% margin) in the previous fiscal. The operating margin moderated slightly on-year owing to increasing employee expenses, shift in product mix because of low contribution of the high-margin accruing general merchandise and apparel business as well as increase in wages and warehousing costs. However, improving operating leverage will continue to support the overall operating margin over the medium term. Consolidated profitability is expected to remain at similar level, backed by fast breakeven of stores, superior per-store revenue compared with peers, stable proportion of non-F&G sales, high inventory turnover as well as maintenance of gross margin at 15% despite increase in competitive intensity. 

The financial risk profile shall remain robust over the medium term, driven by strong tangible networth and a debt-free balance sheet. Liquidity was also strong with cash and bank balance of ~Rs 720 crore as on March 31, 2025. Strong annual cash accrual of over Rs 4,000 crore should be sufficient to meet annual capital expenditure (capex) requirement of Rs 3,200-4,000 crore.

**<u>Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description</u> Strengths:** 

**Strong position in the organised retail segment:** Market position is reinforced by steady same-store growth (barring pandemic-hit fiscal 2021), retail productivity and short gestation for new stores. The company operates 424 stores (as on June 30,2025) under the DMart brand. 

Strong procurement abilities and low-priced products, along with high-cost control, will lead to great footfall. This results in large inventory turnover and revenue per square foot (sq ft) and translates into industry-leading retail store productivity. Aggregate revenue per sq ft of Rs 33,896 in fiscal 2025 (Rs 32,941 in fiscal 2024) was higher than most retailers in the same segment. 

Operations are largely concentrated in west and south India with the company now foraying into northern India. Expected large cluster-focused store addition over the next three fiscals will help diversify geographical reach. Track record of outpacing peers in growth, strong merchandising and compelling value proposition and benefits of economies of scale will strengthen market share over the medium term. 

**Robust financial risk profile and strong liquidity:** Networth was sizeable at Rs 21,319 crore as on March 31, 2025, while strong annual cash generation continued despite steady store addition. The company maintained healthy operating metrics while adding stores, and maintained debt-free balance sheet as on March 31, 2025. 

Strong annual cash generation of over Rs 4,000 crore is expected to be sufficient for capex, resulting in low dependence on external borrowings. The company incurred capex of over Rs 3,430 crore in fiscal 2025 and increased retail space to over 17.2 million sq ft as on March 31, 2025, from 15.1 million sq ft in the previous fiscal. 

# **Weakness:** 

**Susceptibility to increasing competition:** Organised penetration in the F&G segment currently remains limited (~6%). Also, the organised F&G space faces intense competition from unorganised players, particularly from unorganised brick and mortar kirana stores. Competitive intensity is also increasing because of higher focus of e-retailers in the F&G space. While ASL is a small player in the online F&G space, other players such as Blinkit, BigBasket and Zepto, and other peers such as JioMart are registering aggressive growth. Though e-retail is a small proportion of overall rating, it will remain monitorable over the medium term. 

# **<u>Liquidity: Superior</u>** 

Liquidity is backed by healthy cash accrual, nil debt and cash and equivalent of ~Rs 720 crore as on March 31, 2025. Utilisation of fund-based working capital limit of Rs 445 crore was negligible for the 12 months through June 2025. The capex to be incurred for opening stores over the next three fiscals is expected to be funded through internal accrual, besides healthy cash and equivalent. 

# **ESG profile** 

The environment, social, and governance (ESG) profile of ASL supports its credit risk profile. The retail sector has low environmental impact, primarily in the form of low emissions and water consumption and increasing focus on the use of sustainable packaging. The sector has moderate social impact because of its direct bearing on the health and wellbeing of its workers and customers. 

# **ASL key ESG highlights:** 

- In fiscal 2025, the company’s specific scope 1 and 2 emissions stood at 12,800 tCO2e per million sq ft, which was a ~5% increase from fiscal 2024. Similarly, specific energy consumption stood at 20,680 MWh per million sq ft, which was a ~4% increase from fiscal 2024. 

- As part of their commitment for clean energy, the company commissioned 28 new solar plants with capacity of 5.2 MW in fiscal 2025, adding to a cumulative capacity of 41.2 MW as on March 31, 2025. Currently, nearly 23.4% of their total energy requirement is met through solar energy. 

- About 61% of the stores have obtained green building certificates from various certifying agencies. 

- In fiscal 2025, the company reported that nearly 98% of their waste generated is being recycled. The company also recycled nearly 34% of total water withdrawn in the year, supported by presence of sewage treatment plants at 217 stores (of 410 as on March 31, 2025). 

- Avenue reported a gender diversity of ~38% among permanent employees and attrition rate of ~11%, which was better than its peers’ average. 

- Its governance profile is characterised by 44% of board being independent,33% women in board of directors, split in chairman and CEO positions and extensive financial disclosures 

There is growing importance of ESG among investors and lenders. The commitment of ASL to ESG will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence and access to capital markets 

# **<u>Outlook: Stable</u>** 

The credit risk profile of ASL will continue to benefit from its business risk profile, supported by steady store expansion that will benefit revenue and also ensure healthy operating profitability. The company is also expected to sustain its debt metrics at superior level with strong annual cash generation and healthy financial flexibility, while pursuing organic growth. 

**<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u> Downward factors:** 

Significant decline in revenue and operating margin because of large gestation losses from new stores, impacting overall cash generation 

Larger-than-expected debt-funded capex, weakening gearing to above 0.20 time on sustained basis 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

ASL is engaged in the organised retail business through its DMart chain of stores. The company was incorporated in 2000 and is promoted by Radhakishan Damani, an equity market investor. Ignatius Navil Noronha is the chief executive officer and managing director. As of June 2025, ASL had 424 hypermarket stores across 14 states and union territories. 

Align Retail Trades Private Ltd procures grocery items (including pulses, rice, wheat) from local agricultural produce market committees and packages and supplies them to ASL. Avenue Food Plaza Private Ltd runs fast food counters outside the DMart stores. Avenue Ecommerce Ltd e-retails F&G and operates in certain regions of Mumbai and a few other cities.
