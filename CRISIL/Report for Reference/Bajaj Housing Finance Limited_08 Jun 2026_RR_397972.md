### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has assigned its **‘Crisil AAA/Stable’** rating to the Rs 10,000 crore non-convertible debentures of Bajaj Housing Finance Limited (BHFL) and has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the bank facilities and other debt instruments. 

Crisil Ratings has also **withdrawn** its rating on the Rs 10,340 crore of non-convertible debentures (see the Annexure - Details of rating withdrawn' for details) on receipt of independent confirmation that these instruments are fully redeemed. The withdrawal is in line with its Crisil Ratings rating withdrawal policy. 

The ratings factor in expectation of strong financial, operational and managerial support for BHFL from its parent, Bajaj Finance Ltd (BFL: rated 'Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’) both on an ongoing basis, and in the event of distress. The expectation reflects the strategic importance of BHFL to BFL and the strong moral obligation of the latter to support the former. The rating also continues to consider the healthy market position in the mortgage loans as well as healthy capitalisation metrics and comfortable earnings profile. These strengths are partially offset by limited seasoning of the loan portfolio. While the company continues to maintain range-bound and comfortable asset quality metrics, the ability to continue maintaining the asset quality at healthier level amid seasoning of the portfolio will remain monitorable. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

For arriving at the ratings, Crisil Ratings has evaluated the standalone business and financial risk profiles of BHFL and has factored in expectation of support that BHFL is expected to receive from BFL , both on an ongoing basis, and in the event of distress given its high strategic importance to BFL as the company’s housing finance arm and the strong moral obligation of the parent to support the same. 

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

### **Expectation of strong support from BFL** 

BHFL’s parent, BFL, is a subsidiary of Bajaj Finserv Ltd, the financial services arm of the Bajaj group. In September 2024, BHFL got listed to meet the mandatory listing conditions pursuant to BHFL’s classification as an Upper Layer NBFC (nonbanking financial company). Thereafter, the stake of BHFL in the company reduced to 88.75% as on September 30, 2024, from 100% previously; and holds 86.70% stake as on March 31, 2026. 

BFL is one of the largest retail asset financiers NBFCs in India. It has a diversified product suite, comprising key businesses such as vehicle loans (two- and three-wheelers manufactured by Bajaj Auto Ltd), consumer durable loans, personal loans, mortgage loans, small business loans, and loans against securities, commercial finance, and rural finance. Crisil Ratings believes BHFL is strategically important to BFL, being its vehicle for growing the mortgage business, which is among the key focus areas for the Bajaj group. BFL plans to continue holding a majority stake and is willing to continue to infuse capital, after having infused over ~Rs 9,000 crore from fiscal 2017 till date, to support growth requirements over the medium term. The shared name also enhances BFL’s moral obligation to support BHFL. 

# **Healthy market position in mortgage loans** 

BHFL has been in operation since 2017 and within a short period of its operations, BHFL has emerged as one of the largest housing finance companies in India, with assets under management (AUM) of Rs 1,40,706 crore as on March 31, 2026, registering compounded annual growth rate (CAGR) of ~58% over fiscals 2018–2026 to reach AUM of Rs 1,40,706 crore as on March 31, 2026. Growth has been supported by the company’s pan-India presence. 

As on March 31, 2026, BHFL’s loan book is well diversified. Home loans constituted around 54%, lease rental discounting was 22%, developer financing was 12%, loan against property was 11% and the balance was unsecured loans. The management has laid down prudent underwriting practices and focuses on existing customers having exposure to other banks while maintaining geographical diversification in its loan book. 

According to management, BHFL expects to broadly maintain its existing portfolio mix over the medium term, with prime home loans remaining the anchor segment. Moreover, the company intends to expand its presence in near-prime and affordable housing, which currently forms around 12% of the home loan portfolio. 

# **Healthy capitalisation** 

Capitalisation metrics continue to be healthy, with net worth and gearing of Rs 22,523 crore and 5.4 times, respectively, as on March 31, 2026 (Rs 19,947 crore and 4.9 times, respectively, as on March 31, 2025), supported by regular capital infusion by the parent and healthy internal accrual. Overall capital adequacy ratio also remained comfortable at 22.5% as on March 31, 2026. 

BFL has consistently infused equity, with the most recent infusion of Rs 2,000 crore in April 2024. Prior infusions include Rs 2,500 crore in fiscal 2023, Rs 1,333 crore in fiscal 2020, Rs 2,000 crore in fiscal 2019, and Rs 1,200 crore in February 2018. Furthermore, during fiscal 2025, the company completed an initial public offering (IPO), raising Rs 3,560 crore, of which Rs 2,000 crore was through fresh equity infusion. Besides, the company continues to generate healthy accrual, with profit after tax of Rs 2,560 crore in fiscal 2026 as against Rs 2,163 crore in fiscal 2025 and Rs 1,731 crore in fiscal 2024. Thus, Crisil Ratings expects capitalisation to remain healthy supported by steady improvement in internal accrual and demonstrated ability to raise capital. 

# **Comfortable earnings profile** 

BHFL’s profitability has remained stable, with return on managed assets (RoMA) at 2.0% in fiscals 2026 and 2025, compared with 2.1% in fiscal 2024. The earnings profile has been supported by stable net interest margin (NIM) of 3.4% in fiscal 2026 and 2025, low credit costs and improving operating efficiency. 

However, NIMs may moderate marginally over the near term because of competitive pressure on lending yields and some increase in cost of funds, especially from tighter pricing in market borrowings. Nonetheless, the impact is expected to be partly offset by operating leverage benefits and BHFL’s calibrated focus on higher-yielding segments such as near-prime and affordable housing. 

Overall, BHFL’s ability to maintain NIMs, while maintaining stable credit cost and operating expenses, will remain monitorable for sustenance of comfortable earnings profile. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Comfortable asset quality metrics albeit sustenance remains to be seen given limited seasoning of the portfolio** 

As BFL’s vehicle facilitating growth in the mortgage business, BHFL has attained significant size and scale, as reflected in its share within overall AUM for the Bajaj group (BFL, at a consolidated level). Consequently, the share of BHFL in the overall Bajaj Finance group’s AUM stood at ~28% as on March 31, 2026. 

Asset quality metrics remain comfortable, with overall gross non-performing assets at 0.27% as on March 31, 2026, compared with 0.29% as on March 31, 2025. Credit costs have also remained consistently low at 0.1% in fiscals 2026, 2025 and 2024. However, the portfolio has grown rapidly, with AUM registering a CAGR of around 27% over the past three years, and a meaningful portion of the book remains relatively under-seasoned. 

The company’s wholesale exposure, comprising developer finance and lease rental discounting, is sizeable at around 34% of the overall portfolio. While these segments have demonstrated healthy performance so far, their ability to sustain asset quality as the portfolio seasons will remain monitorable. 

Overall, BHFL has demonstrated strong asset quality management. However, sustenance of performance while maintaining stable credit costs will remain monitorable as the rapidly grown book seasons, particularly given the company’s increasing focus on relatively higher-risk segments such as near-prime and affordable housing loans. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Superior 

The asset liability maturity (ALM) profile of BHFL remains comfortable as of March 31, 2026. There were no cumulative mismatches across buckets (including the committed lines of credit). As on April 30, 2026, the company had unencumbered cash and equivalents of Rs 4,537 crore, with Rs 2,423 crore of cash credit/working capital demand loan lines. This is sufficient to meet debt obligations for over next one month. Additionally, the company had committed credit lines from bank, including national housing bank (NHB) of Rs 6,200 crore as on April 30, 2026. Furthermore, liquidity is also supported by the expectation of need-based support from the parent. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes BHFL will continue to maintain comfortable capitalisation and will benefit from strong financial, managerial and operational support from BFL over the medium term.
