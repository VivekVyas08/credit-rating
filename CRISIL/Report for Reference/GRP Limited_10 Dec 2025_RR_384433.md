### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has downgraded its ratings on the bank loan facilities of GRP Limited (GRP) to **'Crisil BBB+/Stable/Crisil A2’** from 'Crisil A-/Stable/Crisil A2+'. 

The downgrade factors in weakening of the business and financial risk profile of the company on account of weaker than expected operating performance and debt protection metrics. 

Revenues in H1FY26 remained flat YoY at Rs. 256 crores (vs Rs. 258 crores in H1FY25) with EPR credit sales amounting to ~Rs. 9 crores (vs Rs. 6 crores in H1FY25). However, revenues were lower than expected on account of volumes degrowing by 4% owing to impact of US tariffs and underperformance in the non-reclaim rubber business. These headwinds were slightly offset by strong domestic sales and higher EPR credit sales. Revenue may rise by low single digits in fiscal 2026 owing to a weak H1FY26 and expected continuance of the impact of US tariffs on GRP’s volume growth. The modest growth will be on account of some recovery in volumes in H2FY26 and higher EPR credit sales. Thereafter, a growth of 9-11% is expected, with enhancement in capacities, normalization of impact of global consumption, improving production efficiency and maturing EPR regime. 

The overall earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortisation margin declined to 8.02% in H1FY26 from 8.75% in H1FY25. The decline in margins are on account of the reduction in volumes as well as impact on realization owing to the US tariffs along with the further increase in the prices of butyl rubber prices (which is a key raw material). Margins are expected to remain 8-10% in FY26 owing to continued high raw material costs and the impact of US tariffs. EBITDA margins are expected to gradually improve to 9-11% in the medium term driven by enhanced capacities through debottlenecking activities in the reclaim rubber segment, additional volumes from the ongoing capex on green energy and higher sales of EPR credits to tire companies. 

The financial risk profile of the company remains adequate with net worth at 191 crores in FY25 as compared to Rs. 167 crores in FY24. Debt metrics, however, weakened in H1FY26 with debt increasing to ~Rs. 205 crores from Rs. 146 crores in March 2025 on account of increased long-term debt raised for the ~Rs. 250 crores capex towards the crumb rubber and pyrolysis facility in Solapur and increase in short-term debt to fund capex related needs as well as the working capital of the company. The company has taken an External Commercial Borrowing (ECB) loan which has a sanctioned limit of EUR 12 million (~Rs. 126 crores) out of which ~Rs. 75 crores have been drawn down as of September 2025. Net cash accruals of Rs. 35-50 crores over the medium term will be sufficient to cover the yearly debt obligations of Rs. 5-10 crores till FY27 and Rs. 25-30 crores in FY28. Apart from the crumb rubber facility which was commissioned in FY25, the pyrolysis plant has also commissioned in early fiscal 2026. The completion of the Phase 1b has been delayed by 1-2 months and is expected to be commissioned by May or June 2026. This delay will have some cost overruns. Liquidity remained modest with cash balance at ~Rs. 24 crores as of September 2025. Utilization was high at 90% during past 12 months ended September 2025. The financial risk profile is likely to remain adequate even with the expected deterioration of debt metrics, with gearing below 1.2 times over the medium term with interest coverage to remain above 3 times. 

The ratings continue to reflect the established market position of GRP in the reclaimed rubber industry, beneficiary of the EPR norms for tire manufactures and importers and adequate financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by susceptibility to fluctuations in raw material prices and exposure to tire industry concentration in revenue.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Established market position in the reclaimed rubber industry** 

GRP is one of the top three manufacturers of reclaimed rubber globally and the largest in India. The company has built healthy relationships with domestic and international tire manufacturers and distributors. Exports to the US and Europe contributed 60% of the total export revenue in fiscal 2025 and H1FY26. Domestic customers include several large and prominent original equipment manufacturers. Increase in composition of reclaimed rubber in tires manufactured by global players should augur well for GRP. Allied businesses such as engineering plastics (EP), polymer composites (PC) and custom die forms (CDF) have contributed to 12% of total revenue in fiscal 2025 and in H1FY26. Ramp-up in PC and EP capacities on the back of healthy demand and the recent capex should support further diversification in the revenue profile. 

# **Beneficiary of inclusion of tire manufacturers and importers under the EPR norms** 

The company is set to benefit from the regulations that include tire manufacturers in the EPR framework of the government. Since the mechanism for sale of credit has been put in place in fiscal 2024, GRP has sold the remaining credits generated for fiscal 2023 and fiscal 2024, in fiscal 2025. In H1FY26, GRP sold EPR credits of Rs. 8.91 crores. The mechanism for awarding and consequential selling of the same is more established now but the formulae may still undergo a change as deemed necessary by the regulatory authorities. 

# **Adequate financial risk profile** 

The financial risk profile has been adequate. Networth stood at Rs 191 crore in fiscal 2025, increasing from Rs 167 crore in fiscal 2024, with steady accretion to reserve. Debt as of Sep-25 stood at ~Rs. 205 crores, of which a majority is short-term working capital debt. Although debt protection metrics have weakened, they remain adequate, with interest coverage ratio of over 3 times for H1FY26 and gearing of 1.12 time as on September 30, 2025. The company has taken an External Commercial Borrowing (ECB) loan which has a sanctioned limit of EUR 12 million (~Rs. 126 crores) out of which ~Rs. 75 crores have been drawn down till September 2025. The loan will be fully drawn down by FY27. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Susceptibility to fluctuations in raw material prices** 

Raw material cost accounts for around half of the operating income. End-of-life rubber tire, the input, is procured from an extensive chain of suppliers. Changes in raw material prices could impact operating margin, which has fluctuated between 6% and 13% over the five fiscals through March 2025. Prices of butyl rubber which is a key raw material for the company saw an sharp increase in fiscal 2025 and H1FY26 which led to an impact in the gross margins. 

# **High concentration of revenue from the tire industry** 

GRP is highly dependent on the performance of the tire industry, which accounts for 65-70% of its revenue. While the company has diversified into multiple segments such as EP, PC and CDF, the extent of revenue contribution from these remains critical. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Adequate 

Net cash accrual stood at Rs 47 crore in fiscal 2025, against debt obligation of Rs 10-12 crore. Net cash accrual is projected at Rs 35-50 crore per fiscal, sufficient to meet yearly debt obligations of Rs. 5-10 crores till FY27 and Rs. 25-30 crores in FY28. Capex requirement of ~Rs.60-80 crores per annum over the medium term will be funded by debt and internal accruals. Bank limit utilisation was around 90% during past 12 months ended September 2025. Liquidity stood at ~Rs 24 crores as on September 30, 2025. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Rating believes that GRP will benefit from the cost optimization measures through debottlenecking along with the enhancement of capacities though its capex plans which will enable GRP to counter the business slowdown along with maintaining a healthy financial risk profile driven by strong gearing and debt protection metrics. 

# **<u>Rating Sensitivity Factors</u>** 

# **Upward Factors** 

Sustained double-digit revenue growth backed by increase in volume while maintaining operating profitability above 1315% 

Improvement in the financial risk profile 

# **Downward Factors** 

Sustained decline in revenue and operating profitability increasing leverage levels deteriorating the interest coverage to below 2.5 times 

Increase in working capital debt or large, debt-funded capex. 

**<u>About the Company</u>** 

Established by Mr Rajendra V Gandhi in 1974, GRP manufactures reclaimed rubber (recycled rubber) from end-of-life tires and tubes. Having commenced operations as a tire recycling company, GRP has transformed into a sustainable materials company over the years. It also separates nylon from tires to produce raw material for sale to engineering plastic component manufacturers for automotive and electrical applications. The residue rubber from the above businesses is used by yet another business to blend with recycled plastic waste and produce composite material, which replaces wood and concrete. 

The company is a leader in the domestic tire recycling industry, and among the top five manufacturers globally. GRP has total installed capacity of 84,200 tonne per annum across all business segments. 

In the first six months of fiscal 2026, GRP achieved profit after tax of Rs 4 crore on operating income of Rs 256 crore, as against Rs 7 crore and Rs 258 crore, respectively, in the corresponding period of the previous fiscal.
