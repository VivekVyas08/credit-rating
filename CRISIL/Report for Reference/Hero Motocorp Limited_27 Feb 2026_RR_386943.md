#### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its 'Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+' ratings on the debt programmes and bank facilities of Hero MotoCorp Ltd (HMCL). 

The ratings continue to reflect the strong business risk profile of HMCL supported by its leading position in the domestic twowheeler market in India, and the company’s robust financial risk profile owing to strong networth, negligible debt and substantial cash surplus. These strengths are partially offset by exposure to intense competition, rising raw material prices, modest presence in the premium motorcycles and low geographic diversity. 

Revenue grew 11.2% on-year to Rs 34,433 crore in the first nine months of fiscal 2026 on the back of ~6.5% volume growth and ~4.5% increase in realisation. The volume growth was led by a strong festive season on the back of Goods and Services Tax (GST) rate cuts and new model launches across all categories. The domestic scooter volume rose ~45% onyear, while motorcycle volume grew 0.5% with higher growth expected in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026. Further, exports saw volume growth of 48.2% on-year albeit on a lower base. The export growth being broad based was primarily due to higher demand in key markets such as Bangladesh, Nepal, Sri Lanka and Columbia. Realisations increased because of factors such as price hikes, improved sales mix and higher revenue contribution of spare parts. 

The operating margin improved to ~14.9% in the first nine months of fiscal 2026 from 14.6% in the corresponding period of the previous fiscal on the back of consistent efforts to reduce cost, benefits of operating leverage on account of higher volume and increasing average selling prices (though partially offset by rising input prices). During fiscal 2025, HMCL reported on-year revenue growth of 8.3% to Rs 40,923 crore and operating margin of 14.5% driven by higher volume and realisation. 

Revenue is expected to grow at 5-7% over the medium term supported by a gradual recovery in rural demand, new model launches and continued ramp-up in exports. Additionally, growth will likely be fueled by an uptick in the executive, premium and ultra-premium segments, as well as strong demand in the scooter segment, which is poised to outperform the industry from a lower base. The expected pick-up in electric vehicle (EV) demand and regular price hikes across segments, resulting in higher realisation, will also contribute to the growth momentum. 

The operating margin is expected to remain healthy at 14-15% over the medium term, driven by the benefits of operating leverage, improving sales mix, continued premiumisation as well as optimisation of loss in the EV segment with gradual scale up moderated by inflation in raw material cost and depreciation of the Indian rupee. 

HMCL remains invested in its associate companies Hero FinCorp Ltd (HFCL; ‘Crisil AA+/PPMLD AA+/Crisil AA/Stable/Crisil A1+’) and Ather Energy Pvt Ltd (Ather). The investments are of strategic importance. Notably, Ather's shares were listed in 

May 2025, with HMCL holding ~30.14% stake on a fully diluted basis as of December 2025. HMCL has expanded its portfolio with a strategic investment in Euler Motors Pvt Ltd (Euler Motors), acquiring 34.1% stake for Rs 510 crore at the start of fiscal 2026, making it an associate company. HMCL plans to further invest up to Rs 275 crore at the start of fiscal 2027, which will increase its stake in Euler Motors. This investment enables HMCL to strengthen and diversify its emerging mobility portfolio, particularly in the rapidly growing electric three and four-wheeler market, while unlocking adjacent business opportunities. 

The financial risk profile remains strong backed by robust networth, negligible debt, and strong debt protection metrics and cash reserve. Adjusted networth was Rs 15,464 crore as on March 31, 2025, and interest coverage stood at 96.7 times in fiscal 2025. Over the medium term, the interest coverage is expected to remain strong. The financial risk profile is strengthened by strong cash reserve of Rs 8,824 crore as on March 31, 2025 (~Rs 13,286 crore as on December 31, 2025) and unutilised bank limit. 

# **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of HMCL, its wholly owned subsidiaries in the Netherlands and Bangladesh, and HMC-MM Auto Ltd (‘Crisil A1+’). The ratings factor in support to HFCL and adjustments have been made to the networth accordingly in line with the capital allocation criteria.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Leading position in the two-wheeler market in India** 

The market share of HMCL in the two-wheeler industry dipped to ~24% for the nine months through December 2025 from ~25% a year earlier. Despite the decline in overall market share, the company maintains a dominant position in the entry level motorcycle segment (75cc to 110cc). The market position is backed by strong brand appeal, wide dealer distribution and service network. 

Within the electric two-wheeler segment, HMCL had ~10.8% market share during the third quarter of fiscal 2026, up from 6% in the corresponding quarter of the previous fiscal led by the recently introduced VIDA VX2 portfolio. In the EV segment, improving average selling price and unit economics have reduced losses at the earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (Ebitda) level. One VIDA model has been approved under the Production Linked Incentive scheme, with additional models under development to increase domestic value addition and eligibility. 

HMCL will continue to focus on improving market share in the executive and premium motorcycle segments. In fiscal 2025, the company launched several new models or upgraded versions of existing models such as Xtreme 250R and the XPulse 210, Glamour X, cosmetic refreshes in Xtreme 125R with a dual channel ABS variant, etc. Market share in the above 200cc motorcycle segment remained ~1.5% for the nine months through December 2025 with plans to launch new products within the premium category to improve market share. HMCL’s premium network has now expanded to 106 exclusive stores. 

# **Robust financial risk profile** 

The financial risk profile remains strong owing to negligible debt, large networth and strong cash reserve. The company maintains a negative net working capital cycle, resulting in limited working capital requirement. Additionally, capital expenditure (capex) is largely towards maintenance. As a result, the company’s balance sheet is virtually debt free except for working capital borrowings at the subsidiary level. Furthermore, internal cash accrual is more than sufficient to cover fixed obligation, thereby resulting in strong free cash generation and cash reserve build-up. Adjusted networth and cash reserve stood at Rs 15,464 crore and Rs 8,824 crore, respectively, as on March 31, 2025. Over the medium term, the company’s working capital requirement and yearly capex of Rs 1,000-1,300 crore shall be met through internal cash accrual. Interest coverage stood at 96.7 times in fiscal 2025 and is expected above 100 times over the medium term. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Exposure to intense competition and modest presence in the premium motorcycle segment** 

The Indian two-wheeler market remains highly competitive given the presence of major players such as Honda Motorcycles & Scooters India Pvt Ltd (HMSI), Bajaj Auto Ltd (Bajaj; ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’) and TVS Motors Ltd. Furthermore, players continue to launch new models. The EV space has become extremely competitive with new original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) expanding in the segment. However, HMCL has maintained its leadership position in the entry level motorcycle segment backed by new products and strong dealership network. The company will continue to focus on inhouse research and development (R&D) to launch new models with its own technology and has been open to partnerships from time to time. HMCL has modest presence in the premium motorcycle segment with domestic market share of ~1.5% for the nine months through December 2025. 

# **Susceptibility to rural demand owing to low geographic diversity and segmental concentration in revenue** 

Motorcycles accounted for around 91% of total volume during the first nine months of fiscal 2026 and scooters accounted for the balance. Within motorcycles, around 94% of the volume comes from the domestic market with major concentration in the entry-level motorcycle segment (around 88% of domestic volume), demand for which is rural and price centric. Multiple price hikes followed by low rural disposal income impacted volume recovery post pandemic. However, rural sentiment is expected to be favourable in the upcoming quarters on the back of GST rate cuts. Improvement in the geographic mix can support/cushion slowdown in the domestic market. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Superior 

Given the negligible term debt obligation, annual expected cash accrual of Rs 2,300-2,800 crore will sufficiently cover yearly capex of Rs 1,000-1,300 crore (excluding any unplanned strategic investment) and limited working capital requirement. Liquidity was supported by cash reserve of Rs 8,824 crore as on March 31, 2025 (~Rs 13,286 crore as on December 31, 2025) and unutilised bank limit. 

# **<u>ESG Profile</u>** 

Crisil Ratings believes HMCL’s Environment, Social and Governance (ESG) profile supports its already strong credit risk profile. The company’s commitment to ESG will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence, given the moderate shareholding by foreign portfolio investors and access to domestic and/or foreign capital markets. 

# **ESG highlights:** 

- HMCL plans to achieve carbon neutrality for its operations by 2030, focusing on emission reductions. In fiscal 2025, the company achieved ~20% reduction in scope 1 and 2 emission intensity and ~8% decrease in energy consumption compared to the previous year. 

- In fiscal 2025, the company achieved targets pertaining to attaining 500% water positivity, achieving 100% zero waste to landfill and 95% product recyclability. During fiscal 2025, 497 lakh units of renewable power were consumed. All the offices and manufacturing plants of HMCL are single-use plastic free certified. In fiscal 2025, HMCL had ~13% female representation among its employees and workers. Additionally, the company's attrition rate dropped to 8% in fiscal 2025 from 13% in fiscal 2024. 

- HMCL aims to accomplish 100% Green Dealership by 2030, the implementation has touched the 30% mark. Furthermore, HMCL secured a customer experience index of 89.9 and ~99% of the customer complaints were resolved. 

- On the corporate social responsibility (CSR) front, higher education scholarships were awarded to 711 students pursuing B.Tech, LLB and MBA. There were a total of 8.47 lakh beneficiaries across different CSR initiatives. HMCL's governance structure is characterised by ~50% of its board comprising independent directors, 30% women board directors, high attendance of independent directors in board and committee meetings, and extensive financial disclosures. 

- HMCL has established a comprehensive information security policy providing a robust framework for safeguarding the information assets. It has also scaled up third-party assessment through sustainable partner development and green dealership programmes. 

- Commitment to ESG and integrating sustainability principles throughout the organisation and value chain will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence and access to capital markets. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

The business risk profile of HMCL shall continue to benefit from its established market position in the overall two-wheeler industry followed by dominant market share within the domestic entry level motorcycle segment, while also continuing to maintain healthy operating efficiency. HMCL is expected to sustain its debt metrics at superior level with strong annual cash accruals and robust financial flexibility. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Downward factors** 

Sustained decline in overall market share to below 18-20%, leading to a sharp fall in the operating margin Sizeable cash outflow in the form of dividends or share buyback, severely depleting cash surplus disproportionately to the cash accrual 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

HMCL, formerly Hero Honda Motors Ltd, was established in 1984 as a joint venture (JV) between the Hero group (owned by the Munjal family based in Ludhiana, Punjab) and the Japan-based Honda group, and began manufacturing motorcycles in 1985. In 2011, the JV partners separated. HMCL has six plants in India: one each in Dharuhera and Gurugram, Haryana; Haridwar, Uttarakhand; Halol, Gujarat; and Chittoor, Andhra Pradesh, and a global parts centre in Neemrana, Rajasthan, with combined manufacturing capacity of 92 lakh units per annum. It also has plants in Villa Rica, Columbia; and Jessore, Bangladesh. HMCL has total production capacity of 9.5 million units per annum across the manufacturing plants. HMCL also has two R&D centres at Centre of Innovation and Technology in Jaipur and Hero Tech Center in Germany.
