### **<u>Detailed rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has upgraded its rating on the long-term bank facilities of Heritage Foods Ltd (HFL, part of the Heritage group) to **‘Crisil AA-/Stable’** from ‘Crisil A+/Positive’. The rating on the short-term bank facilities has been reaffirmed at ‘Crisil A1+’. 

The rating upgrade is driven by Heritage group’s strong & dominant market position in the region it operates, continued healthy operating performance and its robust financial risk profile and liquidity. The group has operations in 14 states with a significant market share in Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka and Tamilnadu. Operating performance remained healthy driven by a steady revenue growth, moderate profit margins and efficient working capital management. Revenue from operations grew at a healthy compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of ~16% for the three fiscals through 2025, to Rs 4,134 crore. Operating Income is estimated to grow at a healthy double-digit growth of 10-12% over the medium term mainly supported by strong revenue growth of over 15-18% in the value-added products. Operating margin although moderated in Q3 of fiscal 2026 on account of sharp increase in procurement prices is expected to improve with revision in prices going forward. Margin is expected to sustain in the range of 6-7% in fiscals 2026 and 2027 and with higher share of value-added products, the margins are expected to improve further in the ensuing years. Working capital requirements are -prudently managed with moderate inventory of around a month and low debtors of 2-3 days resulting in gross current assets of 40-50 days; return on capital employed is also expected to remain healthy at 16-18% level over the medium term. 

The rating action also factors in the group’s strong financial risk profile and robust liquidity with healthy net cash accruals, barely utilized working capital limits and sizeable unencumbered cash & bank balances. Adjusted networth and gearing are estimated at Rs 1100 crore and 0.3 time respectively as of March 31, 2026. The group is estimated to undertake sizeable debt funded capex mainly for enhancing the capacities of value-added products which will support the long-term revenue growth. Annual capex is estimated to be around Rs 200 crore funded partly by debt. Despite this capital structure is expected to remain strong with gearing ratio estimated to remain under 0.5 times over the medium term. Liquidity too should remain strong supported by healthy net cash accruals of Rs 210-275 cr per fiscal over the medium term. 

The ratings continue to reflect the group’s strong market position in the dairy business, its well-established distribution network, prudent working capital management and robust financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by exposure to volatility in milk prices, geographic concentration in revenue, regulatory changes and inherent epidemic-related factors. 

### **<u>Analytical approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has consolidated the financials of HFL and its subsidairies, Heritage Nutrivet Ltd (HNL) and Heritage Novandie Foods Pvt Ltd (HFNPL). These entities, collectively referred to as the Heritage group, are under the same management with significant business and financial linkages. Crisil Ratings has also proportionately consolidated its associate, SKIL (Raigam) Power India Ltd (SKIL), to the extent of HFL’s shareholding. However, there are no operations in this entity.

### **<u>Key rating drivers - Strengths</u>** 

**Strong market position in the dairy industry in south India** 

HFL is one of the largest private dairy players in south India with milk processing capacity of 2.83 million litres of milk per day. It enjoys significant presence in Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Maharashtra, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu. The company has a wide product portfolio with over 400 stock-keeping units. It markets the products through distributors and exclusive Heritage distribution centres and Heritage parlours. The 33-year legacy of the company, along with experience of the promoters and their healthy relationships with customers and farmers, should continue to support the business risk profile. 

# **Prudent working capital management** 

The working capital cycle will continue to be managed efficiently over the medium term. Gross current assets are estimated to remain low at 45 days as on March 31, 2026, because of moderate inventory of 35 days while receivables will remain low at 2-3 days.. Inventory tends to increase towards the end of the fiscal as the company builds up a large stock of skimmed milk powder (SMP), to be utilised in the first half of the subsequent fiscal. 

# **Robust financial risk profile** 

The group had a strong networth of ~Rs 970 crore as on March 31, 2025 which  is projected to increase to Rs 1,100 crore by March 31, 2026 supported by strong accretions. Gearing and total outside liabilities to tangible networth ratios are projected to remain healthy at 0.3 time and 0.7 time, respectively, as on March 31, 2026. Despite the debt-funded capex plans, the capital structure is expected to remain strong at similar  levels over the medium term. The debt protection metrics are also expected to remain healthy with interest coverage and net cash accrual to adjusted debt ratios estimated at healthy level of 15-16 times and 0.7 time, respectively, in fiscal 2026. 

# **<u>Key rating drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Exposure to volatility in milk prices, geographic concentration in revenue** 

Profitability remains susceptible to fluctuation in milk prices while realisation is further impacted by volatility in global SMP prices. Amid the intense competition, the company has limited scope to pass on the hike in prices to customers. Though HFL is present in 14 states, it derives over 70% of its revenue from two states and thus remains exposed to high geographical concentration risk. 

# **Susceptibility to regulatory changes and epidemic-related factors** 

Milk prices remain sensitive to any change in government policies and environmental conditions and thus have a direct impact on the operating margin of dairy product manufacturers. Dairies are also vulnerable to risks of failure in milk production owing to outbreak of cattle diseases. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Superior 

Annual cash accrual of Rs 210-275 crore is expected to be sufficient to cover the minimal maturing term debt obligations of Rs 25-60 crore per fiscal over the medium term. Bank limits were unutilised for the 12 months ended December 31, 2025. Moreover, the group also had sizeable investments in mutual funds of Rs 235 crore and unencumbered cash balance of Rs 61 crore as of December 2025, which cushions liquidity. The current ratio is also estimated to remain healthy  at above 1.5 times as on March 31, 2026. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes HFL will continue to benefit from the established brand presence, and strong procurement network of farmers and experienced and professional management. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Further strengthening of market position marked by a robust growth in revenue and geographical diversification of revenue profile, along with improvement in operating margin resulting in increase in net cash accrual to over Rs 350400 crore 

Further strengthening of financial risk profile along with improvement in liquidity with an increase in cash surplus 

# **Downward factors** 

Substantial increase in debt levels driven by higher-than-expected debt funded capex or stretch in working capital cycle, resulting in increase in gearing ratio to over 0.75 time 

Significant decline in revenue and operating margin, leading to net cash accrual below Rs 150 crore on sustained basis 

# **<u>About the group</u>** 

HFL was incorporated in 1992. It is the parent entity of the Hyderabad-based Heritage group, which sells milk and dairy products under the Heritage brand. The group also has captive solar and wind power plants with an installed capacity of 11.70 megawatt. HFL is listed on the Bombay Stock Exchange and the National Stock Exchange. 

HNL, a wholly owned subsidiary of HFL, was incorporated in 2008. The company manufactures and sells cattle feed. Its plants at Hindupur and Mallavalli in Andhra Pradesh have installed capacity of 200 tonne per day each. 

SKIL, is an affiliate of HFL. 

HFNPL was incorporated as a 50:50 JV between HFL and Novandie SNC, France to manufacture VAPs, particularly yogurt. It commenced operations in February 2021. Effective from August 12, 2025, following the transfer of equity shares from 

Novandie to HFL, the shareholding pattern of the company has been revised. The company has now become a subsidiary of HFL which holds 94.40% in this entity.
