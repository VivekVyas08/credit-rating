### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ratings on the bank loan facilities of Deepak Builders and Engineers India Ltd (DBEIL) at ‘Crisil BBB+/Stable/Crisil A2’. 

The ratings continue to reflect DBEIL's established market position, backed by the extensive industry experience of its promoters and healthy financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by declining operating margins and working capital intensive operations. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has evaluated the standalone business and financial risk profiles of DBEIL. 

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Established market position and extensive industry experience of the promoters** : DBEIL has been operating in the civil construction industry, primarily building construction, for over three decades and has developed an established and successful track record in Delhi, Haryana, Punjab and Rajasthan, catering to both government departments and private players. The company has established relationships with its customers, including government agencies on account of regular orders and the successful execution of large projects. Its healthy execution capabilities have led to a robust order book from government departments, HSCC India Ltd, National Project Construction Corporation Ltd (NPCC), Wapcos Ltd, Engineer India Ltd, and Indian Railways among others. Outstanding orders of more than Rs 1554.63 crore as of October 2025, provide healthy revenue visibility and should result in revenue of Rs 550-600 crore in fiscal 2026 and above Rs 600 crore annually over the medium term. The business risk profile is supported by strong execution capability and track record of execution of infrastructure projects. Crisil Ratings believes the timely execution of the order book with stable operating profitability will further strengthen the market position of the group. 

**Healthy financial risk profile:** Networth is expected to be comfortable at Rs 430–450 crore as on March 31, 2026, with healthy accretion to reserve. The capital structure is comfortable, with gearing expected at 0.6-0.7 time and total outside liabilities to tangible networth ratio (TOLTNW) at 0.8-1 times as on March 31, 2026 (0.66 time and 1.03 times, respectively, a year earlier). However, debt protection indicators — especially interest coverage, remained at 2.89 times in the first nine months of fiscal 2026 due to high dependence on working capital debt. It is expected to remain at 3.0-3.1 times in fiscal 2026 (3.94 times in fiscal 2025). Crisil Ratings believes that, with no major debt-funded capital expenditure (capex), the financial risk profile is expected to remain comfortable over the medium term as well. 

### **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Declining operating profitability:** The operating margin declined to approximately 19.12% in fiscal 2025, from 22.3% during previous fiscal, and is expected to moderate further to 13–15% in fiscal 2026, thereby constraining efficiencies. The execution of railway orders, which required simultaneous work on adjacent tracks within each railway station for laying and modifying blocks, was impacted by site unavailability for the required number of man-hours. This affected the absorption of overheads deployed for these projects, leading to an impact on revenue execution during 9M-FY26, which in turn affected the absorption of fixed overheads and consequently, the operating margin. The operating margin declined to approximately 9% during the October–December quarter of fiscal year 2026. Although improved revenue booking is likely to aid profitability in the last quarter, the overall profitability of 13–15% for fiscal 2026 will remain lower than Crisil Rating's earlier expectation 

of approximately 20-21%. Going forward, any further weakening in operating profitability can adversely impact cash flows and liquidity, and thus, will remain a key monitorable. 

**Working capital intensive operations:** Gross current assets (GCAs) [excluding cash] are expected to be increased significantly to 350-400 days as on March 31, 2026 (more than expected of 220-250 days) due to high unbilled revenue with inventory holding increasing to 325-350 days now in 2026. This is primarily because the company was not able to book revenue on the partially completed sites based on milestone billing due to extended monsoons in second quarter. Further, the railway projects required high inventory to be placed on-site by more than 60-90 days than average inventory required in other projects. Crisil Ratings believes that overall inventory holding and GCAs shall moderate over the medium term after the execution of railway track projects; however, sustained improvement in the same will remain a key monitorable. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Adequate 

Bank limit utilisation averaged a moderate 83% over the 12 months through January 2026 net of quarter-end free cash and bank balance. Expected cash accrual of Rs 40-45 crore comfortably cover annual repayment obligations of Rs 11-14 crore over the medium term. In addition, it will act as cushion to the liquidity of the company. Average cash and bank balance stood at Rs 7-8 crore as on December 31, 2025. Current ratio is expected to be ~1.7-2.0 times over the medium term. Crisil Ratings believes the net cash accrual and cushion in bank lines shall be sufficient to meet any business requirements over the medium term. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings has evaluated the standalone business and financial risk profiles of DBEIL. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Sustained and significant improvement in revenue to over Rs 750 crore with stable operating margin of 16-17% Prudent working capital management leads to lower dependence on external debt and an improved liquidity position 

# **Downward factors** 

Delay in order execution or lower profitability below 10%, leads to lower-than-expected net cash accrual of below Rs 30 crore 

- Further stretch in the working capital cycle, leading to higher dependence on the bank lines and thus, impacting the financial risk profile and liquidity 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

DBEIL was established as a proprietorship firm in 1984 by Deepak Kumar Singal. Later, the firm was converted to a partnership firm in 1990, and subsequently reconstituted as a private limited company in 2017. In 2022, the company was converted into a closely held public company under the current name. DBEIL is engaged in the business of government contract work and construction of infrastructure facilities including hospitals, bridges, and government buildings. It is based in Ludhiana, Punjab. The company was listed on the Bombay Stock Exchange in 2024. DBEIL is promoted by Deepak Kumar Singal and his wife Sunita Singal.
