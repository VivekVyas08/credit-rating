### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A/Stable/Crisil A1’ ratings on the bank facilities of TeamLease Services Limited (TLSL). 

The reaffirmation factors in the company’s healthy business risk profile, supported by revenue growth of 6% in fiscal 2026. This is driven by steady growth across key operating segments, with general staffing revenue growing 5%, specialised staffing revenue 13%, and continued growth of HR services. The growth was achieved despite moderation in headcount in the general staffing segment due to regulatory-driven transition with one large NBFC (non-banking financial company) insourcing. The operating income is expected to remain healthy over the medium term on the back of underlying demand across segments and increasing traction in higher-value segments such as specialised staffing and digital HR services. The rating also factors in the diversified client base, with no single industry accounting for more than 20% of revenue and top 10 clients contributing 31% of revenue in fiscal 2026. The company caters to a diverse set of industries, supporting stability in revenue. 

Operating margin remained rangebound at 1.33% in fiscal 2026 and is expected to remain 1.3-1.4% over the medium term. Any material moderation in profitability will remain a key rating sensitivity factor. 

The financial risk profile remained strong, as reflected in healthy capital structure with adjusted networth of Rs 798 crore and gearing of 0.04 time as on March 31, 2026. While the approved buyback of shares of Rs 238 crore in fiscal 2027 may lead to some moderation in networth, it is not expected to materially impact the financial risk profile as it will be funded through internal accrual and existing cash balance. There is no long-term debt on the books of the company and net cash accrual is sufficient to cover working capital cycle, which is reflected in average bank limit utilisation of ~21% over the 12 months through March 2026. The financial risk profile was further supported by free cash and equivalent of Rs 600 crore as of March 2026, and free liquidity of Rs ~300-350 crore is expected to be maintained at all times. 

The company addressed the ongoing regulatory and legal matters across jurisdictions this fiscal. With respect to the regional EPFO (Employees’ Provident Fund Organisation) show-cause notice in Bengaluru, the company successfully received a stay order on May 20, 2026, in response to the writ petition filed on May 12, 2026, with the High Court of Karnataka. Regarding the EPFO notice in Vadodara (Gujarat), the company filed a writ petition on May 7, 2026, with the High Court of Gujarat, seeking an appropriate stay on the operation of the order and previously obtaining a favourable interim stay from the High Court of Madras; establishing a precedent on a similar notice. In relation to GST notices, the company has filed a writ petition on June 12, 2026, with the High Court of Karnataka. 

The ratings continue to reflect the company’s dominant position in the organised staffing segment, healthy financial risk profile, and prudent working capital management. These strengths are partially offset by exposure to intense competition in general staffing and risk stemming from acquisitions. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of TLSL and its operating subsidiaries. This is because of the strong business and financial linkages among these entities, collectively referred to as TeamLease. 

For analytical purposes, Crisil Ratings amortises goodwill created upon acquisition for a period of five years. 

From fiscal 2027, TeamLease Skills University (TLSU) is proposed to be discontinued and will cease to exist, with its operations to be transferred to TeamLease Education Foundation (TLF); accordingly, as TLF is already consolidated, TLSU will not be consolidated in the analytical assessment from fiscal 2027 onwards.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Dominant position in the organised staffing segment:** TLSL is one of the largest players in the domestic human resource services industry with a strong position in the temporary staffing segment. Its large base of more than 3,40,600 associates and trainees has contributed to strong revenue growth. The company has been consistently growing its associate or trainee base, despite large attrition in the temporary staffing segment. 

Consolidated revenue grew ~6% in fiscal 2026 to Rs 11,791 crore, driven by growth in general staffing (~5%), specialised staffing (~13%) and continued expansion in HR services. While headcount moderation in the general staffing segment owing to regulatory transition, along with insourcing by a large BFSI (banking, financial services, and insurance) client, impacted overall growth; steady demand across other segments and increased traction in higher-value businesses such as specialised staffing and digital HR services supported the revenue trajectory. 

The company will maintain its strong position over the medium term, driven by its increasing presence across India, wellentrenched relationships with over 3,800 clients and growing associate or trainee base. 

**Healthy financial risk profile:** There is no long-term debt on the books of the company and negligible debt of ~Rs 34 crore on March 31, 2026. The healthy financial risk profile is reflected in its low leverage indicators, with gearing of 0.04 time and total outside liabilities to adjusted networth ratio of 2.18, supported by a strong networth of Rs 798 crore. The company is expected to remain net debt-free over the medium term. 

Cash surplus stood at Rs 600 crore, thereby supporting liquidity. The company has made acquisition of ~Rs 350 crore in the eight fiscals through 2018, funded through internal accrual and surplus cash. The company has approved a buyback of shares amounting to Rs 238 crore for fiscal 2027, which will be funded through internal accrual. While this is expected to result in some moderation in networth from fiscal 2026 levels, it is not anticipated to have a material impact on the financial risk profile. Any large, debt-funded acquisition will remain monitorable. 

**Prudent working capital management:** TLSL follows a collect-and-pay model for more than 87% of its contracts, which supports liquidity and limits working capital requirement. The company’s working capital cycle has remained stable and efficient, with receivables maintained at 17–18 days. Additionally, the company has demonstrated strong operating performance, with a return on capital employed (RoCE) of ~20.2% in fiscal 2026, indicating efficient utilisation of capital. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Exposure to intense competition in general staffing and to risks stemming from inorganic growth:** The staffing industry remains highly competitive with the presence of several organised players alongside numerous unorganised regional participants offering services at lower cost, resulting in sustained pricing pressure. This is particularly relevant for TLSL, as the general staffing segment contributes over 90% to its revenue, where mark-ups are inherently low at ~Rs 689 PAPM (Per Associate Per Month) for the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 (compared with ~Rs 744 in fiscal 2018), thereby constraining operating profitability. While the company is gradually shifting towards variable mark-up contracts with 70% of the new mandates signed on variable markup model to improve realisations, the acceptance and scaling of such models remain monitorable. 

Furthermore, the nature of the business involves high workforce churn, with attrition levels remaining elevated due to competition-driven poaching of trained manpower. This exposes the company to risks related to workforce availability, which could impact client servicing and revenue continuity, although this is mitigated by its established market position and scale. 

Additionally, TLSL has pursued growth through selective acquisitions to expand its capabilities, including investments in RegTech, HRTech, and staffing adjacencies. While these initiatives support diversification and long-term scalability, they also entail execution and integration risks. Any inorganic expansion, including its funding structure and successful integration, will remain monitorable for the company’s risk profile. 

**Low operating margin:** TLSL continues to operate at structurally low operating margin due to the inherent nature of the general staffing business, which contributes over 90% to revenue and offers limited headroom margin amid intense competition from the unorganised segment, and fixed mark-up contracts. However, margin remained broadly stable in fiscal 2026 at ~1.3%, supported by improved cost efficiency, operating leverage and variable mark-up hires despite moderation in revenue growth due to headwinds from the BFSI sector. Incremental hiring under variable mark-up contracts and improving realisation across segments are gradually aiding profitability. The operating margin is expected to remain rangebound at 1.3–1.5% over the medium term, with gradual improvement driven by a higher contribution from specialised staffing, 

increased share of variable mark-up contracts, and continued investments in technology and automation to enhance productivity and reduce hiring costs. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

The liquidity is supported by healthy net cash accrual of Rs 180–190 crore annually over the medium term, coupled with no long-term debt obligation and minimal capital expenditure requirement of ~Rs 20 crore. TLSL had unencumbered cash and equivalent of over Rs 600 crore as on March 31, 2026. Unencumbered cash is expected to be maintained at Rs 300-350 crore on an ongoing basis. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes TLSL will continue to benefit from its strong position in the temporary staffing segment and its healthy financial risk profile over the medium term. 

# **<u>ESG profile</u>** 

Crisil Ratings believes that TLSL’s environment, social, and governance (ESG) profile supports its credit risk profile. 

The staffing sector has a low impact on the environment because of the lower greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, and low waste generation by its core operations. The sector has a moderate social impact because of its large workforce and the impact on the health and wellbeing of its workers on account of its nature of operations. The company is focusing on mitigating environmental and social risks. 

- The company has taken initiatives to reduce its energy consumption in its offices by taking measures such as shifting to LED lights, opting for better efficiency standards for its equipment and lowering its emissions. 

- TLSL is developing a roadmap towards carbon neutrality. Current areas of focus include improvements in energy efficiency, sustainable infrastructure, green transport frameworks and flexible working arrangements. Gender diversity of the company continued to be healthy at ~30%, though attrition rate remained high at 43% for fiscal 2025 due to the company being in the staffing segment. 

- TLSL’s governance structure is characterised by 70% of its board comprising independent directors, presence of investor grievance redressal mechanism, and high quality of financial disclosure. 

There is growing importance of ESG among investors and lenders. TLSL’s commitment to ESG principles will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence, given medium share of market borrowings in its overall debt and access to both domestic and foreign capital markets.

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Established in 2002 by Manish Sabharwal and Ashok Reddy, TLSL provides temporary staffing solutions and has over 3,500 clients and 2,90,000 associates and trainees. It acquired Indian Institute of Job Training in fiscal 2010 for Rs 24 crore, largely funded through private equity investors. The company signed a memorandum of understanding with the Government of Gujarat in 2011 for setting up TLSU. In February 2016, Rs 150 crore were raised through an IPO. The total IPO raised was Rs 423.68 crore, comprising a fresh issue of Rs 150 crore and an offer for sale of Rs 273.68 crore. The company entered the specialised staffing segment in fiscal 2017 by acquiring three companies in the IT staffing business: Asap Infosystems Pvt Ltd, Nichepro Technologies Pvt Ltd and Keystone Business Solutions Pvt Ltd. In fiscal 2018, it acquired Evolve Technologies and Services Pvt Ltd in the telecommunication staffing segment. In fiscal 2019, it acquired the IT staffing vertical of eCentric Solutions, and in fiscal 2020, IMSI Staffing provides IT infrastructure staffing solutions. While no acquisitions were made during fiscals 2021 and 2022, the company increased its stake in existing subsidiaries. While there were no acquisitions in fiscals 2023 and 2024, in fiscal 2025 the company has picked up stake in TSR Darashaw and Wallet HR, HR services platform and Ikigai Enablers, which will support IT staffing in newer geographies such as Singapore. From fiscal 2027, TLSU is proposed to be discontinued, with its operations to be transferred to TLF.
