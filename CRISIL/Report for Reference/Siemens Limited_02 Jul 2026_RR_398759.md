### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the bank facilities of Siemens Limited (SL). 

The company continues to maintain strong business risk profile, supported by its diversified presence across Smart Infrastructure (SI), Digital Industries (DI), and Mobility segments and robust order book providing long-term revenue visibility. Revenue for 18-month period ending 31 March 2026 stood at Rs 24,846 crore, driven by strong demand in Smart Infrastructure, particularly in electrification, buildings, and energy efficiency solutions, steady execution in the Mobility segment, led by railway and metro projects, gradual recovery and normalization in Digital Industries, following earlier demand volatility. All three segments delivered double-digit growth during the year, demonstrating broad-based demand across end-user industries such as infrastructure, data centers, manufacturing, and railways. Over the medium term, revenue is expected to grow at steady rate annually, supported by strong public and private capex cycle, growth in new age sectors such as data centers, semiconductors, electronics, batteries, renewable power and automation and continued focus on digitalization and sustainability solutions. 

The company continues to benefit from a robust and expanding order book of ~Rs 45,000 crore as of March 2026, which has grown ~2.5x over the past three years. Key order book highlights include order from Indian Railways (9000 HP locomotives) providing execution visibility over a longer term, strong pipeline in metro, signaling, electrification, and propulsion systems including export orders, increasing traction in data centers and smart infrastructure, overall uptick in the private capex and tendering pipeline supported by public capex, quarterly order inflows with book-to-bill is consistently above 1x, indicating replenishment of backlog. 

Operating margin moderated to ~11%* for 18-month 2026 from higher levels in the previous fiscal due to increase in commodity prices (especially copper, silver, and aluminum), forex headwinds particularly euro appreciation affecting imported components and higher project business mix. Despite these pressures, margins are expected to be broadly resilient supported by price hikes operating leverage in mobility segment, continued focus on localization and potential benefits from EU-FTA trade. 

Financial risk profile remains supported by ample liquidity buffers with cash and cash equivalent of ~Rs 5600 crore, nil utilization of fund-based limits, strong net worth of ~Rs 12,236 crore as on March 2026. Planned capex for expansion is expected to be funded through internal accruals, with no reliance on external debt. 

The ratings reflect the diversified portfolio of Siemens, its strong market position, technical support and know-how from the parent, Siemens AG, and the strong financial risk profile of the company. These rating strengths are partially offset by intense competition in the capital goods industry. 

_*Crisil Ratings adjusted margin_

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

- **Strong market position and diversified portfolio:** Presence across multiple businesses shields SL from cyclicality associated with individual businesses. The company has categorised its business under the Smart Infrastructure, Digital Industries and Mobility businesses. Its strong market position is supported by access to latest technology and brand equity of its parent, diverse product portfolio, wide geographical reach and established track record of timely project execution. 

Furthermore, acquisition of C&S Electric Ltd (CSEL; ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’) in fiscal 2021 has shown a good synergy with the company.  It helps expansion of its presence in the low-voltage power distribution and electrical installation segment in India. Post the acquisition, CSEL has become a manufacturing hub for Siemens AG to supply low-voltage power distribution goods to growing markets in developing countries. 

SL has an established market position with a diversified business portfolio, and sizeable order book of ~Rs 45,000 crore as on March 31st, 2026, should help the company maintain healthy operating performance over the medium term. Of this, Rs 26,300 crore (received in early 2023) is a part of the mobility business and is expected to be executed over a decade with Siemens. It received the order for 1,200 locomotives of 9,000 horsepower (HP) from the Indian Railways, marking the single largest order received by the company. The company will design, manufacture, commission and test the locomotives. Locomotive delivery is planned over 11 years followed by 35 years of full-service maintenance. Locomotive assembly and maintenance will be implemented with the staff of Indian Railways. 

- **Technical support and knowhow from the parent, Siemens AG, Germany:** SL receives technical support and knowhow from Siemens AG. This enables the company to produce high-quality products and improve service capabilities and thus maintain healthy market position. 

- **Strong financial risk profile:** The financial risk profile is supported by nil balance sheet debt, healthy adjusted tangible net worth of around Rs 12,236 crore as on March 31, 2026, and superior liquidity backed by healthy cash and equivalent (~Rs 5,612 crore as of March 2026). 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

- **Exposure to intense competition:** SL faces intense competition from several domestic and international players. The competitive bidding process for most projects, along with the macroeconomic slowdown, has led to heightened competition and pressure on profitability. Crisil Ratings believes company will continue to bid prudently for such projects. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Superior 

Liquidity is supported by cash and equivalent of around Rs 5,612 crore as on March 31, 2026, strong cash accrual and unutilized fund-based bank limit. Existing cash and equivalent and internal cash accrual will sufficiently cover capex (including regular maintenance capex) and working capital requirement. 

# **<u>ESG Profile</u>** 

# **<mark>Environment, social and governance (ESG) profile</mark>** 

Crisil Ratings believes the ESG profile of SL supports its already strong credit risk profile. With a strong focus on sustainability, SL supports customers across verticals such as power utilities, datacenters, semiconductors, F&B, pharmaceuticals and rail transportation in achieving their environmental and social goals through innovative technologies that enable decarbonization and resource efficiency. These solutions help create lasting value for businesses, society, and the environment while promoting health, safety and well-being across the value chain. 

# **<mark>Key ESG highlights:</mark>** 

- Company under its DEGREE framework is targeting ~90% Scope 1 & 2 and 30% Scope 3 emission reduction by 2030, with net-zero by 2050. 

- Environmental performance remains supported by 100% renewable electricity usage, Scope1+ Scope2 emissions reduced to 4.9kt (-88% from FY22); Scope3 emissions reduced to 68,660 kt (-2% from FY22); ~97% waste recovered by recycling/reuse and progress toward zero waste to landfill. 

- Social profile is supported by strong human capital metrics, including ~42.5 learning hours per employee and >6,000 volunteering hours. 

- The business model is sustainability-aligned, targeting >1,000 Mt cumulatively by 2030. 

- Governance focus on targeting training 100% of employees on its Business Conduct Guidelines every three years, alongside cybersecurity (~62% coverage, fiscal 2025) and anti-corruption initiatives. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes SL will maintain its healthy market position over the medium term backed by its technological superiority. The financial risk profile is also likely to remain strong given the conservative financial policy and robust capital structure. 

**<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** **<mark>Downward factors:</mark>** 

<mark>Slump in order inflow or dip in operating margin below 5% on a sustained basis, straining the operating performance. Large, debt-funded capex or acquisition, weakening the financial risk profile along with substantial decline in liquidity.</mark>

Siemens leverages its deep domain know-how to apply AI – including generative AI – to real-world applications, making AI accessible and impactful for customers across diverse industries.
