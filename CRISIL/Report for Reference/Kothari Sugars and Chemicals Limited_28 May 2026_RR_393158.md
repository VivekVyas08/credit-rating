### **<u>Detailed rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has placed its ratings on the bank facilities of Kothari Sugars and Chemicals Limited (KSCL; part of the HC Kothari group) on **‘Rating Watch with Developing Implications’** 

The rating action follows KSCL’s announcement on May 19, 2026, that the board of directors has approved the scheme of amalgamation of KSCL (transferor company) with Kothari Petrochemicals Ltd (KPL; transferee company). The transaction will be completed subject to necessary approvals from National Stock Exchange (NSE), the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), shareholders, creditors and National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) as both the companies are listed on stock exchange. 

KPL is engaged in the business of manufacturing polyisobutylene, which is applied in segments such as lubricant & dispersant, fuel additive, grease, adhesive, sealant, rubber manufacturing, personal care and masterbatch. 

Both companies are part of the HC Kothari group and the scheme of amalgamation does not involve any cash consideration whereas 1 (one) fully paid-up equity share of Rs 10/- each of KPL shall be issued and allotted for every 5 (five) fully paid-up equity shares of Rs 10/- each held in KSCL. The proposed amalgamation will simplify the group structure, improve operational efficiency by eliminating duplicate costs and strengthen the combined entity’s financial position, enabling better resource utilisation, strategic flexibility and long-term value creation for stakeholders as per the announcement. 

Crisil Ratings notes that the merger strengthens the financial risk profile of the combined entity. However, upon receipt of finer details, Crisil Ratings will further evaluate the underlying business profile of KPL and the management’s medium-term strategic plans. Additionally, KPL informed the stock exchanges on March 11, 2026, regarding restrictions in gas supply arising from the ongoing geopolitical situation in the West Asia. The company further communicated that the Union Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas has issued a directive to refineries to prioritise and divert supplies for domestic LPG requirement, resulting in the suspension of feedstock supplies to downstream industries and thus constraining raw material availability for the company. Crisil Ratings will continue to engage with the management to seek further clarity on the aforementioned matter and closely monitor its development. This remains a key rating sensitivity factor. 

Crisil Ratings will resolve the watch post receipt of necessary approvals and completion of requisite documentation for the merger and receipt of necessary information from the management KSCL and KPL. 

The ratings continue to reflect the longstanding presence of KSCL in the sugar industry, its integrated operations, diverse product portfolio and healthy financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by susceptibility to the downturn in the sugar business and exposure to regulatory changes in the industry and scale of operations. 

### **<u>Analytical approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has evaluated the standalone business and financial risk profiles of KSCL. 

### **<u>Key rating drivers - Strengths</u>** 

**Longstanding presence in the sugar industry:** Backed by the presence of over seven decades, KSCL has established itself as a significant player in the sugar segment. Over the years, the company's promoters and management have gained good insights into the industry and have established diverse customer base and healthy relationships with sugarcane farmers. The company has also weathered the inherent cyclicality in the sugar industry. A sugarcane crushing capacity of around 6,400 tonne per day, distillery of 60 kilolitre per day (KLPD) of output and a co-generation unit of 33-megawatt (MW) capacity have supported the business risk profile. With increasing focus on distillery operations, vulnerability of performance to volatility in sugar production and prices are expected to gradually reduce over the medium term. 

**Healthy financial risk profile:** The capital structure was healthy due to low reliance on external funds yielding gearing of 0.24 time and low total outside liabilities to adjusted networth (TOLANW) ratio of 0.51 time as on March 31, 2026. Debt protection metrics, particularly the interest coverage ratio, weakened and remained negative in fiscal 2026 due to subdued profitability. However, the impact is mitigated by the healthy liquidity position as reflected in free cash and cash equivalents of around Rs 121 crore as on March 31, 2026, and hence, no material pressure on debt servicing is expected in the near term. 

# **<u>Key rating drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Susceptibility of business performance to downturn in the sugar business and volatile margin:** Sugar prices are largely market driven and dependent on production for the sugar season and inventory prevailing in the country. Hence, higher production, which increases inventory, may lead to steep fall in prices and impact profitability severely as the cost of production is relatively sticky. Monsoon also has a bearing on cane production and recovery rate of cane, impacting the overall sugar production in the country. Additionally, the government has taken measures to encourage increased diversion of sugarcane to ethanol instead of sugar and promote exports in the past to address the excess inventory and arrest the fall in prices. The operating margin has been volatile for the four fiscals through 2026 due to the inherent cyclical nature of the industry. Notably, the operating margin fluctuated between -6% and 10% during this period, highlighting the challenges posed by industry cyclicality. 

**Exposure to regulatory changes in the sugar industry:** While sugar prices are market driven, the government is empowered to fix the price paid to cane growers annually. KSCL’s profitability, mainly because of its sugar segment, remains vulnerable to material changes based on the regulatory changes in the sugar industry. Any change in the regulatory stance and continuation of government support to sugar sector (including distilleries and ethanol pricing) are key rating sensitivity factors. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Adequate 

Bank limit utilisation was low at 56% on average for the 12 months ended May 31, 2026. The company has no term debt obligation in the medium term. The current ratio was adequate at 2 times as on March 31, 2025. Healthy cash and cash equivalent of around Rs 121 crore as on March 31, 2026, further supports liquidity. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors:</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Enhanced sugarcane crushing, resulting in high revenue or operating margin above 6% Sustenance of financial and liquidity risk profiles 

# **Downward factors** 

Decline in revenue by 20% or lower-than-expected profitability, leading to low net cash accrual Weakening of the financial or liquidity risk profiles 

# **<u>About the company</u>** 

Incorporated in 1961, KSCL manufactures sugar and has two plants in Tamil Nadu, one in Kattur village and the other in Sathamangalam village with a combined installed capacity of 6,400 tonne crushing per day (TCD). Also, the sugar mill has a 33-MW captive power generation capacity, which it fuels using bagasse generated in the sugar production and 60 KLPD molasses-based distilleries.
