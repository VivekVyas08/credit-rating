### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has placed its rating on the long-term bank facilities of Inox Wind Limited (IWL) on **‘Rating Watch with Developing Implications** ' while reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A1’ rating on the short-term bank facility. Crisil Ratings has simultaneously **withdrawn** its ratings on the bank facilities of IWL at the company’s request and on receipt of no objection certificate from the lenders. The withdrawal is in line with the Crisil Ratings policy on withdrawal of ratings. 

The rating has been placed on watch following the announcement by Inox Clean Energy Ltd (ICEL) to acquire renewable energy (RE) assets of around 6 GW from Vena Energy India. This is because the ratings of IWL factor in its strong group linkages as part of the INOXGFL group. The INOXGFL group has presence in specialty chemicals through Gujarat Fluorochemicals Limited (GFL; ‘Crisil AA+/Stable/Crisil A1+’), and supply of wind turbine generators and associated turnkey engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) contracts, along with operations and maintenance (O&M) of RE assets, through IWL and its subsidiaries. The group has ventured into the renewable sector as an independent power producer by developing and operating RE projects as well as manufacturing solar cells and modules, through ICEL. 

ICEL has articulated targets of 14 GW in RE generation by the end of fiscal 2029 and manufacturing capabilities of 11 GW each for solar cells and modules over the medium term, requiring significant capital. In addition to the proposed acquisition of RE assets from Vena Energy India, ICEL, in the past 12 months, has acquired Macquarie-owned Vibrant Energy, Indian assets of SunSource Energy, and CalPERS-backed SkyPower. ICEL has also announced acquisitions of US-based Boviet Solar’s manufacturing assets, including its Africa business. The impact of the INOXGFL group’s capital allocation strategy in the clean energy vertical and the credit risk profiles, including capital structures, of these assets on the group’s credit profile is critical. 

The ratings continue to reflect the healthy business risk profile of IWL, driven by its improved operating performance, as reflected in healthy order execution over the past two fiscals. The financial risk profile remains healthy driven by the company’s deleveraging efforts through successful equity raises during fiscals 2023-2025, and around Rs 1,600 crore raised in fiscal 2026. The working capital intensity, however, remains high as reflected in large receivables, underpinning the business risk profile. The management expects the working capital cycle to moderate in the near term, which will be monitorable. 

The company reported consolidated revenue of Rs 4,397 crore and operating profit of Rs 891 crore (operating margin of 20.3%) in fiscal 2026, as against Rs 3,564 crore and Rs 757 crore, respectively, with operating margin of 21.2% in fiscal 2025. The increase in revenue was driven by ramp-up in order execution while profitability was affected because of supply chain challenges on account of geopolitical issues, mainly in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026. 

The overall order book stands at around 3.1<sup>1</sup> GW as on March 31, 2026, providing healthy medium-term revenue visibility. The order book mostly comprises high-margin 3.3-MW turbines, which shall result in healthy earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (Ebitda) margin. Timely execution of the order book remains monitorable. 

IWL, through its subsidiary Inox Renewable Solutions Ltd (IRSL; ‘Crisil A/Watch Developing/Crisil A1’), has taken measures to improve backward integration, such as crane services, which shall help improve margins for the EPC and O&M segments. The ability to maintain profitability while scaling up operations will be monitorable. 

The working capital intensity remains high with receivables increasing to 353 days and inventory to 186 days as on March 31, 2026, from 280 days and 145 days, respectively, a year earlier. The receivables increased to Rs 4,249 crore as on March 31, 2026, from Rs 2,687 crore as on March 31, 2025, mainly due to delayed collection from customers linked to project milestones. The company’s 

management expects these dues to be recovered in the near term, which should improve cash flow. Timely collection of receivables and smooth execution of orders will be critical factors in assessing the credit profile. 

Inventory remains high on account of stock maintained for the common infrastructure business. With significant increase in sales, rationalisation of working capital, including receivables and inventory, will be monitorable. 

The financial risk profile remains healthy supported by net debt to Ebitda ratio of 0.43 time for fiscal 2026, compared with 3.44 times for fiscal 2024. The improvement over the last two fiscals was supported by increase in scale and equity raise through rights issue of around Rs 660 crore (net of Rs 590 crore redeemed to promoters for cumulative redeemable preference shares issued earlier). With turnaround in operating profitability and reduction in debt, the company’s interest coverage improved sharply to above 5 times through fiscal 2026 and is expected to remain healthy over 5 times over the medium term. 

The ratings continue to reflect the strong linkages with the INOXGFL group and the extensive experience of its promoters in the wind turbine business. These strengths are partially offset by large working capital requirement and exposure to intense competition.

# **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of IWL and its subsidiaries, Inox Green Energy Services Ltd (IGESL; ‘Crisil A/Watch Developing/Crisil A1’) and IRSL. These entities, collectively referred to as IWL, are in related businesses and have common promoters. 

Crisil Ratings has applied its group notch-up framework to factor in the strong strategic and financial support from the INOXGFL group, which includes IWL, GFL, ICEL and their subsidiaries.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Strong linkages with the INOXGFL group** 

The promoter group holds around 44% stake in IWL, with the INOXGFL group maintaining complete control over operations. The INOXGFL group has extended support to IWL and IGESL through group entities by way of non-convertible debentures, term debt and working capital facilities as and when required. Moreover, group entities have provided support through capital advances and intercorporate deposits in the past. Given the improvement in profitability and deleveraging over the past two fiscals, Crisil Ratings believes IWL will service its debt through its own accrual. The INOXGFL group has entered the renewable sector as an independent power producer by developing and operating RE projects as well as manufacturing solar cells and modules. This business is held by ICEL. While execution risk persists for the projects under ICEL, IWL and its entities will benefit from ICEL’s plans to develop capacities in the wind sector. 

# **Healthy order book and experienced promoters** 

Order book of around 3.1 GW provides healthy near-term revenue visibility. Commercial production and final type certificate for the 3.3 MW turbines and ramp-up in execution have supported profitability. Launch of 4.4 MW turbines in the near term will further support profitability. Furthermore, as on March 31, 2026, IWL had an O&M portfolio (including capacities under pipeline) of over 13 GW (around 10.5 GW of wind assets and the balance solar assets), up from 3.2 GW as on March 31, 2024, driven by inorganic expansion. The company plans to acquire 6.5 GW of operational wind assets through investments in two companies, expected to conclude in fiscal 2027. The extensive experience of the promoters and tailwinds in the wind sector should help IWL sustain its healthy operating performance in the near term. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Large working capital requirement** 

Operations are working capital intensive, as reflected in receivables of 353 days and inventory of 186 days as on March 31, 2026 (280 days and 145 days, respectively, as on March 31, 2025). The working capital requirement was large under the feed-in tariff regime as there were delays in commissioning or signing of power purchase agreements. In the past, large working capital requirement and delays in order execution had led to write-offs and pressure on cash flow. While IWL’s order book comprises strong counterparties, timely order execution, leading to realisation, and further stretch in the working capital cycle will be monitorables. 

# **Exposure to intense competition** 

The business environment for the wind energy sector continues to be challenging. While IWL is one of the prominent domestic players, it remains exposed to intense competition from other domestic players and foreign players. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Adequate 

The liquidity position of IWL remains adequate, as reflected in healthy cash and equivalent of Rs 612 crore as on March 31, 2026. The cash balance, along with expected cash accrual, will be sufficient to cover principal repayments against term loans of Rs 52.5 crore in fiscal 2027. The liquidity is enhanced by unutilised fund-based working capital limit of Rs 200 crore as on March 31, 2026. 

IWL has refinanced its bank limit without any support from the INOXGFL group, resulting in higher financial flexibility. The group has provided support in the form of intercorporate deposits and advances for supplies, and helped IWL to avail funds from banks, supported by guarantees, letters of comfort or by pledging its own funds. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u> Upward factors** 

Track record of high execution with sustenance of operating margin over 15% Streamlining of working capital cycle, strengthening the financial risk profile 

# **Downward factors** 

Change in the shareholding of, or support from, the INOXGFL group Lower-than-expected order execution with operating margin below 12% 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Incorporated in April 2009, IWL is a part of the INOXGFL group. The company manufactures nacelles, hubs, rotor blades and towers used to make wind turbines. It also provides associated services, such as O&M of wind turbines, project execution and infrastructure development for wind farms. The company has four units: at Una in Himachal Pradesh for nacelles and hubs, at Rohika in Gujarat for blades and towers, at Barwani in Madhya Pradesh for nacelles, hubs, blades and towers, and a newly tied-up nacelle manufacturing facility at Bhuj in Gujarat. IWL has a technical tie-up with AMSC Windtech, which provides control systems and vets suppliers for other parts from across the world.
