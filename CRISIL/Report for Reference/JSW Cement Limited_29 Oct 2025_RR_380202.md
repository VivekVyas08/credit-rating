### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has upgraded its ratings on the bank facilities, short-term debt programme and commercial paper programme of JSW Cement Ltd (JSWCL) to **‘Crisil AA-/Stable/Crisil A1+’** from ‘Crisil A+/Stable/Crisil A1’. 

The rating upgrade reflects the expected improvement in financial risk profile of the company as evident from declining leverage trajectory despite sizeable capital expenditure (capex) plans and enhanced financial flexibility backed by the recently concluded initial public offering (IPO). The rating also reflects healthy business risk profile of the company, backed by growing scale of operations, increasing geographical diversification and healthy operating efficiency supported by synergy benefits derived from being part of the JSW group. The rating is also supported by the strong credit profile of the JSW group and financial flexibility that JSWCL enjoys from being part of the group. 

The company was listed on the stock exchanges in August 2025, which resulted in fresh issuance of Rs 1,600 crore and balance Rs 2,000 crore was offered for sale to its investors who have infused funds via compulsorily convertible preference shares during fiscal 2022. Proceeds of the fresh issuance will be utilised to partly fund the capex, prepay debt obligations and for general corporate purposes. 

As per Crisil Ratings, the financial risk profile is expected to improve with net debt to earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (Ebitda; at consolidated level including JSW Cement FZC) likely to fall below 3.5 times going forward (against 5.9 times in fiscal 2025) and interest coverage to increase over 3 times (against 1.6 times in fiscal 2025). This is despite the sizeable capex of Rs 6,000-6,500 crore to be incurred by the company over fiscals 2026-2028, to be funded via a mix of internal accrual and debt. The rating also factors in the management’s philosophy of improving leverage levels and keeping the capex plans flexible to maintain prudent leverage in case of lower than expected improvement in operating performance. 

JSWCL had an installed capacity of 21.6 million tonnes per annum (MTPA) as on October 15, 2025, which is expected to further increase to 24.1 MTPA by the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 by commissioning of an integrated unit in Rajasthan. Further, the company is expected to grow its capacity to ~32 MTPA by fiscal 2028 end through planned and announced greenfield and brownfield projects. These projects will enhance geographical diversity and further strengthen the business risk profile of the company. 

In fiscal 2025, JSWCL’s consolidated revenue (including JSW Cement FZC) dipped by 3% on y-o-y basis, owing to a sharp decline in realisations, despite 9% growth in volume. With industry-wide pressure on realisations, Ebitda per tonne also declined to Rs 664 in fiscal 2025, against Rs 886 in fiscal 2024. However, the Ebitda per tonne improved to Rs 975 in the first quarter of fiscal 2026, against Rs 757 in corresponding quarter of previous fiscal, as cement prices rebounded. Going forward, Ebitda per tonne to sustain over Rs 925 supported by investments in green energy and efficiency-improvement projects, growing share of blended cement (including ground granulated blast furnace slag; [GGBS]), etc. 

These strengths are partially offset by susceptibility to volatility in input cost and realisations coupled with cyclicality in the cement industry, exposure to project-related risks of timely commissioning and ramp up of capacities, and relatively higher receivables days. 

# **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of JSWCL, including its subsidiaries and joint ventures (JVs) and has applied its group notch-up framework to factor in the support expected from the JSW group. Crisil Ratings believes JSWCL will likely to receive distress support from JSW group for timely debt servicing, given the operational linkages between key group companies (specifically JSW Steel Ltd and JSW Energy Ltd), past instance of equity infusion by the JSW group, and the shared name and logo, which imply a high moral obligation on the group to support JSWCL, if required. JSWCL’s net worth has been adjusted for intangible assets.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Group support and financial flexibility being part of JSW group** 

The JSW group is a leading conglomerate with a strong presence in steel, energy, cement, ports, and paints sectors. Its listed companies had a combined market capitalisation of over Rs 4.75 lakh crore as on September 30, 2025, and the group has healthy financial flexibility and ability to raise funds from lenders and capital markets as and when required. 

JSWCL was initially set up to utilise the slag being produced as a by-product by the group's flagship company, JSW Steel Ltd, to manufacture slag-based cement. JSWCL is majorly owned by Adarsh Advisory Services Pvt Ltd, which is fully owned by the Sajjan Jindal Family Trust. Mr. Parth Jindal, son of Mr. Sajjan Jindal, is the managing director of the company. JSWCL receives operational support through supply of slag from JSW Steel Ltd, power from JSW Energy Ltd and port service from JSW Infrastructure Ltd. The group infused equity of Rs 535 crore in JSWCL in fiscal 2018 to support its expansion plans. The company also benefits from the group’s common logo and brand, adjacent premises with JSW Steel Ltd in Dolvi, Maharashtra, and Vijayanagar, Karnataka, and centralised coal procurement and logistics arrangement. Crisil Ratings believes JSWCL will continue to receive operational and managerial support on an ongoing basis and financial support on need basis. 

The rating of JSWCL is sensitive to the credit risk profile of the group. Weakening in the group’s credit risk profile or significant increase in debt in promoter holding companies as against the market value of their investments in the operating companies will be a key rating sensitivity factor. 

# **Increasing geographical diversification in revenue** 

JSWCL has established its market position in south, west and east India. The company’s cement capacity stands at 21.6 MTPA distributed between south (11.0 MTPA), east (6.1 MTPA) and west (4.5 MTPA). This includes commissioning of 1 MTPA cement capacity in Sambalpur, Odisha under its subsidiary Shiva Cement Ltd in October 2025. The company aims to diversify its geographical reach further by setting up an integrated unit in Nagaur, Rajasthan with 3.5 MTPA of cement capacity, and a split grinding unit in Punjab with 2.75 MTPA. These capacities will be commissioned by fiscal 2027. Additionally, it has planned brownfield addition in its Vijayanagar and greenfield addition in Dolvi units, which will grow its capacity to ~32 MTPA by fiscal 2028. Additionally, the company also has long-term plans to set up cement capacities in Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh as well to establish its presence in the central region. 

# **Healthy operating efficiency** 

Operating efficiency of the company is underlined by sale of Portland slag cement (PSC; having high blending ratio of slag) and GGBS in south and west India, which reduces power, fuel and raw material consumption per tonne of cement. Further, proximity of grinding facilities to raw material sources as well as markets reduces freight cost. 

Profitability was impacted in fiscal 2025, with consolidated Ebitda per tonne moderating to Rs 664 from Rs 886 in fiscal 2024 amid industry wide subdued pricing. However, with improvement in pricing, expectation of better demand, higher contribution from new more-efficient units, increasing contribution of blended cement in overall mix and cost saving measures undertaken, Ebitda per tonne expected to improve to more than Rs 925 from fiscal 2026 onwards. 

# **Healthy financial risk profile** 

During fiscal 2025, the financial ratios moderated owing to the combined impact of weak profitability and front loading of capex towards Rajasthan integrated unit. Net debt to Ebitda moderated to 5.9 times against 4.5 times in fiscal 2024. Going forward, company has sizeable capex plans of Rs 6,000-6,500 crore to be incurred over fiscals 2026-2028, to be funded via a mix of internal accruals and debt. Despite this, the consolidated net debt to Ebitda ratio is expected to remain below 3.5 times going forward supported by expected improvement in operating performance and fresh equity of Rs 1,600 crore raised as part of the recently concluded IPO. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Substantial capacity expansion plan** : JSWCL is undertaking aggressive capacity additions, wherein overall cement grinding capacity is expected to increase to ~32 MTPA by fiscal 2028 from 21.6 MTPA in October 2025. The expansion would entail a capex to the tune of Rs 6,000-6,500 crore over fiscals 2026-2028. Given the size of capex relative to existing 

operations, JSWCL will be exposed to risks related to project execution and the ability to ramp-up new capacities. Timely commencement of commercial operations, within the budgeted cost, will remain monitorable. 

**Relatively higher receivable days:** A higher proportion of institutional sales and sales push to increase utilisation of the increased capacity have led to higher working capital intensity as seen in receivables of 45-55 days over the past three fiscals ended March 31, 2025. The debtor days are expected to remain relatively higher owing to continued sales to institutional customers given the product mix and sales push with increase in capacity. 

**Susceptibility to volatility in input cost and realisations, and cyclicality in the cement industry:** Capacity addition in the cement industry tends to be sporadic because of the long gestation period involved in setting up a facility and with numerous players adding capacity during the peak of a cycle. This has led to unfavourable price cycles for the sector in the past, last witnessed in fiscal 2025. Increase in pet coke prices during fiscals 2022 and 2023 impacted the profitability of several cement players. Realisations and profitability are also affected by demand, supply, offtake and other regional factors. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Cash and cash equivalents stood at Rs 935 crore as on September 30, 2025, large portion of which will be utilised to fund the ongoing capex. The company is expected to maintain cash and cash equivalents of over Rs 100 crore on an ongoing basis over the medium term. Sanctioned working capital limits of around Rs 2,200 crore remain moderate. The company is expected to generate cash accrual of more than Rs 950 crore in fiscal 2026 and more than Rs 1,250 crore in fiscal 2027 which together with the available liquidity will be sufficient to meet its annual repayment obligation of around Rs 1,000 crore in fiscal 2026 and fiscal 2027 and to part fund the capex. Financial flexibility is supported by being part of JSW group, access to capital market to service debt obligations in a timely manner and moderately utilised bank limits. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes JSWCL will continue to benefit from healthy operating efficiency, increasing geographical presence with operationalisation of new capacities and overall healthy demand outlook for the cement sector. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Improvement in net debt to Ebitda ratio (at consolidated level including JSW Cement FZC) below 2.25 times driven by sustained improvement in profitability or accelerated deleveraging Significant improvement in the credit risk profile of the JSW group 

# **Downward factors** 

Net debt to Ebitda ratio (at consolidated level including JSW Cement FZC) remaining above 3.5 times on a sustained basis over the medium term due to lower-than-expected profitability or higher debt availed for capex/acquisitions or a combination of both 

- Significant weakening in the credit risk profile of the JSW group or change in support philosophy of the group towards JSWCL. 

Weakening liquidity due to higher working capital intensity or cost overruns in capacity expansion or incremental investments/loans extended to group companies 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

JSWCL was incorporated in 2006, and its first unit of 0.6 MTPA grinding capacity in Vijayanagar was commissioned in fiscal 2009. Presently JSWCL has cement manufacturing capacity of 21.6 MTPA spread across south (11.0 MTPA), west (4.5 MTPA) and east (6.1 MTPA) India. It manufactures various grades of cements such as PSC (Portland Slag Cement), OPC (ordinary portland cement), CHD (Concreel HD), GGBS (ground granulated blast furnace slag) and CPC (composite cement). In August 2025, the company was listed on stock exchanges which resulted a reduction in promoter shareholding to 72.33% as on September 30, 2025. 

# **<u>About the Group</u>** 

The JSW Group is a leading Indian conglomerate with a diverse portfolio of businesses, including steel, energy, cement, infrastructure, paints and sports. Founded by OP Jindal in 1982, the group has grown significantly over the years, with presence in over 100 countries. The group's steel business, JSW Steel, is one of the largest steel producers in India, with an installed capacity of over 35.7 million tons per annum (MTPA). The energy business under JSW Energy has installed power generation capacity of over 12.8 GW. The ports business under JSW Infrastructure has operational cargo-handling capacity of 177 MTPA while the cement business under JSW Cement has installed grinding capacity of over 21.6 MTPA. The group also has business interests in paints, B2B trading, electric vehicles and sports segment.
