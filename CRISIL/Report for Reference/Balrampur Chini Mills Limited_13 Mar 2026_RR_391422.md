### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AA+/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the long-term bank facilities and commercial paper programme of Balrampur Chini Mills Ltd (BCML). 

The ratings continue to reflect the company’s dominant market position in the sugar and allied sectors in north India, its established relationships with farmers, superior operating efficiency, and strong financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by susceptibility to cyclicality and regulatory changes in the sugar business, including movement in the state advised price (SAP) for sugarcane in Uttar Pradesh (UP), the minimum selling price (MSP) for sugar or regulations around distillery operations. The ratings also take into account the risk associated with the ongoing polylactic acid (PLA) project, which has necessitated significant capital expenditure (capex), and is in the advanced stages of completion. 

The business risk profile remains strong, driven by integrated operations and the company’s presence across sugar and distillery segments. Revenue has grown by 19% on-year for the first nine months of fiscal 2026, wherein the sugar segment recorded ~13% growth, backed by better realisations. Distillery revenue grew by around 36%, driven by healthy rise in sales volume, once the government restriction was lifted on use of sugar for ethanol manufacturing. The operating margin improved to 9.8% for the first nine months of fiscal 2026, from 8.6% in the corresponding period of the previous fiscal, led by better realisations in the sugar segment and higher volume in the distillery segment. With healthy profitability likely to sustain in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026, given the seasonal nature of the business, the operating margin is expected to remain steady at 13.0-13.5% in fiscal 2026 (13.5% in fiscal 2025). 

Crisil Ratings continues to monitor the progress towards the PLA project, which is currently progressing as per envisaged timelines. The company has already incurred Rs 1,421 crore (out of the total project cost of Rs 2,850 crore) till January 31, 2026, funded via term loans of Rs 790 crore (out of total planned debt of Rs 1,650 crore). The project is expected to commercialise from the third quarter of fiscal 2027. The UP government had also announced the Bio Plastic Industrial Policy, 2024, to promote bioplastic manufacturing, that is intended to provide various incentives, including capital subsidy, interest subsidy and SGST reimbursement. After considering capital cost, the project cost stands at Rs 1,750 crore. The initial claim is likely to be filed, once operations commence. That said, given the large size of the project, its timely completion without any cost overrun, ramp-up of utilisation and receipt of incentives will be monitorables thereafter. 

The financial risk profile remains comfortable, supported by steady cash generation and controlled debt. Sizeable adjusted networth stood at Rs 3,513 crore (reported networth at Rs 3,635 crore as on September 30, 2025), with limited debt and cane dues enabling adequate leverage. Against this, overall debt was 677 crore, which includes long-term debt pertaining to the PLA project and outstanding existing term debt, likely to be repaid by fiscal 2028, before commencement of repayment of the PLA project debt. Debt level could peak in fiscal 2027. Despite the expected increase in debt, debt protection metrics will likely remain comfortable. The total outside liabilities to adjusted networth (TOL/TNW) ratio is expected to be below 1.2 times and interest coverage ratio at over 5 times over the medium term. The debt will carry a moratorium of 4.5 years and repayment tenure of five years commencing fiscal 2029 i.e. post the full repayment of existing term debt. 

Liquidity is adequate, marked by healthy annual cash accrual of over Rs 500 crore, that covers the debt obligation and partly meets capex. Fund-based bank limit was utilised at 56% on an average for the 12 months through January 2026. 

# **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has followed the capital allocation approach and arrived at the adjusted networth of BCML by knocking off the investment in Auxilo Finserve Pvt Ltd (Auxilo; ‘Crisil A+/Stable’), wherein BCML holds 30.47% stake. As on December 31, 2025, the investment stood at Rs 175 crore. 

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Established market position and diversified revenue profile** 

BCML is one of the largest sugar producers in India, with crushing capacity of 80,000 tonne per day (TPD) of sugarcane, exportable (surplus) power capacity of 175.7 megawatt (MW) and distillery capacity of 1,050 kilo litre per day (KLPD). The company has 10 sugar factories: eight in eastern UP and two in central UP, thus having access to a large market in north India. A 320 KLPD distillery was commissioned in sugar season (SS) 2023, and this distillery can operate on dual feed, sugarcane syrup during the crushing season and B-heavy / C-heavy molasses and grains during the off-season, ensuring full utilisation and aiding profitability. 

Fully integrated operations enable all supplementary businesses associated with sugar, such as distillery and power, to be major contributors to profitability and de-risk the sugar business. The distillery business offers stable profit and thus helps moderate the impact of cyclicality inherent in the sugar business. Ethanol prices are a function of sugarcane prices and cost of sugar production; thus, there is little chance of ethanol prices being lowered. While there has been no increase in ethanol prices from the juice and the B-heavy route over the past 2-3 years, any announcement by the government in this regard remains monitorable. Further, with BCML’s entry into the PLA business, the revenue profile is likely to diversify further. 

Crisil Ratings believes the company will continue to benefit from its dominant market position in the sugar industry and fully integrated operations. The diversified revenue streams will continue to offset the cyclicality in the sugar business. 

# **Superior operating efficiency** 

Operating efficiency is supported by fully integrated operations, sizeable contribution to profitability from the distillery segment, healthy sugar recovery rates and high capacity utilisation, enhancing fixed cost absorption. The company’s distillery capacity is adequate to utilise molasses produced through crushing and the cogeneration segment can cater to the entire power requirement during the crushing season from the bagasse produced, resulting in fully integrated facilities. 

The company has been continuously engaging with farmers to produce an early variety of cane, along with implementing varietal change, which has more sucrose content, and ensuring they are less prone to disease and can acclimatise quickly. 

Operating profitability is likely to remain stable and be supported by the sugar as well as distillery segments. While the margin should steady between 13% and 13.5% in fiscal 2026 (13.5% in fiscal 2025), led by better realisations for the sugar segment, this is partially being negated by the increasing cane cost. With no  upward revision in ethanol prices, profitability in the ethanol segment could see slight moderation. 

# **Healthy financial risk profile; some moderation expected in debt metrics** 

The financial risk profile continues to be healthy, supported by comfortable capital structure and adequate debt protection metrics. The short-term debt is usually high towards the year-end (month of March), owing to the seasonal nature of business. However, the working capital debt becomes negligible during September, just before commencement of the next sugar season. Long-term debt was around Rs 821.75 crore as on December 31, 2025, including Rs 702 crore availed for the PLA project (Rs 790 crore as on January 31, 2026). Due to sizeable term debt of over Rs 1,650 crore to be added for the PLA capacity, overall debt levels have risen gradually from fiscal 2025, with long-term debt likely to peak in fiscal 2027. While gearing may see some moderation, increasing to 0.8-0.9 time as on March 31, 2027; the interest cover and TOL/ANW ratios will remain adequate at more than 5 times and 1-1.1 times, respectively, over the medium term. The company has been buying back its shares at regular intervals though amounts have not been material. However, with large capex in the near to medium term, payout to the shareholders might remain limited. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Susceptibility to downturns in the sugar business** 

Sugar prices are largely market-driven and dependent on production for the sugar season and inventory levels prevailing in the country. Hence, higher production, which increases inventory, may lead to a steep fall in prices and impact profitability severely as the cost of production is relatively sticky in nature. Monsoons too have a bearing on cane production and recovery rates of cane, impacting overall sugar production in the country. Fall in sugar prices is cushioned by the MSP declared by GoI (Rs 31 per kg at present). The government has promoted exports to address excess inventory and arrest the fall in prices in the past. In the current sugar season, nearly 4 lakh tonne of sugar is likely to be exported. 

BCML may not be materially impacted by industry downcycles, given its superior operating efficiency, increasing contribution of the distillery segment, and the integrated nature of operations. Further, its Maizapur distillery will have the flexibility to operate around the year, helping buttress any fluctuation in availability of sugar. 

**Exposure to regulatory risk** 

While input prices are driven by the government, sugar prices are volatile, and based on open market prices (which are dependent on production levels), thus leading to volatility in profitability of players. Besides, the government regulates domestic demand-supply through restrictions on imports and exports, and stock holdings. Regulatory mechanisms and dependence on monsoons have rendered the sugar industry cyclical. Sugarcane pricing is controlled through SAP in UP. Though higher SAP increases the cost of production for UP-based mills, varietal re-balancing, characterised by better recovery rates, reduces the impact considerably for players such as BCML. Profitability of the company, mainly its sugar segment, remains vulnerable to material changes in SAP and other regulatory changes in the sugar industry. The central government had increased the fair and remunerative price (FRP) for sugarcane by 8%. Also, the UP government increased the SAP by Rs 30 per quintal, reaching Rs 400 per quintal for SS 2026. 

The GoI advanced the 20% ethanol blending target (with gasoline) to 2026 from 2030. That said, revenue from the ethanol segment witnessed healthy growth in sales volume, following removal of the ban on use of sugar for manufacture of ethanol. 

However, despite increase in cane prices seen over the last couple of years, and rising operating cost, ethanol prices under the B-heavy and juice routes have not been revised for three years, and this exerts pressure on the profitability of distillery segment. As the sugar industry is highly regulated, any change in stance and continuation of government support to the sugar sector (including distilleries and ethanol pricing) will remain key monitorables. 

# **Strong growth potential from diversification into new PLA venture, albeit certain potential risk associated with the project** 

PLA is a biopolymer with applications in industries such as packaging, printing and textiles. The PLA project, being set up in UP, will help BCML diversify from the sugar and ethanol segments, which are highly regulated. In the absence of any major PLA capacity in India, there is high reliance on imports to fulfil the overall demand. Sugar will be a key raw material to produce PLA. At full capacity, BCML would need around 1.3 lakh tonne of sugar, which is expected to be met captively. 

The overall project cost for the PLA venture is estimated at Rs 2,850 crore and being funded through debt of Rs 1,650 crore and the rest via internal accrual. As on January 31, 2026, the company had incurred expenses of nearly Rs 1,421 crore towards the project, which should be completed by the third quarter of fiscal 2027. Further, the company has indicated that at full utilisation level, the yearly revenue and operating margin potential from PLA would be nearly Rs 2,000 crore and 35%, respectively. 

However, given the large size of the project, timely completion with no further material cost overrun, ramp up of utilisation levels post commencement, with gradual pick-up in offtake, as envisaged, will remain monitorable. 

In addition, the UP government announced its Bio Plastic Industrial Policy 2024 in October 2024, to promote bioplastic manufacturing. The policy intends to provide various incentives for eligible companies, including capital and interest subsidy, SGST reimbursement, etc. As indicated by the company, the revised project cost, after considering the capital subsidy, stands at Rs 1,750 crore. 

However, adequate government intervention to ensure enforcement of ban of single-use plastics and timely receipt of incentives would remain critical. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Fund-based working capital line of around Rs 2,400 crore was utilised at 59% on an average for the six months through December 2025. Expected cash accrual of Rs 500-550 crore per annum from the existing business, will suffice to cover yearly debt obligation pertaining to existing debt of Rs 89-90 crore per fiscal in 2026 and 2027 and fund annual maintenance capex of around Rs 100 crore. Further, repayments pertaining to project related debt will commence from the second half of fiscal 2029, providing sufficient cushion between commercialisation of the new project and meeting the underlying repayment. 

# **<u>ESG Profile</u>** 

Crisil Ratings believes the environment, social and governance (ESG) profile of BCML supports its already strong credit risk profile. 

The sugar industry has a moderate impact on the environment, owing to moderate emissions, water consumption and waste generation. The social impact is also moderate, considering the impact of operational activities on employees. The company is focusing on mitigating environmental and social risk. 

# **Key ESG highlights:** 

Energy consumption and greenhouse gas (GHG) emission intensity, as a percentage of revenue, marginally decreased in fiscal 2025, as against the previous fiscal. The company is taking several steps to reduce GHG emissions and for energy conservation. These include investing in lower steam consumption through installation of SRTC (short retention time clarifier) units and optimising imbibition of water quantity to increase the rate of crushing so as to achieve maximum efficiency of equipment at full crush rate. 

Gender diversity improved in fiscal 2025, yet lagged the sector average. The company holds workplace inspections and analyses incident reports on a regular basis. It utilises job safety analysis techniques to identify work-related hazards 

and assess risk. 

Attrition rate has increased in fiscal 2025, but was lower than the sector average. 

As on March 31, 2025, the governance profile is characterised by independent directors and women forming around 57.14% and 42.86% of the board, respectively, and underscoring the company’s commitment to gender diversity) and presence of robust internal control systems and processes. The company has extensive disclosures. 

BCML’s commitment to ESG and integrating sustainability principles throughout the organisation will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence and access to capital markets. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes BCML will continue to benefit from its established market position, superior operating efficiency of the sugar business, sustained contribution from the relatively more stable distillery business and high growth potential for diversification into the new PLA business. The financial risk profile should remain comfortable, with healthy debt metrics. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

- Substantial increase in cash accrual, driven by improvement in business diversity while maintaining market leadership across multiple segments 

- Sustenance of strong financial risk profile and further improvement in debt metrics with average TOL/ANW below 0.40.5 time on a sustained basis 

Healthy build-up of cash surplus 

# **Downward factors** 

Material decline in sugar realisations, significant drop in cane crushing volume, owing to adverse monsoon or pest attacks, sustained decline in contribution from the ethanol business, or adverse government regulations, impacting profitability and cash generation 

- Large debt-funded capex, or acquisition or material infusion into the non-banking financial company associate (Auxilo), impacting financial risk profile and debt metrics; average TOL/ANW in excess of 1.5-1.7 times 

Delay in timely completion and ramp-up of the PLA project impacting cash flow from operations, and straining liquidity 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

BCML is one of the largest integrated sugar manufacturers in India. Operations are forward integrated into manufacturing ethanol, using molasses (a by-product of sugar), and power, using cogeneration from bagasse. It has 10 sugar manufacturing units, 5 distillery plants and 10 cogeneration units spread across eastern and central UP. It has cane crushing capacity of 80,000 TPD, 1,050 KLPD of distillery and 175.7 MW of saleable cogeneration capacity. 

As on December 31, 2025, the promoters held 42.85% stake in BCML, followed by domestic mutual funds (25.24%), foreign shareholders (10.95%) and public and others (holding the residual stake). 

As on December 31, 2025, BCML reported profit after tax (PAT) of Rs 196 crore (Rs 124 crore a year earlier) on net revenue of Rs 4,667 crore (Rs 3,912 crore).
