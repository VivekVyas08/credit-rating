### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A+/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the bank facilities of Shoppers Stop Limited (SSL). 

The ratings continue to reflect the established position of SSL in the retail industry, particularly the departmental stores category, its prudent working capital management, healthy financial risk profile, brand loyalty and strong financial flexibility derived from its association with the K Raheja group. These strengths are partially offset by susceptibility of operating performance to economic downturns, slow ramp-up in new ventures and increasing competition in the apparel retail segment. 

The business risk profile remains healthy, supported by widespread presence in the premium and luxury segments. Additionally, it has been gradually expanding its presence in the value segment through its brand 'Intune'.  SSL has around 295 stores (excluding shop-in-shop) across 73 cities and caters to more than 800 brands. Moreover, SSL's subsidiary, Global SS Beauty Brands Ltd (Global SS), has forged partnerships and secured exclusive distribution rights with several international brands, thereby further strengthening its beauty distribution business. 

During fiscal 2026, consolidated revenue grew ~9% year-on-year to Rs.5043 crore on the back of healthy like-for-like growth of around 5% in department stores that had remained flat for the past two fiscals, continued store additions and steady traction in the beauty distribution segment. Growing premiumisation trend translated into increase in average selling price by 6% on-year and thus reflected in growth of 7% in average Transaction Value. Despite the growth in revenue, the post IndAS operating profitability moderated by to 14.8% in fiscal 2026 (pre Ind-AS : 3.2%) from 15.3% in fiscal 2025 (pre Ind-AS : 3.9%) on account of continued investments towards scaling up value fashion format (84 Intune stores as on March 31, 2026 against 71 stores as on March 31, 2025) as well as   online website of ssbeauty.in and subdued discretionary demand in the third quarter which is generally the strongest for the company. As result, it reported net loss of Rs.36 crore during fiscal 2026. 

Crisil Ratings expects consolidated revenue to grow 9-10% during fiscal 2027 and thereafter at a steady pace of high single digits over the medium term driven by healthy like-for-like growth, revenue accretion from new stores, premiumisation trend and continued expansion of the beauty distribution business.  The company has initiated measures to improve its profitability in the near term including reduction in losses from Intune through improved intake margins, lower discounting and tightening of cost and integration of ssbeauty.in with its main website shopperstop.com. Furthermore, the company plans to focus on driving efficiencies of existing Intune stores in H1FY27 and thereafter evaluate further expansion. This coupled with operating leverage benefit will drive improvement in post Ind-AS operating margins to ~16% (pre Ind-AS : 4- 4.5%) over the medium term. 

Financial risk profile shall remain comfortable with consolidated post Ind-AS adjusted networth of Rs246 crore as on March 31, 2026 (pre Ind-AS adjusted networth of Rs.860 crore). Prudent working capital management alongwith repayment of term debt has resulted in reduction of external debt to Rs236 crore as on March 31, 2026 from Rs 340 crore as on March 31, 2025. However, lease liabilities have increased to Rs3081 crore as of March 2026 from Rs2974 crore in Mach 2025 owing to continued store expansions, resulting in overall post Ind AS debt (lease liabilities plus external debt) remaining stable at Rs.3317 crore in fiscal 2026. As a result, post Ind AS adjusted gearing stood higher at around 13.5 times (pre Ind-AS gearing at 0.3 times) against 12.2 times as on March 31, 2025 (pre IndAS : 0.4 times). The company plans to add 9 

departments stores and renovate 5 stores in the current fiscal. Crisil Rating’s expects annual net cash accruals (Profit after tax plus depreciation less dividend) of Rs.250-300 crore on pre Ind-AS basis over the medium term to  be adequately matched for the funding the operational capital expenditure (capex and deposits) spends of around Rs.180-200 crore and term debt obligation of Rs.50-60 crore. The Company is expected to be gradually debt free over the medium term at standalone level. Furthermore, Crisil Rating expects the company to sustain negative working capital cycle of 100-110 days (111 days during fiscal 2026) with working capital intensity remaining modest and thus reliance on external borrowing shall be limited. Pre Ind-AS adjusted gearing is expected to remain comfortable at 0.1-0.2 times over the medium term; adjusted gearing including for lease liabilities is estimated at 11-13 times. Pre and post Ind-AS interest coverage ratio will  also remain comfortable at over 9 times and 3 times respectively. 

Overall liquidity remains comfortable, supported by cushion available in working capital limit, unencumbered cash surplus of Rs13 crore as on March 31, 2026, and financial flexibility with the company’s ability to access capital markets, as demonstrated earlier. 

# **<u>Analytical Approach</u>**

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Established departmental store retailer with rising non-apparel sales and premiumization initiatives** 

SSL is one of India’s leading retail store chains with retail carpet space of 4.5 million square feet (sq ft) as on March 31, 2026. With a diverse range of offerings (apparel, baby care, footwear, personal accessories, and furniture), the format targets the relatively less price-sensitive, upper and upper-middle-class consumers. The company has established a strong brand equity in this target demographic, with repeat customers (through its loyalty programme, First Citizen) contributing to around nearly 84% of sales. The company has also successfully scaled up its business through regular store additions. As on March 31, 2026, it has 113 department stores, 84 Intune store, 12 premium home concept stores, 73 Specialty Beauty stores and 13 airport stores across India; and aims to add around 9 new stores in fiscal 2027. Continued brand additions and rising share of revenue from existing brands in the beauty distribution channel through Global SS will also support growth over the medium term. The company's product offerings are distinguished from its competitors by a broad nonapparel portfolio that spans categories such as watches, beauty, handbags, sunglasses, footwear, and home goods. Premiumization in these categories has contributed to increased productivity and a higher proportion of non-apparel sales. 

# **Prudent working capital management** 

Inventory management practices are robust. In fiscal 2026, over 70% of revenue was derived from merchandise procured on Returnable outright (ROR) sale-or-return (SOR) basis. This optimal mix ensures an adequate gross margin, while reducing susceptibility to inventory obsolescence and build-up during a slowdown. Besides, negative working capital minimises dependence on the working capital limit. 

# **Healthy financial risk profile** 

Financial risk profile shall remain healthy with pre Ind-AS adjusted networth of around Rs. 860 crore (,) as on March 31, 2026 (post Ind-AS adjusted networth is estimated around Rs. 246 crore) and pre Ind-AS adjusted gearing for external borrowings of around 0.30 time; post Ind AS adjusted gearing (including the lease liabilities of around 13.5 times [12.2 times as on March 31, 2025]. Lease liabilities over the medium term are expected to increase with store additions. Capex for store additions and renovation will be funded through internal accruals. Prudent working capital management will lead to lower dependence on external debt and gradually, the company is expected to become external debt-free on standalone level. Over the medium term pre Ind-AS adjusted gearing will remain comfortable at 0.10-0.20 times; including for lease liabilities, adjusted gearing is estimated at 11-13 times. Pre and post Ind-AS interest coverage ratio to also remain comfortable at over 9 times and 3 times respectively. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Susceptibility of operating performance to economic downturns** 

The branded apparel segment relies heavily on the disposable income of its customer segment and hence remains susceptible to economic cycles, given the discretionary nature of purchases.  In a cautious spending scenario, discretionary segments such as gems and jewellery and apparel are impacted the most while non-discretionary segments such as food and grocery and pharmacy are impacted less. For instance, temporary store closures, restricted mobility and curtailed discretionary spending because of the pandemic restricted growth in fiscals 2021 and fiscal 2022. 

# **Slower than anticipated ramp-up in profitability in new ventures and large annual addition of stores** 

Losses in the new ventures (Intune and ssbbeauty.in) doubled in fiscal 2026 from fiscal 2025 owing to slower growth vis-àvis  continued investments towards expansion.  ,While the company plans to pursue a calibrated expansion strategy and improve the store profitability as well as per square feet productivity over the medium term, weaker-than-expected improvement in store productivity will have a bearing on the profitability and return on capital employed (RoCE). 

Large expansion by retailers can exert pressure on operating margin as earnings from existing stores may not adequately offset losses from new stores. While a large portion of stores might have break even, significant improvement in operating profitability becomes unlikely due to gestation losses from new stores and inflationary pressure on raw material costs. 

# **Exposure to intensifying competition in the apparel retail segment** 

The attractiveness of the apparel segment has led to increasing competition with presence of India’s large corporate groups, including Tatas, Aditya Birla Fashion and Retail Ltd and Reliance Retail Ltd Additionally, the sector has established players such as Lifestyle International Pvt Ltd  and Raymond Lifestyle Ltd. Large global apparel brands such as Marks and Spencer Plc, Inditex SA and GAP have also entered into joint ventures with local partners or expanded their individual presence in the domestic market, further intensifying competition. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

At a standalone level, working capital limits of Rs.148 crore was utilised 67% on average for the 12 months through April 2026. The unutilised portion coupled with unencumbered cash surplus of around Rs 13 crore as on March 31, 2026 will support the overall liquidity. Liquidity will be bolstered by financial flexibility to access capital markets as demonstrated in the past. 

The company continues to fund its capex primarily from internal funds. Over the medium term, Crisil Ratings expects  the company to generate pre Ind-AS net cash accruals of Rs 250-300 crore per annum (post Ind-AS net cash accruals of Rs 600-750 crore per annum), which shall be sufficient to cover yearly operational capex spends and term debt obligations beginning fiscal 2027. Working capital requirement shall remain modest owing to the sustenance of a negative working capital cycle and shall be met through internal cash accruals with limited dependance on working capital borrowings. The Company is expected to be debt free at standalone level in the medium term. In addition to the above, strong parentage of the K Raheja group also supports financial flexibility and overall liquidity. 

# **<u>ESG Profile</u>** 

The environment, social and governance (ESG) profile of SSL supports its strong credit risk profile. 

The retail sector has low environmental impact in the form of low emissions and water consumption and increasing focus on the usage of sustainable packaging. The sector has moderate social impact because of its direct bearing on the health and wellbeing of its workers and customers. 

The company’s increasing focus on addressing ESG risks supports its ESG profile. 

# **Key ESG highlights of SSL:** 

- The company is committed to embedding environmental sustainability across its operations and has undertaken several initiatives in this regard, including implementation of advanced IoT-based monitoring systems across stores to enhance operational efficiency and adoption of optimal power factors to improve energy efficiency 

- On gender diversity, the company has rolled out mentorship and structured career progression programmes to strengthen female representation in its workforce. Accordingly, over fiscal 2023–2025, the share of female employees increased ~2 percentage points to ~33%, remaining higher than the peer average 

- With respect to workplace safety, the company reported nil Lost Time Injury Frequency Rate (LTIFR) and fatalities in fiscal 2025. Furthermore, a higher-than-peer-average share of the workforce (~73%) received health and safety training during the year 

- The company’s governance structure is characterised by 55.55% of its board comprising of independent directors, 22.22% woman directors, along with a dedicated investor grievance redressal system and extensive financial disclosures 

- There is growing importance of ESG among investors and lenders. The company’s commitment to ESG will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence given its access to domestic capital markets. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes SSL’s business risk profile will continue to be healthy over the medium term supported by its established position in the domestic apparel retail sector, strong relationship with leading apparel brands, and wide geographical presence. Also, its financial risk profile is expected to remain comfortable, supported by steady cashgenerating ability, moderate capex and prudent working capital management. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

- Sustained improvement in operating performance with significant increase in scale of operations while maintaining pre Ind-AS operating margins of over 5% (post Ind-AS of 17-18%) leading to steady improvement in cash accruals. Maintenance of strong financial risk profile with efficient working capital management Build-up of surplus cash 

# **Downward factors** 

Lower than anticipated growth with slower than expected ramp up of newly added stores; pre Ind-AS operating margins falling below 4% (post Ind-AS of 12-13%) and post Ind-AS RoCE  weakening below 7% a sustained basis, based on Crisil Ratings adjusted financials. 

Larger than expected debt funded capex or inorganic acquisition, or elongation in working capital, leading to material moderation in debt metrics

All these 295 stores are spread over 4.5 million sq.ft area as on March 31, 2026.
