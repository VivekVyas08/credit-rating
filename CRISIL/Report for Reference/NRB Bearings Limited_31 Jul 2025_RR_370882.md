### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AA-/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the bank facilities and commercial paper programme of NRB Bearings Ltd (NRB). 

Crisil Ratings has taken note of the family settlement arrangement among the promoter family members of the NRB group, which vests sole and exclusive ownership and control of the NRB group with the Harshbeena Sahney Zaveri family (HSZ family), including through the Trilochan Singh Sahney Trust 1 (TSS Trust 1). The arrangement was funded through borrowings secured by pledging shares held by HSZ and TSS Trust 1 in NRB. As on June 30, 2025, 65.9% of the total promoter stake is pledged, down from the peak of 91.35% in March 31, 2025. The repayment of this debt and the release of the pledged shares will remain a key monitorable over the medium term. 

NRB derives 65-67% of its revenue from domestic automotive (auto) original equipment manufacturers (OEMs), 10-12% from the domestic aftermarket and 20-25% from exports. During fiscal 2025, the company registered on-year revenue growth of 9% to Rs 1,199 crore, driven by growth in demand from the domestic auto sector, especially the two-wheeler segment. Growth in revenue was affected by a fire at the company’s plant in Waluj (Maharashtra) which was back to full capacity in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025. The operating income is expected to witness a steady growth over next 2 fiscals, driven by focus on components fetching higher realisations, technology-agnostic products and new products in the electric vehicle (EV) segment. Growth in fiscal 2028 is expected to be higher driven by commencement of new capacities which the company has recently announced entailing a capex of Rs.200 crore. 

Operating margin stabilised at 16.6% during fiscal 2025 in line with fiscal 2024. NRB sustained its gross margin at 54-55%, led by cost-optimisation measures, price hikes and its ability to pass on volatility in input prices. The operating margin is expected to improve gradually over the medium term, benefitting from operating leverage arising from increasing scale of operations, albeit partly offset by inflationary pressure. 

The financial risk profile continues to be supported by the management’s efforts to keep debt under check, given the cyclical nature of the auto business. Consolidated debt, including lease liabilities, stood at Rs 187 crore as on March 31, 2025, up from Rs 178 crore a year earlier, and pertains only to working capital borrowings as the company repaid its long-term debt as of the end of fiscal 2025. The company's liquidity position is comfortable, backed by unencumbered cash surplus of Rs 88 crore as on March 31, 2025. Expected annual net cash accrual of Rs 125-150 crore over the medium term and the planned additional debt of Rs 150 crore will be sufficient to fund total capital expenditure (capex) of around Rs 300-325 crore, including incremental capex, over the medium term. The adjusted gearing stood at 0.2 time as on March 31, 2025, and is expected to remain below 0.3 time over the medium term despite the expected incremental debt. 

The ratings continue to reflect NRB’s market leadership in the needle and cylindrical roller bearing segments, diverse customer profile and strong technical expertise. These strengths are partly offset by large working capital requirement, vulnerability to cyclicality in the end-user industry and pricing pressure from OEMs.

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description</u>** 

# **Strengths:** 

**Market leadership in the needle and cylindrical roller bearing segments in India:** The company has an estimated market share of 60% in the needle bearing industry in India, with strong presence in the cylindrical roller, special tapered roller and special ball bearings segments. This is aided by the promoters’ extensive industry experience, robust in-house research and development facilities and strong client relationships. 

**Diverse customer profile:** NRB has longstanding relationships with Indian and global auto OEMs and Tier 1 vendors. It has customers across segments, including two-wheelers, commercial vehicles, passenger cars, utility vehicles, farm equipment and tractors, off-highway vehicles, railways and defence. Domestic OEMs contributed the most to its revenue (65-67%) in fiscal 2025, while aftermarket and export sales contributed the rest. Despite the large proportion of revenue from OEMs, no single customer with its Tier-1 supplier accounts for more than 10% of the total revenue, thereby limiting customer concentration risk. 

**Strong technical expertise:** NRB has been operating in India for over six decades and has over time built strong technical expertise and knowhow covering the full range of bearing design, from conception to software-aided simulation, testing, validation, benchmarking and production. The product portfolio spans over 3,000 designs, which are also made to suit customer requirements. In fiscal 2000, NRB established a dedicated engineering and design centre. In addition, there is another centre that focusses on process engineering at the plant site in the Waluj Maharashtra Industrial Development Corporation. Besides, its plants use automated production and assembly machinery designed in-house, reflecting the strong technical capabilities of its team. 

**Healthy financial risk profile:** The financial risk profile is supported by healthy networth of Rs 933 crore and moderate gearing of 0.2 time as on March 31, 2025, as against Rs 871 crore and 0.2 time, respectively, a year ago. Despite the expected incremental debt, gearing is expected to remain below 0.3 time over the medium term. The financial risk profile continues to be supported by the management’s efforts to keep debt in check, given the cyclical nature of the auto business. As on March 31, 2025, the consolidated debt, including lease liabilities, stood at Rs 187 crore, up from Rs 178 crore a year earlier. However, the long-term debt was entirely repaid by the end of fiscal 2025. The company's liquidity position is comfortable, with unencumbered cash surplus of Rs 88 crore as of March 31, 2025, and expected net cash accrual of Rs 125-150 crore over the medium term. The healthy cash accrual, combined with the planned additional debt of Rs 150 crore, will be adequate to fund total capex of over Rs 300 crore, including incremental capex, over the medium term. 

The total outside liabilities to adjusted networth ratio improved to 0.39 time as on March 31, 2025, from 0.41 time a year earlier, but is expected to increase to 0.4-0.45 time over the medium term due to the anticipated debt of Rs 150 crore. The repayment of long-term borrowings and subsequent decrease in finance costs in fiscal 2025 resulted in a significant increase in interest coverage to 20.21 times from 8.33 times in fiscal 2024. With sustained profitability and moderate debt, the interest coverage is expected to remain above 9 times over the medium term. 

# **Weaknesses:** 

**Large working capital requirement:** Due to the just-in-time delivery to OEMs and large number of product stock keeping units across more than 500 products, inventory is high at 130-170 days. Inventory levels are maintained higher due to longer lead times for imported raw materials. The company also holds strategic inventory as a sole supplier to certain customers to ensure uninterrupted operations. Furthermore, payment terms from auto component manufacturers vary, resulting in receivables of 60-90 days. Higher receivables days are also on account of exports to the EU and US, wherein the shipment transit time is of 4–6 weeks. Also, the company imports 60-70% of the key raw material (steel) from Europe (mainly Germany), Japan and other countries. Considering a transit time of 90 days and to avoid the impact of supply chain issues arising from geopolitical issues, the company is required to maintain huge inventory. 

**Vulnerability to cyclical demand in the auto bearings segment and to pricing pressure from OEMs:** High dependence of bearing manufacturers on the auto sector exposes them to cyclicality in demand. While bearing manufacturers enjoy reasonable pricing flexibility with OEMs, backed by mutual interdependence, and capital and technology intensity of operations, price negotiations happen with a lag, leading to price adjustment delays and impact on profitability in the interim. Furthermore, in case of prolonged slowdown and decreasing demand, it is not always possible for OEMs to completely pass on input price increases to end users. Hence, any significant hike in prices is absorbed jointly by the OEMs and their component suppliers. Additionally, any significant decline in demand will lead to under-absorption of overheads, thereby affecting the profitability of component suppliers. 

# **<u>Liquidity: Strong</u>** 

Cash accrual is expected at Rs 120-150 crore per annum, sufficient to meet debt obligation over the medium term. Liquidity is also supported by cash surplus of Rs 88 crore as on March 31, 2025. Further, the fund-based working capital limit of Rs 

442 crore was utilised 16% on average for the six months ended May 30, 2025. The buffer in the form of unutilised limit provides sufficient cushion in case of exigencies. 

# **<u>Outlook: Stable</u>** 

NRB will sustain its strong business risk profile with growth in demand from the auto industry and sustained profitability, resulting in higher cash accrual. The financial risk profile is expected to remain healthy due to prudent funding of capex and steady cash accrual. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Significant increase in scale of operations while maintaining operating margin, leading to return on capital employed ratio improving to over 16-18% on a sustained basis 

Sustenance of healthy financial and liquidity risk profiles 

# **Downward factors** 

Decline in revenues or deterioration of operating margin to below 15% on a sustained basis. 

Sizeable stretch in the working capital or debt-funded capex/inorganic expansion leading to deterioration in capital structure, with gearing above 1 time on a sustained basis 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Founded by the late Mr Trilochan Singh Sahney in 1965, NRB manufactures needle, cylindrical, special ball, and taper-roller bearings. It also makes other friction solution components such as planetary shafts, crank pins and kingpins. The company has four subsidiaries: NRB Holdings Ltd (holding company of NRB Bearings Europe GmbH, and NRB Bearings USA Inc), SNL Bearings Ltd (SNL) and NRB Bearings (Thailand) Ltd. NRB's manufacturing facilities are at Thane, Aurangabad, Jalna, and Waluj in Maharashtra; Hyderabad; and Pantnagar in Uttarakhand. SNL's facility is at Ranchi in Jharkhand, while NRB Thailand's plant is at Rayong in Thailand. The product engineering centre at Turbhe (Maharashtra) and process and advanced materials-based engineering centre at Waluj are government accredited. 

Ms Harshbeena S Zaveri, daughter of Mr Trilochan Singh Sahney, is the vice chairwoman and managing director of NRB and the chairwoman of the board of SNL.
