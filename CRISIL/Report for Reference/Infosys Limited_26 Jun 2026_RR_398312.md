### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the long-term debt programme and commercial paper programme of Infosys Limited (Infosys). 

The ratings continue to reflect the strong market position of Infosys, being the second largest player in the Indian information technology (IT) services space, the company’s healthy operating efficiency and strong financial risk profile. These strengths are tempered by intense competition in the global IT services industry, both from established peers and - emerging AI driven disruption 

Infosys reported revenue growth of 9.6% in FY2026 to Rs. 178,650 crores, primarily supported by currency tailwinds due to INR depreciation. Growth in constant currency (CC) terms remained modest at around 3.1%, driven by key segments such as Financial Services and Manufacturing, as well as contributions from acquisitions. However, overall growth was constrained by muted volume expansion amid a challenging macroeconomic environment. 

Revenue growth in CC terms is expected to remain moderate in FY2027, at ~1.5%–3.5%, supported by ramp-up of large deals and increasing traction in AI-led transformation initiatives. However, this will likely be partially offset by persistent macroeconomic uncertainties in key markets (US ~56%, Europe ~32%), continued caution in discretionary spending, and pricing pressures arising from AI-driven productivity gains. 

Basis Crisil Ratings–adjusted figures, operating margins for FY ’26 improved by ~90 basis points from the previous year to ~25.3% (vs ~24.4% in FY25), driven by cost optimization under Project Maximus, favorable currency movements, and improved pricing realization, which helped offset headwinds such as higher employee costs and increased investments in AI and growth initiatives. Going forward, Crisil Ratings expects margins to remain steady in the range of 24%–26% over FY27 and the medium term, supported by continued focus on efficiency and delivery optimization, though partly offset by AI-led productivity pass-throughs and ongoing capability investments. 

The company’s financial risk profile remains strong, supported by robust net worth of ~Rs. 75,000 crore and healthy liquidity of ~Rs. 47,000 crores as on 31<sup>st</sup> March 2026, with nil debt. Strong internal accruals of more than ~Rs. 17,000 crores are expected to comfortably fund capex requirements. Infosys also undertook a buyback of Rs. 18,000 crores in FY26 which was in-line with the company’s ~85% capital allocation policy. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of Infosys and its subsidiaries, held directly or indirectly, as all the companies have a common management and are in the same business. 

Goodwill on acquisitions have been amortised over five years. 

With adoption of Ind AS 116 with effect from April 1, 2019, long-term lease liabilities are included in debt along with related adjustments in Ebitda for depreciation and amortisation and interest costs.

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Leading position in the Indian IT services space** 

Infosys is the second-largest listed Indian IT services company in terms of revenue. It has a diversified range of offerings across service segments and industry verticals. Most of the revenue continues to come from digital services, driven by higher demand for cost optimisation programmes and other digital products and services. In terms of verticals, Infosys derived about 27.9% of its revenue from the banking, financial services, and insurance (BFSI) segment during fiscal 2026. The company also has presence in other verticals, such as Manufactuing (16.3%), Energy, Utilities, Resources & Services (13.3%), Retail (12.9%), Communication (12.2%), Hi-Tech (7.8%), Life Sciences (6.9%), and others. 

# **Large scale of operations and healthy operating efficiency** 

The business risk profile of Infosys is supported by its leading market position, large scale of operations with a skilled resource base of 328,594 employees (as on March 31, 2026), proven project execution skills, and strong offshore delivery capability. The company continued to win large deals amounting to $14.9 billion during fiscal 2026, compared with $11.6 billion in the previous fiscal, which will fuel revenue growth. Revenue growth and profitability are supported by premium pricing power, increasing share of digital and AI-led services, and a diversified service portfolio, which are critical in large deal wins. Operating margins are expected to remain stable, supported by moderation in attrition, an optimized onsite– offshore mix, and disciplined utilization levels, though partly offset by AI-led productivity pass-throughs and ongoing capability investments. The company’s profitability remains among the best in the industry, underpinned by its favorable revenue mix, cost optimization initiatives, and strong delivery capabilities. 

# **Strong financial risk profile and robust liquidity** 

Infosys has a strong financial risk profile, driven by a solid networth of ~Rs 75,000 crore and surplus cash of ~Rs 47,000 crore as on March 31, 2026. Net cash accrual stood at ~Rs 15,700 crore for fiscal 2026, after a dividend payout of Rs 18,656 crore (Crisil Ratings adjusted figures) along with a buyback of ~Rs. 18,000 crores. The total debt of Rs 9,176 crore as on March 31, 2026, is completely attributed to lease financing. Despite the policy to return 85% of free operating cash flow to shareholders cumulatively over the five years from fiscal 2025, liquidity will remain robust, supported by sufficient funds to finance working capital and capital expenditure (capex) requirement over the medium term. Infosys is also likely to invest in niche acquisitions to strengthen its domain expertise over the medium term. These will be largely funded through cash surplus and healthy accrual. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Exposure to intense competition in the global IT industry** 

The moderation in discretionary spending by large clients has impacted revenue growth in the IT services industry over the past two fiscals, particularly in traditional outsourcing services. Furthermore, as a leading IT services player, Infosys faces competition from domestic peers such as Tata Consultancy Services Ltd, HCL Technologies Ltd, and Wipro Ltd, as well as global players such as Capgemini SE, Cognizant Technology Solutions, and Accenture Plc. While deal wins remained strong, supported by cost optimization and AI-led transformation initiatives, cautious client spending and macroeconomic uncertainties continue to weigh on growth, intensifying competitive pressures. 

- Additionally, the increasing adoption of AI and automation poses a structural risk to the traditional labour intensive delivery model, as clients achieve similar outcomes with lower human effort, thereby compressing deal sizes and pricing. The rise of AI-native service providers and outcome-based delivery models may further intensify competition and exert pressure on revenue growth and margins. Despite the challenging environment, Infosys’ focus on expanding capabilities in highermargin digital, consulting, and AI-led services, along with increasing automation and efficiency in core delivery, is expected to mitigate the impact on profitability over the medium term. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Superior 

Liquidity remains superior driven by cash surplus of ~Rs 47,000 crore as on March 31, 2026. The company has moderate capex plans, which include acquisitions of companies from diverse domains. Infosys does not have any long-term debt. Crisil Ratings believes Infosys will maintain strong liquidity over the medium term, as the existing funds and expected cash accrual will be more than sufficient to finance working capital, capex, investment in various subsidiaries, as well as capital return to investors. 

# **<u>ESG Profile</u>** 

Crisil Ratings believes the Environment, Social and Governance (ESG) profile of Infosys supports its already strong credit risk profile, which benefits from support from the Government of India (GoI). 

The IT sector has a low impact on the environment because of the inherent nature of digital services, core operations as well as products. The sector has a social impact because of its large workforce. Infosys has continuously focused on mitigating its environmental and social impact. 

# **Key ESG highlights:** 

Infosys continues to be carbon neutral for the seventh year in a row in fiscal 2026, when factoring in all emissions categories (scope 1, 2, and 3). 

Eleven campuses have achieved TRUE Zero Waste certification, reflecting the scale and consistency of our circular economy approach across geographies.Attrition rate for Infosys’ IT services operations (excluding business process 

management services) dropped to 12.6% in fiscal 2026 compared fiscal 2023 level of 20.9% and was lower than the peer average. 

In fiscal 2026, there were zero reported instances of material data breaches. 

- The company’s governance structure is characterized by a majority independent Board (7 out of 9 directors, ~78%), with women directors comprising ~22% of the Board. All key Board committees—including audit, nomination and remuneration, risk management, stakeholders’ relationship, corporate social responsibility, and ESG—are fully independent, supported by comprehensive and transparent financial disclosures. 

There is growing importance of ESG among investors and lenders. The commitment of Infosys to ESG principles will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence, given the high share of market borrowings in the company’s debt and its access to domestic and foreign capital markets. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes Infosys will maintain its strong position in the Indian IT services sector and will sustain its robust financial risk profile and liquidity over the medium term. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Downward factors:** 

Sustained slowdown in revenue growth or decline in operating profit before depreciation, interest, and tax (OPBDIT) margin below 20%, due to regulatory changes in operating regions, or reduction in client spending, weakening the business risk profile. 

Consistent reduction in cash surplus due to large acquisitions or capital return to investors, affecting financial flexibility 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Infosys is a large Indian IT services company, offering a range of digital and traditional IT services. Digital services include revenue from emerging segments such as artificial intelligence-based analytics and big data, engineering digital products and Internet of Things (IoT), application programme interface, and micro services, modernisation of legacy technology systems, migration to cloud applications, and implementation of advanced cyber security systems. Traditional IT services include application development and management services, product engineering and management, infrastructure management services, enterprise application implementation, support and integration services. Infosys caters to industry verticals such as financial services, retail, communication, manufacturing, hi-tech, life sciences, and energy, utilities, resources and services. 

As on March 31, 2026, Infosys had 1,965 active clients and presence in 59 countries. The promoters held 13.30% stake in the company as on March 31, 2026.
