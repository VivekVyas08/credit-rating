### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AA-/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the fixed deposit programme, certificate of deposits and short-term fixed deposits of The Jammu and Kashmir Bank Limited (J&K Bank). 

The ratings continue to factor in J&K Bank’s healthy capitalisation profile along with the importance of the bank to the government of Jammu and Kashmir (J&K) and Ladakh, given its importance to the financial ecosystem in the geography and its strong position in the union territories of J&K and Ladakh.  Further, ratings also continue to factor in the bank’s healthy resource profile with higher than industry average current and savings account (CASA) ratio. 

These ratings, however, are partially offset by the vulnerability of the asset quality to socio-political sensitivities and regulatory developments, although the same has been improving in the last few fiscals; bank’s small scale of operations in the overall banking industry with geographical concentration and improving, albeit modest, earnings profile. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

For arriving at the ratings, Crisil Ratings has assessed the standalone credit risk profile of J&K Bank. The ratings also factor in the support, in case of any requirement, from the governments of J&K and Ladakh, given the importance and strong position of the bank in the union territories. 

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

### **Healthy capitalisation, aided by steady accruals as well as support from the J&K government and the government of Ladakh** 

The bank’s capital position remains healthy, with networth of Rs 16,750 crore as on March 31, 2026 (Rs 14,252 crore as on March 31, 2025), supported by steady internal accruals. Moreover, as on March 31, 2026, the bank reported a common equity tier (CET) 1 of 13.54% and an overall capital adequacy ratio (CAR) of 16.55%. 

It is the largest bank in J&K and Ladakh region and given its high systemic importance to the territory’s economy, the government of J&K has extended need-based support to the bank in the past and is expected to continue doing so. The bank has received Rs 1,000 crore from the government of J&K since fiscal 2019 till date in the form of equity capital (excluding capital raised via employee stock purchase schemes). This strongly reflects the intent of the government to extend support to the bank on an ongoing basis. The governments of J&K and Ladakh, cumulatively, have retained majority stake in the bank even after the change in the political status of J&K and are expected to continue holding majority stake in the bank over the medium term. 

Thus, the bank is expected to get continued support from the government of J&K and the government of Ladakh, as and when required, as it is the primary lender for the union territories. Moreover, in addition to the governments of J&K and Ladakh, which will retain majority stake in the bank over the foreseeable future, the involvement of the central government and expectation of potential support from it, has also increased after change in the political status of J&K. Any material changes in the shareholding and/or change in the importance of the bank to the governments of J&K and Ladakh will remain a key rating sensitivity factor. 

**Healthy resource profile** 

J&K Bank's resource profile remains healthy evidenced by its stable retail deposit base and leadership position in the union territories of J&K and Ladakh, which allows it to hold majority of the territory's deposit market. As on March 31, 2026, the bank’s overall deposits stood at Rs 1,65,354 crore, which marks an annual growth of ~11% over fiscal 2025. Of this, ~83% were housed in the union territory of J&K. 

The bank’s deposit franchise is expected to remain healthy and benefit from its strong foothold in the deposit market of the territory. Furthermore, the share of low-cost CASA deposits in total deposits stood healthy at ~45.7% as on March 31, 2026, which reduced from 47.0% as on March 31, 2025. However, the same is significantly higher than the industry average. 

Also, the fixed deposit (FD) base of the bank is granular in nature, with ~26% of the FDs under Rs 5 lakh and ~66% of the FDs under Rs 1 crore ticket size as on March 31, 2026. The depositor profile base for the bank remains strong with wellspread maturity profile of deposits. Additionally, retail term deposit renewal rate over the past five fiscals has been in the range of 80-90%. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Improving asset quality, albeit vulnerable to socio-political sensitivities and regulatory developments** 

Asset quality for the bank continued to improve during fiscal 2026, with its gross non-performing asset (GNPA) reducing to 2.5% as on March 31, 2026, as compared with 3.4% as on March 31, 2025, and 4.1% as on March 31, 2024. The improvement was primarily driven by healthy recoveries or upgradations and lower slippage (as a percentage of opening gross advances) which have reduced to 0.8% during fiscal 2026 (1.0% in fiscal 2025). 

As on March 31, 2026, the provisioning coverage ratio (PCR; excluding technical provisions) maintained by the bank was ~74.9%, leading to comfortable net NPAs (net non-performing assets) of 0.6%. In terms of segmental GNPAs, the asset quality of bank’s corporate portfolio has seen a significant improvement with GNPA reducing to 2.5% as on March 31, 2026, from 5.3% as on March 31, 2025. Over the last few years, the bank has provided for the stressed accounts in this book and has also been cautious in account selection. 

Given that the bank has strengthened its underwriting practices and has also established a dedicated branch for impaired asset recoveries, hence asset quality is further expected to improve. Nevertheless, given the bank’s exposure towards micro, small and medium enterprises (MSME) segment, the asset quality of the bank remains vulnerable to economic and regulatory developments. Moreover, the bank’s portfolio remains highly susceptible to socio-political developments in the union territories of J&K and Ladakh, given the high regional concentration in operations. 

# **Improving, albeit modest, earnings profile** 

Profitability for the bank has improved with an all-time high profit after tax (PAT) of Rs 2,363 crore in fiscal 2026 as compared to a PAT of Rs 2,082 crore in fiscal 2025. It reported Return on assets (RoA) of 1.4% during fiscal 2026, as against 1.3% in fiscal 2025 (1.2% in fiscal 2024). The earning profile was supported by improvement in the operating expenses as well as controlled credit costs. Operating expenses as a percentage of average total assets improved to 2.1% for fiscal 2026 from 2.5% in fiscal 2025. Further, credit costs stood nil over last two fiscals. 

However, the reported net interest margin (NIM) of the bank moderated to 3.6% in fiscal 2026 from 3.9% in fiscal 2025, primarily because of faster transmission of RBI repo rate cuts to advances, while the transmission of rate changes to the liability side being slower due to rising competition in the region. However, NIM is expected to improve going forward with the bank’s increased focus on the retail loans. Improvement in the earnings profile and its sustenance will remain monitorable. 

# **Small, albeit growing, scale of operations with geographical concentration** 

Total gross advances for the bank grew by ~17% in fiscal 2026 and stood at Rs 1,24,981 crores as on March 31, 2026, as compared to Rs 1,06,985 crore as on March 31, 2025. Further, total deposits stood at Rs 1,65,354 crore as on March 31, 2026, growing from Rs 1,48,569 crore as on March 31, 2025. However, J&K Bank remains a small-sized bank with a market share of less than 1% in the overall banking sector. 

As on March 31, 2026, of the total advances - 67% were housed in the territory of J&K and Ladakh region, which has come down from 73% as on March 31, 2022. The same indicates improvement in the geographical diversification outside the J&K region. Further, the bank has started doing co-lending partnerships with fintechs and plans to add new branches as well in the rest of India region to reduce its geographical concentration. 

While the bank plans to reduce its concentration risk to the union territory of J&K by expanding its retail portfolio across India, the same will remain monitorable going forward. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

The bank's liquidity position is strong, supported by a strong retail deposit base that forms significant part of the total deposits. As on March 31, 2026, the bank’s liquidity coverage ratio (LCR) was 115.74% and its excess statutory liquidity ratio (SLR) was 5.62%. The bank's liquidity also benefits from access to systemic sources of funds such as the liquidity adjustment facility from the RBI, access to the call money market, and refinance limits from development institutions. 

**<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

J&K Bank’s capitalisation and resource profile is expected to remain healthy being aided by continued support from the governments of J&K and Ladakh, given the bank’s strategic importance and stable retail deposit base and leadership position in the union territories of J&K and Ladakh. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Sustained improvement in the GNPA ratio to below 3.5% 

Significant improvement in the earnings profile demonstrated by sustained improvement in RoA Increased geographical diversity of operations 

# **Downward factors** 

Material and prolonged weakening in the asset quality reflected in the GNPAs rising above 6%, leading to moderation in the earnings profile 

Significant and sharp reduction in cushion in capital adequacy ratios over regulatory stipulation 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

J&K Bank, headquartered in Srinagar, was established in 1938. As on March 31, 2026, The J&K and Ladakh governments owns 59.4% shareholding in the bank. The bank provides a variety of products including retail loans, corporate loans, agriculture loans as well as MSME loans. As on March 31, 2026, it had a branch network of 1,017 branches, of which 878 branches operate in the regions of J&K and Ladakh. 

For fiscal 2026, the bank reported a net profit of Rs 2,363 crore on a total income of Rs 14,085 crore. This was against a net profit and total income of Rs 2,082 crore and 13,666 crore respectively for fiscal 2025.

# **<u>Any other information:</u>** 

Crisil Ratings also took note of the demand order received by the bank, as intimated by the bank on February 5, 2025, and as on March 24, 2026, pertaining to the goods and services tax (GST) liability. The demand notice is related to interest receivable under the transfer pricing mechanism (TPM) between the bank’s corporate headquarters and branches from common pool of funds, which has been treated as financial services and GST has been levied thereon. 

Crisil Ratings understand from the management that the said matter is under litigation. However, High Court J&K has given a stay order on both the above orders, and the same is unlikely to have any material financial impact on the bank. Nevertheless, the final judgement in this regard is awaited and will remain monitorable.
