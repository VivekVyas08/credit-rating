### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ratings on the bank facilities of Krishna Institute of Medical Sciences Ltd (KIMS) at **‘** Crisil AA/Stable/Crisil A1+’. 

The ratings continue to reflect the established market position of the ‘KIMS’ group in Telangana and Andhra Pradesh clusters through its network of fifteen hospitals under the KIMS Hospital brand, and sound operational efficiencies, as reflected in stable occupancy and high average revenue per operating bed per day (ARPOBD), leading to healthy operating profitability. The rating also benefits from the adequate and improving financial risk profile of the KIMS group. These strengths are partially offset by high dependence on the flagship hospital in Secunderabad for revenue and profit, geographical concentration of its hospitals, and regulatory risks associated with the hospital sector. 

Crisil Ratings has taken note of the successful completion of KIMS’ Qualified Institutional Placement (QIP), through which the company raised Rs.1,500 crore. The issue opened on June 16, 2026, and was concluded on June 19, 2026 and was oversubscribed by ~4.5 times. The funds were received in the designated escrow account on June 19, 2026, followed by the completion of share allotment on June 20, 2026. Of the total proceeds, approximately 75% (around Rs.1,125 crore) is earmarked for debt repayment by June 30, 2026 across multiple lenders. This significant deleveraging initiative is expected to strengthen the company’s balance sheet, improve its leverage metrics, and enhance overall financial flexibility, thereby supporting its credit profile. 

Crisil Ratings also notes KIMS’ recent announcement regarding a proposed Extraordinary General Meeting (EGM) scheduled for July 9, 2026, to seek approval for a preferential issue of 77.02 lakh fully convertible warrants to its promoters and promoter group entities, aimed at raising Rs.600 crore. Each warrant is priced at Rs.779 and is convertible into one equity share within a period of 18 months from the date of allotment. As per the terms, subscribers will pay 25% of the issue price at the time of subscription, with the balance payable upon exercise of the warrants during the conversion period. Of the total proceeds, approximately 25% is expected to be received in the second quarter of fiscal 2027, with the remaining 75% anticipated in the first half of fiscal 2028. 

KIMS reported a 26% year-on-year revenue growth during fiscal 2026 achieving Rs.3930 crores, driven by a strong pace of bed additions, improvement in average revenue per operating bed per day (ARPOBD) and healthy occupancy. The company added approximately 1,600 beds, including the commissioning of its 800-bed greenfield hospitals at Bangalore and through inorganic expansions in Maharashtra, Telangana, and Kerala. In fiscals 2027 and 2028, Crisil Ratings expects KIMS to register revenue growth of ~18-20%, driven by steady bed additions across new hospitals, including Nashik, Thane, Bangalore, Kondapur, and Rajahmundry in the recent past, as well as expected addition of 900 beds through brownfield expansions and new specialties (mother & child and oncology) in existing hospitals. Growth in ARPOBD is also expected to drive to double digit revenue growth. 

KIMS’ operating margins declined to ~21% in fiscal 2026 from 26.3% in the previous year, primarily due to operating losses associated with the commissioning of new hospitals in Bangalore, Nashik, Thane, and Kollam, which collectively recorded operating losses of ~Rs.134 crore.  Besides, operating profitability was also impacted by a slower-than-expected revenue ramp-up in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026, largely attributable to pending insurance and Central Government Health 

Scheme (CGHS) empanelment, particularly at the Nashik and Thane hospitals where insurance contributes nearly 50% of the patient mix. While General Insurance Public Sector Association (GIPSA) empanelment has since been completed at Thane and Nashik, empanelment for the Bangalore hospitals is expected to be finalized during the second quarter of fiscal 2027. Nevertheless, all new hospitals, except the Electronic City unit in Bangalore, have achieved operating breakeven in the first quarter of fiscal 2027; the latter is expected to break even in the second quarter of fiscal 2027.  Over the near to medium term, KIMS’ operating margins are expected to improve to 22–23%, supported by the steady ramp-up of these new facilities, alongside continued strong performance in established clusters such as Telangana and Andhra Pradesh, besides better case mix. 

KIMS Group’s financial risk profile has improved post the recent QIP fundraise of Rs.1,500 crore, with ~75% (Rs.1,125 crore) is expected to be utilized for debt reduction by June 30, 2026, and the balance retained as liquidity to support future capex and initial losses from new units. Accordingly, total debt (including lease liabilities) is expected to reduce to approximately Rs.3,000–3,100 crore by March 31, 2027. This is likely to result in net debt/earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA, post Ind-AS) improving to below 3.0x in fiscal 2027, besides improving leverage metrics and interest cover. Further, in the absence of any large capex plans beyond brownfield expansions and routine maintenance, net debt/EBITDA is projected at ~2.0–2.5x over the medium term. This will remain a key monitorable. 

Earlier, the financial risk profile moderated in fiscal 2026, due to front ended capital expenditure which were majorly debt funded. The company incurred capex of ~Rs.1600 crore in fiscal 2026, largely towards greenfield hospitals in Thane and Bengaluru, along with brownfield expansions across locations such as Kondapur, Srikakulam, Anantapur, and Ongole. Consequently, long-term debt increased to Rs.2,673 crore as of March 31, 2026, from Rs.1,776 crore a year earlier. Additionally, lease liabilities rose to around Rs.1,010 crore (Rs.651 crore as of March 31, 2025) due to newly added leased assets. As a result, total debt, including lease liabilities, increased to Rs.4,253 crore as of March 31, 2026 (Rs. 2,557 crore as of March 31, 2025), leading to a moderation in gearing to 1.80x (1.22x) and a rise in net debt/EBITDA to 5.3x (3.2x in fiscal 2025). 

Recently, Dr. Bhaskar Rao, founder promoter of KIMS has raised loans aggregating Rs.750 crores against pledge of 10.7% stake in KIMS (nil as on September 30, 2025). The loans were utilized as follows: Rs. 400 crores to repay loans taken by his brothers Mr. Seeniah Bollineni and Mr. Krishnaiah Bollineni and other family members, which had been subsequently extended to Dr Bhaskar Rao, and Rs. 350 crores for loans to promoter-owned companies and for strategic investments. The interest on these loans is being serviced from the cash flow and interest earned on investments. The promoter plans to repay the personal loan within 36 months, post which the share pledge is expected to be released. 

# **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

For arriving at its ratings, Crisil Ratings has fully consolidated the business and financial risk profiles of KIMS and its subsidiaries, which are strategically important to KIMS and have a significant degree of operational integration with it. These companies are together referred to as the KIMS group. Crisil Ratings considers these entities as being strategic to KIMS in view of their common line of business and management and strong integration with the operations of KIMS. Furthermore, the group allows transfer of funds among entities depending upon the requirement. 

Crisil Ratings has amortised the goodwill of Rs 212 crore on acquisition of Sarvejana Healthcare Pvt Ltd (SHPL) over a period of five years starting April 2022.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Established market position:** Through its network of 25 hospitals under the KIMS Hospital brand, the group has an established presence in the south India market. The group also has a long operational track record of more than two decades in the tertiary and the quaternary healthcare segments and benefits from the strong brand reputation and the extensive experience of the group’s promoters in the healthcare industry. 

In terms of specialties, cardiac treatments account for the highest share of revenue at ~17%, followed by neurosciences at ~11% and renal sciences at ~9%. With the acquisition of SHPL in fiscal 2023, which is primarily an orthopedics player, the share of revenue from orthopedics increased to 14% compared with 5% before the pandemic. The balance is spread across oncology, mother and child, gastric sciences and others. 

The group, with a combined bed capacity of 6464 beds as of March 2026, is one of the leading players in the tertiary care segment in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. The group’s Secunderabad facility is one of the largest single location hospitals with ~1,000 beds, offering multi-specialties. Addition of SHPL has further enhanced the market presence of the KIMS group in Hyderabad, which is a core market. KIMS commissioned two greenfield hospitals in Bangalore (Karnataka Cluster) and one greenfield hospital in Thane (Maharashtra cluster) during fiscal 2026 

KIMS already has presence in Maharashtra through Nagpur facility (acquisition of SPANV Mediresearch Pvt Ltd) in fiscal 2023 and Nashik Facility which was commissioned in October 2025. Besides, KIMS also signed O&M agreements and expanded its presence into Kerala market as well with latest being the lease agreement signed with Avitis Super Specialty 

(Palakkad) during April 2026. The expansion into adjacent markets contribute to revenue diversity and enhances the market position of KIMS in south India. The company’s revenues are expected to register growth of ~18-20% over medium term. 

**Sound operating efficiency:** The group has reported above-average operating profitability of more than 25% (barring an exceptional 31.8% in fiscal 2022, which was buoyed by high occupancy during the second wave of the Covid-19 pandemic) despite constant capacity addition, including through acquisitions. The policy of providing equity partnerships to its key doctors has enabled the company to attract talent in Tier-2 locations and maintain low attrition and tight control over costs. These factors, coupled with prudent capital spending, have helped KIMS to turn around acquired hospitals as well as achieve breakeven at newer hospitals in a short span of time, translating into healthy operating capabilities. 

However, operating profitability declined to ~21% during fiscal 2026 from 26.3% during fiscal 2025 due to flattish occupancy levels at 50-50.5% with the commissioning of new hospitals in Bangalore, Thane & Kollam.  Further, revenue ramp-up at newer units witnessed some delays, primarily due to pending insurance and CGHS empanelment, which impacted profitability as insurance and government scheme patients form a significant share of the patient mix at these facilities. While GIPSA empanelment has been completed at Thane and Nashik, empanelment for the Bangalore hospitals are expected to be finalized shortly. Besides, all new hospitals are expected to have broken even by the second quarter of fiscal 2027. Operating margins are projected to improve to 22–23% over the near to medium term, supported by a steady rampup in these facilities, continued strong performance in established clusters such as Telangana and Andhra Pradesh and other metropolitan cities, where ramp-up and stabilization timelines are typically faster compared to smaller cities. 

Healthy profitability and increasing occupancy, enabled steady improvement in the group’s return on capital employed (RoCE), which stood at above 24.3% in fiscal 2023, compared with 13-15% in earlier years. With the company entering capex cycle, RoCE declined to 16.5% in fiscal 2025 and ~11% in fiscal 2026. RoCE will settle at ~12-15% over the near to medium term, in line with better profitability, and continuing capex. 

**Adequate and improving financial risk profile:** KIMS Group’s financial risk profile has improved post the recent QIP fundraise of Rs.1,500 crore, with ~75% (Rs.1,125 crore) is expected to be utilized for debt reduction by June 30, 2026, and the balance retained as liquidity to support future capex and initial losses from new units. Accordingly, total debt (including lease liabilities) is expected to reduce to approximately Rs.3,000–3,100 crore by March 31, 2027, from Rs.4,253 crores at March 31, 2025. This is likely to result in net debt/EBITDA improving to below 3.0x in fiscal 2027, from 5.2x a year earlier, besides improving leverage metrics and interest cover. Further, in the absence of any large capex plans beyond brownfield expansions and routine maintenance, net debt/EBITDA is projected at ~2.0–2.5x over the medium term. This will remain a key monitorable. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Revenue and geographical concentration risks:** The group has had a high reliance on its flagship hospital in Secunderabad, which contributed 60% of revenue and 66% of Ebitda in fiscal 2018. With steady organic and inorganic expansion, this has reduced the revenue dependance to <35% in fiscal 2026. 

While the contribution of the Secunderabad unit has reduced in recent years, the flagship hospital is likely to continue to be the key revenue and profitability driver (contributing to at least 30-35% of overall operating profits) over the medium term exposing the group to revenue and geographical concentration risks. Besides, a sizeable number of the company’s other hospitals are in Telangana and Andhra Pradesh (over 90% of its revenue), which renders operations partly vulnerable to any regulations imposed by authorities in these states. The geographical diversification is modest compared to other peers in the healthcare space. Even as the group continues to expand in existing markets, it is also diversifying geographically by building or acquiring new hospitals in the neighbouring states of Karnataka (Bengaluru), Maharashtra (Thane, Nashik, Nagpur) and Kerala (mostly O&M). Land for a new hospital has been acquired at Chennai, but construction is not expected to commence for atleast a year. KIMS commissioned two greenfield hospitals in Bangalore (Karnataka Cluster) during fiscal 2026; earlier Nashik hospital commenced operations in October 2024 and Thane hospital commenced operations in May 2025. However, there are already established players in these geographies, and the ability of KIMS to ramp-up occupancy will be critical to reduce revenue dependence on its flagship hospital. 

**Exposure to regulatory risk:** The group, like other hospital chains, remains exposed to regulations which may come into play, as introduced. For instance, the performance of private hospitals was significantly impacted on account of price caps imposed on cardiac stents and knee implants in the last quarter of fiscal 2017. Besides, the cap on cash transactions, up to Rs 2 lakh, also caused temporary challenges when introduced in fiscal 2018. In addition, the recent Supreme Court proposal to standardise the prices for different procedures across public and private hospitals, if implemented, will be monitorable. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Liquidity is supported by an unencumbered cash balance of Rs.90 crore as of March 31, 2026, along with adequate headroom in existing fund-based facilities. Expected annual cash accruals of Rs.600–800 crore are likely to be sufficient to meet annual repayment obligations of Rs.~300-400 crore per annum and partly fund expansion requirements. The group has also secured long-tenure debt for capex funding, resulting in relatively moderate annual repayment obligations. The longer maturity profile of borrowings, in line with industry practices, provides sufficient time for new hospitals to stabilize without exerting pressure on liquidity. 

Additionally, proceeds from the QIP are expected to alleviate balance sheet leverage and support funding requirements for capex and initial losses of new units. Dividend payouts are likely to remain modest over the medium term, with a continued focus on reinvesting cash flows to support growth and deleveraging. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes that KIMS will continue to benefit over the medium term from its established presence in the South Indian market and diversified revenue stream, including from acquired and upcoming new hospitals. Continuing healthy operating profitability will ensure good annual cash generation, which along with prudent capex going forward will lead to gradual improvement in debt metrics. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward Factors** 

Sustained double-digit revenue growth, supported by contribution from newer hospitals and reducing dependence on the flagship hospital, leading to material improvement in the scale of operations and geographical diversity, while maintaining healthy operating margin of over 22-24% 

Sustaining healthy debt protection metrics, considering organic and inorganic expansion plans 

# **Downward Factors** 

Sluggish revenue growth and deterioration of operating margin to ~16-18%, affecting cash generation 

Higher-than-expected, debt-funded capex or acquisitions, or additional debt raised to fund cost overruns in ongoing projects, leading to moderation in key debt protection metrics; for instance, net debt to Ebitda ratio (post Ind-AS) in excess of 3 times on sustained basis. 

Adverse regulations impacting the hospital sector, including KIMS. 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Founded by Dr Bhaskar Rao Bollineni, a renowned cardiothoracic surgeon, KIMS operates a chain of multispecialty hospitals in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, with a focus on tertiary and quaternary healthcare. It began its journey in 2004 with a 300-bed hospital in Secunderabad. KIMS is one of India's leading multi-disciplinary integrated private healthcare service providers offering comprehensive healthcare services across specialties and super specialties. 

The flagship hospital of the group is at Secunderabad and has a capacity of 1,000 beds. KIMS has a network of 23 hospitals (including under construction hospitals) across five states – Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra, Kerala and Karnataka with a combined bed capacity of 6,464 beds as on March 31, 2026. 

In June 2018, General Atlantic invested over USD 130 million, in a combination of primary capital and secondary purchases, to acquire a significant minority stake of 42.6% in KIMS. This included the takeover of 30% stake from India Advantage Fund, India's largest private equity fund held by ICICI Ventures. The company went public in June 2021, and General Atlantic completely exited in September 2022. As on March 31, 2026, Dr Bhaskar Rao, held 34.11 % stake in KIMS, mutual funds 27.41%, insurance companies 4.59%, foreign portfolio investors 14.57%, Directors and relatives 4.34% and public the balance.
