### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its 'Crisil BBB+/Stable' rating on the long-term bank loan facilities and fixed deposits of Dmcc Speciality Chemicals Limited (DMCC). 

The rating continues to reflect the extensive experience of the promoters in the chemical industry and their longstanding customer relationships, large portfolio of bulk and specialty chemicals and limiting concentration risks and the aboveaverage financial risk profile of the company These strengths are partially offset by the moderate working capital requirements and susceptibility of the operating margins to volatility in raw material prices and regulatory risks. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has evaluated the standalone business and financial risk profiles of DMCC. 

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Extensive experience of the promoters and established customer relationships:** The three-decade-long experience of the promoters, their strong understanding of market dynamics, and established relationships with customers and suppliers, will continue to support the business. The promoters along with the professional management oversee the day-to-day operations. The company caters to a diversified customer base, spread across several end-user industries such as dyes, pigments, pharmaceuticals, cosmetics and detergents, among others. Backed by healthy customer demand, revenue has grown from Rs 328 crores in fiscal 2024 to Rs 431 crores in fiscal 2025. Furthermore, revenues of Rs 403.9 crores have been booked for the first nine months of fiscal 2026 and it is likely to grow at a similar pace over the medium term. 

**Large portfolio of bulk and specialty chemicals limiting concentration risks:** DMCC has built a large product portfolio over the years. The company manufactures around 38 products across bulk and specialty chemicals, in the sulphur and boron chemistry. This enables the company to cater to diverse range of customer requirements and should continue to support revenue growth and profitability in the near term. As a result, top 10 customers account for 27%-29% of total sales with no single customer accounting for more than 5% of sales. Crisil Ratings believes that the diversified product basket and customer base will continue to support business risk profile limiting the concentration related risks. 

**Comfortable financial risk profile:** Networth was healthy at Rs 208 crores as on March 31, 2025 (around Rs 215 crores as on September 30, 2025). Gearing and total outside liabilities to adjusted networth were low at 0.34 time and 0.69 time, respectively, as on March 31, 2025, and remained at similar levels as on September 30, 2025. Debt protection metrics were healthy, as reflected in interest coverage and net cash accrual to total debt ratios of 5.5 times and 0.5 times, respectively, in fiscal 2025, and interest coverage continues to remain healthy at above 7 times for the first nine months of fiscal 2026. The financial risk profile will continue to be aided by steady reserve accretions and the absence of any large debt-funded capital expenditure (capex) plans for the medium term 

### **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Susceptibility of operating margin to volatility in raw material prices and regulatory risks** : Profitability remains susceptible to fluctuations in prices of raw materials and end-products. Key raw materials include sulphur, benzene, ethanol, and boron, and raw material cost account for about 60–65% of total sales. Any sharp fluctuation in raw material prices can 

impact profitability, given the lag involved in passing on the hike in cost. The operating margin has fluctuated between 8.9% and 13.3% over the three fiscals ending March 31, 2025 (11.3% in the first nine months of fiscal 2026). Further, as the company manufactures chemicals related to sulphur and ethanol chemistry, and these are hazardous in nature, it also remains exposed to regulatory risks. 

Furthermore, availability of raw material amidst ongoing geopolitical issues may also constrain operations. While the company faced issues with regard to availability of boron during the first nine months of fiscal 2026, the supply chain has now been restored resulting in the ramp up of the manufacturing plant, which is likely to continue. However, availability of raw materials such as sulphur which are primarily by-products of crude oil, remains monitorable. The long-term contract with supplier, for procurement of raw materials at Dahej, ensures optimum utilisation of the facility. However, the facility at Roha depends on imports and other suppliers, and hence, sustenance of healthy capacity utilisation amid geopolitical developments remains a key rating sensitivity factor. While increase in realisations could partially offset the moderation in capacity utilisation leading to no major impact on revenues or profitability, however, the same remains a key rating sensitivity factor. 

**Moderate working capital requirement:** Gross asset assets (GCAs) were around 111 days as on March 31, 2025, (122 days as on March 31, 2024) led by receivables of 64 days and inventory of 42 days. The company provides customers with a moderate credit period but payments are realised in a timely manner, which supports the debtor cycle. Inventory is maintained for business requirements and given the moderate lead times. The working capital cycle is partially supported by creditors and bill discounting. Management of working capital requirements, amidst increasing scale of operations, will remain monitorable over the medium term. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Adequate 

Bank limit utilisation was low, averaging around 47% for the 12 months ending February 28, 2025. Working capital limit was enhanced by Rs 10 crores in March 2026 to support the additional working capital requirements and the extent of utilisation of the same remains monitorable. Expected net cash accrual of over Rs 35-40 crores should suffice to cover the term debt obligation of Rs 24 crores over the medium term. Unencumbered cash and cash equivalents were in the range of Rs 3–4 crore as on September 30, 2025. Current ratio was moderate at around 1.23 times as on March 31,2025 and is expected to continue to remain at moderate levels over the medium term. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes that DMCC will continue to benefit from its established market position in the chemical industry and comfortable financial risk profile. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Sizeable increase in revenues aided by volume growth and steady operating margin, leading to higher-than-expected net cash accrual, with cushion between cash accrual and repayment obligation improving to over 2 times Sustenance of comfortable financial risk profile with prudent working capital management 

# **Downward factors** 

Decline in revenue and operating margin, leading to lower cash accrual, and cushion between net cash accrual and repayment obligation moderating to less than 1.25 times on a sustained basis 

Large capex or stretch in the working capital cycle, weakening the financial risk profile 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Incorporated in 1919, DMCC (formerly known as The Dharamsi Morarji Chemical Company Limited) primarily manufactures bulk and specialty chemicals in the boron and sulphur chemistries, with plants in Roha (Maharashtra) and Dahej (Gujarat). Borax Morarji Ltd, a group company, was amalgamated with DMCC with effect from April 01, 2016. DMCC is listed on the Bombay Stock Exchange and National Stock Exchange. 

DMCC was earlier engaged in manufacturing phosphate fertilisers, such as single super phosphate, under the well-known ‘Ship’ brand. The company has discontinued fertiliser manufacturing since 2007.
