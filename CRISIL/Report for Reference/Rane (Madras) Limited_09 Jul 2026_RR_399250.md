### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has upgraded its ratings on the bank facilities of Rane (Madras) Limited (RML; part of the Rane group) to **‘Crisil AA-/Stable/Crisil A1+’** from ‘Crisil A+/Stable/Crisil A1’. 

On June 30, 2026, RML had intimated the stock exchanges that it has entered into a business transfer agreement (BTA) with Hindustan Composites Ltd (HCL) for the acquisition of its friction business as a going concern on a slump sale basis for a cash consideration of Rs 370 crore. The friction business includes the manufacturing and sale of fibre-based friction material, consisting of brake liners, roll lining, brake blocks and clutches that generated a revenue of ~Rs 315 crore in fiscal 2026 with operating profitability of ~14%. The acquisition is expected to be completed on or before September 30, 2026, and will be partly funded through debt financing, subject to the fulfilment of customary closing conditions and the receipt of certain approvals under the BTA. 

The ratings upgrade reflects the continued improvement in RML’s business risk profile, supported by strong domestic demand from original equipment manufacturers (OEMs), improved export sales and healthy aftermarket demand. Furthermore, the successful completion of the acquisition of the friction business of HCL is expected to facilitate RML’s entry into the commercial vehicle (CV) braking segment. RML already holds a leadership position in the friction (brake lining) business across passenger vehicles (PVs), two-wheelers (2Ws), the aftermarket segment and has presence in railways and aftermarket exports business. 

With the acquisition, revenue from the friction segment is expected to exceed ~Rs 1000 crore and is likely to unlock operational synergies through an expanded distribution network and enhanced research and development (R&D) capabilities. In addition to the high-margin nature of the business, the acquisition is expected to improve RML’s revenue diversification, as the target business derives ~42% of its revenue from railways, and ~40% from the CV segment. 

The acquisition should boost RML’s existing business growth trajectory through additional revenue and an improved customer and revenue mix. Supported by steady demand and the addition of new customers, revenue is expected to grow by ~10% over the medium term. The superior profitability profile of HCL’s business, along with an improving product mix, higher export contribution and better operating leverage will help buttress commodity price inflation and support operating margins at over ~9.0%-9.2% in fiscal 2027 and improve to 9.5–10% over the medium term. Healthy cash accruals, proceeds from monetisation of factory land at Velachery, Chennai (totalling Rs 361 crore spread over fiscals 2026– 2028), and ongoing debt repayment are expected to mitigate the impact of higher debt levels arising from the planned capital expenditure (capex) of Rs 300 crore and investments in new office building. Consequently, debt is estimated to increase to Rs 900-950 crore at the end of fiscal 2027 from Rs 634 crore as on March 31, 2026. Leverage metrics are expected to weaken temporarily, with gearing and net debt-to-EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation) projected at ~1.2 times and ~2.3 times, respectively, in fiscal 2027, compared with 0.85 time and 1.73 times, respectively, in fiscal 2026. However, these metrics are expected to improve thereafter, supported by healthy cash generation, and receipt of proceeds from non-core asset sale. 

RML's revenue grew by 13% to around Rs 3863 crore in fiscal 2026, compared to Rs 3406 crore in fiscal 2025 primarily driven by sustained demand from domestic OEMs, particularly in the PV and farm tractor segments, which witnessed higher offtake. Additionally, exports experienced growth, fuelled by strong demand for steering products and steady aftermarket sales. The revenue distribution was as follows: 57% from domestic OEMs, 27% from exports, and 16% from the aftermarket segment. Within the OEM segment, PVs constituted the largest share of revenue, accounting for over 55%, followed by commercial vehicles (CVs) at 22%, tractors at 12%, and two-/three-wheelers at 9%. RML's operating profitability has remained at ~8.8-8.9% in the last two fiscals with cost optimisation measures being implemented following the merger of Rane group entities, helping offset the higher increase in input costs and freight expenses. 

As on March 31, 2026, RML’s total debt stood at Rs 634 crore, comprising short-term borrowings of Rs 586 crore and longterm debt of Rs 48 crore. The company significantly reduced its long-term debt from Rs 336 crore as on March 31, 2025, primarily through the utilisation of Rs 145 crore in proceeds received from the monetisation of its Velachery land asset during fiscal 2026. In June 2025, RML entered into a definitive agreement with Canopy Living LLP, a joint venture between Arihant Foundations & Housing Ltd and Prestige Estates Projects Ltd, for the sale of its 3.48-acre facility and premises at Velachery for a total consideration of Rs 361 crore. Under the transaction, the company will retain 1.02 acre of land for the development of its new corporate office, subject to the receipt of requisite approvals. 

The ratings also reflects the healthy market position of RML in the domestic automotive steering components segment, its diversified revenue profile and benefits from being part of the Rane group. The ratings also factor in the improving financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by the sizeable investment in the domestic diecasting business and modest though improving operating profitability. RML also remains susceptible to cyclicality in demand and pricing pressure arising from sizeable exposure to OEMs in the automobile industry.

Crisil Ratings has knocked off intangible assets worth ~Rs 300 crore from networth arising out of the acquisition of the friction business of HCL.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Leading position in India’s auto steering market** 

RML is a leading player in the domestic steering market with strong presence in mechanical steering gears and hydrostatic gear systems. Further, RML has longstanding relationships with marque clients across vehicle segments, such as Maruti Suzuki India Ltd (MSIL; rated ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’), Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles Ltd (TMPL; rated ‘Crisil AA+/Stable/Crisil A1+’), Tractors and Farm Equipment Ltd (TAFE; rated ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’) and Mahindra & Mahindra Ltd (M&M; rated ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’). RML also acquired the steering component business of Yagachi Technologies in November 2021, for a consideration of Rs 23.19 crore. This acquisition has provided RML access to other major OEMs and has enhanced its leadership position in the domestic PV market. 

Supported by its established market presence and longstanding customer relationships, RML has been able to increase its share of business with existing customers by securing large orders.  RML bagged new orders of ~Rs 711 crore in fiscal 2026 and ~Rs  574 crore in fiscal 2025, which has boosted revenue visibility for the next 18–24 months. Besides, in-house capabilities have enabled the company to enhance products as per the requirement of key customers and sustain its healthy market position, despite competition from established peers such as ZF Steering Gear (India) Ltd and JTEKT India Ltd. 

# **Diversified revenue profile post-merger completion** 

Post merger with Rane group entities, RML’s revenue profile is more diversified with presence across market segments, namely domestic OEMs, aftermarket and exports. Domestic OEMs contribute 57% of revenue. Within OEMs, RML caters to PVs, CVs, Farm tractors, 2-wheelers and railways. The company also derives 15–20% of revenue from die-casting components sold to domestic and overseas customers. The company manufactures steering linkage products, engine valves, light metal castings, friction material and brake linings providing revenue diversity. In fiscal 2024, RML had also floated a new subsidiary in Mexico, Rane Auto Components Mexico (RACM), which is likely to assemble some of its products from fiscal 2027, for supplies to customers in the US, Mexico and Canada. The company had invested $5 million till fiscal 2025 in RACM, however considering the current global scenario, has decided to act cautiously on its plans. 

Crisil Ratings expects RML to post healthy double-digit revenue growth over the medium term, supported by revenue addition from the newly acquired business, steady demand from OEMs, and better aftermarket sales and healthy export growth over the medium term. 

# **Improving financial risk profile** 

RML’s financial risk profile improved in fiscal 2026 driven by reduction in debt levels to Rs 634 crore from Rs 761 crore in fiscal 2025 aided by the land sale advance of Rs 145 crore received. With improved profit accretion, networth increased to Rs 747 crore as on March 31, 2026 from Rs 663 crore as on March 31, 2025, and this is expected to witness further improvement supported by increasing profitability and from one-time gains of ~Rs 360 crore arising on the sale of non-core assets, boosting networth to an estimated ~Rs 900–920 crore. Debt levels are expected to peak in the current fiscal with debt being availed for the acquisition, part funding of the capex (estimated at Rs 300 crore annually) and investment in office building . Gearing is expected to rise temporarily to ~1.2 times as on March 31, 2027, from 0.85 time as on March 31, 2026. 

Debt protection metrics such as interest cover and net debt/EBITDA are expected at ~6.5–6.7 times and at ~2.3 times, respectively, in fiscal 2027 as compared to 6.00 times and 1.87 times in fiscal 2026. Leverage and debt metrics too will gradually improve over the medium term, supported by the monetisation of non-core assets, which is expected to be completed by fiscal 2028, and healthy cash generation. 

Earlier, in September 2023, RML divested its step-down subsidiary, Rane Light Metal Castings Inc. (RLMC), which was incurring operational losses of Rs 40-50 crore per annum. Sluggish demand conditions, leading to delay in breakeven, would have resulted in sustained dependence on RML, which had already invested Rs 450-500 crore between fiscals 2016 and 2024. Towards losses on investments in RLMC, an impairment of Rs 223 crore was taken in fiscal 2023, while Rs 122 crore was recorded in fiscal 2024. 

# **Established presence of Rane group** 

RML is part of the Chennai-based Rane group of companies, which has a consolidated turnover of around ~Rs 6,000 crore and manufactures a diverse range of automotive components, such as steering components, engine valves and brake components. Backed by a vintage of more than 80 years, the group has forged strong ties with leading OEMs in India and abroad. 

Rane Holdings Ltd (RHL), the holding company of RML, infused equity of Rs 65 crore in fiscal 2018, Rs 15 crore in fiscal 2019, Rs 55 crore in fiscal 2021 and another Rs 30 crore in fiscal 2022, to support operations at RML, including part funding of capex. Supply of components, along with those of other group companies, to common customers, also helps RML rationalise freight cost. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Sizeable investments in the domestic die casting business in the past; modest but improving operating profitability** RML had made sizeable investments to expand its domestic die casting division during fiscals 2016 and 2017. However, slower-than-expected ramp up of facilities, due to volatility in end-user demand, led to net losses. The company has undertaken measures to tie-up businesses to enhance utilisation and there has been substantial improvement in the die casting division over the past two fiscals. 

RML acquired RLMC in the US in 2016, but the subsidiary struggled with continuous losses, weighing on the company's returns. Initially, a 4–5 year turnaround period was anticipated, but RLMC continued to post significant net losses of over Rs 175 crore between fiscals 2017 and 2022. The losses were partly due to restructuring efforts, but weak demand from a major customer led to low revenue, perpetuating the losses. In fiscal 2023, RLMC recorded operating losses of over Rs 40 crore, prompting RML to divest its stake in the subsidiary in September 2023. The cumulative losses had a negative impact on RML's networth. However, the sale of RLMC in fiscal 2023 resulted in a deferred tax benefit of Rs 113 crore, which is expected to reduce RML's tax liability till the current fiscal, providing a positive offset to the earlier losses. 

RML's consolidated operating margin was previously constrained by the modest profitability of its light metal castings division, dropping to 3–5% in fiscals 2020 and 2021. However, margins recovered to 8.4% in fiscal 2023, driven by improved standalone profitability. In fiscal 2024, margins declined to 7.4% due to weak sales in the CV segment and exports. The merger with group entities during fiscal 2025 has led to cost savings and efficiency gains, including unified procurement, logistics, and optimised support functions aiding improvement in operating margins to 8.9% in fiscal 2025 and 8.8% in fiscal 2026. Now with the acquisition of high margin friction business, operating margins are expected to improve to 9–10% over the medium term. This has also led to return on capital employed improving to a healthy ~15.8% in fiscal 2026 and expected to remain at 14–17% over the medium term. 

Besides, exports to the US are moderate at ~10–12% of overall revenue, however, there has not been a material impact on the business on account of the tariffs as most of its business to the US is basis duty paid by customer. RML remains cautious in its approach towards expansion of the business in the US and Mexico, especially in terms of making further investments. 

# **Exposure to demand cyclicality and pricing pressures from OEMs in the automobile industry** 

High dependence on OEMs renders the performance of RML partly vulnerable to the inherent cyclicality in the automobile industry and any prolonged slowdown particularly in the CV segment. However, revenue from aftermarket and exports provide respite, besides presence across sub-segments, which is expected to lend some stability to business. Raw material cost accounted for a substantial portion of revenue, while about 57% of the topline is derived from auto OEMs during fiscal 2026 as compared to two-thirds prior to the merger between group entities in fiscal 2024. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Expected cash accruals of Rs 300-320 crore should comfortably cover debt obligations of Rs 33 crore in the current fiscal and Rs 70–90 crore over the medium term. RML is expected to receive ~Rs 83 crore in the current fiscal, which will be utilised towards part funding the capex requirements of ~Rs 300 crore, while the remaining will be met through internal accruals. Additionally, the construction of a commercial office building for Rane group companies will be partly funded through internal accruals and receipt of final leg of land sale proceeds. The company also has access to working capital limits of Rs 1260 crore, which were utilised at an average of 71% on drawing power over the 12 months through May 2026. Crisil Ratings notes that the company's liquidity is further supported by the possibility of equity infusion from its group, as seen in the past, when the holding company provided support to the loss-making operations of RLMC. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

The business risk profile of RML is expected to strengthen, supported by the successful integration of HCL’s high margin friction business, sustained domestic demand arising from OEMs, and a steady improvement in export revenue driven by new orders. These factors are expected to support a gradual improvement in operating profitability and cash generation. Combined with prudent funding of capex, and the completion of monetisation of non-core assets, financial risk profile and debt metrics are expected to strengthen over the medium term. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Sustained healthy business performance resulting in steady cash generation. 

Prudent capital spending and working capital management, and faster than expected reduction in debt levels, leading to improvement in financial risk profile and debt metrics – for instance net Debt/EBITDA sustaining at below 1.5-1.6 times 

# **Downward factors** 

Moderation in operating performance impacting annual cash generation 

Large, debt-funded capex or acquisition or significant stretch in working capital requirement, impacting debt metrics; for instance net Debt/EBITDA of over 2.60-2.75 times on sustained basis 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

RML commenced manufacturing operations in 1960 and is one of the leading tier 1 automotive component suppliers engaged in the manufacturing of manual steering gears, hydrostatic steering systems, and steering and suspension linkages, which together account for about 80% of the overall revenue. The balance comes from its high-pressure aluminum die casting division. The steerling linkage division and light metal casting division, erstwhile traditional RML business, has manufacturing units at Varanavasi, Mysuru, Puducherry, Pantnagar and Hyderabad (2 units). The engine component division has manufacturing facilities at Ponneri and Tiruchirapalli (Tamil Nadu), Tumkuru (Karnataka), and Aziz Nagar and Medchal (Telangana), while the brake component division has plant locations in Chennai, Hyderabad, Puducherry and Tiruchirapalli. 

RML is the flagship company of the Rane group, with the group holding company, RHL having 63.8% stake. Other group companies include ZF Rane Automotive India Pvt Ltd (joint venture) and Rane Steering Systems Pvt Ltd (wholly owned subsidiary of RHL, rated ‘Crisil A-/Stable’).
