### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AA/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the bank facilities of Music Broadcast Limited (MBL). The rating on preference shares worth Rs 120 crore has been **withdrawn** , following receipt of independent confirmation of full redemption and a withdrawal request from the company. This is in line with Crisil Ratings withdrawal policy. 

The ratings continue to reflect MBL’s strong managerial, operational and financial linkages with its parent, Jagran Prakashan Ltd (JPL; ‘Crisil AA+/Stable/Crisil A1+’), moderate market position in the FM radio broadcasting industry and its strong financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by susceptibility to economic cycles, intense competition and industry headwinds. 

The operating performance of MBL moderated in fiscal 2026 amid industry-wide demand weakness and heightened competition. Revenue declined to Rs 175 crore in fiscal 2026 from Rs 235 crore in fiscal 2025, while operating profitability weakened more sharply to 4% from 12% during the same period. In response, the company implemented several costoptimisation initiatives and undertook strategic reorganisation measures aimed at improving revenue growth and profitability fiscal 2027 onwards. Nevertheless, profitability is expected to remain modest over the medium term. 

The digital and non-free commercial time (FCT) segments are expected to drive growth in revenue and profitability this fiscal and over the medium term. However, the sustainability of profitability improvement and revenue growth will remain monitorable given the continuing headwinds facing the radio industry and the increasing shift of advertisers toward digital platforms. 

The financial risk profile is expected to remain strong, supported by negligible debt levels following the redemption of preference shares in fiscal 2026 and a sizeable cash balance. Cash and equivalent stood at Rs 268 crore as on March 31, 2026, providing substantial financial flexibility. 

The parent, JPL, maintains a healthy business risk profile and a robust financial risk profile, characterised by negligible debt and healthy cash balance. 

Crisil Ratings continues to be cognizant of the ongoing litigation among the promoters of JPL. Mr Mahendra Mohan Gupta (Chairman of JPL), along with Mr Shailesh Gupta (whole time director of JPL) and VRSM Enterprises LLP (collectively, the petitioners), had filed an oppression petition before the National Company Law Tribunal, Allahabad, on July 10, 2023. The petitioners have 16.18% shareholding in Jagran Media Network Investment Pvt Ltd (JMNIPL), which holds 67.97% shareholding in JPL. The petitioners’ indirect and direct shareholding in JPL aggregates to 11.29%. The shareholding of JMNIPL is completely held by the members of the Gupta family, which includes the petitioners. The petition raises issues concerning oppression of the minority shareholders, that is, the petitioners, by the majority shareholders, that is, the other members of the Gupta family, both at the JMNIPL and the JPL levels. However, the petition does not allege any mismanagement in the affairs of JPL. 

Crisil Ratings understands that the ongoing litigation is unlikely to have any material financial implications and is not expected to adversely affect  the credit risk profiles of either JPL or MBL. Nonetheless, Crisil Ratings will continue to closely monitor 

developments related to the matter. Any adverse outcome that adversely impacts the credit risk profiles of the rated entities will remain a key rating sensitivity factor. 

# **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has considered the standalone financials of MBL and has applied its parent notch-up framework to factor in the strong operational, financial and managerial linkages between MBL and JPL. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Strengths</u>** 

# **Established linkages with JPL** 

MBL has helped JPL diversify its reach into the radio broadcasting segment and, thus, remains strategically important to the latter. It complements the print business of JPL and enables the parent to offer a strong and differentiated product portfolio to advertisers. MBL enhances the geographical reach by adding cities where JPL has limited presence in print. Furthermore, radio stations of MBL, acquired during the phase III auctions, are in areas where JPL has a strong reach, thereby providing synergies to the former. 

In the past, JPL had facilitated the issuance of non-convertible debentures (NCDs) by providing a corporate guarantee to MBL and later replaced it with a letter of comfort. JPL also offered liquidity support through a debt service reserve account (DSRA), covering six months of debt obligation. The extensive experience of JPL’s management in the media and entertainment business will continue to strengthen the business risk profile. 

# **Robust financial risk profile with moderate market position** 

The financial risk profile is expected to remain supported by strong liquidity, with cash and liquid investments of ~Rs 268 crore as on March 31, 2026, following the full redemption of preference shares. The financial risk profile should remain healthy over the medium term, driven by improvement in cash accrual. 

MBL, a part of the JPL group, operates 39 radio channels under the well-established Radio City brand. While its market position moderated in fiscal 2026 due to intense competition and subdued industry demand, the company continues to benefit from a strong presence across tier-2 and tier-3 cities. However, the increasing contribution of these markets relative to metropolitan cities resulted in a decline in average advertising rates during the year. 

Revenue growth is expected to be supported by the company's increasing focus on non-free commercial time (FCT) segments, particularly digital media, content solutions and events. The pace of growth in FCT revenue, along with improvement in the profitability of the core radio business, will remain key monitorables over fiscal 2027 and the medium term. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

# **Susceptibility to economic cycles, competition and industry headwinds** 

Operating performance of radio operators remains vulnerable to economic slowdowns, as advertising (ad) revenue is closely linked to the overall macroeconomic conditions. Furthermore, the industry is facing some headwinds with a declining market size due to competition from the digital segment and shrinking profit margins, as companies invest in digital segments to stay competitive. Moreover, the limited scope for product differentiation in the radio broadcasting industry results in price-led competition among radio players for the available advertising revenue. Therefore, growth in ad revenue will continue to be monitorable. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Liquidity is supported by cash and liquid investments of around Rs 268 crore against nil debt as on March 31, 2026. Capital expenditure (capex) is expected to remain moderate. Furthermore, MBL has high financial flexibility and can get support from its parent, JPL, in case of any exigencies. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

MBL will continue to benefit from the healthy market position of Radio City, strong liquidity and linkages with JPL.

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

MBL was the first private FM radio broadcaster in India. It operates FM radio channels under the Radio City brand. In fiscal 2016, the company acquired 11 new stations in batch I of FM phase III auctions. Also, eight radio stations under the Radio Mantra brand, operated by the promoters of JPL under Shri Puran Multimedia Ltd, were merged with MBL and rebranded as Radio City in fiscal 2016. The company is present in 39 cities across India. It also operates 2 web-based stations. 

**<u>About the Group</u>** 

The JPL group is a media conglomerate with interests spanning printing and publication of newspapers and magazines, FM radio, digital, outdoor advertising and promotional marketing, event management and activation businesses. JPL is the flagship company of the JPL group that is based in Kanpur, Uttar Pradesh. The group publishes eight publications from 13 states/union territories in five languages.
