### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AA+/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the bank facilities of Dalmia Bharat Limited (DBL; part of the DBL group). 

Crisil Ratings has taken note of the announcement made by the company on May 22, 2026 regarding the execution of business transfer agreement by Dalmia Cement (Bharat) Ltd (DCBL), its wholly owned subsidiary, with Jaiprakash Associates Ltd (JAL; which has been acquired by Adani group under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code [IBC]) and Adani Infra (India) Ltd for acquisition of cement undertaking at an enterprise value of Rs 2,850 crore through a mix of debt and internal accruals. The transaction was consummated on May 29, 2026. 

The assets under consideration comprise cement capacity of 5.2 million tonne per annum (MTPA), clinker capacity of 3.3 MTPA, thermal power plants of 99 megawatt (MW), railway sidings and limestone reserves of more than 100 million tonne (MT). Crisil Ratings believes that the acquisition of cement assets of JAL will further strengthen the group’s business risk profile and will aid in growing its presence in the central region. It will also support the company’s plan to increase its consolidated cement capacity to 75 MTPA by fiscal 2028. While the plants are not operational at present, the group expects to commence operations by second quarter of fiscal 2027. The ability to successfully ramp up JAL’s plants to desired utilisation levels and achieve cost optimisation at a consolidated level will remain monitorable. 

The acquisition is expected to be funded through a combination of debt and available liquidity. The group is expected to incur additional capital expenditure (capex) towards refurbishment and efficiency improvement to the tune of ~Rs 550 crore over fiscals 2027-2028. Additionally, the group will continue with its organic capex plan of Rs 3,200-3,400 crore annually, going forward, towards greenfield and brownfield capacity additions. Sizeable outflow towards capex and acquisition in fiscal 2027 will lead to a sharp increase in net debt to Ebitda (excluding market value of investment in IEX) in fiscal 2027 from 0.8 time in fiscal 2026 but will remain below 2 times. Expected ramp up of the acquired as well as upcoming capacities coupled with sustained healthy profitability shall lead to a gradual decline in net debt to Ebitda, going forward. The comfort is also derived from group’s track record of turnaround of acquired assets and ability to deleverage following acquisition/ higher capex. 

In fiscal 2026, the group’s operating income increased 5.8% on-year supported by recovery in realisation even as volume growth remained muted at 2%. Higher realisations also led to improvement in operating profitability, with Ebitda per tonne increasing to Rs 1,028 in fiscal 2026 from Rs 823 in fiscal 2025. In fiscal 2027, Crisil Ratings expects volume to improve driven by incremental volumes from the central region. However, Ebitda per tonne is expected to moderate given the increase in input costs owing to the West Asia conflict and its extent will depend on the company’s ability to pass on the increased cost to consumers in the form of price hikes. 

The ratings continue to reflect the strong business risk profile of the group, backed by its established and diversified market position across southern, eastern, north-eastern and western regions, and its cost-efficient operations. The ratings factor in the strong financial risk profile and robust liquidity. These strengths are partially offset by the relatively low return on capital employed (RoCE) and susceptibility to risks related to volatility in input cost and realisations, as well as inherent cyclicality in the cement industry. 

# **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of DBL, DCBL, and Dalmia Power Ltd (DPL; a nonoperational company holding part of the group’s investment in Indian Energy Exchange Ltd [IEX]) and its other subsidiaries/associates and joint ventures, as the entities, collectively referred to as the DBL group, have common management and strong business and financial linkages. 

Crisil Ratings has adjusted the networth for amortisation of goodwill on account of acquisitions.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

# **Strong market position in the cement industry** 

With a track record of more than eight decades, the DBL group is the fourth-largest cement player (by capacity) in India, with operational capacity of 49.5 MTPA as on March 31, 2026. The group has diversified presence, with capacities in the east (44% of total capacity) in Odisha, West Bengal, Jharkhand and Bihar; south (34%) – Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and Karnataka; northeast (16%) – Assam and Meghalaya; and west (6%) – Maharashtra. The group has outperformed the market, with volume growing faster than overall cement demand over the past decade, resulting in a gain in market share across key markets. 

The market position is expected to further strengthen with the acquisition of cement undertakings of JAL of 5.2 MTPA and commissioning of sizeable capacity over the next two fiscals. The acquisition will enable the group to penetrate the central region. It is also setting up a greenfield capacity of 3 MTPA in Pune (Maharashtra) and brownfield grinding capacity of 3 MTPA in Belgaum (Karnataka) supported by clinker capacity of 3.6 MTPA in fiscal 2027. It is also setting up an integrated brownfield expansion of 6 MTPA grinding and 3.6 MTPA clinker capacity at its existing facility at Kadapa, Andhra Pradesh; along with 3 MTPA bulk terminal facility in Chennai by the third quarter of fiscal 2028. 

The group derives substantial volume from the east and north-eastern markets. DCBL commissioned 2.9 MTPA grinding capacity in these regions in fiscal 2025 and has become the largest player in the northeast region. The group has also enhanced its clinker capacity in fiscal 2026 by 3.6 MTPA in Assam. The company is also expected to increase penetration in the central region with the acquisition of cement undertaking of JAL. These regions are expected to see higher growth than pan-India markets over the medium term. This should aid in better absorption of the incremental capacity in the regions. 

# **Healthy operating profitability, driven by cost efficiency** 

Operating performance remains strong with Ebitda per tonne consistently above Rs 900 over the decade through fiscal 2026 (except fiscal 2025), driven by its cost leadership position. The high Ebitda per tonne was also aided by aboveaverage realisations, led by pick-up in demand and strong brand, focus on premium segments, and relatively stable operating cost, supported by various measures boosting overall cost efficiency. 

Some of the measures taken by the group to improve efficiency include a) increasing the share of renewable energy in the power mix b) increasing the share of blended cement in the overall mix, resulting in reduced consumption of energy and clinker per tonne of cement; c) lowering lead distances, with most cement units located close to raw material sources and major cement markets; and d) switching to multi-fuel kilns, which has helped to manage overall fuel cost optimally. 

In fiscal 2027, Crisil Ratings expects Ebitda per tonne to decrease owing to a sharp increase in petcoke, packaging and diesel costs due to the West Asia conflict. However, the group has the ability to partially pass on the higher cost to consumers by undertaking price hikes. Increasing share of renewable power in the energy mix should also aid in navigating higher input cost of petcoke. 

# **Strong financial risk profile** 

Financial leverage, as measured by the ratio of net debt to Ebitda (excluding market value of investment in IEX) decreased to 0.8 time in fiscal 2026 (from 1.2 times the previous fiscal) with improvement in profitability and sale of partial stake in IEX. Adjusted gearing rose to 0.4 time from 0.3 time as the group undertook debt-funded capex. Considering large organic and inorganic capex plans of the company, with total outflow of Rs 6,000-6,200 crore (including outflow towards acquisition) in fiscal 2027, the net debt to Ebitda ratio is expected to increase but will remain below 2 times in fiscal 2027. However, expected ramp-up in volumes and profitability, will lead to a gradual decline in leverage fiscal 2028 onwards. 

Debt protection metrics, measured by adjusted interest coverage and net cash accrual to adjusted debt ratios, remained at 6.9 times and 0.4 time, respectively, in fiscal 2026 as against 6.7 times and 0.4 time, respectively, in fiscal 2025. With a substantial increase in debt, the metrics are expected to decline but remain healthy over the medium term. 

The group also holds 10.8% stake in IEX, valued at Rs 1,106 crore as on March 31, 2026, which should act as a buffer in case of liquidity constraints. DBL has strong financial flexibility arising from its 100% owned subsidiaries, DCBL and DPL. Crisil Ratings expects timely infusion of funds in the subsidiaries for meeting any debt obligation, considering the common management and treasury operations across group entities. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u> Moderate RoCE** 

The group undertook a few restructuring exercises, resulting in recognition of intangibles. Moreover, it is taking up various capex projects to augment capacity to 75 MTPA by fiscal 2028. This should lead to a significant increase in capital employed, and a resultant rise in depreciation and amortisation expenses, which may lead to subdued RoCE. With the completion of the ongoing capex, Crisil Ratings expects RoCE to improve gradually with scaling up of assets and stable profitability. Any marked deviation due to lower profitability and higher-than-expected debt funding or acquisition, leading to higher debt obligation, will be monitorable. 

# **Susceptibility to risks related to volatility in input cost or realisations, and cyclicality in the cement sector** 

Capacity addition in the cement industry tends to be sporadic because of the long gestation period for setting up a facility and numerous players adding capacities during the peak of a cycle. This had led to unfavourable price cycles for the sector in the past. Moreover, profitability remains exposed to volatility in the cost of inputs, including raw material, power, fuel and freight. Decline in cement prices across the industry during fiscal 2025 has also impacted the profitability of cement players. Realisations and profitability are also affected by demand, supply, offtake and regional factors. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Financial flexibility remains strong, as reflected in cash and equivalent of Rs 4,218 crore as on March 31, 2026. It is supported by the investment in IEX, valued at Rs 1,103 crore on the same date. The company also has access to fundbased bank limit of Rs 1,069 crore, which remains moderately utilised. Healthy net cash accrual of Rs 2,250-2,500 crore and Rs 2,750-3,000 crore in fiscal 2027 and 2028 respectively and surplus liquidity should suffice to cover cumulative debt obligation of Rs 650-700 crore and part of capex. 

# **<u>ESG Profile</u>** 

Crisil Ratings believes that DBL’s ESG profile supports its already strong credit risk profile. 

The cement sector has a significant impact on the environment owing to higher emissions, waste generation and water consumption. This is because of the energy-intensive cement manufacturing process and high dependence on natural resources such as limestone and coal as key raw materials. The sector has a social impact due to its nature of operations, affecting local community and health hazards. DBL has continuously focused on mitigating these environmental and social risks. 

- DBL aims to become carbon negative by 2040 and its target to reduce Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions per tonne of cementitious material by 32% and 61.9%, respectively, by 2034, from fiscal 2019 baseline, are validated by the Science Based Targets initiative. Against it, the company’s Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions per tonne of cementitious materials had reduced by 14% and 48%, respectively, by fiscal 2025. 

- The company targets to double its energy productivity by 2030 under EP100 (36% increase in energy efficiency from fiscal 2011 baseline) and consume minimum 60% renewable electricity in the operations under RE100. 

- The company’s Scope 1 and 2 emissions and energy consumption intensities at ~0.54 tCO2E and ~0.7 MWh per tonne of cementitious material produced, respectively, is lower compared with peers. 

- The company’s gender diversity of workers at ~4% is higher compared with peers. Furthermore, its lost time injury frequency rate (LTIFR) for contract workforce at 0.16 in fiscal 2025 is broadly in line with the peer average. Though, it has increased from 0.032 in fiscal 2024. 

- Its governance structure is characterised by 50% of the board comprising independent directors, ~13% being woman directors, dedicated investor grievance redressal system, and extensive financial disclosures. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

The DBL group will continue to benefit from its strong market position, healthy operating efficiency and comfortable liquidity. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

- Ramp-up of existing and upcoming capacities while sustaining healthy profitability, resulting in marked improvement in RoCE 

- Healthy profitability and declining debt resulting in net debt to Ebitda ratio (excluding the value of IEX investment) less than 1.25 times on a sustained basis 

# **Downward factors** 

Further moderation in the RoCE on a sustained basis 

- Higher-than-expected debt-funded capex or acquisition, or decline in profitability resulting in net debt to Ebitda ratio (excluding the value of IEX investment) of more than 2.0 times on a sustained basis 

# **<u>About the Group</u>** 

DBL is the listed holding company of the cement business of the DBL group. It owns 100% of DCBL, which is the main operating company and houses the entire cement business of the group. DBL also owns 100% in DPL (non-operational company). At a standalone level, it derives revenue by providing management services to group companies. 

DCBL is the fourth-largest cement manufacturer in the country, with an installed capacity of 49.5 MT as on March 31, 2026. The company has presence across the east (capacity of 21.6 MTPA), south (17.0 MTPA), northeast (8 MTPA) and west (2.9 

MTPA). It also has captive thermal power plants of 212 MW, group captive renewable power of 217 MW, solar power plants of 143 MW and a waste heat recovery system (WHRS) of 88 MW. 

DPL is a non-operational company. Its assets mainly include a part of the group’s investment in equity shares of IEX (listed entity). 

The investment of the DBL group in IEX is valued at Rs 1,106 crore as on March 31, 2026. The group has categorised the investment as non-core and classified it under current assets in the balance sheet. All the group entities have common management and treasury operations. 

For fiscal 2026, operating income at DBL, at a consolidated level, stood at Rs 14,804 crore against Rs 13,989 crore during the previous fiscal. Ebitda per tonne increased to Rs 1,028 from Rs 823 in fiscal 2025. Profit after tax (PAT) stood at Rs 1,157 crore in fiscal 2026 as compared with Rs 697 crore in fiscal 2025.
