### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has revised its outlook on the long-term bank facilities of Electrosteel Castings Limited (ECL) to **‘Negative’** from ‘Stable’ while reaffirming the rating at ‘ **Crisil AA** ’. The rating on the short-term bank facilities and commercial paper programme has been reaffirmed at ‘Crisil A1+’. 

The group is one of the largest players in the DI pipe segment with total DI pipe manufacturing capacity of around 8,95,000 metric tonnes per annum (MTPA) as on December 2025 along with integrated manufacturing capacities across pig iron, sponge iron, coke amongst others. 

The negative outlook factors in weaker than expected operating performance in the first two quarters of fiscal 2026 basis slowdown in one of the key projects of the government in water supply i.e. Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM). The group continues to face headwinds amid challenges in terms of domestic sales volumes and realisations in the DI pipe segment, H1FY2026 consolidated revenue being around Rs 2953 crore (down from around Rs 3840 crore in H1FY2025). Capacity utilization in the DI pipe segment moderated to around 60% in the first six months of fiscal 2026 as against 90% in fiscal 2025 impacting volumes. As a result, accretion to reserve also remained subdued with consolidated profit after tax (PAT) of Rs 167.36 crore in the first six months of fiscal 2026 (down from about Rs 381 crore in H1FY2025), consolidated operating profitability of about Rs 263 crores in H1FY2026 and 8.90% (down from about Rs 621 crores and about 16% in H1FY2025). While the EBITDA margins have been impacted owing to lower economies of scale, the gross margins remain intact at around 48-49%. EBITDA per tonne remained around Rs 8700 per tonne in the first six months of fiscal 2026 as against Rs 15000 per tonne in FY2025. 

Recovery in scale of operations and further growth supported by healthy volumes resulting in EBITDA margin of around 1213% yielding higher net cash accruals which is expected from last quarter of fiscal 2026 shall remain a key sensitivity factor. 

The ratings remain underpinned by robust financial risk profile and ample liquidity despite ongoing challenges in the industry. Liquidity remains supported by healthy unencumbered investments of around Rs 580 crore as on December 2025, healthy cushion in the bank lines and steady cash accrual generation expected over the medium term. Amid steady accretion to reserves, the group has significantly reduced its dependency on external working capital and term debt borrowings, which has consequently improved the capital structure with net debt/EBITDA expected below unity over the medium term. In the absence of large planned debt-funded capital expenditure (capex), steady cash generation is expected to further strengthen financial flexibility 

The ratings continue to reflect established market position of the group in the DI pipe industry, healthy operating efficiency driven by integrated operations and prudent working capital management and robust financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by exposure to volatility in the prices of inputs and finished goods and exposure to inherent cyclicality and inherent slowdown in the end user industry.

# **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Established market position supported by leadership position in the industry, experienced management and reputed clientele base:** The Electrosteel group is one of the largest players engaged in the manufacturing of DI pipes with combined capacities of DI pipes at around 8,95,000 metric tonnes per annum (MTPA) as on December, 2025. The promoters have been associated in the industry for over five decades and their extensive experience has helped them gain significant market presence, expand product capacities and have established forward as well as backward integrated operations. Diversified product mix also includes DI fittings, CI pipe, pig iron, sponge iron, ferro alloy, cement, coke amongst others. Market position remains supported by demand through government’s schemes of JJM, Har Ghar Nal se Jal and AMRUT 2.0 with the aim of providing tap water connections to rural as well as urban households. The company maintains strong trade relationships with reputed overseas customers and all major players in the domestic market. Limited competition, owing to large capital requirement and necessity to have critical accreditations and customer approvals, bolster the business risk profile. 

The group’s presence is diversified with facilities in India and overseas, which enables it to cater to geographically diverse customers, counter protectionist policies in some global markets, and guard against economic downturns in specific regions. The geographically diversified presence mitigates the concentration risk, which is extremely critical in the DI pipe segment. Furthermore, the group has two dedicated manufacturing facilities to manufacture DI pipes, one each in Eastern as well as Southern India, both having necessary certifications and accreditations enabling it to have a PAN India presence well supported by three additional facilities for backward integration which lends support to overall operations. 

Consolidated revenue from operations stood at Rs 7359 crore in FY2025 as against Rs 7488 crore in the previous fiscal amid volumetric sale of around 7,81,000 tonnes in fiscal 2025 across DI pipes, DI fittings and CI pipes as against 7,93,000 tonnes in fiscal 2024. The slight dip in volume was on account of maintenance activity in the blast furnaces during the year. 

Amid slowdown in domestic industry demand for DI pipes in the first two quarters of fiscal 2026, through JJM, H1FY2026 consolidated turnover stood at Rs 2953.40 crore as against Rs 3839.70 crore in H1FY2025 owing to lower volumes at around 3,03,000 tonnes across the main product lines as against around 4,00,000 tonnes in H1FY2025. The sales dip was on account of lower volumes and realisations recorded in the DI pipe segment while volumes and realisations in the DI fittings and CI pipes remained steady. While domestic DI pipe realisations declined by more than 15% as against fiscal 2025, export realisations remained steady. While domestic volumes remained lower, export contribution to overall revenue increased to about 25% as against about 17% in the previous fiscal. Overall volumes across DI Pipe, CI pipe and DI fittings is expected to be around 6,65,000 tonnes for FY2026 as against 7,85,000 tonnes in the last fiscal, dip being in DI pipe volumes in the domestic market. Turnover for FY2026 is expected to be around Rs 6000 crores with total DI pipe volume being around 5,75,000 tonnes. 

Order book which is to be executed over the next 6-9 months provides healthy revenue visibility in the near term. Improvement in domestic industry demand for DI pipes leading to revival in scale of operations and further growth supported by healthy volumes amid healthy capacity utilization shall aid in revenue generation over the medium term. 

**Healthy operating efficiency driven by integrated operations and sustained working capital management:** Operations of the group are well integrated in the form of backward and forward integrated operations (from LAM coke, blast furnace manufacturing facilities to the presence of captive power for manufacturing of end products i.e. DI pipes, DI fittings and CI pipes. As such, integrated nature of operations and leadership position in DI pipes continue to support operating efficiency. Apart from the benefits derived from the value chain, the group is supported by its presence in railway siding and captive power plant segments. Furthermore, its flexibility in terms of customer base, captive consumption, and outside sale requirements; helps maximise realisations and profitability. Consolidated EBITDA margins for the group stood at 14.87% in fiscal 2025 (stood over 16% in fiscal 2026) supported by healthy operational efficiency through benefits derived from integrated operations, moderation in raw material costs (i.e coking coal and iron ore), sustenance of healthy realisations of finished products and healthy capacity utilization. The slight dip in profitability was owing to lower volumes in the last quarter. 

Amid subdued demand scenario on an industry level, the profitability levels have also come down with EBITDA margin being around 8.90% as of H1FY2026 and expected to be around 10.50-11% for the full fiscal (expectation remained at 17%) backed by better flow of demand amid revival of industry demand scenario expected from last quarter of fiscal 2026. While the EBITDA margins have been impacted owing to lower economies of scale, the gross margins remains intact at around 4849%. EBITDA per tonne currently remains around Rs 8700-9000 per tonne on domestic level and about Rs 14,000-15,000 per tonne on export level. Backed by expectation of improved industry demand from last quarter of fiscal 2026 onwards, EBITDA/tonne is expected to rebound to around Rs 9800 per tonne for the full fiscal.  Gross margin remains healthy at par with FY2024 and FY2025 at 48-49% amid lower RM prices (that of coking coal and iron ore) which are expected to be steady across FY27-FY28 and amidst revival of demand supporting economies of scale, the EBITDA per tonne is expected to inch back to over Rs 12000-13000 per tonne and shall remain a key monitorable. 

Healthy capacity utilisation levels across different product lines amid revival in demand shall also support operating efficiencies resulting in steady profitability and Return on Capital Employed (RoCE) for the group. Profitability will be supported by improving capacity utilisation thereby supporting healthy volumetric growth amid expectation of improved demand scenario over the medium term supported by recent budgetary allocations fetching healthy realisations and consequently supporting healthy profitability amid demand supply mismatch situation additionally supported by moderated input prices. Moreover, bulk procurement of raw material such as iron ore and coal and close proximity to raw material sources help in cost saving. Furthermore, the group purchases raw material back-to-back and maintains order-backed inventory, which mitigates price fluctuation risk of the key inputs such as coking coal and iron ore. As such, operating margin is expected to remain healthy around 12-13% over the medium term. Extensive experience of promoters and their close monitoring of the working capital cycle has ensured prudent working capital management over the years, limiting dependence on external debt for working capital. The strong accrual generation ability and prudent working capital management should also result in positive cash-flow from operations for the group going forward. Despite challenges in terms of industry demand in the first six months of the current fiscal, no major increase in receivables and inventory has been reported. 

**Robust financial risk profile:** The financial risk profile should continue to remain robust over the medium term supported by substantial reduction in net debt levels supported by healthy networth. Consolidated networth of the group stands robust at around Rs 5894 crore as on September 30<sup>th</sup> , 2025 (stood at around Rs 5776 crore as on March 31<sup>st</sup> , 2025) and is expected to increase over the medium term backed by steady accretion to reserves. Capital structure also remains strong marked by negligible gearing and total outside liabilities to tangible networth (TOL/TNW) expected around 0.20 time and 0.50 time respectively going forward, thereby enhancing financial flexibility. Amid steady accretion to reserve, the group has significantly reduced its dependency on external working capital and term debt borrowings, which has consequently improved the capital structure with net debt/EBITDA expected below unity over the medium term. In the absence of large planned debt-funded capital expenditure (capex), steady cash generation is expected to further strengthen financial flexibility.  Debt protection metrics continue to be strong, with interest coverage and net cash accrual to adjusted debt (NCA/AD) ratios at healthy levels in FY2025 and H1FY2026 and expected to remain healthy going forward supported by steady profitability generation and reduced gross debt levels. However, any major debt-funded capex along with an unexpected large rise in working capital requirement will remain a key monitorable. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Vulnerability to volatility in the prices of raw material and finished goods:** In addition to demand risk from end-user industry, the industry remain exposed to volatility in raw material prices (such as iron ore and coking coal) and finished product realisations, which can impact operating margin. The raw material prices are largely subject to global commodity prices. However, this is partially offset by the integrated nature of operations and strong ability to pass through any change in raw material prices to customers on the back of the group’s leadership position in the DI pipe industry. 

As such, amid slowdown in domestic demand for DI pipes via delay in government spending in JJM, accounting for about 50% of the industry DI pipe demand, domestic realisations for DI pipe fell by more than 15% in the first six months of fiscal 2026 amid lower raw material prices. Coupled with lower scale of operations, EBITDA margin has moderated to around 9% in the first six months of fiscal 2026 and is expected to be around 10.50-11% for the full fiscal. Margins remained supported by steady volume and realisations in value added products such as CI pipes and DI fittings, better revenue contribution from the export market as well as lower raw material prices, that of coking coal and iron ore. 

Further significant reduction in demand and prices adversely impacting operating margin and cash accruals of the group will remain a key monitorable. 

**Susceptibility of operating performance to cyclical demand in end-user industries and tender-based business:** The demand for the group’s manufactured products and sustenance of operating performance is mainly linked to the capex programmes of end-user industries such as water supply, sanitation, irrigation and housing which account for about 80% of the industry’s total demand thereby exposing the group to the budgetary investment plans in these segments especially during an economic slowdown when the governmental organisations may defer capex. ECL undertakes manufacturing of DI pipes, DI fittings and CI pipes and bags some of its orders by submitting bids for tenders floated by government entities. Hence, revenue and profitability depend on supply of manufactured products to large EPC players and non-renewal/receipt of contracts could affect revenue. Furthermore, the group also derives about 25% of its total revenue from exports. Any delay or deferment of capex in end-user industries in the domestic as well as export market could 

constrain scalability. As such, despite leadership position in the DI pipe industry, operating performance got impacted in the first two quarters of fiscal 2026 basis slowdown in one of the key projects of the government in water supply i.e. JJM. Growth in revenue also depends largely on the company's ability to successfully get orders and remains susceptible to intense competition from other market players. Sustenance of healthy order book and healthy operating profitability amid susceptibility to budgetary investment plans in the end user industries as well as cyclicality of export demand shall remain a key monitorable. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Bank limit utilization of fund-based limits was around 32% during the past twelve months through December 2025. Expected annual cash accrual of over Rs 600-700 crore per annum will be more than sufficient to meet yearly term debt repayment obligation of around Rs 50-150 crore per annum, over the medium term; the remaining accrual will cushion liquidity. In addition, it shall enhance financial flexibility to cushion any exigencies, incremental working capital requirement or capex plans of the group.  Healthy unencumbered liquidity in the form of cash and bank balance, fixed deposits and investments of around Rs 580 crore as on December 2025 further support liquidity. Low gearing and strong networth support financial flexibility and provide the necessary financial cushion in case of any adverse conditions or downturn in the business. ECL further does not have any immediate debt funded capex plans over the medium term to increase its manufacturing capacity, future capex being contingent on improvement in industry demand which further supports liquidity. Materialisation of compensation from pending arbitrations further strengthening the liquidity profile shall remain a key monitorable. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Negative 

Electrosteel group will continue to benefit from its established market position marked by its leadership position in the DI pipe industry, integrated nature of operations, thereby ensuring steady cash generation. The financial risk profile shall continue to remain healthy, driven by conservative capital structure backed by reduction in net debt and sustenance of healthy unencumbered liquidity levels. Recovery in operating performance with revival of demand from JJM shall remain a key monitorable. 

# **<u>Rating Sensitivity Factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Improvement in domestic industry demand for DI pipes leading to revival in scale of operations and further growth supported by healthy volumes resulting in consolidated earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortisation (EBITDA) margin of around 12-13% yielding higher net cash accruals 

- Phased capex and sustenance of working capital operations, leading to sustenance of financial and liquidity risk profile with consolidated net debt to EBITDA below 1 time on sustained basis, along with healthy unencumbered liquidity levels 

# **Downward factors** 

Sustained deterioration in scale of operations due to weakened demand leading to significant weakening of operating efficiency marked by continuation of operating margin below 9.50% thereby reducing the net cash accruals 

- Larger-than-expected capex or acquisition or stretch in working capital cycle resulting in material increase in leverage (net debt/EBITDA) and thereby weakening of financial and liquidity risk profile, on a sustained basis. 

# **<u>About the Group</u>** 

The company was incorporated in November 1955, as Dalmia Iron & Steel Ltd and re-incorporated under the name of ECL in May 1965.  ECL manufactures ductile iron (DI) pipes with combined installed capacity of 8,95,000 metric tonne per annum (MTPA). Combined production capacity for DI fittings and cast iron (CI) pipes is 21,000 MTPA and 90,000 MTPA, respectively. Through backward integration, the company also operates a blast furnace, coke oven and waste heat recoverybased power plant and manufactures pig Iron, metallurgical Coke, sponge iron, sinter, cement and ferro products. Plants are in Khardah, Bansberia, Haldia in West Bengal, Elavur in Tamil Nadu, and Srikalahasthi in Andhra Pradesh. ECL has been increasingly increasing its presence in value added products and export market through strategic linkages via acquisitions. The company has approved the acquisition of 70% stake in Arabian Water Tech LLC in Oman, to expand its presence in Oman market. Additionally, Singardo International Pte Ltd has incorporated Electrosteel Vietnam Limited as a wholly-owned step-down subsidiary.
