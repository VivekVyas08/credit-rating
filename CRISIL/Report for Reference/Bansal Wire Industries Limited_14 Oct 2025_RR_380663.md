### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ratings on the bank facilities of Bansal Wire Industries Ltd (BWIL; part of the Bansal Wire group) at **‘** Crisil A+/Stable/Crisil A1 **’.** 

The ratings factor in the strong market position as the group is the largest stainless steel wire manufacturer and the second largest steel wire manufacturer by volume in India. Furthermore, the group is estimated to clock a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of nearly 10% over the five fiscals through 2026 on the back of timely addition of new products and healthy demand from existing products, supported by ramping up of operations at its Dadri (Uttar Pradesh) plant; hence, capacity constraint will not be a challenge over the medium term. Business risk profile is also aided by sustenance of operating efficiency, with improvement in operating margin to 7.6% in fiscal 2025, which is expected to remain at 7.5-8% in fiscal 2026. Prudent working capital management also led to lower dependence on working capital limit. Operating efficiency is likely to improve further over the medium term, led by addition of new products and better economies of scale, though its sustenance is monitorable. 

The ratings also factor in the robust financial risk profile of the group with efficient working capital management. No major debt-funded capital expenditure (capex) is planned, and the capital structure is expected to remain comfortable, supported by the expected accretion to reserve over the medium term. Liquidity was comfortable, backed by healthy net cash accrual vis-à-vis maturing debt, cushion in bank limit and unencumbered cash reserves. 

The ratings continue to reflect the group’s established market position, supported by diversified product and customer profiles, and strong financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by vulnerability to volatility in the prices of raw material and finished goods and large working capital requirement. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has revised its analytical approach following the expected merger of Bansal High Carbon Pvt Ltd (BHCPL) and Balaji Wires Pvt Ltd (BWPL) into BWIL. As a result, Crisil Ratings now assesses the combined business and financial risk profiles of BWIL and its wholly owned subsidiary, Bansal Steel and Power Ltd (BSPL), collectively referred to as the Bansal Wire group. 

Furthermore, the management incorporated BWI Steel Pvt Ltd (BWI) as a 100% subsidiary of BWIL for future capex requirements. Therefore, considering the expected synergies, Crisil Ratings will be consolidating BWI with BWIL.

### **-** **<u>Key Rating Drivers Strengths</u>** 

**Established market position, supported by diversified product and customer profiles:** The group is one of the largest players in India with well-entrenched presence owing to large manufacturing capacities, established brand and longstanding relationships with customers. The promoter has been associated with the steel industry for over four decades and has established relations with its customers and suppliers across the steel industry. The clientele comprises over 500 customers, including traders and manufacturers, across the engineering and infrastructure, automobile components and household segments. The product portfolio includes different varieties and sizes of galvanised iron (GI), mild steel (MS), high carbon (HC) and stainless steel (SS) wires. 

Market position is marked by substantial growth in scale of operations backed by robust volumetric growth through enhanced capacities, as the group was in continuous expansion mode and demand scenario is healthy. Consolidated turnover was over Rs 3,685 crore in fiscal 2025 and is expected to achieve Rs. 4,200-4,400 crore in fiscal 2026. This will be backed by expected volumetric growth of over 15-20% in fiscal 2026. Led by infrastructural push, the automobile segment, and accrued benefits from operationalisation of recent capacity expansion towards new products, revenue growth is expected to remain healthy over the medium term. Also, expected moderation in raw material prices (especially iron ore and coal) over the medium term and diversified product profile will provide cushion against reduced steel prices and support operating profitability. 

**Strong financial risk profile:** The financial risk profile should remain robust over the medium term, backed by steady accretion to reserve. Consolidated networth is expected at over Rs 1,700 crore as on March 31, 2026, and is likely to increase further over the medium term, backed by steady accretion to reserve. Capital structure is also likely to remain strong, with negligible gearing and total outside liabilities to tangible networth ratio expected to be 0.4-0.5 time and 0.6-0.7 time, respectively, thereby enhancing financial flexibility. Financial risk profile is expected to remain strong with no major debt-funded capex planned for the medium term. 

Debt protection metrics continue to be healthy in fiscal 2025 and are expected to remain strong over the medium term, with expected interest coverage and net cash accrual to adjusted debt ratios of over 6 times and above 0.5 times, respectively, supported by healthy profitability. Any larger-than-expected debt-funded capex or acquisition weakening the metrics and capital structure will remain monitorable. 

# **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Large working capital requirement:** Gross current assets (GCAs) are estimated to be 120-150 days over the three fiscals through 2025 and are expected to remain at a similar level in fiscal 2026. The high GCAs are on account of moderate receivables of 40-60 days, and large inventory of 60-80 days that increased from 49 days as on March 31, 2024, on account of start of the Dadri plant. The company also holds large inventory due to the nature of the business as it deals in a variety of products. Due to this the dependence of working capital limits increased as evident with increased utilisation of working capital limit at more than 70% over last 6 months through June 2025 _(Total limits of Rs. 610 crores in BWIL and BSPL)_ . Prudent management of receivables has resulted in timely realisation in the past and timely liquidation of inventory. Furthermore, continuous efficient working capital management amid revenue growth will be closely monitored. 

**Vulnerability to volatility in the prices of raw material and finished goods:** In addition to demand risk, the end-user industries also remain exposed to volatility in raw material prices and finished product realisations, which can impact operating margin. The prices are subject to global commodity prices. However, this is partially offset by the integrated nature of operations, flexibility in changing revenue mix across multiple industry and steel products, and ability to pass through any change in raw material prices to customers. Any significant reduction in demand and prices adversely impacting operating margin and cash accrual will remain monitorable. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** Strong 

Bank limit utilisation was around 49% for the 12 months through June 2025. Expected cash accrual of Rs 200-220 crore will be more than sufficient to meet yearly term debt obligation of Rs. 20-25 crore, over the medium term; and the remaining will cushion the liquidity. Current ratio was healthy, estimated at 1.6-1.8 times as on March 31, 2025. The promoter is likely to extend equity and unsecured loans to meet working capital requirement and debt obligation. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** Stable 

Crisil Ratings believes the Bansal Wire group will continue to benefit from the extensive experience of its promoter and its established relationship with clients. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors** 

Sustained and significant volumetric growth while keeping operating profitability steady at 7-8% leading to higher-thanexpected net cash accruals. 

Prudent working capital management leads to lower dependence on working capital debt. 

# **Downward factors** 

Decline in revenue and steep decline in operating margin resulting in lower-than-expected cash accrual. Weaking of financial risk profile with Interest coverage ratio weakening to below 3 times. 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

BWIL, the flagship company of the Bansal Wire group, was established in 1985 by Mr Arun Gupta; he also set up the group's other three companies. BWIL manufactures SS and MS wires. 

The promoter acquired M/s Garg Inox Ltd through the National Company Law Tribunal in March 2019 and subsequently renamed it BSPL. It produces SS, HC, GI and cable armouring wires at its facility in Bahadurgarh, Haryana. 

BWI Steel Private Limited (BWI) was incorporated in fiscal 2025 for purchase of Land in Sanand, Ahmedabad. It’s a 100% 

subsidiary of BWIL. The management will set up one plant for manufacturing steel rods going forward.
