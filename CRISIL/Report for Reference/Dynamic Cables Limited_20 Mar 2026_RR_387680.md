### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ratings on the bank loan facilities of Dynamic Cables Ltd (DCL) at ‘Crisil A/Stable/Crisil A1’. 

The ratings continue to reflect the company’s established market position and strong financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by moderate scale of operations and exposure to intense competition and volatility in raw material prices 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has evaluated the standalone business and financial risk profiles of DCL. Unsecured loan of Rs 1.88 crore as on March 31, 2025, from the promoters has been treated as debt as the loan is likely to be repaid as and when required. 

### **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Strengths</u>** 

**Established market position:** The promoters have experience of over three decades in the conductors and cables industry. This has helped them develop sound understanding of the market dynamics and healthy relationships with customers and suppliers. The company’s market position is supported by diversified clientele and product mix. Increasing revenue share from newly developed products also supports the business risk profile. DCL has lowered its reliance on direct government entities for work to 10-20% of its turnover from 50-55% around five years ago. The company has a fairly diversified presence in the domestic market. Furthermore, the management is focusing on diversification of the product portfolio and has added data centre cables as well as solar cables with advanced technology to its portfolio which is expected to aid further growth and diversification, with revenue expected to grow at a CAGR of 20-22% through three fiscal 2026 with revenue expected at Rs 1150-1250 crore in fiscal 2026. 

**Strong financial risk profile:** The financial risk profile is supported by strong networth and gearing expected at Rs 450-470 crore (Rs 373.6 a year ago) and below 0.2 time (0.19 times a year ago), respectively, as on March 31, 2026, respectively. With no debt-funded capital expenditure planned in the near term, the capital structure should remain strong, supported by healthy accretion to reserves. Furthermore, the debt protection metrics are likely to be strong, with an interest coverage ratio expected to be 10.5–11 times in fiscal 2026 (7 times a year ago), backed by steady operating profitability and low interest expenses. The overall financial risk profile is expected to remain healthy over the medium term. 

### **<u>Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses</u>** 

**Moderate scale of operations:** Although the group is expected to show a compound annual growth rate of 20–22% over the three fiscals through fiscal 2026, the revenue is expected to be modest at Rs 1150–1250 crore in fiscal 2026, due to intense competition from larger players, which have extensive product portfolios, strong product development capabilities, and economies of scale. The company has booked a revenue of Rs 840 crore until December 2025 and has an unexecuted order book of Rs 800 crore; however, its ability to ramp up operations quickly with the launch of new high-demand products, as well as the continuous addition of new customers, will remain monitorable. 

**Exposure to intense competition and volatility in raw material prices:** The main raw material includes copper and aluminum which has seen lot of volatility in the past. These raw materials form ~75-80% of overall RM cost and any sharp variations can impact the company’s operating performance. However, the company’s strong pricing power wherein majority of RM price changes are passed on to the customer, mitigates the risk to an extent. The conductors and cables industry is highly fragmented, which restricts the ability of players to command pricing and grow rapidly, thus constraining profitability. The profitability remains vulnerable to any sharp and sudden commodity price fluctuations; however, with sound policies to 

hedge the volatility in the prices of raw materials, the company’s operating margin rose to ~10.92% as of December 2025. Sustenance of operating margin at 10-11% amid improvement in revenue will therefore remain a key rating sensitivity factor. 

# **<u>Liquidity</u>** <u>Strong</u> 

Bank limit utilization averaged 23.7% over the 12 months through December 2025. Cash accrual is expected to be Rs 80– 90 crore in fiscal 2026, compared to Rs 74.7 crore in fiscal 2025, which is sufficient to meet the term debt obligations of Rs 1-2 crore over the medium term and will cushion liquidity, helping to fund incremental working capital requirements. The current ratio is expected to be healthy at 2.4–2.6 times as of March 31, 2026. The group also held mutual funds worth Rs 42.8 crore as of March 31, 2025, which are sufficient to fund any capital expenditure (capex) requirements over the medium term. Strong gearing and net worth provide financial flexibility to withstand adverse conditions or business downturns. Crisil Ratings expects internal accruals, cash & cash equivalents and unutilized bank lines to be sufficient to meet its repayment obligations as well as incremental working capital requirements. 

# **<u>Outlook</u>** <u>Stable</u> 

DCL will continue to benefit from the extensive experience of the promoters and their established relationships with clients. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors:** 

Sustained increase in operating income to Rs 1,300-1,400 crore aided by volume growth and sustenance of operating margin leading to higher net cash accrual 

Sustenance of financial risk profile and working capital cycle 

# **Downward factors:** 

Decline in revenue or fall in operating margin below 7% leading to lower net cash accrual Stretched working capital cycle or large, debt-funded capex affecting the financial risk profile and liquidity 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Set up in 1986 as a partnership firm -- Dynamic Engineers -- by the Mangal family, the entity got reconstituted into a privatelimited company in 2007 and was converted into a public-limited entity with the current name in 2017. The company manufactures conductors and cables such as low-voltage, medium-voltage and high-voltage power cables, aerial bunches cables, aluminum conductors (steel-reinforced and aluminum alloy conductors) and railway signaling cables. It has three manufacturing facilities at Jaipur in Rajasthan. Mr Rahul Mangal and his brother, Mr Ashish Mangal, manage the business.
