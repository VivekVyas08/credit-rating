### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ratings on the bank facilities of Knowledge Marine & Engineering Works Limited (KMEW) and subsequently **withdrawn** the ratings at the company’s request and on receiving no-objection certificate from the lender. This is in line with the Crisil Ratings withdrawal policy. 

The ratings reflects the extensive experience of KMEW promoters in the shipping industry along with the established track record, healthy orderbook, sound operating efficiency and healthy financial risk profile of the company. These strengths are partially offset by susceptibility to risks related to the tender-based business and timely execution of orders and dependence on the shipping and maritime industry. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has consolidated the business and financial risk profiles of KMEW with its subsidiaries — KMEW offshore Pvt Ltd, Indian Ports Dredging Pvt Ltd, Knowledge Infra Ports Pvt Ltd, Knowledge Marine Co WLL and Knowledge Dredging Co WLL. This is because these subsidiaries are strategically important to KMEW and have a significant degree of operational integration with it.

### **<u>Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description</u>** 

### **Strengths:** 

**Extensive experience of the promoters along with established track record:** The promoter family has been associated with the shipping industry for over 15 years. They have established a strong relationship with port authorities, including Kolkata Port Trust, Vishakhapatnam Port Trust, Deendayal Port Trust and Dredging Corporation of India. Moreover, the company has demonstrated robust execution capabilities in terms of quality and pace, which has resulted in a strong reputation. Also, the promoters have strong technical know-how and healthy track record in shipping ancillary segments – chartering of speed boats and dredging of ports. Established market position should continue to support the business risk profile over the medium term. 

**Sound operating efficiency:** The operating margin has remained healthy at above 30% over the four fiscals through 2025. The operating efficiency should remain robust at 35-40%, driven by high economies of scale and in-house capabilities to refurbish vessels, which provides competitive advantage over peers. Although operating margins dropped to 30% in fiscal 2024, the same recovered in fiscal 2025 to around 39%. Sustenance of healthy operating margins remains a key monitorable. Return on capital employed ratio stood at 23.4% in fiscal 2024 and is expected to remain at 17.5-20% over the medium term. 

**Strong financial risk profile:** Networth was around Rs 219 crores as on March 31, 2025, with gearing and total outside liabilities to adjusted networth (TOLANW) ratio of 0.6 time and 0.8-0.85 time. Despite planned debt-funded capital expenditure (capex), gearing and TOLANW ratio are expected to remain below 1 time over the medium term owing to low reliance on external funds to fund the working capital requirement and steady accretion to reserve. The company has further received board approval for raising funds through QIP and the same is expected to be incurred in fiscal 2026. Debt protection metrics were comfortable with interest coverage ratio over 8 times and net cash accrual to adjusted debt ratio 

of 0.4-0.5 time in fiscal 2025. This is due to moderate leverage and healthy operating profits. The financial risk profile is likely to remain strong over the medium term. 

# **Weaknesses:** 

**Modest scale of operations and susceptibility to risks related to the tender-based business:** Revenue declined in fiscal 2024 to around Rs 164 crore but bounced back to around Rs 201 crore in fiscal 2025. While the scale of operations is expected to improve over the medium term, driven by healthy orderbook of Rs 1,000 crore as of July 2025, timely execution of orders leading to sustained growth in scale of operations will be monitorable. The company operates in a tender-based industry, which has predefined criteria with respect to track record and physical infrastructure. Business certainty depends on timely order execution, which in turn relies on various external factors such as customer clearances and any change at their end. 

**Dependence on the shipping and maritime industry:** The shipping industry handles 95% of the international trade volume and almost 70% of overall trade volume of India. Despite being intensely competitive, the industry has improved owing to the recent economic recovery. Better performance of end-user industries prompts customers to invest in modernisation of infrastructure and technology systems. Hence, entities in the segment are dependent on the business outlook of customers. 

**Large working capital requirement:** Gross current assets (GCAs) were high at 230-240 days as on March 31, 2025. Receivables increased to 130-135 days as on March 31, 2025, compared with 96 days a year ago owing to large revenue booking during the year-end. Inventory remained low. Sustenance of the working capital cycle through timely payment from customers will remain monitorable over the medium term. 

# **<u>Liquidity: Adequate</u>** 

Annual cash accrual is projected at Rs 70-80 crore in fiscals 2026 and 2027 against yearly debt obligation of Rs 15-30 crore. Unencumbered cash and cash equivalent stood at Rs 45 crore as on March 31, 2025. The fund-based limit was utilised at 46% on average for the 10 months through May 2025. The surplus cash available in cash accrual and bank limit, along with cash and cash equivalent, will aid financial flexibility. 

# **<u>Outlook: Positive</u>** 

KMEW will continue to benefit from the extensive experience of its promoters and expected increase in the scale of operations, driven by healthy orderbook. 

# **<u>Rating Sensitivity Factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors:** 

Sustained growth in revenue with sustenance of operating margin above 35%, leading to healthy cash accruals Sustenance of strong financial risk profile 

# **Downward factors:** 

Lower-than-expected revenue or profitability, resulting in cash accrual below Rs 55 crore 

Large, debt-funded capex or substantial stretch in the working capital cycle, leading to deterioration in the liquidity profile 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

KMEW, incorporated in 2015, provides dredging services, owning and operating marine craft, and repairing, maintaining and refitting marine crafts and marine infrastructure. Its head office is in Mumbai and operations are spread across major ports in the country through branch offices in Kolkata, Vishakapatnam, Kandla, Vadinar and Sittwe, Myanmar. Saurabh Daswani and Kanak Kewalramani are the promoters.
