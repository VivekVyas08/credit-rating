### **<u>Detailed Rationale</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has revoked the suspension of its ratings on the bank facilities of Sumeet Industries Ltd (SIL) and assigned its **'Crisil BB+/Stable/Crisil A4+’** ratings to the bank facilities of SIL. 

Crisil Ratings had suspended the ratings on July 11, 2012, on account of non-cooperation by SIL with the efforts of Crisil Ratings to undertake a review of the ratings. SIL has now shared the requisite information enabling Crisil Ratings to assign its ratings. 

The ratings reflect the extensive experience of the promoters in the textile - manmade industry along with integrated operations and prudent working capital management of the company. These strengths are partially offset by average financial risk profile, exposure to volatility in raw material and fuel prices and competition from large players, restricting pricing power. 

### **<u>Analytical Approach</u>** 

Crisil Ratings has evaluated the standalone business and financial risk profiles of SIL. 

### **<u>Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description</u>** 

### **Strengths:** 

**Extensive experience of the promoters and integrated operations:** SIL is an integrated producer of pet chips, partially oriented yarn/fully draw yarn (POY/FDY), texturised and carpet yarns. The company has been operating for more than three decades, having long track record of operations. SIL was acquired by the Eagle group from erstwhile promoters -- Sumeet Shankarlal Somani and his family members, through a resolution plan approved by The National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) on July 16, 2024, and acquisition completed in December 2024. 

The Eagle group and its promoters have more than three decades of experience in the textile - manmade industry. Their strong understanding of market dynamics and healthy relations with customers and suppliers should continue to support the business. The Eagle group is a Surat (Gujarat)-based business conglomerate with an established presence in the textile and polyester yarn industry, spearheaded by Mr Radheshyam Jaju. The group undertakes manufacturing and trading of polyester filament yarn, POY and related textile intermediates, catering to the Surat and south Gujarat textile markets. SIL has successfully completed its backward integration cum expansion project and now producing 300 tonne of polyester yarns (POY and FDY) per day. The company further plans to enhance its manufacturing capacity of both POY and FDY by introducing bright and nylon yarns. 

**Prudent working capital management:** Gross current assets have been 89-99 days over the past three fiscals and were 99 days as on March 31, 2025, driven by debtors of 31 days and inventory of 39 days. The company needs to extend long credit period in line with the industry standards; also, customers are small and medium-sized players who require credit. Furthermore, it holds large work in process and inventory to meet business requirement. 

### **Weaknesses:** 

**Average financial risk profile:** Networth stood at Rs 157 crore as on March 31, 2025, with a resolution plan involving a significant haircut to creditors and fresh equity infusion. The capital structure is average, with gearing of 0.63 time and 

total outside liabilities to tangible networth ratio of 1.73 times as on March 31, 2025. However, the debt-funded capital expenditure (capex) planned for fiscal 2026 is expected to deteriorate these ratios to ~1.27 times and ~2.35 times, respectively, as on March 31, 2026. Debt protection metrics may continue to be average, with interest coverage ratio of ~2.5 times and net cash accrual to adjusted debt ratio of ~0.12 time for fiscal 2026. 

**Susceptibility to volatility in raw material and fuel prices:** Raw material cost accounts for 80-85% and fuel costs comprise 10-11% of the total operating income. The prices of key inputs, monoethylene glycol and pure terephthalic acid, are linked to crude price, which is volatile and continues to constrain the business risk profile. This is mitigated by moderate inventory. The operating margin was 1.25% in fiscal 2025 but may improve by ~5% in the coming fiscals owing to improvement in raw material prices, capex related to plant and machinery addition and modification and capex towards the solar power plant, which will increase consumption of in-house captive power. However, any increase in the operating margin during the coming fiscals shall remain a key monitorable. 

- **Exposure to competition from large players, restricting pricing power:** The polyester yarn industry is highly competitive with multiple large players and many small and medium-sized manufacturers, which constrains SIL’s ability to dictate favourable prices, thereby constraining profitability. The top-five players -- Reliance Industries Ltd (RIL; 'Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’), Indo Rama Synthetics, Century Enka Ltd (‘Crisil A+/Stable/Crisil A1+’), JBF Industries Ltd and Garden Silk Mills -- hold more than 65% of the industry capacity. RIL alone accounts for close to 40% of the POY market. The large and integrated players control the pricing of the products, while the smaller players are generally price takers and have to bear the brunt of any price reductions by the large players. Though intense competition limits pricing power, large scale and integrated operations of the company partially offset this risk. 

# **<u>Liquidity: Adequate</u>** 

Bank limit utilisation was 66.69% for the seven months through June 2025. Cash accrual is projected at more than Rs 24 crore per annum, against debt obligation of Rs 3.2 crore in fiscal 2026 and Rs 17.3 crore in fiscal 2027; the surplus cash will act as cushion to liquidity. Current ratio stood healthy at 1.65 times on March 31, 2025. The company has settled most of its legacy dues and fresh liabilities are limited. However, cash flow from operations remains low and working capital needs may rise as operations scale up. Further, the company had an unencumbered fixed deposit of Rs 10.45 crore as of June 2025. The promoters are likely to extend need-based funds (equity and unsecured loans) to aid operations. 

# **<u>Outlook: Stable</u>** 

SIL will continue to benefit from the extensive experience of its promoters and their established relationship with clients. 

# **<u>Rating sensitivity factors</u>** 

# **Upward factors:** 

Healthy revenue growth and operating margin over 5%, leading to better net cash accrual Timely completion of ongoing project and ramp-up of operations 

Sustained working capital cycle and improvement in financial risk profile, including liquidity 

# **Downward factors:** 

Subdued revenue growth or operating margin below 2.5% resulting in lower net cash accrual Stretch in the working capital cycle or large, debt-funded capex or capital withdrawal Time or cost overrun in the project 

# **<u>About the Company</u>** 

Incorporated as Sumeet Synthetics Pvt Ltd in 1988, the entity was converted into a public-limited company in 1992; it got its current name in 1996. SIL was acquired by the Eagle group from erstwhile promoters – Mr Sumeet Shankarlal Somani and his family members -- through a resolution plan approved by NCLT on July 16, 2024. 

SIL manufactures POY and fully FDY, polyester chips and texturising yarn. The manufacturing unit is located at Karanj (Surat), with production capacity of 1,00,800 tonne per annum (TPA) for continuous polycondensation; 52,500 TPA for POY; 45,500 TPA for FDY; 5,400 TPA for texturising yarn; and 2,700 TPA for recycled chips. 

The company’s shares are listed on the Bombay Stock Exchange and National Stock Exchange. 

Mr Radheshyam Bhawarlal Jaju, Mr Pratik Rajesh Jaju and Mr Rohan Dipak Modh are the promoters.
